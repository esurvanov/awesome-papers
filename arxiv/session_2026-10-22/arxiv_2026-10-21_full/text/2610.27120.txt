Abstract

Multimodal question answering (MMQA) is based on integrating heterogeneous data sources, selectively leveraging relevant modalities, and ignoring distractors. Existing approaches based on Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) improve performance by splitting the task into intermediate steps, but do not quantify how each modality contributes to reaching the final answer. We argue that quantifying the effect that each modality has on the answering process improves process traceability and final performance. Thus, we propose Entropy-Driven Multimodal Answering (EDMA), which uses logical entropy to formalise multimodal QA as an iterative process of entropy reduction and to quantify the contribution of each modality in distinguishing correct from incorrect candidate answers. This enables the construction of quantifiable answering trajectories, where each step is associated with a measurable entropy reduction. EDMA introduces (i) modality-conditioned partitioning to estimate unimodal entropy reduction, (ii) entropy-driven multimodal fusion to capture complementary information across modalities, and (iii) entropy-based selection of the answering trajectory that minimises the most logical entropy. Evaluated across standard MMQA benchmarks, EDMA outperforms competitive prompting baselines, with average gains of 12.8 F1 and 4.6 LLM-J points over Planning Prompting and 18.6 F1 and 8.3 LLM-J points over Chain-of-Thought. Moreover, EDMA outperforms self-consistency baselines and is competitive with the multi-agent system baseline. Moreover, we show that higher entropy correlates with more false positives and, through controlled trajectory selection, demonstrate that logical entropy serves as an effective, monotonic selector for correct reasoning paths, validating entropy level as a reliable proxy for performance. 11

## 1 Introduction

Multimodal question answering (MMQA) requires the integration of heterogeneous data sources such as text, images, and tables Talmor et al. (2021); Luo et al. (2023); Foroutan et al. (2025). For example, answering the question “What is the state in the Contour Airlines city destinations, that has a tall metal arch on a river?” requires identifying visual evidence of a metal arch in images while simultaneously retrieving structured information from tables to determine the destinations of the airline, and combining the evidence.

Recent Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have shown strong performance on such tasks by leveraging unified representations across modalities Gao et al. (2023); Zhang et al. (2025a), and are currently being employed to solve domain-specific problems Li et al. (2025a); Yang et al. (2025); Xia et al. (2024); Wang et al. (2024a); Zhao et al. (2025). Several techniques have been proposed to improve multimodal inference, most notably Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting Wei et al. (2022); Zhang et al. (2023); Zheng et al. (2023), which decomposes complex tasks into intermediate steps. Building on this paradigm, extensions such as compositional prompting Mitra et al. (2024), knowledge-augmented reasoning Mondal et al. (2024), PEAR-CoT Li et al. (2025b), frameworks such as Chain-of-Action (CoA) Pan et al. (2024) and Visualization-of-Thought (VoT) Wu et al. (2024) have been proposed to address limitations in compositionality, grounding, and spatial awareness. To further improve the inference process, recent work has incorporated uncertainty quantification, enabling models to adaptively seek information or refine intermediate steps Hu et al. (2024); Yin et al. (2024); Liang et al. (2024). However, while these approaches improve robustness by structuring the inference process in intermediate steps, augmenting contextual information, and guaranteeing higher robustness, they do not provide explicit stepwise quantification of how individual modalities and their fusion contribute to reducing uncertainty over the space of candidate answers. As a result, it remains unclear how different modalities contribute to the inference process, limiting interpretability and controllability in multimodal settings.

To address these limitations, we propose Entropy-Driven Multimodal Answering (EDMA), a methodology that models QA as a process of iterative entropy reduction over candidate answers. As Figure 1 shows, EDMA quantifies the impact of incorporating a specific modality and fusing modalities by measuring how this refines the space of possible answers. Compared to CoT, which splits the answering process in steps without quantifying the contribution of each modality, EDMA builds quantifiable answering trajectories, where each step and each modality is associated with a measurable contribution in the reduction of entropy. At the core of EDMA lies logical entropy Ellerman (2021), which measures distinctions between possible outcomes. This formulation is particularly well-suited for multimodal QA, where the objective is to progressively separate correct from incorrect candidate answers based on evidence from different modalities. By framing answering as partition refinement, EDMA treats each piece of evidence as a condition that splits the current answer space into two subsets of answers: those that satisfy the evidence and those that do not. This systematic partitioning provides a natural and interpretable mechanism for tracking exactly how information from each modality contributes to narrowing down the final prediction.

We evaluate EDMA on challenging multimodal QA benchmarks Talmor et al. (2021); Hannan et al. (2020) and show that it outperforms best-practice prompting baselines and, on average, state-of-the-art multi-agent systems, while providing traceable uncertainty estimates that guide modality selection and the answering process. Our contributions are as follows:

• 

EDMA, a methodology that formalizes multimodal QA as modality-conditioned entropy minimization over the space of alternatives.

• 

A modality-conditioned entropy reduction objective that enables explicit measurement of each modality’s contribution.

• 

We establish the relation between entropy minimization and lower false positives (FPs) levels, thus validating entropy minimization as a proxy for performance.

## 2 Entropy-Driven Multimodal Answering (EDMA)

## 2.1 Problem formulation

The problem setting involves one question $Q$ , the set of modalities available to answer the question denoted by $M$ and, for each $m\in M$ , the elements associated with that modality denoted by $\Omega_{m}$ , and one Answerer denoted by $LLM$ . To answer each question, the $LLM$ must find the modalities of interest, find the elements within those modalities that contain the necessary information to answer the question, and then combine the information to provide a cross-modal answer, denoted by $a^{*}$ .

## 2.2 Overview of EDMA

Figure 2 shows that EDMA is composed of four stages: (a) prediction of the modalities to be used and extraction of answer requirements from the question, (b) generation of unimodal partitions and calculation of unimodal logical entropy, (c) generation of the multimodal partitions with paired multimodal logical entropy, and (d) answer generation by selecting the answering trajectory that minimized the most the logical entropy. The process allows to reduce at each step the number of elements which represent the possible alternatives by checking which ones fit the requirements extracted from the question, returning an answer based on the answering trajectory that minimised the most the logical entropy. In Algorithm 1 in Appendix B, we describe EDMA’s answering process.

Figure 2: EDMA is composed of four stages: (a) modality prediction and extraction of answer requirements, (b) generation of unimodal partitions and calculation of unimodal logical entropy, (c) generation of multimodal partitions and calculation of multimodal logical entropy, (d) selection of the answering trajectory which minimises the logical entropy and generation of the answer.

## 2.3 Extraction of Answer Requirements

Supposing we want to answer the question “What is the state in the Contour Airlines city destinations, that has a tall metal arch on a river?”, EDMA begins by extracting the set of answer requirements, representing the characteristics that the final answer $a^{*}$ must satisfy. We denote the answer requirements as $R$ . As shown in Figure 2, the $LLM$ generates $R$ by identifying the key elements of the question. In this specific case, the final answer has to: (i) be a State, (ii) be a destination of Contour Airlines, and (iii) be characterized by a Metal Arch. We denote the extraction of $R$ as $R=LLM(Prompt_{R},Q)$ , where the $LLM$ receives in input the user instructions $Prompt_{R}$ and the question $Q$ . In this case, $R=\{S,C,A\}$ , where we use $S$ to point to State, $C$ for Contour Destination, and $A$ for Metal Arch.

## 2.4 Prediction of Starting Modalities

EDMA predicts the subset of modalities $M_{s}\subseteq M$ representing the modalities with which to start the analysis. This phase does not exclude the possibility of incorporating additional modalities later in the answering process. The $LLM$ receives the input, the user instructions $Prompt_{M_{s}}$ , the question $Q$ , and for each modality $m$ , the corresponding set of elements $\Omega_{m}$ . It is then tasked with predicting the combination of modalities that is most likely to contain the elements needed to reach the correct answer. This operation can be summarized as $M_{s}=LLM(Prompt_{M_{s}},Q,\{\Omega_{m},m\in M\})$ . The modalities $M_{s}$ are then added to $M_{a}$ , which is the list that keeps track of the modalities used for the analysis. In Figure 2, the predicted modalities are Table (T) and Image (I).

## 2.5 Entropy-Driven Answer Search

To identify the correct answer in a multimodal setting, EDMA models inference as an entropy-driven search over the space of candidate answers. It quantifies how individual modalities and their interactions reduce uncertainty over the answer space. To this end, EDMA first induces partitions over the answer space using unimodal evidence and then refines these partitions through sequential cross-modal conditioning. This process enables systematic measurement of entropy reduction from both unimodal and multimodal evidence.

Quantifying Uncertainty of Answer Partitions

We adopt logical entropy Ellerman (2021), which measures uncertainty in terms of distinctions induced by a partition of a candidate set. Let $S$ denote a finite set of candidate elements and let $P_{S}=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\}$ be a partition of $S$ into disjoint non-empty blocks. To ensure compatibility across open-source and closed-source models without requiring calibrated probability estimates from the underlying model, we assume a uniform distribution over $S$ , such that each block has probability mass $p_{i}=\frac{|B_{i}|}{|S|}$ . Logical entropy is defined as $H(P_{S})=1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{2}$ , and can be interpreted as the probability that two independently sampled elements from $S$ belong to different blocks.

For example, consider $|S|=10$ and a binary partition $P_{S}=\{S_{r},S_{\neg r}\}$ induced by a requirement $r$ . The set $S_{r}$ contains elements whose information overlaps with $r$ , while $S_{\neg r}$ contains elements that do not. If $|S_{r}|=5$ and $|S_{\neg r}|=5$ , then $H(P_{S})=0.50$ , corresponding to maximal entropy for a binary partition under a uniform prior. If instead $|S_{r}|=1$ and $|S_{\neg r}|=9$ , then $H(P_{S})=1-(0.1^{2}+0.9^{2})=0.18$ , reflecting reduced uncertainty due to the increased concentration of mass.

In our multimodal QA setting, the answering process is modeled as a sequence of partition refinements over $S$ , where information extracted from the elements of each modality introduces distinctions that separate relevant from irrelevant candidates. We restrict attention to valid modality-induced refinements where the predicate-satisfying subset is non-empty, $S_{r}\neq\emptyset$ , ensuring that entropy reduction is grounded in non-vacuous evidence.

Logical entropy thus quantifies the effectiveness of each modality in refining the answer space, allowing to prioritize modalities that maximize entropy reduction, guiding the system toward increasingly discriminative evidence.

Generation of Unimodal Partitions

EDMA analyses the elements $\Omega_{m}$ of each modality $m\in M_{a}$ . For each element $\omega\in\Omega_{m}$ , the $LLM$ evaluates, for each answer requirement $r\in R$ , whether $\omega$ contains information overlapping with $r$ . This operation can be formally expressed as $LLM(Prompt_{Check},\omega,r)$ . Based on this evaluation, EDMA constructs, for each set $\Omega_{m}$ and requirement $r$ , a partition $P_{m}^{r}=\{\Omega_{m}^{r},\Omega_{m}^{\neg r}\}$ , where $\Omega_{m}^{r}$ contains elements whose information overlaps with $r$ , and $\Omega_{m}^{\neg r}$ contains elements that do not. Under the assumption of uniform distribution over elements in $\Omega_{m}$ , the logical entropy of $P_{m}^{r}$ is:

 $H(P_{m}^{r})=1-\left(\left(\frac{|\Omega_{m}^{r}|}{|\Omega_{m}|}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{|\Omega_{m}^{\neg r}|}{|\Omega_{m}|}\right)^{2}\right)$ 

(1)

This formulation expresses logical entropy as a function of partition cardinalities, enabling estimation without relying on confidence estimates from the $LLM$ . Logical entropy measures the distinction between elements that satisfy a given condition and those that do not. It attains its global minimum of $0$ when either $\Omega_{m}^{r}=\emptyset$ or $\Omega_{m}^{\neg r}=\emptyset$ . Otherwise, it is strictly positive and is minimized when one subset is highly concentrated (i.e., when a single candidate is clearly distinguished).

In Figure 2, unimodal partitions illustrate how different modalities contribute to distinguishing relevant from irrelevant candidates. The image-based partition $P_{I}^{\text{$A$}}$ yields a relatively low entropy ( $0.38$ ), indicating that the visual modality already provides a strong separation by isolating the candidate satisfying the requirement $A$ . In contrast, the table-based partitions exhibit higher entropy values (e.g., $0.49$ for the $S$ requirement), reflecting a weaker discriminative effect when considered in isolation. Since lower entropy corresponds to sharper distinctions among candidates, this comparison indicates that the image modality is more informative at this stage for narrowing the answer space. EDMA leverages this signal to prioritize modalities that induce stronger reductions in uncertainty, guiding the answering process toward partitions that more effectively constrain the set of plausible answers.

Generation of Multimodal Partitions

Once unimodal partitions $P_{I}^{\text{$A$}}$ , $P_{T}^{\text{$S$}}$ , $P_{T}^{\text{$C$}}$ are constructed, EDMA proceeds to generate multimodal partitions. This step leverages information extracted from multiple modalities to refine candidate elements, progressively reducing logical entropy, with the aim of isolating the correct candidate(s), filtering out the alternatives that are inconsistent with cross-modal evidence, which is information extracted from one modality used to reduce the answer space in another modality. In a multimodal setting, the final answer typically resides in a specific modality, referred to as the target modality, denoted by $m_{t}$ . However, the target modality is not known in advance. To identify it, EDMA systematically treats each modality in turn as a candidate target modality $m_{t}$ , while the remaining modalities $M_{c}=M_{a}\setminus\{m_{t}\}$ serve as conditioning modalities.

For each candidate $m_{t}$ , EDMA applies information from all conditioning modalities sequentially. For each conditioning modality $m\in M_{c}$ and the subset $\Omega_{m}^{r^{\prime}}$ containing elements that satisfy a requirement $r^{\prime}\in R$ , EDMA extracts the cross-modal evidence set:

 $E_{m}=\Big\{\,e\;\big|\;e=LLM(Prompt_{Extract},Q,\omega),\ \omega\in\Omega_{m}^{r^{\prime}}\,\Big\}$ 

(2)

Each piece of evidence represents information from the conditioning modality $m$ and has the same function as the answer requirements. EDMA applies the elements of this set sequentially to iteratively refine the candidates in the target modality $m_{t}$ that satisfy requirements $r\in R$ , where $r\neq r^{\prime}$ . This allows evidence from one modality (e.g., visual) that satisfies one requirement to be used to filter candidates in another modality (e.g., tabular) that satisfy a distinct requirement.

Specifically, for a given requirement $r\in R$ , the target subset $\Omega_{m_{t}}^{r}$ is updated sequentially for each conditioning modality. At each step, EDMA generates a partition $P_{m_{t}|m}^{r,E_{m}}=\{\Omega_{m_{t}}^{r,E_{m}},\Omega_{m_{t}}^{r,\neg E_{m}}\}$ of the elements of modality $m_{t}$ consistent with requirement $r$ , where $\Omega_{m_{t}}^{r,E_{m}}$ contains the elements that are also consistent with the evidence $E_{m}$ from the current conditioning modality, and $\Omega_{m_{t}}^{r,\neg E_{m}}$ contains those that are not. Only the subset $\Omega_{m_{t}}^{r,E_{m}}$ is carried forward to the next iteration, while $\Omega_{m_{t}}^{r,\neg E_{m}}$ is excluded from further conditioning, as it cannot lead to the correct answer. This sequential conditioning progressively narrows the answer space of $m_{t}$ , increasing the distinction among the remaining candidates, thus decreasing logical entropy.

By evaluating the multimodal logical entropy after each step, EDMA identifies which $m_{t}$ ultimately yields the partition with the lowest entropy, where distinctions among candidates are maximized, and uncertainty is minimized. Conditional or multimodal logical entropy is computed analogously to the unimodal case:

 $H\Big(P_{m_{t}|m}^{r,E_{m}}\Big)=1-\left(\left(\frac{|\Omega_{m_{t}}^{r,E_{m}}|}{|\Omega_{m_{t}}|}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{|\Omega_{m_{t}}^{r,\neg E_{m}}|}{|\Omega_{m_{t}}|}\right)^{2}\right)$ 

(3)

In the example in Figure 2, cross-modal conditioning yields asymmetric effects depending on the direction of information flow. When table-derived constraints ( $E_{\text{$T$}}$ ) are applied to the image modality ( $P_{I}^{\text{$A$}}$ ), entropy remains unchanged ( $0.38$ ), indicating limited additional discriminative power beyond visual cues. In contrast, conditioning the table modality ( $P_{T}^{\text{$C$}}$ ) using visual evidence ( $E_{\text{$I$}}$ ) significantly reduces entropy ( $0.44\rightarrow 0.20$ ), showing that the image provides high-value filtering information that effectively disambiguates table entries, and showing that EDMA does not treat modalities symmetrically, but instead identifies directional entropy reduction, where certain modalities act as stronger selectors of relevant candidates if the derived information is applied in the right order.

## 2.6 Answer Generation

Trajectory Selection via Entropy Minimization

The systematic generation of unimodal and multimodal partitions effectively maps a set of quantifiable answering trajectories $(\mathcal{T})$ , representing paths in the search process. Each trajectory provides a quantifiable history of how information is fused and how logical entropy changed after the modality fusion. This allows for a modality-level attribution of the logical entropy variation. A trajectory may remain within a unimodal setting ( $m\to m$ ) or bridge modalities to form a multimodal trajectory ( $m\to m_{t}$ ), where the first modality conditions the second $(m_{t}|m)$ . We denote $\Delta H=\frac{H(P_{m_{t}}^{r})-H(P_{m_{t}|m}^{r,E_{m}})}{H(P_{m_{t}}^{r})}$ , the relative change between unimodal entropy and multimodal entropy for modality $m_{t}$ . If $\Delta H>0$ , modality fusion increases the distinguishability among candidates, thereby reducing uncertainty. If $\Delta H\leq 0$ , the conditional modality provides redundant or non-discriminative information for that specific answering path.

In Figure 2, EDMA identifies four answering trajectories, of which two are unimodal and two are multimodal. The unimodal trajectories are represented by $P_{I}^{\text{$A$}}$ (initial $H=0.38$ ) and $P_{T}^{\text{$C$}}$ (initial $H=0.44$ ). The Table-Image Trajectory starts with partition $P_{I}^{\text{$A$}}$ and ends with partition $P_{\text{$I$|$T$}}^{\text{$A$},E_{\text{$T$}}}$ , where table data regarding destinations conditions the image set, resulting in a final entropy of $H=0.38$ with $\Delta H=0\%$ . In contrast, the Image-Table Trajectory, where visual evidence of the Metal Arch conditions the table rows, starts with partition $P_{T}^{\text{$C$}}$ and ends with partition $P_{\text{$T$|$I$}}^{\text{$C$},E_{\text{$I$}}}$ , reducing logical entropy from $H=0.44$ to $H=0.20$ , achieving a significant entropy decrease of $\Delta H=54\%$ , a reduction attributed to the image modality’s influence on the table modality.

As EDMA prioritizes the trajectory whose final partition yields the lowest logical entropy $(t^{*})$ , it selects the Image–Table Trajectory, isolating the table entry corresponding to the city satisfying requirement $C$ , which is also associated with an image from which EDMA extracts visual evidence of the arch. The multimodal partition at the endpoint of $t^{*}$ is chosen as the basis for the final answer.

Final Inference

Once the optimal trajectory, modality, and partition are selected, the $LLM$ receives the question $Q$ , the satisfied requirements, the cross-modal evidence $E^{*}$ (e.g., the context of the arch), and the relevant elements $\Omega^{*}$ that satisfy both the requirements and the cross-modal evidence (e.g., the specific row for St. Louis). Based on this information, the system returns the final answer $a^{*}$ (e.g., Missouri). If the $LLM$ cannot produce an answer (i.e., $a^{*}=\text{None}$ ), EDMA selects a new set of modalities $M_{s}\subseteq M\setminus M_{a}$ , where the newly selected modalities are not contained in the already analysed modality set. EDMA then repeats the process: generating new partitions, recomputing entropy deltas, and attempting to produce the answer with the newly acquired information. The iteration continues until a valid answer is obtained or all modalities are exhausted.

Crucially, by constructing and selecting among quantifiable answering trajectories, EDMA provides a principled mechanism to guide cross-modal inference. Even when SOTA approaches arrive at the same final answer, EDMA makes explicit how that answer is obtained, enabling quantifiable modality-level attribution.

## 3 Experiments

## 3.1 Experimental Setup

We evaluate our methodology across a diverse set of SOTA LLMs, including both closed source models, such as GPT-5.2 OpenAI (2025), Kimi-K2.5 Team et al. (2026), Nova-2-Lite-v1 (Nova-2-Lite) Services (2025), and Claude-Sonnet-4.6 (Claude-4.6) Anthropic (2026), and open source models, such as Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (Qwen3) Bai et al. (2025), and Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2 (Nemotron) NVIDIA (2025). Appendix E reports how to access models and the resources needed to run them, while Appendix K reports the prompts to implement EDMA’s operations.

Baselines

We compare our approach against SOTA multimodal QA system MAMMQA Anvekar et al. (2025) and against several common best-practice prompting strategies: Direct Prompting (DP), where the model generates an answer without explicit intermediate steps, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Wei et al. (2022), which encourages step-by-step generation of intermediate steps within a single generation, and Planning Prompting (PP) Wang et al. (2023), where the model first generates a plan and subsequently executes it, CoT-SC Wang et al. (2022) and PP-SC which improve the greedy versions of CoT and PP by generating multiple reasoning chains and returning the majority-vote final answer.

Datasets

We evaluate our methodology on MultiModalQA Talmor et al. (2021) and ManyModalQA Hannan et al. (2020) datasets which remain, to the best of our knowledge, among the most complex benchmarks for multimodal QA, requiring cross-modal integration across text, tables, and images. They both require deep joint inference over diverse data formats to arrive at a final answer, and thus provide a rigorous testbed for evaluating a model’s ability to integrate and reason over heterogeneous information sources.

Evaluation Metrics

We evaluate all methods using Exact Match (EM) and F1 score, following standard practice in multimodal QA Talmor et al. (2021); Yu et al. (2023); Lim et al. (2024). EM is a strict binary metric that assigns a score of 1 if the predicted answer exactly matches the ground-truth string and 0 otherwise. The F1 score measures token-level overlap between the predicted and reference answers, balancing precision and recall. Because EM and F1 can be overly penalizing for semantically correct open-ended generations, in our evaluation we also include an LLM-Judge (LLM-J) metric Majumdar et al. (2024).

## 3.2 EDMA Performance Evaluation

Here we report the performance of EDMA across benchmarks. We first compare EDMA to standard prompting baselines (DP, CoT, and PP) and then we evaluate EDMA against budget-matched baselines.

## 3.2.1 Comparison between EDMA and Prompting Baselines

Table 1 summarises the performance of EDMA alongside standard prompting baselines across six representative MLLMs. EDMA consistently outperforms Direct Prompting (DP), Chain-of-Thought (CoT), and Planning Prompting (PP) across all evaluated backbones and datasets. On MultiModalQA, EDMA achieves an average score of $80.1$ EM, $81.1$ F1, and $79.3$ LLM-J, outperforming the strongest prompting baseline (PP) by $+22.7$ EM, $+14.0$ F1, and $+8.5$ LLM-J points. On ManyModalQA, EDMA reaches $83.0$ EM, $83.3$ F1, and $89.1$ LLM-J, improving over PP by $+23.0$ EM, $+11.5$ F1, and $+0.6$ LLM-J. Across both benchmarks, EDMA achieves average gains of $+22.9$ EM, $+12.8$ F1, and $+4.6$ LLM-J over the strongest prompting baseline. The smaller LLM-Judge gain on ManyModalQA (+0.6 vs +8.5 on MultiModalQA) stems from lower benchmark complexity, limiting EDMA’s possible improvement space. Nevertheless, EDMA’s strong leads in EM (+23.0) and F1 (+11.5) on ManyModalQA confirm that entropy-guided decomposition remains essential for precise entity extraction even on simpler queries.

## 3.2.2 Comparison between EDMA and Budget Matched Baselines

To determine whether EDMA’s gains result merely from increased inference computation, we evaluate EDMA on MultiModalQA against budget-matched self-consistency baselines (CoT-SC and PP-SC with $N=6$ samples) and the multi-agent framework MAMMQA. As shown in Table 5 (Appendix E), EDMA is approximately $6\times$ computationally more expensive than single-pass CoT and PP, making $N=6$ a direct budget-matched baseline. Furthermore, EDMA’s computational footprint is comparable to MAMMQA, establishing it as an additional budget-matched baseline. We evaluate MAMMQA using exclusively LLM-J, as its verbose answer style would unfairly penalize it under exact-matching metrics like EM and F1.

## 3.3 Qualitative Example

Figure 3: 
Qualitative comparison of CoT and EDMA. EDMA answers both questions correctly, isolating information in the unimodal setting before bridging modalities in the multimodal step, while CoT returns incorrect answers.

We attribute the performance gains achieved by EDMA to its staged reasoning process. Specifically, EDMA first decomposes the answer space into unimodal partitions before extracting explicit cross-modal evidence to construct combined multimodal partitions. This progressive refinement allows the model to isolate and verify relevant information within individual modalities prior to cross-modal integration and trajectory selection, which may improve the reliability of evidence aggregation and reduce ambiguity in cross-modal alignment. In contrast, standard prompting paradigms such as CoT and PP depend on the model capacity to implicitly manage decomposition and multimodal integration within a single inference stream, which may limit their ability to consistently isolate and align evidence across modalities.

Figure 3 illustrates this contrast on two multi-hop examples. In the first, CoT misidentifies the winner of the 33rd TCA Award for Outstanding New Program. EDMA instead isolates the tabular context to identify “This Is Us” as the winner, and then queries the secondary modality to retrieve the correct broadcasting network (NBC). In the second example, CoT retrieves the tabular context (the series “Talking Heads”) but its prediction is not grounded in the visual modality. EDMA conditions its visual query on the tabular entry and correctly aligns the image, identifying the depicted activity as writing.

## 3.4 False Positives Correlate with Higher Entropy

Here we analyse the relationship between entropy and prediction errors to assess the effectiveness of EDMA, examining how entropy correlates with the occurrence of FPs across target modalities. Figure 4(a) shows that across all modalities, FPs are consistently associated with higher entropy values compared to true positives (TPs). This indicates that incorrect predictions tend to occur in regions of higher uncertainty, where the candidate answer space remains insufficiently constrained. In contrast, correct predictions are more likely to arise when entropy is lower, reflecting a more decisive partitioning of the answer space.

## 3.5 Answering Trajectories with Lower Entropy Yield Better Answers

To further show the connection between entropy level and FPs, we perform a controlled intervention analysis to assess whether entropy directly impacts prediction performance. We generate three candidate answers per question by selecting answering trajectories corresponding to the lowest, median, and highest entropy levels. The low-entropy trajectory corresponds to the one selected by EDMA in the main experiments. This design keeps the input question fixed while varying the selected trajectory, and hence its entropy. We evaluate prediction reliability using the False Discovery Rate (FDR), defined as the proportion of FPs among all positive predictions. We compute FDR separately for each entropy group across modalities. Figure 4(b) shows a consistent monotonic trend: low-entropy trajectories yield the lowest FDR, while medium- and high-entropy trajectories exhibit progressively higher error rates. This pattern is consistent across all target modalities, indicating a strong association between higher logical entropy and reduced prediction reliability. By systematically varying the selected trajectory while keeping the input query fixed, we observe a robust degradation in performance as entropy increases. These results support the importance of entropy minimization in EDMA: lower-entropy trajectories are associated with fewer false positives and improved prediction performance. Tables 15–16 in Appendix I, report data regarding statistical significance.

## 4 Related work

MultimodalQA

Multimodal question answering (MMQA) has been introduced as a challenging task requiring the integration of heterogeneous data sources such as text, tables, and images Talmor et al. (2021). Subsequent work has focused on improving representation and integration across modalities, including unified architectures such as UniMMQA Luo et al. (2023), Solar Yu et al. (2023), retrieval-augmented approaches such as UniRAG Lim et al. (2024), and conversational extensions like MMConvQA Li et al. (2022b). More recent approaches leverage LLMs and MLLMs to enhance multi-step inference capabilities, for instance by combining language and vision models Liu et al. (2025), improving cross-modal alignment through similarity computation Zhang et al. (2025b), or framing information synthesis as a multi-agent process, as in MAMMQA Anvekar et al. (2025).

LLMs in MultimodalQA

LLMs have been widely adopted to enhance multimodal QA, often through extensions of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. Prior work includes Chain-of-Action (CoA) Pan et al. (2024), knowledge-augmented CoT (KAM-CoT) Mondal et al. (2024), duty-distinct CoT (DDCoT) Zheng et al. (2023), and frameworks for structured CoT such as UniCoTT Zhuang et al. (2025). These approaches improve inference by decomposing complex tasks into intermediate steps or incorporating external knowledge. Additional adaptations target specific modalities, such as AVQA-CoT for audio-visual QA Li et al. (2024) and methods that leverage textual signals to guide multimodal understanding Wang et al. (2024b). Despite these advances, existing CoT-based approaches do not provide explicit mechanisms to quantify the extent to which each modality contributes to the final prediction.

Uncertainty as Guidance

Recent work has explored incorporating uncertainty quantification into LLM reasoning to improve robustness and decision-making. Hu et al. Hu et al. (2024) propose Uncertainty of Thoughts, which evaluates alternative planning paths based on their expected uncertainty reduction. Yin et al. Yin et al. (2024) introduce uncertainty-aware adaptive guidance, an approach that relies on uncertainty signals from the LLM to guide the reasoning process. Liang et al. Liang et al. (2024) propose introspective planning to align model uncertainty with task ambiguity, while Bayat et al. Bayat et al. (2024) leverage uncertainty estimates to guide answer selection. While these approaches treat uncertainty as a global signal for improving reasoning, they do not provide fine-grained, modality-level attribution of uncertainty reduction, limiting their effectiveness in multimodal QA settings.

## 5 Limitations

EDMA’s worst-case complexity is $O(n^{3})$ when modalities, requirements, and elements grow together. In practice this translates into many LLM calls per question, costing between $5$ and $6.4\times$ more and taking between $20$ and $46\times$ longer than the prompting baselines (Appendix E). Our budget-matched baselines match its monetary cost, but not its latency or number of calls, and a full accuracy–cost analysis is left to future work.

Logical entropy is computed under a uniform distribution over elements, which avoids calibrated probabilities but ignores differences in the relevance of individual elements.

Finally, our methodology does not explicitly address scenarios in which different modalities provide conflicting information. One possible extension would be to partition the answer space based on parallel interpretations of the available evidence, treating them as distinct answering trajectories, which would let the model explore potentially conflicting paths and evaluate their outcomes independently.

## 6 Conclusion

This paper introduces Entropy-Driven Multimodal Answering (EDMA), a novel methodology for multimodal QA based on logical entropy minimization and the construction of quantifiable answering trajectories.

By measuring how each step reduces logical entropy, EDMA quantifies the contribution of every modality, improving traceability. Our analyses show that false positives are associated with higher entropy, and that choosing the lowest-entropy trajectory tends to lead to better answers, making entropy a useful proxy for performance. Overall, on the same backbones, EDMA improves over the baselines it is compared against.

## Appendix B Algorithm

The EDMA algorithm has a worst-case computational complexity of $O(M\cdot R\cdot K+M^{2}\cdot R)$ , where $M$ denotes the number of modalities, $R$ the number of requirements, and $K$ the maximum number of elements per modality. Stage 1 (Unimodal Partitioning) contributes $O(M\cdot R\cdot K)$ , evaluating each of the $K$ elements per modality against each requirement, and also caches the cross-modal evidence extracted for each $(m,r)$ pair. Stage 2 (Cross-Modal Sequential Conditioning) contributes $O(M^{2}\cdot R)$ , refining each of the $M\cdot R$ target pairs by conditioning on the remaining $M$ modalities via $O(1)$ lookups into this cache. Under $M\sim R\sim K\sim n$ , the overall complexity is $O(n^{3})$ .

## Appendix C Experimental Setup

## C.1 Baselines

In this section, we provide detailed descriptions of the baselines used in our experiments. We categorize these into multimodal architectures and prompting-based strategies.

## C.1.1 Multimodal QA Systems

MAMMQA [30]

A multi-agent QA framework for multimodal inputs spanning text, tables, and images. The system comprises two Vision-Language Model (VLM) agents and one text-based LLM agent. The first VLM decomposes the query into sub-questions to sequentially retrieve partial evidence across modalities, the second VLM synthesizes intermediate insights via cross-modal reasoning, and the LLM aggregates the findings into a cohesive final answer.

MultiModalQA [1]

The baseline pipeline introduced alongside the MultiModalQA benchmark. It employs modality-specific information extractors combined with an ImplicitDecomp module to perform multi-step question decomposition and answer synthesis across text, tables, and images.

UniMMQA [2]

A unified multimodal QA framework designed to reduce the modality gap by projecting heterogeneous data (text, tables, and images) into a shared representation space, enabling integrated cross-modal retrieval and answer generation.

Solar [33]

A sequence-to-sequence multimodal framework that unifies visual and textual token processing within a single transformer architecture, optimized for cross-modal alignment through joint pre-training objectives.

UniRAG [34]

A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework that unifies evidence retrieval across textual, visual, and tabular corpora before feeding the aggregated context into a Large Multimodal Model (LMM) for answer generation.

ManyModalQA [23]

The baseline framework introduced with the ManyModalQA benchmark. It incorporates an explicit modality routing mechanism to determine the most relevant data source (text, image, or table) prior to applying specialized extraction modules to return the final answer.

## C.1.2 Prompting Strategies

Direct Prompting (DP)

A standard zero-shot prompting approach where the model receives the input context and query, and directly outputs the final answer without explicit intermediate reasoning steps.

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) [11]

A prompting method that prompts the model to generate a sequence of intermediate natural language reasoning steps before outputting the final answer within a single generation stream.

Planning Prompting (PP) [31]

A two-stage prompting technique that decouples reasoning planning from execution: the model first generates a high-level plan of sub-tasks, and subsequently executes each sub-task to derive the final answer.

CoT Self-Consistency (CoT-SC) [32]

An ensemble strategy that samples $N$ independent Chain-of-Thought reasoning paths and selects the final answer via majority voting.

PP Self-Consistency (PP-SC)

An extension of self-consistency [32] applied to Planning Prompting, sampling $N$ independent plan-and-execute reasoning trajectories and selecting the answer via majority voting.

## C.2 Datasets

MultiModalQA [1] is a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate a model’s ability to perform joint integration across three distinct data modalities: structured tables, natural language text, and images. Unlike earlier multimodal datasets where the answer usually resides within a single source (e.g., just an image or just a paragraph), MultiModalQA is specifically engineered to require multi-hop reasoning. We report results on the validation set since ground-truth annotations for the test set are not publicly accessible.

To further show the generalizability of our methodology, we test it also on ManyModalQA [23], which is a multimodal question-answering benchmark designed to evaluate an agent’s ability to reason across three distinct data types: textual passages, structured tables, and visual images. Unlike standard multimodal datasets where the target modality is often implied by the question, ManyModalQA contains ambiguous questions where the source of the answer cannot be determined by the question text alone.

## Appendix J Impact Assessment

The introduction of the EDMA methodology enhances the ability of LLMs to solve cross-modal problems by guiding the answering process through entropy minimization and producing a quantifiable answer trajectory. This traceability allows users to understand which modalities contributed most to the final answer, improving the interpretability and reliability of LLM predictions in real-world scenarios. By providing structured answering trajectories, EDMA also makes LLM-based systems more robust and trustworthy. While privacy and trustworthiness are general concerns associated with LLMs, our methodology does not introduce additional risks beyond those already inherent to LLMs.

## K.2.2 Prompt for Chain-of-Thought

Table 33: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompt used in our experiments.

System Prompt

You are a multimodal question answering assistant. Your task is to answer a given question using only the provided information sources:
text paragraphs, images (with titles), and tables. You must not rely on any external or prior knowledge. If the provided information is insufficient, output None.

Procedure to follow:

• 

Step 1. Extract information: Identify relevant facts from each modality: text (key statements), images (objects, labels, relationships), and tables (rows, columns, values).

• 

Step 2. Align information: Match entities and concepts that appear across different modalities and explicitly link them.

• 

Step 3. Integrate information: Combine the aligned evidence to form a complete understanding, ensuring that all parts of the question are addressed.

• 

Step 4. Derive the answer: Formulate the final answer, clearly grounded in the combined evidence from the modalities.

• 

Step 5. Verify: Ensure that the answer is fully supported by the provided information and that no relevant modality has been ignored.

Output the reasoning separated from the final response. The final response should be concise and contain only the answer, without any explanation.

User Prompt

Question: {question_text}

Images: {images_text}

Text Paragraphs: {paragraphs_text}

Tables: {tables_text}

Answer the question following the procedure above.
