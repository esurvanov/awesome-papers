Abstract

Industrial fluid transport systems are monitored through heterogeneous sensing streams, but robust fault diagnosis remains difficult because response signals vary with operating conditions and differ from physical variables in sampling rate, observation duration and data granularity. To address these challenges, we propose a physical-variable-informed deep learning framework (PhyNet) for robust fault diagnosis under real-world sampling heterogeneity. PhyNet treats physical variables as global operating constraints and response signals as local diagnostic evidence. Its physical-to-response cross-attention mechanism connects sequence-level physical-variable tokens with a learnable global response token, enabling interaction without strict temporal alignment. Across four benchmarks, including three real-world cavitation datasets and one public two-phase-flow dataset, PhyNet achieves the best performance across all evaluated metrics. Class-wise analyses, together with attention and masking analyses, show improved discrimination of ambiguous states and indicate that physical variables meaningfully constrain local response interpretation. These findings demonstrate a deployment-oriented approach to robust diagnosis in heterogeneous industrial environments.

## 

Industrial fluid transport systems, including control valves, pumps and pipelines, are critical infrastructure for energy, chemical and manufacturing industries, whose safe operation underpins continuous production [1]. When faults occur and persist within the system, they can exacerbate vibration, noise and erosion, potentially leading to fluid leakage and even cascading accidents [2, 3, 4]. Although these systems are increasingly monitored using continuous, multi-source and high-precision sensing, reliable fine-grained diagnosis remains challenging because fault precursors are subtle and monitoring signals are strongly affected by operating-state variations [5, 6, 7]. For instance, characteristic signals during incipient cavitation are often weak and can be easily masked by noise and operational fluctuations. The same fault intensity can also produce different response patterns as load, flow rate or temperature changes, degrading deployed models [5, 8].

Existing approaches primarily address this challenge along three directions, but each exhibits inherent industrial limitations [9]. Data-driven models typically employ convolutional neural networks (CNNs) or Transformers to learn discriminative features and patterns from response signal measurements in an end-to-end manner [10, 11, 12]. However, these methods easily latch onto condition-specific representation patterns and learn spurious correlations, resulting in weak cross-condition generalization when operating regimes change. Physics-driven methods generally utilise conventional machine-learning models to predict system states based on routinely recorded physical variables during operation [13, 14, 15, 16]. While these methods offer clearer interpretability and are more sensitive to operational variations, they can hardly detect weak fault precursors and discriminate fine-grained intensity levels due to their reliance on macro-grained operating parameters. Hybrid multimodal models demonstrate significant potential in artificial intelligence by fusing two or more data sources [17, 18]. These methods often assume temporal alignment and consistent input granularity [19]. In industrial monitoring scenarios, physical variables and high-frequency response signals commonly exhibit mismatched sampling rates, inconsistent sequence lengths and complex missingness patterns, making these assumptions difficult to satisfy [20, 21]. Therefore, merely applying existing hybrid multimodal models can obscure critical causal cues and introduce misalignment noise, limiting their straightforward transfer and reliable deployment in industrial systems.

High-frequency response measurements contain detailed local evidence of fault-related dynamics, but their statistical properties are strongly modulated by operating conditions, making representations that rely solely on response signals susceptible to non-stationary variations. In contrast, physical variables summarize the system operating conditions and provide physical constraints at the level of fault mechanisms, determining the physical plausibility of a fault occurrence and its manifestation in response signals. Their integration provides two complementary functions. Contextual disambiguation separates genuine fault signatures from benign fluctuations induced by changing operating conditions, while physical regularization encourages representations that remain compatible with system-level mechanisms. Both functions are particularly important for fine-grained diagnosis, where weak precursors can resemble random disturbances and where the same local response may have different meanings under different operating states. Because physical variables are often sampled sparsely or irregularly and may cover different durations, an effective framework should use them as global constraints or causal cues that modulate local time-frequency responses without one-to-one correspondence.

To address this issue, we propose a physical-variable-informed deep learning framework (PhyNet), which enables robust and resilient diagnosis of industrial fluid transport systems under real-world sampling heterogeneity. PhyNet follows an asymmetric representation principle, treating physical variables as global constraint carriers and signal responses as local diagnostic evidence. In detail, PhyNet embeds each physical variable sequence as a sequence-wise variate token, which accommodates differences in sampling frequency and observation duration without requiring pointwise temporal alignment. In addition, PhyNet represents high-sampling-rate response signals as local response tokens embedded within time-frequency spectrograms, capturing localized spectral-temporal patterns associated with fault intensity and state transitions. To bridge heterogeneous token granularities, PhyNet introduces a learnable global response token that aggregates macroscopic information from response patches and serves as a compatible interface for physical variable interactions. Meanwhile, PhyNet develops a physical-to-response cross-attention module to inject constraints and causal cues from physical variable tokens into the global response token. Subsequently, the updated global token propagates these constraints and causal information to individual local response tokens via a self-attention mechanism. Without requiring strict alignment between physical variable sequences and response signals, PhyNet achieves physically constrained diagnosis, effectively mitigating information misalignment and the resulting noise while improving robustness across operating conditions. It provides a structured and novel route for fusing physical variables and response signals in deployment-oriented industrial diagnosis.

We evaluate PhyNet across four fluid-transport benchmarks. Our primary case study uses real-world cavitation datasets (Cavitation-Short/Long/Noise) provided by SAMSON AG, which pairs acoustic measurement responses with routinely recorded operational physical variables and covers multiple cavitation operating states under both noisy and noise-free conditions. Beyond cavitation phenomena, we further evaluate PhyNet on one additional fluid-transport benchmark with heterogeneous perception and operating-condition variations, namely VTPFR, an annular-flow image dataset capturing vertical gas-liquid two-phase flow regime dynamics and transitions. These benchmarks collectively cover representative monitoring tasks and diverse physical phenomena in industrial fluid transport systems, reflecting substantial variability arising from operational condition changes and noise-source interference. Across all scenarios, integrating physical variables as global constraints consistently improves diagnostic robustness without rigid alignment. More generally, PhyNet converts irregular and unaligned physical variables into actionable context for local response interpretation, providing a deployment-oriented solution for realistic fluid-transport monitoring. This enables reliable and robust fault diagnosis in fluid transport scenarios under realistic operational variability and offers potential for extension to other industrial settings.

## Architecture of PhyNet

Figure 1a illustrates heterogeneous monitoring in industrial fluid transport systems, where high-frequency responses and conventional physical variables are collected by different sensors. Although different modalities collectively characterise the system’s operational state, they are inherently heterogeneous in sampling frequency, observation duration and information granularity. To address this challenge, PhyNet adopts an asymmetric representation strategy, treating physical variables as carriers of global operating-condition constraints and regarding high-frequency response signals as local diagnostic evidence. As shown in Figure 1b, each physical-variable sequence is first encoded as a sequence-wise variate token, whereas the time-frequency representation of the response signal is partitioned into multiple local patches and encoded as response tokens. In addition, a learnable global response token is introduced to serve as a bridge for cross-modal interaction. Subsequently, physical variables are injected into the global response token via a physical-to-response cross-attention module, and the resulting global contextual information is then propagated back to the local response representation via a response self-attention mechanism, enabling physically informed diagnosis without requiring strict one-to-one alignment between modalities. This design treats physical variables as global context and mechanistic constraints that shape the interpretation of local time-frequency evidence under heterogeneous monitoring conditions. Consequently, the two sensing streams retain their original observational structures rather than being forced into a common temporal grid, which is important when their sampling rates and durations differ substantially.

## Experiments and Performance

We systematically evaluate PhyNet on four representative datasets, including three real-world cavitation datasets from SAMSON AG (Cavitation-Short, Cavitation-Long and Cavitation-Noise) and one additional public fluid transport dataset (VTPFR). These datasets encompass acoustic measurements, image observations, different noise levels and substantial operating-condition variation, providing a testing environment for assessing model generalization in heterogeneous industrial settings. In addition, the experimental comparisons incorporate three categories of representative baselines, comprising data-driven, physics-driven and hybrid data-physics methods. A uniform data-splitting strategy is applied to all datasets, with 80.00 $\%$ of the samples used for training and the remaining 20.00 $\%$ for testing. To prevent data leakage, sliding-window augmentation is performed only after the training and testing sets have been separated. Benchmark, experimental setting and metric details are provided in the “Methods” section.

As shown in Table 1, PhyNet achieves the best performance across all four datasets. It attains accuracies of 93.72 $\%$ , 96.59 $\%$ , 100.00 $\%$ and 96.30 $\%$ on Cavitation-Short, Cavitation-Long, Cavitation-Noise and VTPFR, respectively, while also ranking first in precision, recall and F1-score on all four datasets. This indicates that the advantage is not confined to a single evaluation measure or benchmark, but reflects a systematic improvement in overall discriminative performance across sensing modalities and tasks.

Table 1 shows that the strongest data-driven baselines (Swin-B, PerViT-T, UniFormer-S and DenseNet161) achieve peak accuracies of 90.37 $\%$ , 93.90 $\%$ , 98.71 $\%$ and 89.95 $\%$ on Cavitation-Short, Cavitation-Long, Cavitation-Noise and VTPFR, respectively, which are 3.35 $\%$ , 2.81 $\%$ , 1.29 $\%$ and 6.35 $\%$ lower than PhyNet. For example, on Cavitation-Short, PhyNet improves precision, recall and F1-score over the strongest data-driven baseline (PerViT-T and Swin-B) by 3.04 $\%$ , 3.35 $\%$ and 3.86 $\%$ , respectively. On VTPFR, PhyNet attains precision, recall and F1-score of 96.32 $\%$ , 96.30 $\%$ and 96.29 $\%$ , representing improvements of 6.08 $\%$ , 6.35 $\%$ and 6.47 $\%$ over the strongest data-driven baseline (CFFormer-L and DenseNet161), respectively. These gains indicate that response-only models can learn local patterns and temporal dependencies but remain more susceptible to non-stationary effects under operating-condition variation, noise interference and modality heterogeneity. By contrast, PhyNet interprets local response evidence within a more stable physical context, leading to consistent improvements across all four metrics.

Traditional machine learning methods based solely on physical variables reach best accuracies of 90.00 $\%$ , 93.17 $\%$ , 99.36 $\%$ and 73.02 $\%$ across the four datasets, which are consistently 0.64 $\%$ to 23.28 $\%$ lower than those of PhyNet. For example, on Cavitation-Long, PhyNet improves upon the strongest physics-driven baseline by 5.86 $\%$ , 5.63 $\%$ and 5.86 $\%$ in precision, recall and F1-score, respectively. On VTPFR, PhyNet outperforms the strongest physics-driven baseline by 24.04 $\%$ , 20.43 $\%$ and 23.90 $\%$ in precision, recall and F1-score, respectively. Thus, physical variables provide useful operating context and clearer interpretability but are insufficient alone for fine-grained localized state identification. PhyNet addresses this limitation by retaining discriminative response evidence while using physical variables to constrain its interpretation.

Although hybrid data-physics methods (ViLT, Perceiver, PerceiverIO and MulT) also employ both original response measurements and physical variables, their best accuracies remain at 91.99 $\%$ , 94.03 $\%$ , 99.40 $\%$ and 92.59 $\%$ across four datasets, which are 1.73 $\%$ , 2.56 $\%$ , 0.60 $\%$ and 3.71 $\%$ lower than PhyNet, respectively. Meanwhile, the strongest hybrid baselines (MulT, Perceiver and PerceiverIO) reach F1-scores of 91.32 $\%$ , 93.92 $\%$ , 99.40 $\%$ and 92.71 $\%$ on Cavitation-Short, Cavitation-Long, Cavitation-Noise and VTPFR, all below the corresponding values of 93.57 $\%$ , 96.55 $\%$ , 100.00 $\%$ and 96.29 $\%$ , respectively. These results indicate that the critical factor is not simply “whether physical variables are introduced”, but “how they are incorporated”. Existing hybrid models can process both modalities, but they often still rely implicitly or explicitly on aligned inputs or unified granularity. PhyNet instead uses a global response token and physical-to-response cross-attention to inject physical constraints at the representation level, reducing information mismatch and fusion noise under heterogeneous sampling.

In particular, the improvements of PhyNet are more pronounced on Cavitation-Long, Cavitation-Noise and VTPFR, which are more susceptible to operational drift, prolonged observation durations or complex perceptual variation. These results show that physical variables not only serve as a supplementary information source under heterogeneous industrial observation conditions, but also as a manipulable global operating context that guides the interpretation of local response representations. The improvement therefore reflects a fusion strategy tailored to real-world sampling heterogeneity rather than the simple addition of another modality.

## Mechanistic and robustness analysis

## Class-wise errors and ROC curves

To further determine whether the performance improvement of PhyNet arises from better class-level discrimination, we compare its confusion matrices and ROC curves with those of the strongest hybrid baseline (PerceiverIO) on two representative datasets (Cavitation-Long and VTPFR) with longer observation durations, more pronounced operational variations or more complex perceptual structures, as shown in Figure 2. Corresponding results for the other datasets are provided in the Supplementary Information.

The confusion matrices reveal that the performance gains of PhyNet are concentrated on the most ambiguous and error-prone classes. On Cavitation-Long, both models achieve high accuracy in classifying choked flow cavitation, constant cavitation and non-cavitation, as shown in Figures 2a and 2b. However, PerceiverIO exhibits substantial confusion for incipient cavitation with only 58.16 $\%$ of samples correctly assigned to the diagonal and 30.61 $\%$ misclassified as constant cavitation. By contrast, PhyNet increases the correct recognition rate of incipient cavitation to 77.89 $\%$ and reduces the corresponding confusion with constant cavitation to 11.11 $\%$ , indicating a markedly improved separation of early and adjacent cavitation states under prolonged observation and operating-condition variation. On VTPFR, the largest improvement is observed for the bubbly regime, whose diagonal entry increases from 76.79 $\%$ under PerceiverIO to 94.64 $\%$ under PhyNet. A similar improvement is observed for the slug regime, which rises from 89.47 $\%$ to 94.74 $\%$ , whereas the Taylor bubble regime maintains perfect recognition under both models. This illustrates that the primary advantage of PhyNet lies in reducing confusion among visually or physically adjacent states to mitigate interference from these states under heterogeneous operating conditions.

The ROC curves further support this conclusion by providing a threshold-independent view of discriminative performance. For Cavitation-Long, the AUC for incipient cavitation increases from 0.9568 with PerceiverIO to 0.9877 with PhyNet, whereas the other classes remain close to the theoretical ceiling under both methods. In addition, the micro-average AUC improves from 0.9904 to 0.9945. For VTPFR, PhyNet exhibits superior ROC performance, with the largest improvement observed for the bubbly regime, whose AUC increases from 0.9445 to 0.9899 and the micro-average AUC rises from 0.9703 to 0.9836. These results indicate that the advantage of PhyNet is not tied to a specific classification threshold, but reflects more robust class separability over a broader range of decision thresholds.

## Condition-aware attention

To elucidate how physical variables modulate response representations, we examine the physical-variable attention patterns learned by the physical-to-response cross-attention module on Cavitation-Long, as shown in Figure 3. Representative high-confidence and low-margin boundary samples from choked-flow cavitation, constant cavitation, incipient cavitation and non-cavitation show that PhyNet consistently assigns the highest weights to vapour pressure $p_{v}$ , with additional contributions from volumetric flow rate $Q$ and pressure recovery factor ${X_{f}}$ , as shown in Figures 3a and 3b. This core pattern remains clearly visible even in boundary samples, indicating that the model relies on a set of physically meaningful operating variables when distinguishing between adjacent and difficult states. The class-averaged statistics in Figure 3c further confirm that vapour pressure $p_{v}$ dominates across all flow states, whereas the relative contributions of volumetric flow rate $Q$ and pressure recovery factor ${X_{f}}$ vary with flow conditions. This observation is highly consistent with the physical mechanisms of cavitation. Cavitation is initiated when the local static pressure falls to or below the vapour pressure $p_{v}$ , making the vapour pressure $p_{v}$ a direct thermodynamic threshold for bubble inception. The volumetric flow rate $Q$ strongly affects local velocity and pressure drop through the constriction and therefore modulates the likelihood and intensity of cavity formation. The pressure recovery factor ${X_{f}}$ characterizes the relationship between upstream pressure $p_{u}$ , downstream pressure $p_{d}$ and vapour pressure $p_{v}$ , which is widely used to describe the operating pressure condition associated with cavitation onset and development. Therefore, the dominant contribution of vapour pressure $p_{v}$ and the secondary contribution of volumetric flow rate $Q$ and pressure recovery factor ${X_{f}}$ are not only findings supported by the data, but also consistent with established hydrodynamic cavitation theory [26, 27] (A detailed definition of cavitation is provided in the Supplementary Information). Head-wise visualizations further show that these averaged patterns arise from structured head specialization rather than from uniform weighting across heads (see Supplementary Information).

To determine whether these attention patterns are functionally relevant, we further relate them to diagnostic performance under physical-variable masking. As shown in Figure 3d, the accuracy declines progressively as increasing numbers of physical variables are randomly masked, with the median falling from 0.95 when one or two physical variables are masked to approximately 0.50 when seven variables are masked. This indicates that PhyNet’s diagnosis depends on the overall physical context rather than any single variable. The mean importance of physical variables further ranks vapour pressure $p_{v}$ as the dominant variable (0.673), followed by the pressure recovery factor ${X_{f}}$ (0.189) and volumetric flow rate $Q$ (0.107), whereas the remaining variables contribute only marginally, as shown in Figure 3e. In addition, Figure 3f shows that single-variable masking causes the largest performance drops when the pressure recovery factor ${X_{f}}$ , vapour pressure $p_{v}$ or volumetric flow rate $Q$ is masked, reducing accuracy from 96.59 $\%$ to 67.07 $\%$ , 70.47 $\%$ and 80.60 $\%$ , respectively. By contrast, masking valve stroke $h$ , upstream pressure $p_{u}$ and fluid temperature $T$ , results in only modest degradation. These results further demonstrate that the physical-to-response cross-attention module captures functionally relevant and physically meaningful operating constraints rather than relying on a single dominant variable, and that its learned importance pattern is consistent with cavitation physics.

We present gradient-based localization maps on spectrograms of Cavitation-Long for the full model and PhyNet without physical-to-response cross-attention, as shown in Figure 4. This suggests that physical variables not only provide auxiliary decision cues, but also actively reshape both the distribution and interpretation of local response evidence.

A similarly strong degradation is observed for naive fusion, which attains only 83.76 $\%$ accuracy and 82.85 $\%$ F1-score, corresponding to declines of 12.83 $\%$ and 13.70 $\%$ , respectively. Because this variant still utilises both modalities while removing the encoder-level asymmetric conditioning in PhyNet, this result indicates that the critical factor is not simply whether physical variables are introduced, but how they are incorporated. In particular, late fusion without token-level conditional processing is insufficient to recover the benefits of physically informed response interpretation.

The remaining ablation experiments further elucidate the role of the internal interaction mechanism. The variant without the learnable global response token reduces the accuracy to 85.71 $\%$ and the F1-score to 85.29 $\%$ , indicating that direct coupling between physical variable tokens and local response tokens is less effective than the semantically compatible global-interface interaction. The physical-response cross-attention module replaced with a simple injection module also results in an accuracy reduction to 87.88 $\%$ and an F1-score decline to 88.03 $\%$ , indicating that explicit cross-attention is necessary for effective physical conditioning. Conversely, when the propagation process from global constraints back to local response tokens in the response self-attention mechanism is removed, the accuracy and F1-score of the model decrease to 87.95 $\%$ and 87.60 $\%$ , respectively. The comparable degradation of the without cross-attention and without self-attention propagation variants suggests that simply injecting physical constraints into the global token is insufficient. These constraints must also be redistributed to local time-frequency evidence to achieve robust diagnostics. In summary, these results collectively support PhyNet’s central asymmetric fusion strategy, whereby physical variables are introduced into the model as global constraints via a dedicated semantic bridge and subsequently propagated back to local response representations. Compared with fusion applied only at the classifier stage or with the injection of physical information through simplified global updates, the strategy of PhyNet enables the diagnostic value of physical variables to be exploited more effectively.

## Robustness analysis

To further assess the robustness of PhyNet under variations in signal preprocessing, model configuration and acquisition conditions, we conduct a series of sensitivity experiments, as shown in Figure 5. First, we report the influence of time-frequency preprocessing on Cavitation-Short, as shown in Figures 5a-5c. In the window duration experiments, a window duration of $0.06\text{\,}\mathrm{s}$ obtains the highest accuracy of 93.72 $\%$ , whereas substantially shorter or longer windows reduce performance to 86.65 $\%$ at $0.01\text{\,}\mathrm{s}$ and 90.15 $\%$ at $0.3\text{\,}\mathrm{s}$ , respectively (see Figure 5a). A similar trend is observed for the window overlap ratio (see Figure 5b), where the default value of 0.25 achieves the best accuracy of 93.72 $\%$ compared with 91.85 $\%$ at 0.50 and 82.46 $\%$ at 0.75. In addition, the results of varying the number of continuous wavelet transform (CWT) scales indicate that 512 scales provide the most effective time-frequency representation with an accuracy of 93.72 $\%$ , whilst 128, 256 and 1024 scales achieve 89.89 $\%$ , 91.08 $\%$ and 92.38 $\%$ , respectively (see Figure 5c). In summary, these results indicate that the diagnostic performance of PhyNet is robust within a reasonable range of preprocessing options, whilst overly coarse or overly redundant time-frequency representations can degrade its performance.

Second, we evaluate the sensitivity of PhyNet to architectural hyperparameters on Cavitation-Short, as shown in Figures 5d-5f. In terms of patch size, PhyNet achieves the best performance with a patch size of 16, outperforming patch sizes of 8, 32 and 64 by 1.64 $\%$ , 0.59 $\%$ and 2.52 $\%$ , respectively (see Figure 5d). Similarly, PhyNet demonstrates relative stability across the optimisation range for the number of attention heads, as depicted in Figure 5e. Specifically, PhyNet attains the highest accuracy with 8 attention heads, whereas the accuracy with 4, 6 and 10 attention heads reduces by 1.01 $\%$ , 0.95 $\%$ and 0.14 $\%$ , respectively. Furthermore, the encoder depth of PhyNet exhibits a comparable pattern, as illustrated in Figure 5f. PhyNet with 3 encoder layers obtains the best performance, remains broadly comparable with 1 or 2 encoder layers and declines markedly when the encoder depth is increased to 4 layers. These results illustrate that the proposed PhyNet does not rely on excessive architectural complexity and its moderate configuration is sufficient to effectively capture the interaction between global physical constraints and local response evidence.

Next, the ability to recognize signals captured by low-cost sensors is important and challenging in practical applications. Therefore, we evaluate PhyNet under the original sampling frequency ( $1\,562\,500\text{\,}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}$ ), one-half, one-quarter, one-sixth, one-eighth of the original sampling frequency ( $781\,250\text{\,}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}$ , $390\,625\text{\,}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}$ , $260\,416\text{\,}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}$ , $195\,312\text{\,}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}$ ) and the maximum frequency typically supported by mobile phones ( $$48\,000\text{\,}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}$\approx\frac{1562500}{32}\,$\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}$$ ), as shown in Figure 5g. As the sampling frequency increases from $48\,000\text{\,}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}$ to $1\,562\,500\text{\,}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}$ , the performance rises steadily from 85.63 $\%$ to 93.72 $\%$ , indicating that finer resolution provides more informative diagnostic evidence and PhyNet demonstrates substantial robustness when data quality fluctuates. Moreover, we assess the robustness of PhyNet under additive noise on Cavitation-Short, as shown in Figure 5h. Specifically, PhyNet exhibits a consistent improvement as the signal-to-noise ratio increases, with its accuracy rising from 85.80 $\%$ at $-5\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}$ to 89.56 $\%$ at $0\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}$ , 90.96 $\%$ at $5\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}$ , 92.55 $\%$ at $10\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}$ , 92.63 $\%$ at $20\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}$ and 93.40 $\%$ at $30\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}$ . This trend indicates that PhyNet remains stable under moderate noise and only deteriorates substantially under very severe signal corruption.

Finally, we evaluate the cross-duration generalization capability of PhyNet between Cavitation-Short and Cavitation-Long, as shown in Figure 5i. When models are trained on short-duration observations and tested on long-duration observations, PhyNet achieves an accuracy of 81.92 $\%$ , representing a 5.52 $\%$ improvement over PerceiverIO. Conversely, when models are trained on Cavitation-Long and tested on Cavitation-Short, PhyNet and PerceiverIO obtain accuracies of 84.05 $\%$ and 79.35 $\%$ , respectively. This demonstrates that the proposed physical variable conditioning and asymmetric fusion strategies not only enhance model robustness to preprocessing and acquisition perturbations, but also improve its adaptability to variations in observation duration. In addition, PhyNet remains stable across a broad range of practical variations, providing a more deployment-oriented diagnosis framework for heterogeneous industrial environments.

## Discussion

The results indicate that the advantage of PhyNet arises not simply from adding another modality, but from changing how heterogeneous modalities interact. Across four industrial fluid-transport benchmarks spanning acoustic and visual sensing, noisy and noise-free conditions, and substantial operating variation, PhyNet achieves the best performance on all evaluation metrics. The gains are particularly pronounced on Cavitation-Long, Cavitation-Noise and VTPFR, which are more affected by operational drift, prolonged observation or perceptual variation. These findings suggest that physical variables can provide a stable context for interpreting local response evidence when treated as global operating constraints rather than conventional tabular inputs. This distinction is important because the observed improvements are not confined to a single dataset or sensing modality, but recur across several forms of acquisition heterogeneity and operating-condition variation.

Several observations explain this behaviour. First, the largest improvements occur in ambiguous and error-prone states. On Cavitation-Long, the correct recognition rate for incipient cavitation increases from 58.16 $\%$ with PerceiverIO to 77.89 $\%$ with PhyNet, while the AUC rises from 0.9568 to 0.9877. On VTPFR, the correct rate for the bubbly regime increases from 76.79 $\%$ to 94.64 $\%$ , accompanied by a substantial AUC improvement. Physical conditioning therefore contributes not only to average performance, but also to discrimination where local response evidence is weakest, most non-stationary or most easily confused with neighbouring states. In this sense, PhyNet reduces reliance on condition-specific response patterns and supports more physically grounded class separation.

Second, attention and masking analyses indicate that PhyNet learns physically meaningful constraints rather than arbitrary multimodal correlations. Physical-to-response cross-attention assigns the highest weights to vapour pressure $p_{v}$ , with volumetric flow rate $Q$ and pressure recovery factor ${X_{f}}$ as the main secondary contributors. This pattern persists across high-confidence prototypes, low-margin boundary samples, class-level averages and specialized attention heads. Its functional relevance is supported by masking experiments: removing $p_{v}$ , $Q$ or ${X_{f}}$ produces the largest performance degradation, whereas masking other variables has smaller effects. The agreement between learned importance and established cavitation mechanisms supports both the diagnostic value and mechanistic plausibility of the captured operating constraints. It also suggests that the model does not distribute attention uniformly across available variables, but selectively emphasizes variables that govern cavitation inception and development.

Third, physical variables influence not only global decisions but also the distribution of evidence within response representations. Compared with the model without physical-to-response cross-attention, full PhyNet produces broader and more structured localization patterns on representative spectrograms. Removing cross-attention fragments or shifts these patterns and often biases cavitation samples towards non-cavitation predictions. The ablation results reinforce this interpretation: removing physical variables, the global response token, explicit cross-attention or subsequent self-attention propagation all substantially reduces performance, while naive fusion remains inferior to the full model. Effective industrial multimodal diagnosis therefore depends not only on what information is incorporated, but also on whether it enters through a semantically compatible and structurally appropriate interaction pathway.

The robustness analysis further highlights the practical relevance of this design. PhyNet remains stable across reasonable choices of window duration, overlap ratio, CWT scales and architectural hyperparameters, and achieves its best results with moderate rather than excessive model complexity. Performance also degrades gradually under reduced sampling frequency and additive noise. Moreover, PhyNet retains a clear advantage over the strongest hybrid baseline during cross-duration transfer between Cavitation-Short and Cavitation-Long. These results indicate resilience to variations in sensing quality, observation duration and preprocessing, all of which commonly occur across industrial systems and deployment sites. Such stability is particularly relevant when high-end sensors, long observation windows or identical acquisition settings cannot be guaranteed in practice.

More broadly, PhyNet provides a route for multimodal learning under industrial sampling heterogeneity. Physical variables are often sparse, asynchronous or low-frequency, whereas response measurements are dense, high-frequency and locally informative. Existing multimodal architectures frequently assume aligned sequences or unified granularities, which are difficult to obtain in practice. PhyNet instead embeds physical variables as sequence-wise variate tokens and couples them with local response evidence through a global response token, incorporating mechanistic operating context without enforcing one-to-one correspondence. Although developed for fluid-transport diagnosis, this principle may extend to other industrial systems that jointly monitor slowly varying operating conditions and rapidly sampled responses. In such settings, global operating variables can constrain the interpretation of local vibration, acoustic, visual or electrical evidence without requiring artificial temporal synchronization.

Although the present results show that PhyNet achieves strong performance and mechanistic consistency, there are several limitations that warrant further discussion. The experiments and mechanistic analyses focus primarily on fluid-transport systems, and validation in other industrial domains is needed to establish broader generalizability. The current framework also formulates diagnosis as supervised classification and does not address open-set recognition, fault-progression modelling or early warning. Although attention, variable masking and response localization provide complementary evidence for the role of physical constraints, more granular intervention studies and uncertainty analyses are required to characterize causal effects and failure boundaries. Future work should therefore examine missing or corrupted physical variables, uncertainty-aware modelling and more diverse deployment environments. Overall, the findings support using physical variables as actionable global context rather than auxiliary side inputs or late-stage features, providing a deployment-oriented approach to robust diagnosis in heterogeneous industrial fluid-transport systems.

## Problem Settings

In diagnosis tasks, a signal response sequence ${\mathbf{x}_{1:T}}=\{{x_{1}},{x_{2}},\ldots,{x_{T}}\}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{T\times 1}}$ is collected from an industrial system with a corresponding set of physical-variable sequences ${\mathbf{V}_{1:{T_{p}}}}=\{\mathbf{v}_{1:{T_{p}}}^{1},\mathbf{v}_{1:{T_{p}}}^{2},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{1:{T_{p}}}^{C}\}\in{\mathbb{R}^{{T_{p}}\times C}}$ , where $x_{i}$ denotes the value at the $i$ -th time point, $\mathbf{v}_{1:{T_{p}}}^{i}$ represents the $i$ -th physical variable, $C$ is the number of physical variables, $T$ and $T_{p}$ are the observation lengths of the signal response and the physical variables, respectively. It is worth noting that any variable capable of providing effective physical constraints or causal information for intensity diagnosis can be used as a physical variable and its temporal length is not required to be identical to the signal response length, i.e. $T\neq{T_{p}}$ . To capture the dynamic properties of the signal response under non-stationary operating conditions and construct subsequent fundamental analysis units, the original signal response sequence $\mathbf{x}_{1:T}$ is split into multiple subsequences ${\mathbf{X}_{1:W_{s}}}=\{\mathbf{x}_{1:W_{s}}^{1},\mathbf{x}_{1:W_{s}}^{2},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{1:W_{s}}^{M}\}\in{\mathbb{R}^{W_{s}\times M}}$ via a sliding-window strategy, and then each subsequence $\mathbf{x}^{i}_{1:W_{s}}$ is converted into a corresponding time-frequency spectrogram $\mathbf{s}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W}$ . The above process is described as follows:

## Response Embedding

Most existing Transformer-based diagnosis models typically embed discrete time points or consecutive signal segments as temporal tokens and apply self-attention to model temporal dependencies. To finely capture the evolution characteristics of signal responses within the time-frequency domain, PhyNet adopts time-frequency spectrogram-based patch-wise representations. Specifically, each time-frequency spectrogram $\mathbf{s}^{i}$ is partitioned into non-overlapping patches $\mathbf{Z}=\{\mathbf{z}^{1},\mathbf{z}^{2},\ldots,\mathbf{z}^{N}\}$ along the time-frequency plane. Each patch $\mathbf{z}^{i}$ is subsequently mapped into a time-frequency response token $\mathbf{R}_{re,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times 1}$ via linear projection. Given the distinct semantic roles of physical variables ${\mathbf{V}_{1:{T_{p}}}}$ and signal responses ${\mathbf{x}_{1:T}}$ in intensity diagnosis tasks, PhyNet employs distinct granularity embedding strategies to model them. To avoid the information misalignment caused by directly fusing physical-variable tokens ${\mathbf{P}_{va}}=\{\mathbf{P}_{va,i}\}_{i=1}^{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times D}$ and time-frequency response tokens ${\mathbf{R}_{re}}=\{\mathbf{R}_{re,i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}$ , PhyNet introduces a learnable global response token $\mathbf{G}_{re}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times 1}$ for each signal response, which aggregates the macroscopic time-frequency representation of the signal and serves as a bridge for interaction between the signal response domain and the physical variable domain. This design facilitates the effective incorporation of causal and constraint information from physical variables into the modeling of time-frequency response patches. Overall, time-frequency response tokens ${\mathbf{R}_{re}}$ and the learnable global response token $\mathbf{G}_{re}$ are concatenated and fed into the Transformer encoder. The overall response embedding is formally expressed as follows:

 $\displaystyle\{{\mathbf{z}^{1}},{\mathbf{z}^{2}},\ldots,{\mathbf{z}^{N}}\}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\text{Patchify}({\mathbf{s}^{i}}),$ 

(2)

 $\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}_{re}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\text{PatchEmbed}({\mathbf{z}^{1}},{\mathbf{z}^{2}},\ldots,{\mathbf{z}^{N}}),$ 

 $\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}_{re}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\text{Learnable}(\mathbf{x}),$ 

 $\displaystyle[{\mathbf{R}_{re}},{\mathbf{G}_{re}}]$ 
 $\displaystyle\to\text{Encoder}(\cdot),$ 

where $\text{Patchify}(\cdot)$ denotes the operation of dividing each time-frequency spectrogram $\mathbf{s}^{i}$ into non-overlapping patches of size $h\times w$ , resulting in a total of $N=\left\lfloor{H/h}\right\rfloor\times\left\lfloor{W/w}\right\rfloor$ patches. $\text{PatchEmbed}(\cdot)$ maps each $h\times w$ patch, combined with its positional embedding, into a $D$ -dimensional vector via a learnable linear projection. $[\cdot,\cdot]$ represents the concatenation operation, and $\text{Encoder}(\cdot)$ denotes the Transformer encoder.

## Physical Variables Embedding

The primary role of physical variables is to provide effective physical constraints and causal information for intensity diagnosis. In real-world industrial systems, different physical variables exhibit different sampling frequencies and observation lengths. To naturally capture the interactions among physical variables while avoiding the computational overhead and unnecessary noise introduced by overly fine-grained representations, PhyNet embeds each physical variable sequence $\mathbf{v}_{1:{T_{p}}}^{i}$ as a sequence-wise variate token $\mathbf{P}_{va,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times 1}$ . This design can adapt to various observation lengths and irregularities, providing a robust global representation of physical information for subsequent interaction with the time-frequency response tokens ${\mathbf{R}_{re}}$ . This process is formally represented as follows:

 $\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}_{va}}=\{\mathbf{P}_{va,i}\}_{i=1}^{C}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\text{VariateEmbed}(\mathbf{v}_{1:{T_{p}}}^{i}),$ 

(3)

 $\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}_{va}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\to\text{Encoder}(\cdot),$ 

where $\text{VariateEmbed}(\cdot)$ maps each physical variable sequence $\mathbf{v}_{1:{T_{p}}}^{i}$ into a $D$ -dimensional embedding vector via a trainable linear projection, $C$ is the number of physical variables, $T_{p}$ is the observation length of the physical variables, and $\text{Encoder}(\cdot)$ denotes the Transformer encoder.

## Response Self-Attention

In the intensity diagnosis task, to effectively model the time-frequency dependencies of signal response sequences and their global interactions with physical variable sequences, PhyNet introduces a self-attention mechanism over the time-frequency response tokens ${\mathbf{R}_{re}}$ . Concretely, self-attention among local time-frequency response tokens ${\mathbf{R}_{re}}$ is employed to capture fine-grained time-frequency relationships within the signal response sequence, i.e. ${\rm{Local}}\to{\rm{Local}}$ , while a learnable global response token ${\mathbf{G}_{re}}$ serves as a bridge between the signal response sequence ${\mathbf{x}_{1:T}}$ and the physical variable sequence ${\mathbf{V}_{1:{T_{p}}}}$ . In cross-domain attention, the global response token ${\mathbf{G}_{re}}$ plays an asymmetric role. On the one hand, it aggregates local time-frequency patch information across the entire signal response sequence to form a macroscopic representation of the signal response sequence, i.e. ${\rm{Local}}\to{\rm{Global}}$ . On the other hand, it provides each local time-frequency token with global constraints and correlational information derived from the physical variables, i.e. ${\rm{Global}}\to{\rm{Local}}$ . This mechanism not only enables a comprehensive characterization of the dynamic evolution of signal responses, but also enhances the interaction with irregular physical-variable sequences. The attention mechanism can be expressed as follows:

 $\displaystyle\text{Local}\rightarrow\text{Local}{\rm{:}}\;\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{re}^{l,1}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\mathrm{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{R}_{re}^{l}+\mathrm{Self\text{-}Attention}(\mathbf{R}_{re}^{l})),$ 

(4)

 $\displaystyle\text{Global}\rightarrow\text{Local}{\rm{:}}\;\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{re}^{l,2}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\mathrm{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{R}_{re}^{l}+\mathrm{Cross\text{-}Attention}(\mathbf{R}_{re}^{l},\mathbf{G}_{re}^{l})),$ 

 $\displaystyle\text{Local}\rightarrow\text{Global}{\rm{:}}\;\tilde{\mathbf{G}}_{re}^{l}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\mathrm{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{G}_{re}^{l}+\mathrm{Cross\text{-}Attention}(\mathbf{G}_{re}^{l},\mathbf{R}_{re}^{l})).$ 

The entire process can be further expressed as a single response self-attention computation:

 $\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{re}^{l},\tilde{\mathbf{G}}_{re}^{l}=\mathrm{LayerNorm}([\mathbf{R}_{re}^{l},\mathbf{G}_{re}^{l}]+\mathrm{Self\text{-}Attention}([\mathbf{R}_{re}^{l},\mathbf{G}_{re}^{l}])),$ 

(5)

where $l\in\{0,1,\ldots,L-1\}$ denotes the $l$ -th encoder layer of PhyNet, with $\mathbf{R}_{re}^{0}=\mathbf{R}_{re}$ , $\mathbf{G}_{re}^{0}=\mathbf{G}_{re}$ . $[\cdot,\cdot]$ represents the concatenation of patch-wise time-frequency response tokens ${\mathbf{R}_{re}}$ and the global response token ${\mathbf{G}_{re}}$ along the sequence dimension. By applying a self-attention mechanism to the concatenated response tokens $[\mathbf{R}_{re}^{l},\mathbf{G}_{re}^{l}]$ , PhyNet can simultaneously model the local dependencies among different time-frequency patches and the global relationships between each patch and the entire signal response sequence, achieving a holistic characterization of the dynamic evolution of signal responses.

## Physical-to-Response Cross-Attention

To fully integrate the global constraints and causal information provided by the physical-variable sequences ${\mathbf{V}_{1:{T_{p}}}}$ , PhyNet introduces a cross-attention mechanism between the time-frequency response tokens ${\mathbf{R}_{re}}$ and the physical-variable tokens ${\mathbf{P}_{va}}$ . Specifically, the cross-attention layer takes the time-frequency response tokens ${\mathbf{R}_{re}}$ as queries and the physical-variable tokens ${\mathbf{P}_{va}}$ as keys and values, establishing connections between the signal response and the physical constraints. Since the physical variables are embedded as sequence-wise variate tokens, the learnable global response token is used to aggregate global information from the physical variables. The above process can be formalized as:

 $\tilde{\mathbf{G}}_{re}^{l}=\mathrm{LayerNorm}(\tilde{\mathbf{G}}_{re}^{l}+\mathrm{Cross\text{-}Attention}(\tilde{\mathbf{G}}_{re}^{l},{\mathbf{P}_{va}})),$ 

(6)

Subsequently, all local time-frequency response tokens ${\mathbf{R}}_{re}$ and the learnable global response token $\mathbf{G}_{re}$ are updated through a feed-forward network, which can be formally expressed as follows:

 $\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{re}^{l+1}=\mathrm{Feed\text{-}Forward}(\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{re}^{l}),$ 

(7)

 $\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{re}^{l+1}=\mathrm{Feed\text{-}Forward}(\tilde{\mathbf{G}}_{re}^{l}),$ 

where $l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}$ . This design allows the learnable global response token to simultaneously integrate local patch-wise information and global physical constraints, enabling effective interactions between signal responses and physical variables.

## Optimization Objective

In intensity diagnosis tasks, the physical variables ${\mathbf{V}_{1:{T_{p}}}}$ serve as auxiliary information and are not required to be predicted. Therefore, the diagnostic output is generated by performing a linear projection onto the final response embeddings produced by PhyNet. Specifically, the patch-wise time-frequency response tokens ${\mathbf{R}_{re}}$ and the global response token ${\mathbf{G}_{re}}$ from the last encoder layer of PhyNet are aggregated and then passed through a LayerNorm operation followed by a linear projection to obtain the predicted probability distribution over intensity classes. To measure the discrepancy between the predicted probabilities ${{\hat{y}}_{i}}$ and the ground-truth intensity labels ${y_{i}}$ , PhyNet adopts the cross-entropy loss function for supervised learning, which is defined as:

 $\mathcal{L}=-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{I}{{y_{i}}\log}({{\hat{y}}_{i}}),\;\;\text{where}\;\;{{\hat{y}}_{i}}=\text{Projection}(\text{LayerNorm}[\mathbf{R}_{re}^{L},\mathbf{G}_{re}^{L}]),$ 

(8)

where $I$ denotes the number of intensity classes, $L$ represents the index of the last encoder layer and $[\cdot,\cdot]$ denotes the concatenation operation.

## Baselines

To validate the effectiveness and advantages of the data-physics hybrid PhyNet model, we categorize baselines into three groups based on their utilization of data and physical variables. This categorization enables a systematic evaluation of the benefits of integrating physical information with signal-based representations for intensity diagnosis.

Data-driven methods. Data-driven methods commonly adopt end-to-end deep learning approaches, which are organized into three types: CNN-based methods, Transformer-based methods and CNN-Transformer hybrid methods. In detail, CNN-based methods excel at capturing local patterns from signals and include models such as ResNet [29], DenseNet [30] and ConvNeXt [31]. Transformer-based methods are effective at modelling long-term dependencies from signals, including models such as ViT [32], Swin [33], PerViT [34] and CrossViT [35]. CNN-Transformer hybrid methods combine local feature modeling of CNNs with the global dependency modelling of Transformers, including models such as UniFormer [36], ConFormer [37], CFFormer [38] and LeViT [39]. For all data-driven models, original sensing measurements are directly used as inputs, without explicit incorporation of physical variables, rendering these models purely data-driven baselines.

Physics-driven methods. Physics-driven methods essentially use traditional machine-learning algorithms with physical variables as input features for classification. Specifically, support vector machines (SVM) [40] classify samples by finding the optimal hyperplane that separates different classes. Decision Trees (DT) [41] recursively split the feature space based on input features to generate decision rules. Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) [42] builds an ensemble of gradient-boosted trees to improve predictive performance. The K-nearest neighbors (KNN) algorithm [43] assigns class labels based on the majority label among the nearest neighbors in the feature space.

Data-Physics Hybrid methods. Data-physics hybrid methods are a class of multimodal learning methods that jointly process multiple heterogeneous data sources to capture complementary information for improved representation learning. Concretely, Vision-and-Language Transformer (ViLT) [44] uses a Transformer to encode and align visual and tabular inputs in a unified latent space. Perceiver [45] processes high-dimensional inputs by iteratively mapping them into a fixed-size latent array through cross-attention, enabling flexible multimodal representation. PerceiverIO (PIO) [46] extends the Perceiver architecture to process arbitrary multimodal inputs through iterative cross-attention between latent arrays and input modalities. Multimodal Transformer (MulT) [47] employs cross-modal attention layers to explicitly model interactions between different modalities and fuse information across them. For all data-physics hybrid methods, both original sensing measurements and physical variables are used as inputs, enabling the models to jointly learn from multiple modalities.

## Experimental Settings and Reproducibility

All experiments are conducted on a Linux workstation equipped with two NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 GPUs, using Python 3.10.19, PyTorch 2.8.0, CUDA 12.9 and cuDNN 9.10.2. All models are trained from scratch with randomly initialized parameters and without external pre-trained weights. DenseNet models use an input resolution of $256\times 256$ , whereas all other models use an input resolution of $224\times 224$ . All deep learning models are optimized using the cross-entropy loss and the Adam optimizer with $\beta_{1}=0.9$ , $\beta_{2}=0.999$ , $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$ and zero weight decay. The initial learning rate is set to $1\times 10^{-4}$ , with a batch size of 16 and a cosine learning-rate decay schedule. To improve training stability, the maximum gradient norm is clipped to 4.0. All models are trained for 30 epochs without early stopping. The dropout settings of the baseline models follow their respective implementations. The default PhyNet configuration uses three input channels, a patch size of $16\times 16$ , a 512-dimensional embedding, 8 attention heads, 3 encoder layers, a feed-forward dimension of 2048, a dropout rate of 0.1 and the GELU activation function. All models are trained and evaluated using predefined training and test splits. Further implementation details are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

## Metrics

For intensity diagnosis tasks, model performance is evaluated using a comprehensive set of metrics. We report accuracy (Acc.), precision (Pre.), recall (Rec.) and F1-score (F1) to assess the model’s ability to correctly identify all classes, particularly in imbalanced scenarios [48, 49]. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC) provides a threshold-independent evaluation of discriminative performance [50]. The confusion matrix is also examined to visualize class-wise prediction patterns and common misclassifications [51]. All metrics are calculated on held-out test sets to ensure a robust and comprehensive evaluation of classification performance. Detailed definitions and calculation procedures for these metrics are provided in the Supplementary Information.
