Abdulrahman Alabdulkareem1,

Abstract

Head-dimension compression can reduce the memory required by the
key-value (KV) cache, but existing methods often reconstruct
full-dimensional representations during inference, adding significant decoding FLOPs overhead. We formalize the design considerations for converting pretrained attention into a natively reduced-dimensional form that reduces both KV-cache storage and attention computation using a calibration set. Specifically, we consider reductions that (1) apply a shared projection to queries and keys so that they remain in a common reduced feature space, (2) derive this projection jointly from query and key activations to better preserve their interaction, and (3) are compatible with the pretrained RoPE transformation. To obtain a reconstruction-free realization of this reduction, we restrict it to coordinate selection and theoretically show that RoPE compatibility then holds if and only if complete rotary pairs are retained. Building on this characterization, we introduce RACE
(RoPE-Aligned Attention Compression based on
activation Energy), a post-training method that selects rotary
pairs using their joint query-key activation energy. We theoretically
show that this criterion minimizes a separable, calibration-dependent
upper bound on post-RoPE query-key product approximation error. The
resulting reductions are folded into the model weights and require
neither runtime reconstruction nor specialized kernels. Across three model families and different compression levels, RACE outperforms structured
pruning and remains competitive with reconstruction-based methods on language-modeling, zero-shot, and long-context tasks. At 50% KV-cache retention and 256K context, RACE reduces total
inference FLOPs by 47.8%, yielding $1.79\times$ higher throughput,
43.2% lower time-to-first-token (TTFT), and 28.1% lower allocated GPU memory in comparison to an uncompressed model. We also show that RACE is compatible with token eviction or compaction as well as quantization based KV-cache compression methods.

## 1 Introduction

Autoregressive language-model inference is increasingly constrained by the key-value (KV) cache, especially at long contexts. Each attention layer stores a key and value vector for every preceding token, so cache memory grows linearly with context length and with the dimensionality of the cached representations. Reducing the key and value head dimensions offers the possibility of decreasing both KV-cache memory and native attention computation. Realizing these savings is more difficult than merely storing a low-dimensional encoding. Several head-dimension compression methods project keys and values into compact representations but reconstruct full-dimensional keys during inference to effectively apply rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) (Saxena et al., 2024; Chang et al., 2025). Such methods reduce cache storage but introduce decompression and additional arithmetic into the attention path during inference. To obtain the full benefit of head-dimension compression, the attention layer itself must operate directly in the reduced dimensions.

This raises a more fundamental question: when can a pretrained RoPE-based attention layer be converted into a natively lower-dimensional attention layer without reconstructing the discarded coordinates? Since attention logits depend on the post-RoPE product $QK^{\top}$ , we view head-dimension reduction as an approximate matrix multiplication (AMM) problem along the shared feature dimension. However, preserving this interaction in the post-RoPE space is not sufficient for native inference. It must be realizable before RoPE. This imposes an additional structural constraint that the reduction must commute with the pretrained rotary transformation. We characterize this condition exactly for coordinate-selection reductions.

These observations define an admissible family of native Q/K reductions: queries and keys can share a reduced feature space, and the reduction must respect the pretrained RoPE structure. The remaining question is which admissible dimensions to retain. We introduce RACE, which ranks complete rotary pairs using joint activation energy for each RoPE pair $m$ , $E_{m}=\|Q_{m}\|_{F}^{2}+\|K_{m}\|_{F}^{2}$ , and retains the highest-energy pairs. This criterion minimizes a separable, calibration-dependent upper bound on post-RoPE query-key product approximation error. The resulting Q/K reduction, together with a calibrated value subspace, is folded into the attention projections, enabling RoPE, caching, and attention to operate entirely in the reduced dimensions without runtime reconstruction or specialized kernels. Summarizing our contributions:

•

## 2 Related Work

Compression along other KV-cache axes.

KV-cache memory can be reduced along the token, precision, or head-dimension
axes as shown in Fig. 8. Token-level methods reduce the number of cached positions ( $S$ in Eq.7), through eviction, retention, or compaction
(Zhang et al., 2023; Xiao et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2024; Fu et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2025). Quantization methods instead reduce the number of
bits used for each cached element, ( $b$ in Eq.7),
(Liu et al., 2024; Hooper et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025). Our method preserves the
retained token positions and reduces the number of elements in every key and
value vector. It is therefore complementary to both token reduction and
quantization; we evaluate representative combinations in
Sec. 4.3.

Head-dimension KV-cache compression.

Head-dimension methods reduce the width of the cached representations ( $d_{k}$ and $d_{v}$ in Eq.7) for keys and values respectively.
EigenAttention (Saxena et al., 2024) derives low-rank subspaces from
calibrated activations, whereas PALU (Chang et al., 2025) factorizes attention
projection weights. The dense low-rank compression subspaces in these and similar methods are not generally invariant under the pretrained
RoPE transformation and so these methods recover full-dimensional keys before applying RoPE. This can be compute-intense and so, methods like PALU (Chang et al., 2025) mitigate this by using a custom kernel that does not materialize the full-dimensional reconstructed key tensor. However the inference FLOPs remains dominated by the dense reconstruction basis.

Thin Keys (Yao et al., 2026) avoids key reconstruction by
reducing the shared Q/K dimension, but retains all dimensions of Values. A concurrent preprint, RAP (Xin et al., 2026), performs RoPE-aligned structured parameter pruning using empirical-Fisher scores computed from gradients of $W_{K}$ and arrives at a similar RoPE-aligned native architecture from a different starting point to RACE. While RAP approaches the problem as structured parameter pruning, RACE begins from the problem of constrained-AMM as shown in Sec.3.1. The methods can therefore produce similar reduced-dimensional architectures, but differ in their compression objective and calibration signal. Additionally, our calibration is forward-only and does not depend on a task loss or the batch-sensitivity of Fisher estimates (Tenison et al., 2026). Figure 1 compares our solution to previous techniques in terms of accuracy and FLOPs across a range of compression levels.

Methods including MatryoshkaKV (Lin et al., 2025), STAR-KV
(Bhatnagar et al., 2026), ThinK (Xu et al., 2025), and OjaKV
(Zhu et al., 2026) additionally study heterogeneous ranks or
input-dependent compression. These methods primarily
address where or when to allocate compression across layers,
heads, or inputs. Our work instead addresses how to construct a fixed,
RoPE-compatible reduced representation that operates natively without runtime
selection, adaptation, or full-dimensional reconstruction.

Reconstruction-based methods (right) may cache low-dimensional representations yet return to the original feature dimension during inference. ## 3 Method

Problem Formulation.

Consider an attention head with pre-RoPE activations
 $Q,K\in\mathbb{R}^{S\times d_{k}}$ and
 $V\in\mathbb{R}^{S\times d_{v}}$ .
Given target dimensions $r_{k}<d_{k}$ and $r_{v}<d_{v}$ , we seek
reduced-dimensional representations

 $Q^{\prime}=QP_{Q},\qquad K^{\prime}=KP_{K},\qquad V^{\prime}=VP_{V},$ 

(1)

with $P_{Q},P_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times r_{k}}$ and
 $P_{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{v}\times r_{v}}$ , such that the resulting layer
stores only $K^{\prime}$ and $V^{\prime}$ in the KV cache and performs attention
directly in dimensions $r_{k}$ and $r_{v}$ , without reconstructing the
discarded coordinates at inference.

For native reduced-dimensional execution, the Q/K reductions must
ultimately be applied to the pre-RoPE activations so that they can be
folded into the query and key projection weights. However, the quantity
that determines attention logits is the post-RoPE query-key product.
We therefore first characterize the low-dimensional Q/K representation
that preserves this interaction, and then determine when it can be
realized equivalently before RoPE. Finally we introduce RACE, a criterion that satifies these conditions and effectively convert a pretrained attention to a natively reduced-dimensional attention.

## 3.1 Native Attention Compression as Constrained AMM

Let $\widetilde{Q}=\operatorname{RoPE}(Q),\widetilde{K}=\operatorname{RoPE}(K)$ .
Since attention logits depend on the post-RoPE product
 $\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}$ , we formulate Q/K head-dimension
reduction as an approximate matrix multiplication (AMM) problem.
We are seeking a column-orthonormal reduction
 $P\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times r_{k}}$ such that,

 $(\widetilde{Q}P)(\widetilde{K}P)^{\top}=\widetilde{Q}PP^{\top}\widetilde{K}^{\top}\approx\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}.$ 

(2)

This is consistent with classical matrix-sketching and AMM results,
where a common lower-dimensional map is applied along the shared
dimension of the two operands
(Woodruff, 2014; Cohen et al., 2016). Detailed explanation is given in A.5.

This view motivates two design principles. First, queries and keys should be represented in a shared low-dimensional subspace, since their
interaction is evaluated along the same feature dimension. We therefore
seek a common reduction $P_{QK}$ for both operands. Second, when this
subspace is chosen from calibration data, its selection criterion should
reflect both matrices whose product is being approximated. Because the
target interaction depends jointly on $\widetilde{Q}$ and $\widetilde{K}$ ,
we construct $P_{QK}$ from statistics of both query and key activations
rather than either operand in isolation as in the concurrent work, RAP(Xin et al., 2026) or ThinKeys(Yao et al., 2026). This is validated empirically in Fig.12.

These observations specify how the Q/K interaction should be reduced, but not yet whether the reduction can be executed natively before RoPE to get the desired systems benefits. Applying $P_{QK}$ only after RoPE would still require full-dimensional query and key projections and full-dimensional rotary encoding, reducing the KV cache width without fully reducing the attention computation as in reconstruction-based methods. Native execution therefore requires the shared reduction to be realizable before RoPE. Section 3.1 establishes how the query-key interaction should be reduced, but not whether the shared reduction can be realized before RoPE. Section 3.2 characterizes the additional structural condition under which this is possible.

## 3.2 RoPE-Compatible Query–Key Reduction

Section 3.1 motivates representing queries and keys
through a shared low-dimensional map. To apply this reduction
before RoPE, it must preserve the corresponding blocks of the
pretrained rotary transformation as detailed in A.3.

Let $R_{t}=\operatorname{diag}(R_{1,t},\ldots,R_{M,t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times d_{k}}$ 
be the pretrained RoPE operator at position $t$ , where each
 $R_{m,t}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}$ acts on one rotary pair. For a retained
pair set $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,M\}$ , define $R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})}=\operatorname{diag}(R_{m,t}:m\in\mathcal{S})\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}\times r_{k}}.$ 
Thus, $R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})}$ is the restriction of the original pretrained
operator to the retained pairs. We say that a reduction
 $P_{QK}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times r_{k}}$ is RoPE-compatible if

 $R_{t}P_{QK}=P_{QK}R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})}\qquad\text{for all }t.$ 

(3)

This condition ensures that applying the reduction before RoPE is equivalent to applying full-dimensional RoPE and then restricting to the retained coordinates. Consequently, query/key projection, rotary encoding, and attention can all be performed directly in dimension $r_{k}$ . A simple admissible family of $P_{QK}$ is obtained by retaining complete RoPE pairs. The following theorem shows that this family exactly characterizes RoPE-compatible coordinate-selection reductions.

Theorem 1 (RoPE-compatible coordinate selection).

Suppose for every rotary pair $m$ , there exists a position $t$ such that $\sin\phi_{m,t}\neq 0$ . A coordinate-selection matrix $P_{QK}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times r_{k}}$ 
satisfies Eq. 3 for all $t$ if and only if
it selects exactly the coordinates belonging to a union of complete
rotary pairs indexed by $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,M\}$ ,
with the selected coordinates ordered consistently with
 $R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})}$ .

The proof is provided in A.6. The remaining question is which admissible pairs should be retained. Section 3.3 introduces a calibration criterion for ranking the admissible pairs.

## 3.3 Calibration-Guided Selection Within the RoPE-Compatible Family

Section 3.2 characterizes a family of structurally valid native reductions by restricting selection to complete RoPE pairs. What remains is no longer a compatibility question, but a ranking question over this admissible pairs to determine what pairs should be retained. Because the approximation target in Section 3.1 depends on both queries and keys, we rank candidate pairs using a joint calibration statistic over the two operands.

Let $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{rope}}=\{(i_{m},j_{m})\}_{m=1}^{M}$ 
denote the RoPE pairs of an attention head, and let $Q_{m}=Q_{:,\{i_{m},j_{m}\}},K_{m}=K_{:,\{i_{m},j_{m}\}}$ .
We assign pair $m$ the joint query-key energy

 $E_{m}=\|Q_{m}\|_{F}^{2}+\|K_{m}\|_{F}^{2}.$ 

(4)

For target dimension $r_{k}$ , we retain the $r_{k}/2$ pairs with the
largest energies, $\mathcal{S}=\operatorname{TopK}_{m\in[M]}\left(E_{m},\frac{r_{k}}{2}\right)$ 
and construct $P_{QK}=[e_{i}]_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{QK}}$ where $\mathcal{I}_{QK}=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal{S}}\{i_{m},j_{m}\}.$ 

The following result provides a calibration-dependent justification for
this selection rule.

Proposition 1 (Pair-energy approximation bound).

Let
 $\widetilde{Q}=\operatorname{RoPE}(Q)$ and
 $\widetilde{K}=\operatorname{RoPE}(K)$ , and let
 $\widetilde{Q}_{m},\widetilde{K}_{m}$ denote the coordinates associated with
RoPE pair $m$ . For any retained pair set
 $\mathcal{S}$ ,

 $\left\|\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}-\sum_{m\in\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{Q}_{m}\widetilde{K}_{m}^{\top}\right\|_{F}\leq\sum_{m\notin\mathcal{S}}\left\|\widetilde{Q}_{m}\right\|_{F}\left\|\widetilde{K}_{m}\right\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\notin\mathcal{S}}E_{m}.$ 

(5)

Consequently, among all subsets containing the same number of complete
RoPE pairs, selecting the pairs with the largest $E_{m}$ minimizes the separable upper bound in Eq. 5. Proof of this proposition is given in A.6. Proposition 1 does not imply that the selected
pair set minimizes the exact post-RoPE query-key product error. Rather, it shows
that the proposed top-energy rule exactly minimizes a worst-case, separable calibration-dependent upper bound in the right-hand side of
Eq. 5. We use this result as a tractable forward-only criterion for choosing within the RoPE-compatible family.
We evaluate the robustness of this
calibration-dependent selection empirically in
Section 4.5.

Value compression.

Values are not subject to RoPE, so we follow the standard low-rank activation projection used in prior head-dimension compression methods (Saxena et al., 2024). Given calibration activations
 $V\in\mathbb{R}^{S_{\mathrm{cal}}\times d_{v}}$ , we retain the top- $r_{v}$ right singular vectors
 $P_{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{v}\times r_{v}}$ and compute $V^{\prime}=VP_{V}.$

## 3.4 Folding into Native Reduced-Dimensional Attention

Having obtained the RoPE-compatible query-key selector $P_{QK}$ and the value subspace $P_{V}$ , we fold both transformations directly into
the pretrained attention projections. For queries and keys,

 $Q^{\prime}=XW_{Q}^{\mathrm{fold}},K^{\prime}=XW_{K}^{\mathrm{fold}}\qquad\text{where,}\qquad W_{Q}^{\mathrm{fold}}=W_{Q}P_{QK},W_{K}^{\mathrm{fold}}=W_{K}P_{QK}.$ 

(6)

 $Q^{\prime}$ and $K^{\prime}$ are
 $r_{k}$ -dimensional queries and keys to which the restricted RoPE operator from
Section 3.2 is applied. Hence, neither the discarded
query/key coordinates nor a full-dimensional RoPE representation is
materialized during inference.

For values, we similarly fold the calibrated subspace into the value
and output projections - $W_{V}^{\mathrm{fold}}=W_{V}P_{V},W_{O}^{\mathrm{fold}}=P_{V}^{\top}W_{O}$ and $V^{\prime}=XW_{V}^{\mathrm{fold}},Y^{\prime}=(A^{\prime}V^{\prime})W_{O}^{\mathrm{fold}}$ 
where $A^{\prime}$ is computed entirely in the reduced dimension
 $r_{k}$ using the retained RoPE pairs.

This folding converts the original pretrained attention layer into a

 $r_{v}$ . The resulting model stores only reduced-dimensional keys and
values and performs RoPE, query-key comparison, value aggregation, and
output projection without reconstructing the discarded dimensions. Consequently, the reduction decreases KV-cache storage, attention
arithmetic, and attention projection parameters simultaneously.

Recovery.

For aggressive compression, we recommend a short one-time post-fold recovery stage using KL distillation from the uncompressed model (Fig. 10). We update the reduced attention parameters $W_{Q}^{\mathrm{fold}},W_{K}^{\mathrm{fold}},W_{V}^{\mathrm{fold}},W_{O}^{\mathrm{fold}}$ while keeping the rest of the model frozen. Recovery is performed directly on the natively compressed checkpoint using 1M FineWeb-Edu tokens. Its effect is modest at upto 75% KV retention but substantial at 50%, where it restores much of the performance. Therefore, while moderate compression requires little or no adaptation; for 50% retention, we recommend a short one-time recovery stage for RACE and RAP.

## 4 Experiments and Results

Implementation Details.

We implement RACE in PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers. Calibration
uses 32,768 FineWeb-Edu tokens, with statistics and SVDs computed in
FP32 during calibration and inference is performed in BF16. We respect each architecture’s
physical rotary-pair layout and retain the original frequency indices
and RoPE scaling rules; selected pairs are not re-indexed as consecutive
frequencies. Compressed checkpoints
resize the attention projections and cache dimensions while maintaining the $\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}$ scaling, and run using
standard layers and PyTorch attention operations. Thus,
RACE requires no specialized CUDA/Triton kernel. While all the results in Sec.4 were reported with SDPA attention backend, Tab.2 shows systems results with eager and flash attention backends. Although different kernels optimize for wall-clock time, the number of FLOPs remian the same. Although optional, all the results of RACE and the concurrent work, RAP involve recovery with
KL-distillation over approximately 1M FineWeb-Edu tokens as detailed in A.7.

## 4.1 Quality at Matched KV-Cache Budgets

We evaluate Qwen2.5-7B,
Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, and Granite-4.1-3B against RoPE-compatible implementations of
PALU-GLRD (Chang et al., 2025), EigenAttention(Saxena et al., 2024), Thin Keys(Yao et al., 2026) and concurrent work,
RAP (Xin et al., 2026). The full results are shown in Table 3 in the Appendix. Fig.3 shows the same on Qwen2.5-7B and
Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct for RACE, the concurrent work and structured pruning baseline: RAP, and the reconstruction-based baseline: PALU. We finetune reduced attention weights of both RAP and RACE for 1M tokens of FineWeb-Edu using KL distillation from the respective uncompressed model for fair comparison. We report
WikiText perplexity, mean accuracy across 15 zero-shot tasks (Tab.3), and LongBench score average over 16 English tasks (Tab.4). At 75% KV-cache retention, RACE preserves 98.5%, 96.5%, and 97.5%
of the uncompressed zero-shot accuracy on Qwen, Granite, and Llama,
respectively. Across compression
levels, on Llama and Qwen models (Figure 3), RACE closely matches PALU on
zero-shot accuracy and LongBench without runtime reconstruction. RACE consistently outperforms RAP. RACE also achieves the lowest perplexity at 50%
and 75% retention.

Reducing KV-cache storage does not automatically reduce inference cost. As discussed in Section 1, this requires native reduced attention. Such native reduced attention methods like RACE eliminates reconstruction at the architectural level, exposing standard reduced-dimensional attention operations that can be supported by general-purpose backends. Figure 4 shows that these measured gains become larger with prompt length as attention accounts for a greater fraction of inference cost. At 256K tokens, 50% retention achieves $1.76\times$ , $1.90\times$ , and $1.79\times$ prefill, decoding, and overall throughput speedups, respectively. It also reduces TTFT by 43.22%, allocated GPU memory by 28.06%, and total inference FLOPs by 47.75% relative to the uncompressed model. The end-to-end memory reduction is smaller than the 50% KV-cache reduction because total allocated memory also includes model parameters, activations, and other components unaffected by the proposed method. Figure.11 shows the memory reduction with compression on total model parameters, attention parameters and KV cache. While parameter memory is unaffected by sequence length, KV Cache memory grows with it.

## 4.3 Compatibility with Token-Dimension Compression and Quantization

RACE reduces the KV-cache feature dimension or head dimension and can therefore be combined with methods that compress its token dimension or numerical precision. We apply 75% RACE retention before H $2$ O and SnapKV token compression and KIVI quantization, each with a 50% cache footprint, yielding 37.5% combined retention. Figure 6 shows that applying RACE to an already compressed model causes greater degradation than applying it to the uncompressed model, indicating that the effects of the two compression stages are not independent. Nevertheless, the combinations provide an additional 25% reduction in cache size and feature-dependent attention FLOPs as well as attention parameters. The degradation is smaller with KIVI, particularly in perplexity, suggesting that RACE is especially complementary to quantization. Overall, RACE can augment existing KV-cache methods with further memory, bandwidth, and, for token compression, decoding-FLOP reductions.

## 4.4 Effect of Calibration Data

Our selection criterion explained in Section 3.3 is derived from a calibration-dependent upper bound on the query-key approximation error, so the retained subspace is expected to reflect the calibration distribution. Figure 6 evaluates this dependence by varying the calibration dataset and reporting performance relative to FineWeb-Edu calibration. Note that for WikiText, the perplexity change is sign-inverted so that positive values consistently denote improvement. Using the same dataset as the downstream task generally gives the strongest results - upto +5.4% on the evaluated datasets with respect to calibration on FineWen-Edu, and calibration on a related task family may also improve performance as observed in evaluation on arc-c with calibration on SciQ. However, the differences are modest $(<6\%)$ for most tasks. Therefore, FineWeb-Edu remains a strong generic calibration source, especially when in-distribution task-specific samples are unavailable or the downstream task family is unknown.

## 4.6 Towards Models with Fewer and Wider Heads

If post-training attention compression is anticipated at deployment time,
larger head dimensions may be a useful architectural choice during
pretraining. We study three matched $\sim 245$ M-parameter GPT-NeoX-style
models pretrained from scratch on FineWeb-Edu for 2.5B tokens:
wide ( $4\times 256$ ), standard ( $8\times 128$ ), and
narrow ( $16\times 64$ ). All three reach comparable uncompressed
WikiText validation perplexity. Figure 7(c) shows that wider-head models
retain perplexity more effectively under aggressive RACE compression. At each
retention ratio, the models have approximately matched aggregate retained
attention dimension, KV-cache memory, attention FLOPs, and compressed
attention parameter count; the primary difference is how this capacity is
partitioned across heads. Wider heads expose more RoPE pairs from which RACE
can select, providing greater flexibility in structured dimensional reduction.
Thus, when post-training attention compression is a foreseeable deployment
requirement, allocating attention width across fewer, wider heads during
pretraining may yield a more favorable quality-efficiency trade-off.

## 5 Conclusion

In this work, we study when and how pretrained RoPE attention can be converted into a natively
lower-dimensional form that reduces KV-cache storage, attention computation,
and parameters without runtime reconstruction. Viewing Q/K compression as
constrained approximate matrix multiplication motivates a shared reduction
derived jointly from queries and keys. This reduction has to be RoPE-compatible to enable reconstruction-free compression. We further show that, for
coordinate-selection reductions family, RoPE compatibility holds if and only if
complete rotary pairs are retained. RACE selects within this admissible family using joint Q/K activation energy,
a forward-only criterion that minimizes a separable calibration-dependent upper
bound on post-RoPE product error. The resulting reductions are folded directly
into the projection weights, enabling attention to execute entirely in the
compressed dimensions without specialized kernels. Across multiple model
families and compression levels, RACE outperforms evaluated reconstruction-free baselines and remains competitive with
reconstruction-based methods while reducing KV-cache memory, attention cost,
and latency.

## A.1 Axes of KV-Cache Compression

Figure 8: Token- and head- dimension KV-cache compression. Token-level methods reduce the number of retained cache entries N, whereas head-dimension methods reduce the dimensionality of each cached representation. These approaches act on orthogonal axes of the KV cache and can be combined.

For a model with $L$ layers, batch size $B$ , context length $S$ , $H_{kv}$ 
key–value heads, key dimension $d_{k}$ , value dimension $d_{v}$ , and $b$ bytes per
element, the KV-cache memory is

 $M_{\mathrm{KV}}=BLSH_{kv}(d_{k}+d_{v})b.$ 

This expression exposes three independent factors that can be targeted by
compression.

Token-level methods reduce $S$ by evicting, retaining, or consolidating cached
positions. Head-dimension methods reduce $d_{k}$ and $d_{v}$ for each retained
position. Quantization reduces $b$ , the storage precision of each element.
As illustrated in Figure 8, these approaches operate on
different factors of the cache size and can therefore be applied independently
or jointly. In particular, combining token and head-dimension compression
changes the cache size to

 $M_{\mathrm{KV}}^{\prime}=BLS^{\prime}H_{kv}(r_{k}+r_{v})b,$ 

where $S^{\prime}\leq S$ , $r_{k}\leq d_{k}$ , and $r_{v}\leq d_{v}$ .

## A.2 Attention and KV caching.

For hidden states $X\in\mathbb{R}^{S\times d_{\mathrm{model}}}$ and attention weights $W_{Q},W_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}\times d_{k}}$ and $W_{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}\times d_{v}}$ , an attention
layer computes $Q=XW_{Q},\qquad K=XW_{K},\qquad V=XW_{V}$ .
For each head, let $Q_{h},K_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{S\times d_{k}}$ and
 $V_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{S\times d_{v}}$ . Its output is $O_{h}=A_{h}V_{h}=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_{h}K_{h}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\right)V_{h}.$ 
During autoregressive decoding, previously computed keys and values are stored
in the KV cache. For $L$ layers, batch size $B$ , context length $S$ , $H_{kv}$ 
KV heads, $H_{q}$ 
Q heads, and $b$ bytes per element, its memory is

 $M_{\mathrm{KV}}=BLSH_{kv}(d_{k}+d_{v})b.$ 

(7)

Thus, reducing $d_{k}$ and $d_{v}$ decreases cache memory without removing cached
tokens. It also reduces the query-key and attention-value products, whose
context-dependent decoding costs scale as
 $\mathcal{O}(BSH_{q}d_{k})$ and $\mathcal{O}(BSH_{q}d_{v})$ , respectively. Their prefill costs scale as $\mathcal{O}(BS^{2}H_{q}d_{k})$ and $\mathcal{O}(BS^{2}H_{q}d_{v})$ , respectively. Complete
FLOP expressions are provided in A.4.

## A.3 RoPE-Compatible Reduction

Rotary positional embeddings.

RoPE partitions the rotary query and key dimensions into coordinate pairs and
applies a position-dependent two-dimensional rotation to each pair
(Su et al., 2024). For the pair associated with frequency $\theta_{i}$ at
position $t$ ,

 $R_{i}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos(t\theta_{i})&-\sin(t\theta_{i})\\
\sin(t\theta_{i})&\cos(t\theta_{i})\end{bmatrix}.$ 

Although implementations may store paired coordinates consecutively or across
two halves of the head dimension, the two coordinates remain coupled by the
rotation. A coordinate-selection method can therefore preserve the pretrained
RoPE operation without reconstruction only if it retains or removes complete
rotary pairs. Architectures with partial RoPE additionally contain non-rotary
coordinates, which need not satisfy this pairwise constraint.

Logical pairs and physical coordinate layouts.

Let $d_{\mathrm{rot}}\leq d_{k}$ denote the number of query and key coordinates
to which RoPE is applied, and let $M=d_{\mathrm{rot}}/2$ . We represent the
logical rotary pairs as

 $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{rope}}=\{(i_{m},j_{m})\}_{m=1}^{M},$ 

where $(i_{m},j_{m})$ identifies the two physical coordinates rotated using the
 $m$ -th rotary frequency. The mapping from logical pairs to physical coordinates
depends on the model implementation. With one-based indexing, common layouts
include

 $(i_{m},j_{m})=\begin{cases}(2m-1,\,2m),&\text{adjacent-pair layout},\\
(m,\,m+M),&\text{split-half layout}.\end{cases}$ 

The former is used by GPT-J-style rotary implementations, whereas the latter
is used by LLaMA-style implementations. Our selection operates on these
logical pairs rather than on neighboring memory locations, so the same
procedure applies to either layout after constructing the appropriate pair
map.

Restriction of the original RoPE operator.

Let $\mathcal{S}\subseteq[M]$ be the retained set of rotary pairs and let
 $P_{\mathcal{S}}\in\{0,1\}^{d_{k}\times 2|\mathcal{S}|}$ contain the standard
basis vectors corresponding to both coordinates of every pair in
 $\mathcal{S}$ . The reduced RoPE operator is formed by retaining the original
rotation block associated with each selected pair:

 $R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})}=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathcal{S}}R_{t,m}.$ 

Provided that the columns of $P_{\mathcal{S}}$ and the blocks of
 $R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})}$ use the same pair ordering,

 $R_{t}P_{\mathcal{S}}=P_{\mathcal{S}}R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})}.$ 

Consequently, for row-wise query activations,

 $(QR_{t})P_{\mathcal{S}}=(QP_{\mathcal{S}})R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})},$ 

and analogously for keys. Thus, selecting coordinates before RoPE and applying
the restricted operator produces exactly the selected coordinates of the
original RoPE output. This is an algebraic restriction of the pretrained
operator, not an approximation introduced by the reduced-dimensional
implementation.

Preserving pretrained rotary frequencies.

Reducing the query–key dimension must not cause the rotary frequencies to be
recomputed from the new dimension $r_{k}$ . Standard RoPE implementations derive
frequencies from their original rotary dimension; reinitializing RoPE with
 $r_{k}$ would generally assign different angles to the retained coordinates and
would therefore alter the pretrained positional encoding. Instead, for every
retained pair $m\in\mathcal{S}$ , we copy its original frequency $\theta_{m}$ :

 $\bm{\theta}^{(\mathcal{S})}=[\theta_{m}]_{m\in\mathcal{S}}.$ 

The selected pairs may be stored in score order or original-coordinate order,
provided that the queries, keys, and frequencies use the same ordering. In our
implementation, we retain the original pair order after selection to make the
folded checkpoints deterministic.

For models using scaled RoPE, the same principle applies: the model’s original
position-dependent frequency transformation and any associated attention
scaling are preserved, and only the entries corresponding to selected pairs
are gathered. This avoids silently replacing the pretrained positional
encoding with a new reduced-dimensional frequency schedule.

Partial RoPE.

Some architectures apply RoPE to only
 $d_{\mathrm{rot}}<d_{k}$ coordinates. Let
 $\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{NoPE}}$ denote the complementary non-rotary coordinates.
The complete-pair constraint is imposed only on
 $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{rope}}$ ; non-rotary coordinates may be selected
individually. If $\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{NoPE}}$ is the
retained non-rotary set, the complete selector is

 $P_{QK}=\left[P_{\mathcal{S}}\;P_{\mathcal{J}}\right],\qquad r_{k}=2|\mathcal{S}|+|\mathcal{J}|,$ 

and the corresponding reduced positional operator is

 $R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{J})}=R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})}\oplus I_{|\mathcal{J}|}.$ 

The identity block leaves the retained non-rotary coordinates unchanged.
Hence, the same commutation relation holds for architectures with full or
partial RoPE.

Grouped-query attention.

Under grouped-query attention, several query heads attend to a shared key head.
For each KV head $g$ , let $\mathcal{G}_{g}$ denote its associated query heads. We
compute the Q/K pair statistics jointly over the key activations of head $g$ 
and the query activations from all heads in $\mathcal{G}_{g}$ . A single pair set
 $\mathcal{S}_{g}$ is then applied to the shared key head and to every query head
in its group. This ensures that each query and the key cache against which it
is evaluated use identical retained coordinates and rotary frequencies. Pair
sets may differ across KV heads, but they cannot differ among query heads that
interact with the same cached key representation.

Implementation consequence.

After selection, the columns indexed by $P_{QK}$ are folded into $W_{Q}$ and
 $W_{K}$ , and the RoPE module is restricted to the corresponding original
frequencies. The resulting layer produces $r_{k}$ -dimensional queries and keys
directly. Neither the discarded coordinates nor a full-dimensional RoPE output
is materialized during prefill or decoding.

## A.4 Inference FLOPs Analysis

We analyze the attention-related floating-point operations (FLOPs) incurred during prefill and autoregressive decoding. We count a multiply-add as two FLOPs and omit element-wise operations such as softmax, normalization, and RoPE, which are lower-order relative to the matrix multiplications considered here.

Let $B$ denote the batch size, $S$ the context length, $d$ the model hidden dimension, $H_{q}$ the number of query heads, and $H_{\mathrm{kv}}$ the number of key-value heads. The original query/key and value head dimensions are $d_{k}$ and $d_{v}$ , respectively. After compression, their corresponding dimensions are
 $r_{k}$ and $r_{v}$ .

For prefill, projections are applied to all $S$ input tokens and attention is computed over an $S\times S$ attention matrix. For decoding, we report the FLOPs for generating one new token given a cache of $S$ previous tokens.

Full Attention

For standard attention, the query, key, and value projections during prefill cost

 $F_{\mathrm{proj}}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BSd\left(H_{q}d_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}d_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}d_{v}\right).$ 

(8)

The query-key product and attention-value product cost

 $\displaystyle F_{QK}^{\mathrm{pre}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=2BS^{2}H_{q}d_{k},$ 

(9)

 $\displaystyle F_{AV}^{\mathrm{pre}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=2BS^{2}H_{q}d_{v},$ 

(10)

and the output projection costs

 $F_{O}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BSH_{q}d_{v}d.$ 

(11)

Thus,

 $F_{\mathrm{Full}}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BSd\left(H_{q}d_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}d_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}d_{v}\right)+2BS^{2}H_{q}(d_{k}+d_{v})+2BSH_{q}d_{v}d.$ 

(12)

During decoding, projections are required only for the newly generated token:

 $F_{\mathrm{proj}}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2Bd\left(H_{q}d_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}d_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}d_{v}\right).$ 

(13)

The query attends to all $S$ cached keys and values, giving

 $\displaystyle F_{QK}^{\mathrm{dec}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=2BSH_{q}d_{k},$ 

(14)

 $\displaystyle F_{AV}^{\mathrm{dec}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=2BSH_{q}d_{v},$ 

(15)

while the output projection costs

 $F_{O}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2BH_{q}d_{v}d.$ 

(16)

Therefore,

 $F_{\mathrm{Full}}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2Bd\left(H_{q}d_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}d_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}d_{v}\right)+2BSH_{q}(d_{k}+d_{v})+2BH_{q}d_{v}d.$ 

(17)

For long contexts, the cache-length-dependent term dominates:

 $F_{\mathrm{Full,LC}}^{\mathrm{dec}}\simeq 2BSH_{q}(d_{k}+d_{v}).$ 

(18)

EigenAttention with RoPE

EigenAttention projects attention representations K and V into learned low-dimensional subspaces. In its RoPE-compatible formulation, compressed keys must be decompressed into the original dimension, which is RoPE-compatible, before query-key attention.

During prefill, the projection costs

 $F_{proj}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BSd(H_{q}d_{k}+H_{kv}r_{k}+H_{kv}r_{V}).$ 

(19)

Transforming the compressed keys to the original key space costs

 $F_{K,\mathrm{rec}}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BSH_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{k}d_{k}.$ 

(20)

Since queries and reconstructed keys interact in the original key dimension,
the query-key product is the same as full attention. The value representation remains compressed to dimension $r_{v}$ , giving

 $F_{AV}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BS^{2}H_{q}r_{v}\qquad F_{O}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BSH_{q}r_{v}d.$ 

(21)

Thus, the prefill cost is

 $F_{\mathrm{Eigen}}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BS\left[d(H_{q}d_{k}+H_{kv}r_{k}+H_{kv}r_{V})+H_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{k}d_{k}+SH_{q}(d_{k}+r_{v})+H_{q}r_{v}d\right]$ 

(22)

During decoding, all $S$ cached compressed keys must be reconstructed before
RoPE, giving

 $F_{proj}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2Bd(H_{q}d_{k}+H_{kv}r_{k}+H_{kv}r_{V}).\qquad F_{K,\mathrm{rec}}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2BSH_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{k}d_{k}.$ 

(23)

The subsequent query-key, attention-value operations, and output cost

 $F_{QK}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2BSH_{q}d_{k}\qquad F_{AV}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2BSH_{q}r_{v},\qquad F_{O}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2BH_{q}r_{v}d.$ 

(24)

Therefore, the cache-length-dependent cost of generating one token is

 $F_{\mathrm{Eigen,LC}}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2BS\left[H_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{k}d_{k}+H_{q}(d_{k}+r_{v})\right].$ 

(25)

This additional $O(BSH_{kv}r_{k}d_{k})$ term arises from reconstructing cached keys
into the RoPE-compatible space.

PALU with G-LRD and RoPE

PALU (Chang et al., 2025) applies group-head low-rank decomposition
(G-LRD) to the key and value projection matrices. Let $s$ denote the
number of KV heads in each group and
 $G=\frac{H_{\mathrm{kv}}}{s}$ 
the number of groups. For each key group $g$ , PALU factorizes the
concatenated projection of $s$ heads as

 $W_{K}^{(g)}\approx A_{K}^{(g)}B_{K}^{(g)},$ 

where

 $A_{K}^{(g)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r_{k}^{(g)}},\qquad B_{K}^{(g)}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}^{(g)}\times sd_{k}}.$ 

The intermediate representation produced by $A_{K}^{(g)}$ is stored in the KV cache. For RoPE-based attention, the cached key latent is reconstructed to the original $sd_{k}$ -dimensional group representation before applying RoPE and computing attention.

Assuming uniform group ranks
 $r_{k}^{(g)}=r_{k}^{G}$ and $r_{v}^{(g)}=r_{v}^{G}$ , the Q/K/V projection cost
during prefill is

 $F_{\mathrm{proj}}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BSd\left(H_{q}d_{k}+Gr_{k}^{G}+Gr_{v}^{G}\right).$ 

(26)

For one decoding step,

 $F_{\mathrm{proj}}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2Bd\left(H_{q}d_{k}+Gr_{k}^{G}+Gr_{v}^{G}\right).$ 

(27)

For each group, reconstructing $S$ cached key latents from dimension $r_{k}^{G}$ to $sd_{k}$ costs $2BSr_{k}^{G}sd_{k}$ . Across all $G$ groups,

 $\displaystyle F_{K,\mathrm{rec}}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BSGr_{k}^{G}sd_{k}=2BSH_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{k}^{G}d_{k}.$ 

(28)

The same cache-wide reconstruction is required for each decoding step:

 $F_{K,\mathrm{rec}}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2BSH_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{k}^{G}d_{k}.$ 

(29)

Because reconstructed keys have the original head dimension $d_{k}$ ,
the query-key products and value products cost

 $\displaystyle F_{QK}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BS^{2}H_{q}d_{k},\qquad F_{QK}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2BSH_{q}d_{k}.$ 

(30)

 $\displaystyle F_{AV}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BS^{2}Gr_{V}^{G},\qquad F_{AV}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2BSGr_{V}^{G}.$ 

(31)

PALU folds the value-side reconstruction into the output projection, so attention-value aggregation can be performed in the grouped latent value space. Let the total latent value width across all groups be $d_{V}^{\mathrm{latent}}=Gr_{v}^{G}$ . The precise arithmetic depends on the mapping between query heads and value groups; we therefore retain the value-side latent width explicitly.

The fused output projection maps the concatenated latent value
representation back to the model dimension, giving

 $\displaystyle F_{O}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BS\,Gr_{v}^{G}d,\qquad F_{O}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2B\,Gr_{v}^{G}d.$ 

(32)

The prefill cost is

 $F_{PALU}^{pre}=2BS\left[d\left(H_{q}d_{k}+Gr_{k}^{G}+Gr_{v}^{G}\right)+H_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{k}^{G}d_{k}+SH_{q}d_{k}+Gr_{V}^{G}(S+d)\right].$ 

(33)

Ignoring the $S$ -independent projection and output terms, the dominant
cache-length-dependent decoding cost is

 $F_{\mathrm{Palu,LC}}^{\mathrm{dec}}\simeq 2BS\left(H_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{k}^{G}d_{k}+H_{q}d_{k}+Gr_{v}^{G}\right).$ 

(34)

Ours

Our method reduces the architectural query/key and value head dimensions to
 $r_{k}$ and $r_{v}$ , respectively. All compression transformations are folded into
the query, key, value, and output projection matrices. Consequently, neither
key nor value reconstruction is required during inference. The native-reduced dimensional attention FLOPs are as follows

During prefill,

 $F_{\mathrm{Ours}}^{\mathrm{pre}}=2BSd\left(H_{q}r_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{v}\right)+2BS^{2}H_{q}(r_{k}+r_{v})+2BSH_{q}r_{v}d.$ 

(35)

During decoding,

 $F_{\mathrm{Ours}}^{\mathrm{dec}}=2Bd\left(H_{q}r_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{k}+H_{\mathrm{kv}}r_{v}\right)+2BSH_{q}(r_{k}+r_{v})+2BH_{q}r_{v}d.$ 

(36)

The cache-length-dependent decoding cost is therefore

 $F_{\mathrm{Ours,LC}}^{\mathrm{dec}}\simeq 2BSH_{q}(r_{k}+r_{v}).$ 

(37)

For $d_{k}=d_{v}$ and $r_{k}=r_{v}=d_{k}/2$ , this term is exactly half that of
full attention:

 $\frac{F_{\mathrm{Ours,LC}}^{\mathrm{dec}}}{F_{\mathrm{Full,LC}}^{\mathrm{dec}}}=\frac{r_{k}+r_{v}}{d_{k}+d_{v}}=\frac{1}{2}.$ 

(38)

RAP follows similar compressed attention as Ours and therefore the prefill and decoding FLOPs would be alike. Similarly, Think Keys Full Values also compresses attention while keeping Values in full dimension $r_{v}=d_{v}$ .

## A.4.1 Derivation of Table 1

Table 1 compares methods under the representative grouped-query attention configuration $d=1536$ , $H_{q}=6$ , $H_{kv}=2$ , and $d_{k}=d_{v}=128$ . For RACE, RAP, and EigenAttention, we use $r_{k}=r_{v}=64$ , while Thin Keys uses $r_{k}=64$ and $r_{v}=128$ . For PALU G-LRD, the two KV heads form a single group ( $G=1$ , $s=2$ ) with group ranks $r_{k}^{G}=r_{v}^{G}=128$ ; these ranks are defined per group rather than per head. We count a multiply–add as two FLOPs and omit elementwise operations. For prefill, we report the coefficient of the dominant $O(S^{2})$ attention computation, while for single-token decoding, we report all operations that scale linearly with the cached sequence length $S$ . Common factors such as $2BS^{2}$ for prefill and $2BS$ for decoding therefore cancel in the normalized ratios. This convention captures the distinction between native dimensional reduction, where attention is performed directly in the compressed representation, and reconstruction-based methods, which incur additional cache-length-dependent work during decoding.

RACE, RAP, and Thin Keys.

Both methods execute attention natively with $r_{k}=r_{v}=64$ , without reconstructing the discarded dimensions. Their cache retention is

 $\rho_{\mathrm{cache}}=\frac{H_{kv}(r_{k}+r_{v})}{H_{kv}(d_{k}+d_{v})}=\frac{64+64}{128+128}=0.5.$ 

The same dimensional reduction applies to the Q, K, V, and output projections, giving $P_{\mathrm{native}}/P_{\mathrm{full}}=0.5$ . Since both the query–key and attention–value products are evaluated directly in dimensions $r_{k}$ and $r_{v}$ , the normalized dominant prefill and decoding costs are also

 $\rho_{\mathrm{prefill}}=\rho_{\mathrm{decode}}=\frac{H_{q}(r_{k}+r_{v})}{H_{q}(d_{k}+d_{v})}=0.5.$ 

For the Thin Keys baseline, $r_{v}=128$ as they do not compress values. This gives $\rho_{cache}=0.75$ and $\rho_{prefill}=\rho_{decode}=0.75$ .

EigenAttention.

EigenAttention stores keys and values in reduced dimensions
 $r_{k}=r_{v}=64$ , giving 50% KV-cache retention. Following the

 $\displaystyle P_{\text{Eigen}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=d\left(H_{q}d_{k}+H_{kv}r_{k}+H_{kv}r_{v}+H_{q}r_{v}\right)+H_{kv}(r_{k}d_{k}+r_{v}d_{v}).$ 

(39)

For the representative configuration used for parameter counting,
 $d=1536$ , $H_{q}=H_{kv}=6$ , $d_{k}=d_{v}=128$ , and
 $r_{k}=r_{v}=64$ , this gives

 $\frac{P_{\text{Eigen}}}{P_{\text{full}}}=0.698$ 

During prefill, key reconstruction scales as $O(S)$ , while the

 $\rho_{\text{prefill}}^{\text{Eigen}}=\frac{H_{q}(d_{k}+r_{v})}{H_{q}(d_{k}+d_{v})}=\frac{128+64}{128+128}=0.75.$

gives

## A.5 Approximate Matrix Multiplication View of Attention Compression

This section formalizes the connection between head-dimension compression and
approximate matrix multiplication (AMM), and clarifies how our
RoPE-constrained coordinate selection relates to existing sampling-based AMM
results.

AMM formulation.

Consider a single attention head with post-RoPE query and key matrices $Q,K\in\mathbb{R}^{S\times d_{k}}$ .
The pre-softmax attention interaction is $L=QK^{\top}$ .

Following the standard AMM formulation, let

 $A=Q^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times S},\qquad B=K^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times S},$ 

so that $A^{\top}B=QK^{\top}$ .
Given a dimensionality-reducing operator $\Pi\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}\times d_{k}}$ ,
AMM replaces this product by

 $(\Pi A)^{\top}(\Pi B)=Q\Pi^{\top}\Pi K^{\top}.$ 

(40)

Thus, dimensionality reduction along the shared row dimension of $A$ and $B$ 
is exactly dimensionality reduction along the head-feature dimension of
 $Q$ and $K$ .

Stable-rank AMM.

For conforming matrices $A$ and $B$ , (Cohen et al., 2016)
define a $(k,\epsilon)$ spectral-norm AMM property of the form

 $\left\|(\Pi A)^{\top}(\Pi B)-A^{\top}B\right\|_{2}\leq\epsilon\sqrt{\left(\|A\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\|A\|_{F}^{2}}{k}\right)\left(\|B\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\|B\|_{F}^{2}}{k}\right)}.$ 

(41)

They show that suitable oblivious embeddings can satisfy this property using

 $m=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k+\log(1/\delta)}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)$ 

rows with probability at least $1-\delta$ .

The stable rank of a matrix $A$ is

 $\operatorname{sr}(A)=\frac{\|A\|_{F}^{2}}{\|A\|_{2}^{2}}.$ 

(42)

Setting

 $k\gtrsim\max\{\operatorname{sr}(A),\operatorname{sr}(B)\}$ 

makes the Frobenius-to-spectral terms in
Eq. equation 41 controlled by the corresponding spectral norms.
Consequently, the required sketch dimension can scale with the effective
spectral dimension of the operands rather than their ambient dimension.
Applied to attention with $A=Q^{\top}$ and $B=K^{\top}$ , this provides theoretical
motivation for reducing $d_{k}$ when the query and key activations have low
stable rank.

From row sampling in AMM to feature selection in attention.

AMM sampling methods act on the shared row dimension of the two operands.
Under the identification $A=\widetilde{Q}^{\top};B=\widetilde{K}^{\top}$ 
the rows of $A$ and $B$ correspond to the feature dimensions of
 $\widetilde{Q}$ and $\widetilde{K}$ . Let

 $P_{\mathcal{I}}=[e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{r}}]\in\{0,1\}^{d_{k}\times r_{k}}$ 

select the coordinate set $\mathcal{I}$ , and take
 $\Pi=P_{\mathcal{I}}^{\top}$ . The AMM approximation then becomes

 $\displaystyle(\Pi A)^{\top}(\Pi B)$ 
 $\displaystyle=\widetilde{Q}P_{\mathcal{I}}P_{\mathcal{I}}^{\top}\widetilde{K}^{\top}$ 

(43)

 $\displaystyle=(\widetilde{Q}P_{\mathcal{I}})(\widetilde{K}P_{\mathcal{I}})^{\top}.$ 

Thus, row selection in AMM is exactly feature selection in attention,
with the same selection matrix applied to queries and keys.

Equation 43 motivates two design choices. First,
queries and keys should use an identical reduction $P_{\mathcal{I}}$ or in the context of RACE, $P_{QK}$ rather than
independently constructed maps $P_{Q}$ and $P_{K}$ .

Separate maps
would produce $(\widetilde{Q}P_{Q})(\widetilde{K}P_{K})^{\top}=\widetilde{Q}P_{Q}P_{K}^{\top}\widetilde{K}^{\top},$ 
which is not the shared-sketch AMM approximation
 $\widetilde{Q}P_{QK}P_{QK}^{\top}\widetilde{K}^{\top}$ .

Second, because the approximation target is the joint product
 $\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}$ , a data-dependent choice of
 $P_{QK}$ should account for both operands. Selecting dimensions using
only query or only key statistics can discard features that have low
importance for one operand but materially affect their interaction.

These observations determine the form of the reduction - a common,
jointly informed Q/K selector, but not its precise selection rule.
General AMM results show that appropriately chosen subsets of the shared
dimension can preserve matrix products. RACE further restricts this
family to selectors that are compatible with RoPE and derives an
activation-energy criterion for choosing among them.

Relation to our selector.

RACE’s compression operator is a more restricted member of this general family.
For each attention head, we use an unweighted binary selection matrix
 $P_{QK}=[e_{i}]_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{QK}}$ ,
where $\mathcal{I}_{QK}$ is chosen from calibration activations. Unlike the general data-dependent sampling operators covered by AMM theory, our selector must additionally satisfy the structural constraints induced by RoPE. Specifically, if $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{rope}}=\{(i_{m},j_{m})\}_{m=1}^{M}$ 
denotes the rotary coordinate pairs, then

 $\mathcal{I}_{QK}=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal{S}}\{i_{m},j_{m}\},\qquad|\mathcal{S}|=\frac{r_{k}}{2}.$ 

(44)

The selected set is therefore a union of complete two-dimensional rotary
blocks.

Energy-based selection and scope of the guarantee.

The AMM results above establish that low-dimensional and sampling-based
representations capable of preserving matrix products exist under appropriate
conditions. They do not imply that every coordinate subset, or specifically
our RoPE-constrained subset, satisfies the same AMM bound.

Our method chooses among the admissible RoPE pairs using calibration energy.
For pair $(i_{m},j_{m})$ , we define

 $E_{m}=\|Q_{:,i_{m}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|Q_{:,j_{m}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|K_{:,i_{m}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|K_{:,j_{m}}\|_{2}^{2},$ 

(45)

and retain the $r_{k}/2$ highest-energy pairs. This criterion favors coordinates
that carry substantial query/key activation energy while remaining compatible
with RoPE.

We therefore use stable-rank AMM and sampling-based AMM as theoretical
motivation for reducing the shared query–key dimension, rather than claiming
a direct AMM guarantee for the proposed energy-based selector. The exact
property guaranteed by our construction is structural: selecting complete
rotary pairs permits RoPE and attention to be executed natively in the reduced
space.

## A.6 Proofs

Proof of Theorem 1.

[RoPE-compatible coordinate selection]

Proof.

Suppose $P_{QK}$ selects exactly the coordinates belonging to the
complete rotary pairs indexed by
 $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,M\}$ , with its columns ordered
consistently with the blocks of $R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})}$ .
Since

 $R_{t}=\operatorname{diag}(R_{1,t},\ldots,R_{M,t})$ 

acts independently on each rotary pair, applying $R_{t}$ to the selected
coordinates applies precisely the blocks $R_{m,t}$ for $m\in\mathcal{S}$ .
Therefore,

 $R_{t}P_{QK}=P_{QK}\operatorname{diag}(R_{m,t}:m\in\mathcal{S})=P_{QK}R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})}$ 

for every $t$ . Hence $P_{QK}$ is RoPE-compatible.

Conversely, suppose

 $R_{t}P_{QK}=P_{QK}R_{t}^{(\mathcal{S})}\qquad\text{for all }t.$ 

The right-hand side lies in $\operatorname{col}(P_{QK})$ , and therefore

 $R_{t}\operatorname{col}(P_{QK})\subseteq\operatorname{col}(P_{QK})\qquad\text{for all }t.$ 

Thus, the coordinate subspace selected by $P_{QK}$ must be invariant
under every $R_{t}$ .

Consider any rotary pair $(i_{m},j_{m})$ . Suppose, for contradiction, that
 $P_{QK}$ selects $i_{m}$ but not $j_{m}$ . Then
 $e_{i_{m}}\in\operatorname{col}(P_{QK})$ , whereas
 $e_{j_{m}}\notin\operatorname{col}(P_{QK})$ .
For the $m$ -th rotary block,

 $R_{m,t}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\phi_{m,t}&-\sin\phi_{m,t}\\
\sin\phi_{m,t}&\cos\phi_{m,t}\end{bmatrix},$ 

so, up to the coordinate convention for the pair,

 $R_{t}e_{i_{m}}=\cos\phi_{m,t}\,e_{i_{m}}+\sin\phi_{m,t}\,e_{j_{m}}.$ 

By assumption, pair $m$ undergoes a nontrivial rotation for at least one
position, so there exists $t$ such that
 $\sin\phi_{m,t}\neq 0$ . For this $t$ , $R_{t}e_{i_{m}}$ has a nonzero
component along $e_{j_{m}}$ and therefore

 $R_{t}e_{i_{m}}\notin\operatorname{col}(P_{QK}),$ 

contradicting the invariance established above.

The same argument applies if $j_{m}$ is selected but $i_{m}$ is not.
Hence, for every rotary pair, either both coordinates are selected or
neither is selected. Therefore the coordinates selected by $P_{QK}$ 
form a union of complete rotary pairs, indexed by some
 $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,M\}$ .

∎

Proof of Proposition 1.

[Error bound for RoPE-pair truncation]

Proof.

Partition the post-RoPE query and key matrices into RoPE-aligned coordinate pairs,

 $\widetilde{Q}=[\widetilde{Q}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{Q}_{M}],\qquad\widetilde{K}=[\widetilde{K}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{K}_{M}],$ 

where each

 $\widetilde{Q}_{m},\widetilde{K}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{S\times 2}.$ 

Because matrix multiplication along the feature dimension decomposes additively
across these blocks,

 $\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\widetilde{Q}_{m}\widetilde{K}_{m}^{\top}.$ 

For a retained pair set $\mathcal{S}\subseteq[M]$ , the corresponding truncated
product is

 $\widetilde{Q}_{\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{K}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\top}=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{Q}_{m}\widetilde{K}_{m}^{\top}.$ 

Hence the truncation error would be,

 $\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}-\widetilde{Q}_{\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{K}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\top}=\sum_{m\notin\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{Q}_{m}\widetilde{K}_{m}^{\top}.$ 

(46)

Applying the triangle inequality for the Forbenius norm gives

 $\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}-\widetilde{Q}_{\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{K}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\top}\right\|_{F}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\notin\mathcal{S}}\left\|\widetilde{Q}_{m}\widetilde{K}_{m}^{\top}\right\|_{F}.$ 

(47)

Since for any conforming matrices $A$ and $B$ , $\left\|AB^{T}\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|A\right\|_{F}\left\|B\right\|_{F}$ , the above equation can be written as

 $\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}-\widetilde{Q}_{\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{K}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\top}\right\|_{F}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\notin\mathcal{S}}\left\|\widetilde{Q}_{m}\right\|_{F}\left\|\widetilde{K}_{m}\right\|_{F}.$ 

(48)

Finally, by the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality, we obtain

 $\|\widetilde{Q}_{m}\|_{F}\|\widetilde{K}_{m}\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\widetilde{Q}_{m}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\widetilde{K}_{m}\|_{F}^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}E_{m}.$ 

(49)

Combining the above inequalities yields

 $\boxed{\left\|\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}-\widetilde{Q}_{\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{K}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\top}\right\|_{2}\leq\sum_{m\notin\mathcal{S}}\left\|\widetilde{Q}_{m}\right\|_{F}\left\|\widetilde{K}_{m}\right\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\notin\mathcal{S}}E_{m}.}$ 

(50)

For a fixed budget $|\mathcal{S}|=k$ , minimizing the right-hand side is
equivalent to maximizing

 $\sum_{m\in\mathcal{S}}E_{m}.$ 

Therefore, the subset consisting of the $k$ largest-energy RoPE pairs
minimizes this upper bound among all subsets of $k$ pairs.
 $\square$ 

Evaluation in the pre- or post-RoPE space

Because each RoPE pair is transformed by an orthogonal $2\times 2\ $ rotation,

 $\left\|\widetilde{Q}_{m}\right\|_{F}=\left\|Q_{m}\right\|_{F}\qquad\left\|\widetilde{K}_{m}\right\|_{F}=\left\|K_{m}\right\|_{F}$ 

Hence,

 $E_{m}=\left\|\widetilde{Q}_{m}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\left\|\widetilde{K}_{m}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\left\|Q_{m}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\left\|K_{m}\right\|_{F}^{2}$ 

∎
The pair-energy criterion can therefore be evaluated equivalently in the pre- or post-RoPE space. The implementation accumulates the joint Q/K second moment and ranks each pair by the sum of its two diagonal entries, which is proportional to this $E_{m}$ . In pour implementations we use the post-RoPE values for the calibration.

Connection to attention-output perturbation.

Let

 $Z=\frac{\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}},\qquad Z_{\mathcal{S}}=\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{K}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}.$ 

The Jacobian of softmax at a probability vector $p$ is
 $\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^{\top}$ , whose spectral norm is at most
 $1/2$ . Applying this result row-wise gives

 $\|A-A_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|Z-Z_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{F}.$ 

Because $Z-Z_{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{R}^{S_{\mathrm{cal}}\times S_{\mathrm{cal}}}$ ,

 $\displaystyle\|Z-Z_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{F}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{cal}}}\,\|Z-Z_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{2}$ 

 $\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{cal}}}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\left\|\widetilde{Q}\widetilde{K}^{\top}-\widetilde{Q}_{\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{K}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\top}\right\|_{2}.$ 

For fixed values $V$ ,

 $\displaystyle\|AV-A_{\mathcal{S}}V\|_{F}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\|A-A_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{F}\|V\|_{2}$ 

 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{cal}}}\|V\|_{2}}{4\sqrt{d_{k}}}\sum_{m\notin\mathcal{S}}E_{m},$ 

where the final inequality follows from
Proposition 1.

More generally, if the downstream calibration loss is locally
 $L_{\mathrm{down}}$ -Lipschitz with respect to the attention output,
then

 $\left|\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{down}}(AV)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{down}}(A_{\mathcal{S}}V)\right|\leq L_{\mathrm{down}}\frac{\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{cal}}}\|V\|_{2}}{4\sqrt{d_{k}}}\sum_{m\notin\mathcal{S}}E_{m}.$ 

This is a conditional, calibration-dependent bound and does not imply
that energy selection globally minimizes language-model loss.

## A.7 Implementation Details

Software and model execution.

We implement RACE in PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers. Our implementation
supports GPT-NeoX, Llama, Qwen2, Pythia, and Granite-style attention modules,

construction use higher-precision accumulation during calibration. The folded compressed model

Triton kernel. Unless otherwise stated, we use PyTorch scaled dot-product
attention (SDPA) for the compressed runtime. The implementation also supports
eager attention and FlashAttention-3 as shown below.

Optional post-compression recovery.

RACE is a post-training compression method and can be evaluated immediately after constructing and folding the reduced Q/K and V subspaces into the pretrained attention projections. At aggressive compression ratios, however, this discrete change in attention dimensionality can perturb the pretrained representation enough that a short optimization stage is highly beneficial. Although optional, all results in the main paper involve this recovery. Following folding, we use the original uncompressed checkpoint as a frozen teacher and optimize the compressed model using KL distillation on FineWeb data. The implementation uses a temperature of $T=2$ and minimizes the divergence between the teacher and compressed-model output distributions. We train for 250 optimizer steps with sequences of length 512, a micro-batch size of 2, and gradient accumulation over 4 micro-batches, corresponding to approximately $1.0$ M training tokens.

Figure 10 separates the effect of this recovery from the compression procedure itself by reporting both the directly folded models (“w/o FT”) and their recovered counterparts. At 75% and 87.5% KV-cache retention, the directly compressed models are already relatively close to their recovered versions, indicating that moderate dimensionality reduction introduces limited disruption. In contrast, at 50% retention, both RACE and RAP experience substantially larger degradation without recovery, particularly in perplexity. The optimization stage is not necessary to define or execute the compressed model. RACE produces a valid reconstruction-free reduced-dimensional attention layer immediately after folding. Rather, recovery provides an optional mechanism for adapting the pretrained weights when the dimensionality change becomes sufficiently aggressive. Moreover, the same recovery procedure also substantially improves RAP, suggesting that much of the recoverable degradation at aggressive compression reflects adaptation to the compressed representation rather than a method-specific training advantage.

Language Modeling and Zero-Shot Performance

The main quality experiments evaluate Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, and
Granite-4.1-3B. We compare against PALU-GLRD, EigenAttention, Thin Keys, and
RAP at matched or reported KV-cache budgets. The full results are presented in Table 3 while only RAP, a reconstruction-free method like RACE, and PALU-GLRD, a reconstruction-based method, are included in Figure 3. Across all three model families, RACE consistently outperforms the
reconstruction-free baselines RAP and Thin Keys, while remaining competitive
with the reconstruction-based EigenAttention and PALU. The difference is most
pronounced under aggressive compression: At 50% KV-cache retention, RACE achieves the lowest WikiText perplexity across all three model families while remaining competitive with PALU in average zero-shot accuracy. among the evaluated compression methods on each of Qwen2.5, Granite,
and Llama. At more moderate compression, RACE remains close to the
reconstruction-based methods; at 75% retention it preserves 98.5%, 96.5%,
and 97.5% of the corresponding uncompressed average zero-shot accuracy on
Qwen, Granite, and Llama, respectively. The per-task results also show that the effect of compression is
not uniform across benchmarks: some tasks remain close to the uncompressed
baseline even at lower retention, whereas others are more sensitive to reduced
attention dimensionality. Table 4 presents the per-task results of Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct on 16 English tasks in LongBench. RACE outperforms RAP at all evaluated retention levels and exceeds PALU at 87.5% and 75% retention on both model families. At 50% retention, RACE remains competitive with PALU, outperforming it on Qwen while trailing it modestly on Llama. Together, these results show that the
quality-compression behavior is consistent across model families rather than
being specific to a single architecture, while retaining the reconstruction-free
execution of RACE.

Additional Systems Results

The systems results in Figure 11 show that RACE reduces both the fixed attention-parameter footprint and the context-dependent KV-cache footprint. As expected, the reduction in attention parameter memory aligns with the compression. However, since the folded projection weights are smaller in comparison to the result of the model parameters and memory consumption elements, the reduction in memory is less prominant in the total parameter memory. Additionally parameter memory is independent of context length. KV-cache memory scales with sequence length and therefore yields increasingly large absolute savings at longer contexts. At 256K tokens, the gap between the base model and the compressed variants becomes substantial, illustrating that the main systems benefit of RACE grows with context length while preserving native reduced-dimensional execution.

Compatibility with Token compression and Quantization

Table 5 reports the full per-task results when RACE is combined with token-compression and KV-quantization methods. Across H2O, SnapKV, and KIVI, applying RACE on top of an already compressed KV cache provides additional multiplicative memory reduction, but also introduces further accuracy and perplexity degradation. This effect is generally more pronounced for token-compression methods than for quantization.

For H2O and SnapKV, reducing the head dimension with RACE consistently lowers the total KV-cache footprint from 50% to 37.5% and 25%, or from 25% to 18.75% and 12.5%, depending on the token-retention ratio. The corresponding degradation is gradual across most zero-shot tasks, while WikiText perplexity increases more noticeably at aggressive combined compression. For example, with H2O at 50% token retention, adding RACE at 75% and 50% head retention reduces total KV-cache usage to 37.5% and 25%, respectively, while average zero-shot accuracy decreases from 0.706 to 0.674 and 0.604. SnapKV follows a similar trend, with average accuracy decreasing from 0.710 to 0.676 and 0.612 under the same settings.

RACE is comparatively more complementary with KIVI. With 4-bit quantization, adding RACE at 75% and 50% head retention reduces the total KV cache from 50% to 37.5% and 25%, while average accuracy decreases from 0.702 to 0.678 and 0.608. Similar behavior is observed with 2-bit quantization. Overall, the results suggest that RACE composes cleanly with both token and precision compression, but the cost of applying additional head-dimension compression is larger once information has already been removed along the token dimension. In contrast, quantization leaves more redundancy for RACE to exploit, resulting in a milder degradation at comparable total cache budgets.

We first examine whether the retained RoPE pairs should be selected using information from queries and keys jointly or from either projection alone. Across all retention ratios, the joint Q/K energy criterion gives the strongest overall results. At 87.5% retention, Q-only and K-only selection remain close to the joint criterion, but the gap becomes clearer as compression increases. At 75% retention, joint Q/K energy achieves 0.61 average accuracy with a WikiText perplexity of 14.95, compared with 0.59/16.78 for Q-only and 0.60/16.17 for K-only. At 50% retention, the joint criterion retains 0.54 average accuracy and 16.02 perplexity, while both Q-only and K-only fall to 0.52 average accuracy with substantially higher perplexity.

This trend is also reflected directly in the approximation of the uncompressed attention interaction. Figure 12 shows the layer-wise error norm of the compressed $QK^{\top}$ product relative to the uncompressed model. Joint Q/K selection gives the lowest average error, 0.105, compared with 0.112 for K-only and 0.116 for Q-only, and the reduction is observed across most layers rather than being concentrated in a small subset. Together, the downstream results and the layer-wise approximation error support deriving the shared selector from both Q and K rather than from either side in isolation.

We next compare different scoring criteria that all operate on joint Q/K information. Joint activation energy consistently outperforms magnitude, random selection, gradient-Fisher scoring, and attention-based scoring, with the differences becoming larger at stronger compression. At 50% retention, RACE achieves 0.54 average accuracy, compared with 0.48 for gradient Fisher and attention score, 0.45 for magnitude, and 0.41 for random selection. The same trend is reflected in WikiText perplexity, where joint Q/K energy also achieves the lowest value. Notably, the gradient- and attention-based alternatives do not recover the performance of the simple forward-pass energy criterion despite requiring more complex statistics. Overall, these ablations indicate that both components of the selector are important: the ranking should be derived jointly from Q and K, and joint activation energy provides an effective criterion for identifying the RoPE pairs that best preserve the original attention interaction.
