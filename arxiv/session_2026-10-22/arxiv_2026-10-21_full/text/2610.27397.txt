Dose-Driven Collapse and Validation-Gated Abstention

Abstract

Learned post-training quantization (PTQ) calibration fits quantizer parameters on a small
calibration set, and for a text embedder the natural supervision is retrieval relevance. We
ask two questions that are usually conflated: how far to calibrate, and whether
to calibrate at all. Across three embedders (e5-base-v2, BGE-M3, EmbeddingGemma-300m) at 4-bit
weight quantization, 161 calibration runs and five learning rates, we find that the outcome
of a fixed procedure ranges from a gain to catastrophic degradation, and that the driver is
the dose of the update, measured as the functional displacement $D_{\mathrm{func}}$ of the quantized
model from its uncalibrated start. On EmbeddingGemma the same learning-rate grid gives
 $+$ 1.47 to $-$ 71.53 points of in-domain NDCG@10 change; a label-free cosine
reconstruction objective collapses at the same dose ( $-$ 69.52 vs $-$ 71.63
in the highest dose bin), so the collapse is a property of learnable calibration rather than of
retrieval supervision. Objective sensitivity is nevertheless heterogeneous: on e5, ranking
calibration is substantially more robust than cosine calibration at matched displacement
( $+$ 14.16 $\pm$ 4.34 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ , 95% t-CI [ $+$ 3.37, $+$ 24.96],
three paired slates), whereas on BGE-M3 the ordering is directionally opposite but unresolved
([ $-$ 5.18, $+$ 2.38]). Dose is the main axis but not a sufficient statistic: equal
 $D_{\mathrm{func}}$ reached along different paths differs by up to 1.61 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ . We therefore treat
calibration as an optional intervention. A pre-registered rule that accepts a checkpoint only
when a per-query paired confidence interval on a 25% calibration-validation split clears the
uncalibrated baseline was run on 6 independent calibration slates and five arms. It never
selected a checkpoint below its slate’s baseline (0 harmful selections in 30
decisions; pre-registered harm-prevention score 0.56), retained 8 of 11
beneficial updates, and paid a mean oracle regret of 1.52 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ (slate-cluster 95%
[0.59, 2.76]) with one abstention that forfeited 11.13 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ . The result is
a safety–opportunity trade-off, not a free improvement. We report what remains open: the ordering
on BGE-M3, the mechanism behind path residuals, and label efficiency, which our audit shows was
never established.

## 1 Introduction

Post-training quantization of a text embedder to 4-bit weights is cheap when the quantizer
parameters are set by a closed-form rule (min–max or percentile scaling) and expensive to get
wrong: the deployed artifact ranks documents, and a small shift in the embedding geometry can
reorder the top of a result list without changing any reconstruction statistic much. Learned
calibration [13, 5, 26, 22, 28] fits those parameters with gradient
steps on a small calibration set, and for a retriever the tempting supervision is retrieval
relevance itself—a ranking loss over labelled queries [3].

This paper is about what that procedure actually does to three embedders, measured carefully
enough to separate two questions that are usually asked together. The first is a dose
question: how far should the quantizer move from its uncalibrated start? The second is a
gate question: should it move at all, and can that be decided without touching held-out
labels? We find that the first question has no universal answer, that the second one has a
usable partial answer, and that several claims one would naturally make along the way—that
retrieval supervision causes the collapse, that a ranking objective is safer, that selected
calibration queries are label-efficient—are not supported by the ledger once the comparison
is dose-matched, slate-paired, or counted in the right unit.

## Contributions.

(1) On EmbeddingGemma-300m the outcome of one fixed procedure spans
 $+$ 1.47 to $-$ 71.53 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ of in-domain NDCG@10 across a four-point learning-rate grid, and
the collapse is reproduced by a label-free objective at the same functional displacement; the
unsafe regime belongs to learnable calibration, not to retrieval supervision (§3).
(2) Objective sensitivity is heterogeneous across models: a large ranking-over-cosine robustness
advantage on e5, no established ordering on BGE-M3 (§4). (3) $D_{\mathrm{func}}$ is a
useful screening variable but not a sufficient statistic; a pre-registered scalar-threshold
hypothesis is rejected (§5). (4) A pre-registered validation rule, run on six
independent calibration slates, never selected a checkpoint below the uncalibrated baseline while
forfeiting some beneficial updates; we report it as a four-number scorecard, keeping the
pre-registered metric beside the realized outcome (§6). (5) An implementation
audit that changed several conclusions is reported as part of the result, including a label-budget
accounting that retracts an earlier efficiency claim (§7, §8).

We claim model-agnosticism of the procedure, not model-invariance of the numbers.
The three embedders differ in backbone, pooling, prefix conventions and normalization; a single
safe-dose constant is neither expected nor observed. What generalizes is that one frozen rule
adapts to each model through its own validation signal—model A receiving a small update and
model B receiving none is the content of the generalization, not a failure of it.

## 2 Experimental setup

## Models.

e5-base-v2 [34] (BERT [9] backbone, mean pooling, “query:/passage:”
prefixes), BGE-M3 [4] (XLM-R [7] backbone, CLS pooling, no prefix) and
EmbeddingGemma-300m [30] (Gemma 3 [17] decoder backbone, mean pooling with a dense head,
task prefixes). All linear layers are quantized to 4-bit weights with a learned step-size quantizer
(LSQ [13], with rounding passed through the straight-through
estimator [2]); the quantizer parameters are the only trainable variables.

## Data.

Calibration draws 32 labelled queries from each of four BEIR [29]
domains (MS MARCO [27], FiQA, NFCorpus, SciFact [32]) over a 2,000-document corpus per
domain. In-domain generalization is measured on 25 disjoint queries per domain that share no
query with the calibration set. Cross-domain generalization is measured on two held-out datasets
never used for calibration (ArguAna, SciDocs [6]; 100 queries, 2,000 documents). Three further datasets
(TREC-COVID [31], Climate-FEVER [10], Quora) remain sealed and were not evaluated in any run reported here.
MS MARCO and SciFact are ceiling-limited in this setting (more than 70% of full-precision queries
already at NDCG@10 = 1.0), so we keep the four-domain macro average only as a continuity figure
and read improvement on the two unsaturated domains and on the held-out sets.

## Objectives.

The retrieval-supervised objective is LambdaRank [3]
over each query’s positives and mined negatives (lambdarank). Two label-free controls
share every other setting: cosine, which pulls quantized embeddings toward their
full-precision counterparts, and mse, which reconstructs weights. Calibration runs for
12 steps with Adam [21] at a learning rate from $\{10^{-4},3{\times}10^{-4},10^{-3},3{\times}10^{-3}\}$ ,
extended to $10^{-2}$ where a control could not otherwise reach the dose of interest.

## Dose.

 $D_{\mathrm{func}}$ is the mean cosine distance between the calibrated and the
uncalibrated quantized embeddings of the calibration queries and their candidate documents at a
given step. It measures how far the quantized function has moved, independently of the
objective that moved it, and is the matching variable for every cross-objective comparison.

## Metrics and noise.

We report NDCG@10 change in percentage points (pp) relative to
the uncalibrated quantized model (step 0) unless stated otherwise; retention is the quantized/full-
precision ratio. Noise floors are measured per model and per learning rate rather than borrowed:
on e5, re-running the same configuration gives $\sigma_{\text{runtime}}=0.184\,\mathrm{pp}$ 
and varying only the optimizer seed $\sigma_{\text{opt}}=0.192\,\mathrm{pp}$ (SciDocs), while varying only
the calibration slate gives $\sigma_{\text{slate}}=0.66\,\mathrm{pp}$ on ArguAna and 2.54 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ on
SciDocs—the slate, not the optimizer, is the dominant variance source. On EmbeddingGemma $\sigma_{\text{opt}}$ 
is heteroscedastic across the learning-rate grid (0.504, 1.141,
3.470, 0.675 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ ), so significance is judged against the cell’s own
 $\sigma$ and repeated statistics are reported as median [min, max] or mean $\pm$ SD with $n$ stated.
Every headline comparison below is paired within a calibration slate.

## Pre-registration.

The selection protocol (§6), the dose-matched
comparator (§3), the saturation criterion and the hypotheses in
§8 were frozen before the corresponding batches ran; where a post-hoc analysis
is added it is labelled as such. All numbers in this paper are generated from the experiment ledger
by code; none is typed by hand.

On EmbeddingGemma the four learning rates give $+$ 1.47, $-$ 6.28, $-$ 30.48 and
 $-$ 71.53 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ (medians of three optimizer seeds; Fig. 1). The same procedure that

this work read the two models as an architectural dichotomy—“works on e5, fails on Gemma”—and
that reading was an uncontrolled learning-rate comparison, not a property of either architecture.

## Is the collapse caused by retrieval supervision?

We ran the label-free controls on
the same slates, quantizer parameters, learning-rate grid, steps and evaluation queries, changing
only the objective. Read by learning rate the generic objectives look stable, but at the same
learning rate they move the quantizer four to six times less; the pre-registered comparison is
therefore dose-matched: points from all trajectories are binned by $D_{\mathrm{func}}$ and the two
objectives are compared within each bin against a tolerance of three times the model’s own
 $\sigma_{\text{opt}}$ (Table 1). On EmbeddingGemma the cosine objective collapses
in the highest dose bin exactly as LambdaRank does ( $-$ 69.52 vs
 $-$ 71.63 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ , both saturated), and after filling the intermediate doses with two extra
learning rates ( $1.5{\times}10^{-3}$ , $2{\times}10^{-3}$ , $n{=}3$ each) the comparator has
7 qualifying bins and returns not_retrieval_specific: at every matched dose the
generic objective is hurt at least as much as the ranking one. The weight-reconstruction control
never reaches the collapse dose—its loss converges quickly and the quantizer stops at
 $D_{\mathrm{func}}=0.107$ even at the largest learning rate—which is not evidence of safety but
of an objective that cannot deliver the dose.

## 4 Result 2: objective sensitivity is heterogeneous across models

Whether the two objectives differ within the safe regime is a separate question, and the
answer differs by model. On e5 the cosine objective stays near $D_{\mathrm{func}}\approx 0.06$ across the
original grid and only reaches the collapse dose at $10^{-2}$ ( $D_{\mathrm{func}}=0.148$ ,
 $-$ 11.5 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ , $n{=}3$ ); in the same $D_{\mathrm{func}}$ bin the ranking objective is still positive
( $+$ 3.22 vs $-$ 9.83 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ , gap $-$ 13.05), and the comparator returns
control_worse. On BGE-M3 neither objective collapses anywhere on the grid
(cosine reaches at most $D_{\mathrm{func}}=0.201$ ), and the slate-0 comparator favours the cosine
objective ( $+$ 5.35 vs $+$ 2.46 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ in the 0.20–0.35 bin, gap $+$ 2.89 against a
tolerance of 0.92 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ ; retrieval_specific).

A single-slate bin comparison is the weaker instrument, so the two decisive cells per model were
re-run on three independent slates and matched step by step (Table 2). On e5 the
ranking advantage is large and clear of zero: $\Delta=$ + $14.16\pm 4.34\,\mathrm{pp}$ ,

We place the paired multi-slate result above the single-slate comparator and state the outcome
accordingly: on e5, ranking calibration is substantially more robust than cosine calibration at
matched functional displacement; on BGE-M3 the ordering is unresolved; on EmbeddingGemma both
collapse. We do not claim that the ordering reverses by model, only that objective sensitivity
is heterogeneous, and we do not claim that either objective is generally safer.

## 5 Result 3: distance explains damage but is not sufficient

 $D_{\mathrm{func}}$ is a far better explanatory variable than the step count: within a model the damage is
monotone in $D_{\mathrm{func}}$ (Fig. 1), and it is the variable that makes the objective
comparison of §3 possible at all. The pre-registered strong form—that performance
is a function of $D_{\mathrm{func}}$ alone—is nevertheless rejected: on e5, trajectories reaching the same
 $D_{\mathrm{func}}$ band along different learning rates differ by up to 1.61 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ in
calibration-validation NDCG@10 (median per learning rate within the band), roughly nine times
 $\sigma_{\text{runtime}}$ . The measurement itself is not the reason. Repeating the collapsing
EmbeddingGemma configuration over five optimizer seeds gives $D_{\mathrm{func}}=0.686$ with
 $\sigma=0.0227$ (3.3%), and at low dose on e5 five repeats give $D_{\mathrm{func}}=0.032$ with $\sigma=0.00026$ 
(0.8%); the path residual is real. (Whether the higher learning rate reaches the
band in fewer steps is confounded with how many updates the objective received, so we do not
attribute the residual to step size.)11

offer a mechanism for the residual and did not search for one; §8 lists it as
open.

## 6 Result 4: selective calibration

If calibration can help or hurt, the operational question is whether the harmful case can be
refused without held-out labels. The rule is simple and was frozen before the multi-slate batch:
hold out 25% of the calibration queries; after each step, compute the per-query NDCG@10 gain over
step 0 on that split; accept the step whose paired bootstrap lower bound clears zero with the
largest mean gain; if no step clears, abstain and ship the uncalibrated quantizer. The held-out
sets never enter the rule. An earlier version of this section reported a plain argmax over the
validation mean; on three optimizer seeds that argmax chose a checkpoint below the baseline once
( $-3.42\,\mathrm{pp}$ against oracles of about $+1.7\,\mathrm{pp}$ ), which is why the confidence-interval rule replaced it.

The rule was run on five arms—EmbeddingGemma at $10^{-4}$ , $3{\times}10^{-4}$ , $10^{-3}$ and e5 at
 $10^{-3}$ , $3{\times}10^{-3}$ —on six independent calibration slates (Table 3,
Fig. 2). Across six independent slates and five arms per slate, no selected checkpoint
fell below its slate’s baseline: 0 harmful selections in 30 decisions. The five
arms within a slate are correlated, so we do not treat this as thirty independent trials; taking
the slate as the unit, zero events in six gives a one-sided 95% upper bound of about
39% on the per-slate probability of a harmful selection. The statement that holds is
that no harmful checkpoint was observed, not that the probability is near zero. The pre-registered
metric is reported unchanged: harm_prevention  $=0.56$ , because it scores
abstention on arms whose final step collapses and the rule increasingly stopped early
instead—on the EmbeddingGemma $3{\times}10^{-4}$ and $10^{-3}$ arms it accepted checkpoints of
 $+1$ to $+2\,\mathrm{pp}$ on slates where the final step was $-10$ to $-32\,\mathrm{pp}$ . We keep both numbers because
they answer different questions; a confirmation study would pre-register the realized harmful
selection rate as the primary safety endpoint.

The cost is opportunity. Regret is computed over all 30 decisions with abstention scored
at the baseline: mean 1.52 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ (SD 2.66; slate-cluster bootstrap 95%
[0.59, 2.76]), abstention rate 14/30, opportunity retention
8/11, and a maximum regret of 11.13 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ on one e5 slate where the rule abstained
from both arms although the oracle checkpoint was strongly positive. Regret over accepted runs
alone is 0.49 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ and is reported only as an auxiliary figure, since it hides exactly the
forfeited opportunities that matter. The selector is therefore conservative with respect to
catastrophic harm and sometimes conservative with respect to large gains; it is a safety–opportunity
trade-off, not a free improvement.

Two further observations bound what the small EmbeddingGemma gains mean. Doubling the validation
split to 50% did not recover the missed $+2\,\mathrm{pp}$ updates (still one of three slates accepted) and
only shrank the optimization set. And the pre-registered shuffled-label null run on EmbeddingGemma
(20 seeds, same cell) passes on the pre-registered metrics but the in-domain gain
of $+$ 2.38 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ sits inside the shuffled-label tail (null maximum $+$ 2.70 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ ,
1 exceedance): the small gain is scored as a missed opportunity in the decision
layer and is not attributed to retrieval supervision in the mechanism layer.

## 7 Validity

## Fake-quantization.

All numbers come from simulated quantization, so every calibrated
checkpoint is also exported to packed INT4 integer codes and compared bit by bit against the
simulated path: 85,524,480 weights across 73 layers on e5 and
101,449,728 across 168 on EmbeddingGemma, zero mismatches, at exactly four bits
per weight. The check runs by default on every run, and objectives without an integer code (soft
rounding) are refused export.

## Evaluation determinism.

Retrieval scores are float32 on GPU and ties near the
cut-off can flip NDCG@10 between otherwise identical runs. An audit records, per held-out
query, the distance from the best positive to the nearest irrelevant score; over all slate runs the worst case on ArguAna is
16 near-ties below $10^{-4}$ with a tie swing of 1.48 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ , and on SciDocs
10 near-ties with a swing of 0.50 $\,\mathrm{pp}$ ; differences below those swings are
below the instrument and are not headlined.

## Label specificity.

On e5 the LambdaRank gain exceeds all 50 within-domain
shuffled-label controls (calibration-fit retention 1.0369 vs a null maximum of
0.7830 over 50 seeds; randomization $p=0.0196$ ), and the same holds for the pre-registered metrics on
EmbeddingGemma. A derangement null and a same-code re-run of the plain null were added as
sensitivity analyses; neither changes the verdict.

## Audit.

A two-pass implementation audit changed several conclusions before this
version and is part of the record: a loss misclassified as label-free, a query-selection routine
that read the relevance labels of its whole candidate pool, an under-exclusion of self-matches in
held-out corpora, a determinism audit that first measured the wrong gap, and a number gate whose
discriminating power turned out to be 54% on perturbed values and was demoted to an existence
check in favour of row-level tests against the ledger.

## Label efficiency.

An earlier draft described the selected calibration set as
“32 labelled queries”. The audit showed that the margin-diversity selector reads the relevance
labels of its entire candidate pool, so the labels accessed exceed the labels
selected, which exceed the labels the gradient uses. In the gate’s own unit
(Table 4) 128 selected and 512 random calibration examples give indistinguishable
point estimates at the present sample size ( $+$ 0.0004 NDCG, Welch 95% CI [ $-$ 0.037, $+$ 0.038],
 $n{=}3$ each), while the selected arm accessed 1,024 labels against 512. The pre-registered
margin of 0.2 NDCG is wider than any plausible effect and is recorded as a specification defect:
the gate passed without information. We therefore make no label-efficiency claim; the supportable
observation is that a smaller selected calibration set produced a similar point estimate to a
four-times-larger random one, at higher label access.

## The BGE-M3 ordering

is unresolved (§4), and BGE-M3 has not been
pushed to a collapsing dose; “every model collapses if pushed far enough” is not a claim this
paper supports.

## Mechanism.

The path residual of §5 and the model dependence of
objective sensitivity have no mechanistic account here; update direction, objective geometry,
representation geometry, curvature and quantizer parameterization are all candidates, and we did
not begin that search because it would have reopened dozens of variables before the descriptive
results were pinned.

## Scale and sealed data.

Held-out evaluation uses 100 queries over 2,000 documents per
dataset, so absolute NDCG is not headlined; three test datasets remain sealed for a single
post-freeze evaluation that this version has not performed.

## 9 Related work

Post-training quantization is surveyed in [18]. Learned PTQ
calibration [13, 5, 26, 22] and the large-model
methods it grew into [15, 14, 23, 28] optimise
reconstruction or output error and report language-model perplexity, as do the outlier-handling
schemes developed alongside them [8, 35]; the question of when the calibration itself becomes harmful
is rarely asked because the loss is bounded by construction. Mixed-precision allocation by
Hessian sensitivity [12, 11], search [33], information
flow [1], layer-wise geometry [36] or inter-layer
interaction [39, 24] concerns which modules to protect, an axis we
hold fixed. Dense
retrievers [20, 19] are commonly evaluated on zero-shot suites such as
BEIR and MTEB [29, 25], and retrieval-specific compression has
focused on the index and the output vectors [37, 38]; our object is the encoder’s weights and the failure mode
is a calibration dose, not a codebook. The closest methodological relative is early stopping with a
validation split, whose failure mode—flat validation curves that let an argmax wander—is exactly
what forced the interval rule of §6. Declining to calibrate when the evidence is
weak is also the stance of selective prediction [16], applied here to a
deployment decision rather than to individual predictions.

## 10 Conclusion

Learned PTQ calibration of text embedders has a dose-driven unsafe regime that label-reading and
label-free objectives share. Inside the safe regime, ranking supervision can be genuinely useful
on some models and is not established as useful on others. A pre-registered, label-free validation
rule refused every harmful update on six independent calibration slates while forfeiting some
beneficial ones; the honest description is a safety–opportunity trade-off whose harm side is
observed at zero and whose opportunity side is measured at 8/11. Calibration should be
treated as an optional intervention with a gate, not as an unconditional optimization.

The runner, the frozen protocol, the pre-registered comparator, the
selection rule, the audit log and the ledger from which every number in this paper is generated
are released with the paper.
