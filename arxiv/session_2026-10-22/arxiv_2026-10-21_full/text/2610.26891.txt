## PW-PRUNING: PHASE-AWARE EXPERT SELECTION

FOR SPEECH-CENTRIC OMNI-MODAL MODELS

Junjie Cao, Fengping Tian, Wenjie Zhou

Xuanfan Ni, Chenyang Lyu, Siyou Liu, Longyue Wang, Weihua Luo

††thanks: *Corresponding author: Chenyang Lyu.

Abstract

Sparse routing reduces per-token computation but leaves the full expert bank
resident in memory. We study expert pruning for speech-centric omni-modal
models, where pooling calibration statistics can obscure the different expert
demands of acoustic-input processing and text-answer generation. We propose
PW-Pruning, a phase-aware extension of DO-ACP that constructs separate
importance-weighted kernels for acoustic-prefill and teacher-forced answer
tokens, then combines phase-weighted log-determinant marginal gains to select one
static expert bank. On Qwen3-Omni, retaining 32 of 128 experts per layer
improves MMAU-mini and MMSU accuracy over matched pooled selection by
 $3.20$ and $4.38$ percentage points, respectively, after supervised fine-tuning,
averaged over three runs. Single-phase and shuffled-label controls on this
public backbone suggest that semantic phase information contributes to these
gains. Experiments on a non-public Qwen3.5-Omni backbone and English-only
zero-shot evaluation of Nemotron provide complementary evidence.
Across the tested Qwen budgets, AU gains are larger under stronger pruning,
without inference-time mask switching.

Index Terms: 

expert pruning, mixture of experts, omni-modal models, audio understanding

## 1 Introduction

Expert pruning reduces the resident memory of mixture-of-experts (MoE)
models [9, 12]: sparse routing lowers per-token
computation but retains the full expert bank [6, 8].
Multimodal applications span speech/music generation [27, 7, 2],
human animation [13], and video protection [3, 14].
Omni-modal deployments such as speech assistants and translation may
prioritize speech-centric capabilities [26, 18],
motivating a smaller shared expert bank that preserves them.

Existing approaches use task-specific selection [4],
output reconstruction [15], or expert importance: PS ranks
routing mass [9], while REAP incorporates router-weighted
output norms [12]. DO-ACP additionally accounts for redundancy
among selected experts through activation-weighted log-determinant selection
 [9]. Yet pooling calibration statistics can obscure
where expert value arises, even within one task. We distinguish two
token regions, or phases, within a speech request: listening comprises
acoustic-prefill input positions that process audio evidence, while answering
comprises response positions that produce output text. Calibration uses
teacher-forced answers as a proxy for generation. An expert redundant in one
phase may complement the retained bank in the other; pooling before selection
can hide this distinction even with task-specific calibration.

On Qwen3-Omni, the phases’ high-frequency expert sets have mean Jaccard
overlap $.179$ at E32 and $.342$ at E64 (Fig. 1a).
Jaccard measures intersection over union: zero denotes disjoint sets and one
identical sets. Weak overlap also appears within tasks, motivating our
hypothesis that preserving phase identity during selection better represents
complementary demands than pooling statistics first. Our contributions are
threefold: (i) we characterize this cross-phase routing disagreement;
(ii) we propose PW-Pruning, whose PW-DO-ACP selector constructs one
importance-weighted Gram kernel per phase and greedily combines
log-determinant marginal gains, retaining one static expert bank
without runtime mask switching; and (iii) we evaluate capability retention
through zero-shot pruning, matched recovery, and semantic controls on
Qwen3-Omni, with complementary evidence from Qwen3.5-Omni and Nemotron.

## 2 Method

## 2.1 Problem formulation

Let $\theta$ be a pretrained omni model and
 $S_{\ell}\subseteq[E_{\ell}]$ the experts retained in layer $\ell$ .
The masks $\mathcal{S}=\{S_{\ell}\}_{\ell}$ define $\theta_{\mathcal{S}}$ , inheriting
surviving weights and native routing fan-in. For task distributions $\mathcal{D}_{g}$ with nonnegative
weights $\pi_{g}$ summing to one, we express capability retention through the
reference loss $\mathcal{L}(\theta;x,y)=-\log p_{\theta}(y\mid x)/|y|$ , with
reference length $|y|$ :

 $\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{S}}{\operatorname{minimize}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\sum_{g}\pi_{g}\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_{g}}\bigl[\mathcal{L}(\theta_{\mathcal{S}};x,y)\bigr]$ 

(1)

 $\displaystyle\text{subject to}$ 
 $\displaystyle S_{\ell}\subseteq[E_{\ell}],\quad|S_{\ell}|=B_{\ell},\quad\forall\ell.$ 

Here $(x,y)$ is an input–reference pair. We fix $B_{\ell}=E_{\ell}/R$ above the
routing fan-in to compare selectors without changing layerwise capacity.
Cross-layer loss optimization is costly. We instead test the assumption that
retaining complementary phase-conditioned features improves capability retention,
using a layerwise log-det surrogate rather than a bound on Eq. 1.
Below we suppress $\ell$ , writing $E$ and $B$ .

## 2.2 From pooled importance to phase-wise coverage

For token $t$ , let $z_{e}(t)$ indicate whether the original router selects
expert $e$ , $q_{e}(t)$ be its full-bank softmax probability before top- $k$ 
selection, and $\mathbf{f}_{e}(t)$ its ungated output. Acoustic-prefill and
teacher-forced-answer tokens define phases $p\in\{\mathrm{pre},\mathrm{ans}\}$ .
The operator $\mathbb{E}_{p}$ averages tokens within each task group and then
weights nonempty groups equally. Applying DO-ACP [9] within
each phase gives conditional routing confidence and an output Gram matrix:

 $\displaystyle c_{e}^{p}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{p}[z_{e}q_{e}]}{\mathbb{E}_{p}[z_{e}]},$ 

(2)

 $\displaystyle G_{ij}^{p}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{p}\bigl[\langle\mathbf{f}_{i},\mathbf{f}_{j}\rangle\bigr].$ 

Set $c_{e}^{p}=0$ for an expert never selected in that phase. ACP importance
 $u_{e}^{p}=c_{e}^{p}\sqrt{G_{ee}^{p}}$ combines conditional confidence with root-mean-square
output magnitude. With $D^{p}=\operatorname{Diag}(\sqrt{\mathbf{u}^{p}})$ , the
importance-weighted kernel is

 $K^{p}=D^{p}G^{p}D^{p}\succeq 0.$ 

(3)

Thus $K^{p}_{ee}=c_{e}^{p}(G^{p}_{ee})^{3/2}$ : the amplitude exponent is inherited from
DO-ACP, not derived from optimal design. Our contribution separates phases
while retaining this weighting. For nonnegative phase weights
 $\alpha_{p}$ summing to one and regularization $\lambda_{p}>0$ , define

 $\displaystyle F(S)$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum_{p}\alpha_{p}\log\det\left(I+\frac{K^{p}[S,S]}{\lambda_{p}}\right),$ 

(4)

 $\displaystyle S^{\star}$ 
 $\displaystyle\in\underset{S\subseteq[E],\,|S|=B}{\arg\max}\;F(S).$ 

Here $K^{p}[S,S]$ is a principal submatrix and $I$ the matching identity.
This regularized feature-volume objective connects DPP diversity with
information-based sensor selection [11, 10].
For an auxiliary Gaussian covariance $K^{p}$ and noise $\lambda_{p}I$ , each
utility equals twice the information gain [23], not the
language model’s Fisher information. We use $\alpha_{p}=1/2$ to
balance phases, not to reproduce their token frequencies. Setting
 $\lambda_{p}=\operatorname{tr}(K^{p})/(BE)$ makes each utility invariant to
positive global kernel rescaling, while preserving relative expert energies.
Zero kernels use a positive floor. These are design choices, not optimality claims.

Pooled DO-ACP instead forms

 $\bar{c}_{e}=\frac{\sum_{p}\alpha_{p}\mathbb{E}_{p}[z_{e}q_{e}]}{\sum_{p}\alpha_{p}\mathbb{E}_{p}[z_{e}]},\qquad\bar{G}=\sum_{p}\alpha_{p}G^{p},$ 

(5)

then constructs one kernel and ridge by the same rule. Conditional ratios,
kernel construction, and log-determinants do not commute with averaging, so generally
 $F_{\rm pool}(S)\neq F(S)$ even with matched weights. PW changes
when phase information is discarded, not the budget or routing.

## 2.3 Greedy selection and deployment

Starting from $S=\varnothing$ , maintain each phase’s Cholesky factor
 $L_{p}L_{p}^{\top}=K^{p}_{SS}+\lambda_{p}I$ , where subscripts denote matrix blocks.
A candidate $e\notin S$ contributes the Schur residual

 $v_{p}(e\mid S)=K^{p}_{ee}-\bigl\|L_{p}^{-1}K^{p}_{Se}\bigr\|_{2}^{2},$ 

(6)

with the second term zero for empty $S$ . It measures what the current subset
does not explain in phase $p$ . The block determinant identity gives

 $\Delta(e\mid S)=\sum_{p}\alpha_{p}\log\left(1+\frac{v_{p}(e\mid S)}{\lambda_{p}}\right).$ 

(7)

We choose the largest $\Delta$ until $|S|=B$ , evaluating $L_{p}^{-1}K^{p}_{Se}$ 
by triangular solves rather than explicit inversion. Monotone submodularity
gives greedy a $1-1/e$ approximation for $F$ [17],
not a guarantee on language loss or benchmark accuracy.
The final subset serves both phases; subsequent recovery keeps its membership
fixed. Inference needs neither phase labels nor mask switching.

recovery, semantic ablations, and cross-model transfer. These experiments test
our central hypothesis: retaining listening and answering as distinct selection
objectives improves capability preservation within a fixed expert budget.

## 3.1 Experimental protocol

Models and census. We evaluate text responses from
Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct [26] (Thinker: approximately
30B total/3B active parameters, 48 layers, 128 experts per layer, top-8
routing [21]). Pruning to
E64/E32 removes expert tensors and router rows, preserving top-8 routing,
perception, and attention. Transfer uses Nemotron
3 Nano Omni-30B-A3B-Reasoning [18] at E64/E32, and a
non-public Qwen3.5-Omni backbone at $2\times/4\times$ expert-bank reduction.
Both Qwen censuses share 2,048 training-source requests balanced across ASR,
speech-to-text translation (S2TT), and audio understanding (AU). We count
acoustic-prefill and teacher-forced answers separately, excluding
instruction/special tokens. Stratified $1/64$ sampling estimates ungated Grams
for all experts. Nemotron uses English calibration and public English-ASR/AU only.

Baselines. We compare with Pooled DO-ACP, which builds one
kernel from pooled statistics;
PS [9] ranks routed probability mass; REAP
 [12] ranks conditional router-weighted output norms from
its all-token census. Pooled shares PW’s calibration, task/phase weights,
budget, regularization rule, and greedy solver.

Ablation. Audio-only and Answer-only select from one phase’s statistics
at the same budget. Shuffled-PW preserves two kernels and phase counts but
permutes labels within each request, testing the value of semantic grouping.
We apply these controls to both Qwen backbones under matched selection and
recovery settings; Table 2 reports mean endpoint accuracies
over repeated recovery runs.

Recovery. All matched selectors use fixed sources of 6,638 ASR,
11,894 S2TT, and 16,758 AU examples with transcription, translation, and
audio-response supervision. Mixing targets equal expected supervised-answer-token
exposure per task. SFT uses 1,024 cross-entropy steps, global batch 32,
learning rate $2\cdot 10^{-6}$ , 5% warmup, sequence cap 2,048, and no packing.
Retained language-model weights are tuned; perception encoders stay frozen.
KD uses reference-prefix KL at temperature 1, renormalized on
the teacher’s top-64 vocabulary support, without CE mixing or student rollout.

Public benchmarks. Selectors share datasets and scoring within each
backbone. ASR comprises 54,494 examples from Common
Voice en/fr/yue/zh [1], LibriSpeech
clean/other [19], and OpenCpop [25]. We retain native WER/CER families.
S2TT uses 1,104 FLEURS-derived examples in 234 directions [5]
with macro-direction SacreBLEU [20].
AU uses 1,000 MMAU-mini, 1,000 MMAR, and 5,000 MMSU multiple-choice questions
 [22, 16, 24]. Greedy decoding uses
ASR/S2TT/AU caps of 512/256/128 tokens. Failures count as errors; metrics
are reported separately by task.

## 3.2 Phase conditioning reallocates scarce capacity

We first examine retained demand through routing preferences and selected
masks (Fig. 1). Qwen3-Omni phase-mask Jaccard averages $.179$ 
at E32 and $.342$ at E64, falling to $.031$ and $.223$ in the final
eight layers. Within-task overlap is also low for ASR, S2TT, and AU
( $J=.262$ , $.232$ , $.113$ at E32). Task mixture alone therefore does not
explain the disagreement; Jaccard does not establish within-phase stability.
The selected masks show the allocation change: at the same E32 budget,
audio routing coverage rises from $26.03\%$ to $34.03\%$ , while answer
coverage falls from $45.37\%$ to $41.63\%$ (Fig. 1b).
The gap narrows from $19.34$ to $7.60$ points, showing that PW reallocates
capacity toward underrepresented audio demand rather than raising both coverages.
Downstream tests assess whether this redistribution preserves capability.

## 3.3 Phase-aware selection improves capability retention

We evaluate selection before and after adaptation through zero-shot,
SFT, and KD comparisons (Table 1). At E32, PW lowers zero-shot
CV-WER from $27.0\%$ to $18.3\%$ and improves all three AU scores over
Pooled: the benefit is already present before recovery.

With matched SFT, PW improves six of eight means over Pooled. AU gains
are largest on MMAU-mini ( $+3.20$ points) and MMSU ( $+4.38$ points),
while MMAR changes more modestly. CV-C and OpenCpop CER remain higher
than Pooled ( $14.25\%$ vs. $13.45\%$ and $5.05\%$ vs. $4.39\%$ ,
respectively); the OpenCpop gap also appears in the E32 zero-shot and
KD comparisons.
Crucially, Pooled already models importance and redundancy, using the same
expert budget and recovery resources. PW’s advantage supports
retaining where an expert contributes,
rather than assessing its pooled value alone. PW also outperforms PS and
REAP on all eight SFT metrics, reducing CV-WER by $26.3\%$ and $24.5\%$ ,
respectively. The E32 KD comparison likewise favors PW on six metrics.
Thus, adapting surviving weights does not eliminate the importance of
phase-aware subset selection.

The AU gains become larger at tighter expert budgets. From E64 to
E32, the SFT PW–Pooled gap grows from $2.08$ to $3.20$ points on
MMAU-mini and from $1.81$ to $4.38$ on MMSU. These larger gains support
the hypothesis that phase-specific functions become harder to replace as
spare capacity shrinks, making their preservation more valuable.

## 3.4 Semantic phases preserve complementary capabilities

To test phase semantics, Shuffled-PW preserves two kernels and phase counts
under matched budgets and recovery (Table 2). Qwen3-Omni PW
exceeds it on MMAU-mini and MMSU at all six reported checkpoints
(Fig. 2), gaining $3.02$ and $4.56$ points at step 1,024.
Qwen3.5-Omni gains $1.30$ , $2.85$ , and $.69$ points across the three
benchmarks, supporting semantic rather than arbitrary grouping.

Among single-phase controls, Audio-only leads MMAU-mini and Answer-only
MMAR on both backbones. Against the better single-phase control, Qwen3-Omni
PW gains $1.51$ and $4.17$ points on MMAU-mini and MMSU but loses $.22$ 
on MMAR. Qwen3.5-Omni PW gains $.25$ and $.21$ on MMAR and MMSU but
loses $.10$ on MMAU-mini. These complementary preferences support joint
phase preservation; the private-backbone differences remain small.

## 3.5 Phase-aware selection transfers across backbones

To assess transfer, we evaluate Nemotron before recovery and Qwen3.5-Omni
after SFT. On Nemotron, PW improves public English
ASR, MMAR, and MMSU at both budgets (Table 4). At E32,
WER falls by $13.3\%$ and MMSU rises by $1.96$ points relative to Pooled,
with MMAU-mini unchanged. With no retraining,
these gains demonstrate transfer of the selection benefit independently of recovery.

On Qwen3.5-Omni, PW improves all eight reported means at $4\times$ 
expert-bank reduction (Table 3): CV-WER falls by $13.0\%$ ,
BLEU rises by $6.6\%$ , and all three AU accuracies improve. Gains across
transcription, translation, and understanding after matched adaptation
complement Nemotron’s zero-shot results, supporting the phase-allocation
principle across backbones and recovery settings.

One static bank retains deployment savings. Finally, H20 batch-1
profiles (64 ASR inputs, eight warmups, three repeats) show $65.6\%$ lower
peak memory and RTF $1.034\to.423$ at $4\times$ (Fig. 3).
These savings address the deployment motivation: PW retains one physically
pruned bank, without phase-dependent mask switching. The efficiency gain
comes from pruning; PW improves how the remaining capacity is allocated.

## 4 Conclusion

In this paper, we propose PW-Pruning to prune omni-modal models for
speech-centric deployment. Phase-wise log-determinant gains preserve
listening and answering demands within one static mask. Zero-shot, recovery,
and semantic-control experiments support phase-aware capability retention,
especially at tighter budgets. Results on Qwen3-Omni, Qwen3.5-Omni, and
English-only Nemotron further demonstrate cross-model transfer.

Limitations and future work. Fixed budgets cannot exploit
depth-dependent phase overlap, while fixed weights and masks limit workload
adaptation. Future work could combine depth-aware allocation, workload-aware
weighting, and dynamic masks, subject to switching and memory costs.
