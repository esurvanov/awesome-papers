Abstract

Depression assessment from counseling speech requires integrating textual and acoustic psychological evidence across turns, yet existing end-to-end classification approaches lack explicit multimodal evidence interpretation and context-aware reasoning.
We propose AudiPsy, an evidence-grounded omni-LLM for depression assessment over spoken counseling conversations.
AudiPsy employs an Observation–Assessment–Summary (OAS) paradigm to integrate dialogue transcripts, textual and acoustic emotions, and textual–acoustic emotion incongruence into structured reasoning. OAS incrementally organizes multimodal observations, performs contextualized assessment, and maintains an evolving dialogue summary for subsequent turns. To support this framework, we construct AudiPsy-Counsel, a synthetic spoken counseling conversation corpus with multimodal context augmentation, and develop a unified three-stage curriculum for depression-oriented reasoning.
Experiments on DAIC-WOZ demonstrate superior depression assessment performance over specialized depression detection methods and general-purpose speech/omni-LLMs, along with improved reasoning quality, while supplementary evaluation on EATD-Corpus supports AudiPsy’s applicability to naturalistic conversations.

## 1 Introduction

Depression affects more than 322 million people worldwide and remains one of the leading causes of disability World Health Organization (2026).
Depression assessment is typically conducted through counseling conversations, where human counselors observe seekers’ behaviors, interpret cues, accumulate evidence and reach a judgment Mao et al. (2023). Recent advances in AI for medical diagnosis have shifted from prediction toward evidence-based reasoning Wang et al. (2025); Zhu et al. (2026).
In the domain of mental health, automated depression assessment similarly calls for evidence-grounded multimodal reasoning across evolving spoken conversations.

Existing depression detection systems predominantly formulate the assessment as a multimodal classification problem, directly mapping linguistic and acoustic representations to diagnostic labels Wu et al. (2023); Zhang et al. (2024b); Zhang et al. (2025). Although effective on benchmark datasets, this paradigm suffers from two limitations.
First, at the representation level, most multimodal methods rely on low-level acoustic features or latent representations whose psychological meaning remains largely implicit.
Second, at the process level, existing approaches typically aggregate multimodal representations into a holistic prediction without interpreting them under the conversational context.
These two limitations are coupled: the model cannot state what it observes if it relies on implicit representations; the model cannot explain how the observed evidence supports its assessment without an interpretive process. We therefore address them jointly.

Figure 1: Overview of the AudiPsy, from structured multimodal reasoning to data augmentation and model training.

For what the model reasons over, we ground the assessment in affective evidence: textual emotion, acoustic emotion, and the incongruence between the two, expressed as affective concepts in natural language so that an LLM can reason over them.
Psychological studies have shown that individuals with depression often exhibit atypical emotional patterns Clark and Watson (1991), and inconsistency between semantic and acoustic emotion, as described by the emotion context insensitivity (ECI) theory Bylsma et al. (2008); Wu et al. (2024).
Recent studies have observed that modeling this emotional incongruence improves depression detection performance Su et al. (2026).
This evidence is psychologically interpretable and naturally amenable to language-model reasoning.

For how the evidence is interpreted, following the structure of clinical progress notes Weed (1968), we introduce the Observation–Assessment–Summary (OAS) paradigm for contextual reasoning over multi-turn spoken counseling conversations. At each conversational turn, OAS extracts structured semantic and acoustic observations (O), interprets them in light of accumulated dialogue context and textual–acoustic emotion incongruence (A), and updates a compact dialogue summary (S) to guide subsequent assessment.
This incremental process preserves the contextual meaning of psychological evidence while reducing the burden of long-context reasoning for current omni-LLMs Chen et al. (2026); Wang et al. (2026).

To this end, we propose AudiPsy, an omni-LLM for depression assessment, which explicitly reasons over spoken counseling conversations in terms of multimodal affective evidence based on the proposed Observation–Assessment–Summary (OAS) reasoning paradigm.
To overcome the scarcity of real-world counseling speech corpus, we further develop a counseling context augmentation pipeline and a unified three-stage curriculum for depression-oriented reasoning.
Experiments on DAIC-WOZ Gratch et al. (2014), featuring structured spoken counseling conversations, demonstrate superior depression assessment and reasoning performance. Additional evaluation on EATD-Corpus Shen et al. (2022a), comprising naturalistic conversations, further examines AudiPsy’s applicability across conversational settings.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:

• 

We propose AudiPsy, an evidence-grounded omni-LLM for depression assessment through structured OAS reasoning.

• 

We construct AudiPsy-Counsel, a bilingual synthetic counseling speech corpus with OAS annotations and controlled emotion-incongruence cases.

• 

We develop a unified three-stage curriculum for Depression-oriented Reasoning, integrating affective perception, structured reasoning, and preference alignment.

• 

Experiments demonstrate improved depression assessment and reasoning quality, while analyses reveal how AudiPsy perceive, utilize, and interpret affective evidence.

## 2 Related Work

Automated depression assessment over spoken counseling conversations leverages linguistic, acoustic, and multimodal signals to identify mental health markers. Existing methods can be broadly categorized into three areas:
(1) Text-based approaches utilize transcripts to capture depressive states. Early methods rely on affective lexicons or neural representations Mallol-Ragolta et al. (2019); Xezonaki et al. (2020), while recent frameworks employ large language models (LLMs) through prompting and instruction tuning Xu et al. (2024); Liu et al. (2025).
(2) Speech-based approaches identify depression-related cues from acoustic characteristics such as prosody and spectral features Sardari et al. (2022); Wu et al. (2023), with recent methods incorporating data augmentation and retrieval-based reasoning to improve robustness Liang et al. (2025); Zhang et al. (2025).
(3) Multimodal approaches integrate linguistic and acoustic signals through feature fusion Shen et al. (2022a); Chen et al. (2024) or LLM-based modeling of textual and speech-derived representations Zhang et al. (2024b); Zhang et al. (2025).
Despite this progress, existing methods predominantly formulate depression assessment as end-to-end classification, leaving the psychological meaning of multimodal representations largely implicit and lacking contextualized interpretation of evidence as the conversation unfolds.

## 3 Methodology

As illustrated in Figure 1, AudiPsy combines the OAS reasoning paradigm with counseling context augmentation and a three-stage training curriculum for depression-oriented reasoning. Together, these components enable interpretable, context-aware multimodal reasoning for depression assessment.

## 3.1 OAS Structured Reasoning Paradigm

We propose an Observation–Assessment–Summary (OAS) reasoning paradigm that decomposes multimodal depression reasoning into three structured fields, as illustrated in Figure 1a.
Given a counseling speech with $T$ dialogue turns, we denote it as $\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{T}\}$ , where each turn is represented by $x_{t}=(c_{t},s_{t})$ , with $c_{t}$ and $s_{t}$ denoting the counselor’s and seeker’s spoken utterances, respectively. The ordering is used only for notation and does not imply which speaker initiates the dialogue.
At each turn, AudiPsy maps the current dialogue turn and the previous summary state to an observation, an assessment, and an updated summary:

 $(O_{t},A_{t},S_{t})=\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(x_{t},S_{t-1}),$ 

(1)

where $\mathcal{F}_{\theta}$ denotes AudiPsy parameterized by $\theta$ , and $S_{t-1}$ summarizes the accumulated dialogue context from previous turns. The three fields are defined as follows.

The Observation $O_{t}=(O_{t}^{\mathrm{dt}},O_{t}^{\mathrm{te}},O_{t}^{\mathrm{ae}})$ consists of the dialogue transcript $O_{t}^{\mathrm{dt}}$ , the seeker’s textual emotion $O_{t}^{\mathrm{te}}$ and acoustic emotion $O_{t}^{\mathrm{ae}}$ . Together, they provide explicit and interpretable multimodal evidence for subsequent reasoning.

The Assessment $A_{t}=(A_{t}^{\mathrm{ei}},A_{t}^{\mathrm{dep}})$ contains the textual–acoustic emotion incongruence (EI) assessment $A_{t}^{\mathrm{ei}}$ and the depression assessment $A_{t}^{\mathrm{dep}}$ , each consisting of a binary prediction answer together with its supporting rationale.
Therefore, the depression assessment can be formulated as a conditional dependency
 $p_{\theta}(A_{t}^{\mathrm{dep}}\mid A_{t}^{\mathrm{ei}},O_{t},S_{t-1})$ , indicating that the reasoning $A_{t}^{\mathrm{dep}}$ is grounded by evidence $A_{t}^{\mathrm{ei}},O_{t}$ and $S_{t-1}$ .

The Summary $S_{t}$ recursively maintains a compact dialogue state, including a concise summary of the counseling content together with accumulated emotional statistics and affective patterns.

Finally, the dialogue-level depression prediction is obtained by aggregating the turn-level depression assessments:

 $\hat{y}=\mathrm{Agg}\left(A_{1}^{\mathrm{dep}},A_{2}^{\mathrm{dep}},\ldots,A_{T}^{\mathrm{dep}}\right),$ 

(2)

where $\mathrm{Agg}(\cdot)$ denotes a consistency-based aggregation function.
In our implementation, $\mathrm{Agg}(\cdot)$ uses majority voting over turn-level binary predictions for robust conversation-level aggregation.
Detailed output templates and field definitions are provided in Appendix A.

## 3.2 Counseling Context Augmentation

Real-world speech-based counseling data is extremely scarce due to privacy concerns and acquisition costs, while text-only counseling resources are considerably abundant, but lack the affective context and its acoustic realization required for multimodal depression reasoning.
To bridge this gap, we formulate data construction as a context augmentation problem. Starting from publicly available text-only counseling records, we progressively enrich them along three orthogonal dimensions: situational, affective, and acoustic, so that each resulting sample supplies the contextual evidence consumed by the OAS paradigm, as shown in Figure 1b.
Although synthetic, the resulting dataset, AudiPsy-Counsel, provides structured supervision for learning how to perceive and interpret multimodal evidence to support depression assessment across dialogue turns.

## Situational augmentation.

The seed corpus is an union of two public text counseling corpora,
CounselChat Bertagnolli (2020) and CPsyCounR Zhang et al. (2024a).
We employ GPT-5 OpenAI (2025) as a situational re-writer which maps each counseling record into a bilingual dialogue transcript $O^{\mathrm{dt}}$ 
between a counselor and a seeker.
The rewriting preserves the original clinical issue while diversifying emotional conditions and interaction trajectories, resulting in richer situational variations while preserving the natural distribution of counseling topics, including depression, relationships, self-development, and more (Figure 2).

## Affective annotation.

For each utterance, GPT-5 also acts as an affective annotator to produce textual and acoustic emotion annotations $O_{t}^{\mathrm{te}}$ and $O_{t}^{\mathrm{ae}}$ , together with the textual–acoustic emotion incongruence annotation $A_{t}^{\mathrm{ei}}$ , while the seeker gender $g$ is annotated at the dialogue level.
Together, these annotations provide explicit ground-truth multimodal evidence including textual–acoustic emotion incongruence, which is rarely available and difficult to verify in organic data.

Conditioned on the annotated $O_{t}$ , $A^{ei}_{t}$ , and $S_{t-1}$ , together with the original counseling record, the LLM generates the depression assessment $A^{dep}_{t}$ following a reasoning skill informed by the ECI theory and PHQ-8 diagnostic algorithm Kroenke et al. (2009). Since each assessment is conditioned on explicit observations and guided by these principles, the resulting reasoning is grounded in multimodal evidence. Consequently, each dialogue turn is annotated under the complete OAS paradigm.

## Acoustic realization.

The annotated scripts are rendered into emotional speech with the
prompt-controlled text-to-speech synthesis model Qwen3-TTS Hu et al. (2026),
where dialogue transcripts $O^{dt}_{t}$ serve as synthesis inputs, the acoustic
emotion descriptions $O^{ae}_{t}$ serve as speech-style prompts, and the speaker gender $g$ serves as the speaker selection reference.

Consequently, the pipeline yields AudiPsy-Counsel, denoted as $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{apc}}$ , a synthetic bilingual speech-counseling dataset with 2,400 conversations for downstream model training.
All the details of the dataset construction, including prompt templates, quality-control procedures, and example instances are provided in Appendix B.

## 3.3 Depression-oriented Reasoning

To equip $\mathcal{F}_{\theta}$ with the capability of evidence-grounded multimodal reasoning for depression assessment, we optimize it through a three-stage training curriculum, as illustrated in Figure 1c. The curriculum progressively develops affective perception, structured counseling reasoning, and corrective preference alignment.

## Stage-1: Affective perception.

We first train $\mathcal{F}_{\theta}$ to learn the observation field $O_{t}$ from emotional speech to enable robust multimodal affective perception under both emotion-congruent and emotion-incongruent conditions.
Specifically, we use IEMOCAP Busso et al. (2008) as the spontaneous corpus, where we annotate the textual polarity of each utterance and construct a balanced training set by randomly downsampling each textual–acoustic emotion label combination.

We further construct a Synthetic Emotion-Incongruence (SynEI) dataset, a depression-related emotional speech corpus derived from the training split of DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus, by altering the speech emotion through speech re-synthesis Zhou et al. (2026) while preserving the original text and speaker characteristics.
Construction and dataset details are provided in Appendices C and D.
Accordingly, the Stage-1 objective is defined as:

 $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S1}}=-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{emo}}}\log p_{\theta}(O_{t}\mid x_{t}).$ 

(3)

## Stage-2: Structured counseling reasoning.

We then continue supervised fine-tuning on AudiPsy-Counsel $D_{apc}$ to adapt the model to depression-oriented counseling reasoning.
 $\mathcal{F}_{\theta}$ learns the complete OAS reasoning process with the Stage-2 objective defined as:

 $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S2}}=-\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{apc}}}\log p_{\theta}\left(O_{t},A_{t},S_{t}\mid x_{t},S_{t-1}\right).$ 

(4)

## Stage-3: Corrective preference alignment.

Finally, we align $\mathcal{F}_{\theta}$ on real depression-assessment data (DAIC-WOZ; EATD-Corpus), denoted as $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{real}}$ . Considering its limited scale, we adopt the lightweight ORPO objective Hong et al. (2024), which jointly performs supervised learning and preference optimization in a single stage.
For EATD-Corpus, the counselor speech signals $c_{t}$ are reconstructed using Qwen-TTS, as they are unavailable in the original dataset.
During ORPO training, the Stage-2 output is treated as the rejected response, while a preferred response is regenerated using a pretrained speech emotion recognition (SER) model Sailor et al. (2025) together with the AudiPsy-Counsel affective annotation pipeline conditioned on the ground-truth depression label.
The model is optimized using the standard ORPO objective:

 $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ORPO}}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{real}}}\left[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OR}}\right].$ 

(5)

We evaluate AudiPsy from three perspectives: (1) depression assessment and human reasoning quality evaluation on DAIC-WOZ; (2) supplementary depression assessment on EATD-Corpus; and (3) multimodal evidence perception, utilization, and interpretation through speech emotion recognition, evidence utilization analysis, and reasoning case studies.

## Training

AudiPsy is initialized from Qwen2.5-Omni-7B Xu et al. (2025a) and trained with the proposed three-stage curriculum using the ms-swift framework Zhao et al. (2024) on 4 NVIDIA H800 GPUs. Stage-1 and -2 perform LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning on the affective perception corpus $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{emo}}$ (2,109 samples) and the AudiPsy-Counsel corpus $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{apc}}$ (2,400 samples), respectively. Stage 3 further aligns the model using ORPO on the depression-assessment corpus $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{real}}$ (280 conversations after conversation-level resampling to mitigate class imbalance).

## Inference

During inference, AudiPsy performs turn-by-turn reasoning, conditioning each turn on the previous summary. Dialogue-level predictions are obtained by majority voting, with ties resolved as yes. Greedy decoding (temperature = 0) ensures deterministic evaluation. Following prior work Wu et al. (2023); Zhang et al. (2024b); Zhang et al. (2025), we evaluate on the DAIC-WOZ development set (33 conversations: 12 depressed, 21 non-depressed) for direct comparability. Separately, we evaluate on the EATD-Corpus validation set (79 conversations: 11 depressed, 68 non-depressed) to assess performance across conversational settings. Further implementation details and prompts are provided in Appendix E.

## 4.1 Depression Assessment Performance

## Baselines

We compare AudiPsy against representative speech-based binary depression classification methods, including BiLSTM+GRU Shen et al. (2022b) and WavLM Layer 10 Wu et al. (2023), as well as multi-stage LLM pipelines, including Speech Landmarks+Llama2 Zhang et al. (2024b) and SpeechT-RAG Zhang et al. (2025). We reproduce BiLSTM+GRU using its official implementation and test set, while reporting published single-model F1 results for the other methods.

For speech LLMs, we select models of comparable scale to AudiPsy’s Qwen2.5-Omni-7B backbone Ding et al. (2025); Yang et al. (2026). For omni-LLMs, we include the Gemini 2.5 series Comanici et al. (2025) as representative proprietary models, together with Qwen2.5-Omni-7B Xu et al. (2025a) and the larger Qwen3-Omni-30B series Xu et al. (2025b) from the same Omni family. A subset of these models is additionally evaluated under the OAS paradigm without task-specific fine-tuning. All evaluated models are listed in Table 1.

## Metrics

We evaluate AudiPsy from two perspectives: depression assessment performance and reasoning quality.
For depression assessment, we report Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), binary F1, recall, precision, and specificity. In addition to standard binary classification metrics, we include MCC and specificity to provide a balanced measure under class imbalance, accounting for performance on both depressed and non-depressed individuals Chicco and Jurman (2020).

For reasoning quality, four practicing psychological counselors evaluate 5 methods on 10 class-balanced conversations from DAIC-WOZ, examining 250 LLM outputs per evaluator. Reasoning quality is rated along four dimensions: Evidence Grounding (EG), Clinical Plausibility (CP), Multi-Modal Integration (MMI), and Contextual Evidence Integration (CEI). Evaluators assigned integer scores on a 1–5 scale. To accommodate dimension-specific rating baselines, we report mean-centered scores and win–tie–loss results across methods for each dimension.
Detailed evaluation protocols are provided in Appendix F.

## Main results

As shown in Table 1, AudiPsy attains the highest MCC,
F1, and precision, outperforming specialized speech-based
methods and general-purpose speech and omni-LLMs on DAIC-WOZ.
Notably, Kimi-Audio fails to generate outputs for long audio inputs, while Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Thinking frequently declines to make depression predictions.

Simply applying OAS to off-the-shelf models does not consistently improve performance. Although OAS explicitly structures multimodal evidence, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B often produces inaccurate observations and assessments, while Gemini 2.5 Pro and Qwen3-Omni-Instruct exhibit high recall but limited MCC, suggesting a tendency toward positive depression predictions.
These results reinforce the coupling between evidence representation and contextual interpretation: structured reasoning alone is insufficient without reliable affective perception and task-specific training.

## Ablation study

We conduct six ablation experiments to examine the contributions of
the training curriculum, emotion incongruence, and the structured OAS
representation. Removing individual training objectives or their
combinations generally degrades performance, showing the necessity of the full three-stage curriculum.
In particular, removing $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S1}}$ leads to a clear
performance drop, highlighting the importance of affective perception
for subsequent depression reasoning.
Removing emotion incongruence also substantially degrades performance,
demonstrating its importance as a source of multimodal evidence.

## 4.4 Probing the Assessment Behavior

## Perceptual foundation: cross-modal speech emotion recognition

We first probe whether AudiPsy perceives speech emotion independently of textual sentiment, which is the perceptual foundation of incongruence analysis and affective evidence integration.
Table 3 reports
per-class F1 scores for four speech emotion categories on the IEMOCAP
test set, stratified by transcript polarity.
The Qwen2.5-Omni-7B backbone relies heavily on textual
affective cues, struggling when speech emotion conflicts with transcript polarity. This tendency persists without $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S1}}$ , whereas full AudiPsy improves emotion recognition, particularly for Neutral and Negative speech, consistent with the downstream ablation results in Table 1.
Meanwhile, Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves comparable emotion recognition but substantially weaker depression assessment following OAS paradigm, suggesting that accurate affective perception alone is insufficient without appropriate evidence interpretation.

## Evidence utilization: impact of emotion incongruence

We next probe how explicit emotion incongruence modeling shapes the evidence used in assessment reasoning. Figure 4 summarizes the percentage of evidence types used in depression assessment reasoning across the DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus test sets. We use GPT-5 to extract and categorize evidence types utilized in the rationale.
The full AudiPsy exploits all five types of evidence in a balanced manner, whereas removing explicit emotion incongruence analysis causes the model to rely more heavily on textual cues while using substantially fewer acoustic emotion cues and accumulated multimodal patterns.
This effect is particularly evident of the “Yes” prediction on EATD-Corpus, where less informative textual content leads the full model to naturally shift toward acoustic evidence, while the ablated model fails to exploit such complementary multimodal information.
Consistent with this shift in evidence utilization, removing emotion incongruence modeling also degrades the overall depression assessment performance in Table 1.
Together, these results suggest that explicit emotion incongruence analysis promotes more effective integration of multimodal evidence, thereby contributing to improved depression assessment.

## Evidence interpretation: contextualizing affective evidence for depression assessment

Finally, we present two illustrative reasoning chains from selected dialogue turns in EATD-Corpus and DIAC-WOZ, respectively, in Table 4, to examine how AudiPsy interprets affective evidence within conversational context.
In Case 1, recurring negative acoustic cues and prior EI, together with regretful reflection, form a consistent pattern of depressive evidence, supporting a positive assessment. In contrast, Case 2 also exhibits negative acoustic affect and prior EI, but the sadness is consistent with the seeker’s interpersonal concerns, with limited cumulative evidence supporting depression. The contrast highlights that similar affective cues can carry different implications for depression assessment depending on conversational context and accumulated evidence. Together, these cases illustrate AudiPsy’s ability to interpret multimodal affective evidence in context, rather than directly mapping emotional cues to depression predictions.

## 5 Conclusion

We presented AudiPsy, an omni-LLM for depression assessment based on the Observation–Assessment–Summary (OAS) reasoning paradigm. By progressively accumulating multimodal evidence and modeling textual–acoustic emotion incongruence, AudiPsy demonstrates the potential of evidence-grounded multimodal reasoning for interpretable depression assessment.

## Limitations

## Evaluation Data and Protocols.

Our evaluation is confined to DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus, which are currently the only publicly available speech datasets for depression assessment. Large-scale speech counseling datasets with depression labels remain scarce due to privacy concerns and high acquisition costs, limiting our ability to further evaluate the generalizability of our findings. Nevertheless, evaluating on these widely used benchmarks aligns with established practices in the field.

We follow the official train/test splits of both datasets, resulting in unavoidable class imbalance in testing. Besides, some prior studies on EATD-Corpus adopt random three-fold evaluation Shen et al. (2022a), this setting is not readily applicable to our multi-stage training and alignment pipeline. Consequently, metrics reported under these different evaluation protocols could not be directly compared numerically.

## Synthetic Counseling Data.

AudiPsy-Counsel is constructed by augmenting real-world textual counseling records with affective annotations and synthesized speech. Its acoustic realizations may therefore differ from naturally occurring counseling speech in acoustic variability and emotional expression. AudiPsy-Counsel is primarily intended to provide controlled supervision for learning structured reasoning and interpreting multimodal affective evidence, rather than to approximate the distribution of real clinical speech.

## Ethical Considerations

This work is intended solely for research and experimental evaluation and should not be used for real-world clinical diagnosis or treatment decisions. Although AudiPsy is designed to reason over multimodal evidence for depression assessment, its predictions and generated rationales may contain clinically inappropriate interpretations. The reported performance on benchmark datasets should therefore not be interpreted as evidence of clinical validity or readiness for deployment. Depression assessment is a sensitive clinical task that requires qualified professional judgment, and outputs from our system should not replace evaluation by mental health professionals. Our experiments use publicly available research and synthetic datasets, which do not involve the collection of new patient data.

## Appendix A Details of OAS Reasoning Paradigm

## A.1 OAS Template

We introduce the Observation–Assessment–Summary (OAS) structured reasoning paradigm. The paradigm organizes the model output into three structured fields: Observation, Assessment, and Summary. This design encourages the model to explicitly separate evidence extraction, cross-modal emotion incongruence analysis and depression assessment, along with updated conversation summary during multimodal depression reasoning.

The Observation records turn-level multimodal evidence, including the dialogue transcript, textual emotion and polarity, speech acoustic emotion and category label, and discretized valence–arousal–dominance (VAD) attributes for acoustic emotion.

The Assessment performs higher-level reasoning based on the extracted evidence. Specifically, the model evaluates cross-channel emotional incongruence between linguistic and vocal expressions, and assesses depressive tendency by considering both current-turn observations and accumulated dialogue context.

The Summary block maintains a compact conversation-level representation consisting of a natural-language conversation summary and cumulative affective statistics (e.g., counts of negative-polarity turns, low-valence speech turns, and emotionally incongruent turns). This summary is recursively updated after each dialogue turn and serves as the conversation context for subsequent reasoning steps.

## A.2 Observation Field Annotation

The Observation field contains six attributes: Dialgoue Transcript, Textual Emotion, Text Polarity, Acoustic Emotion, Acoustic Emotion Category, and VAD. Their annotation procedures are described below.

## Dialogue Transcript.

Each dialogue is represented as an ordered sequence of alternating utterances between the Counselor and the Seeker. The original speaker order is preserved. The ground truth is from the original dataset.

## Textual Emotion.

The Text Emotion attribute is generated using GPT-OSS-120B Agarwal et al. (2025) based solely on the dialogue transcript using the prompt presented in Appendix G.3 to produce a natural-language description of the textual emotional state.

## Text Polarity.

The Text Polarity attribute classifies the transcript into one of three sentiment polarity categories: Positive, Neutral, or Negative. The annotation is performed using GPT-OSS-120B, using the prompt presented in Appendix G.4.

## Acoustic Emotion.

The annotation procedure depends on the source dataset. For AudiPsy-Counsel, the acoustic emotion description is directly obtained from the synthesis prompt used to generate the speech. For DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus, acoustic emotion is predicted using a speech emotion recognition (SER) model Sailor et al. (2025) and hold the same value as Speech Emotion Category.

## Acoustic Emotion Category.

The categorical acoustic emotion is predicted by the SER model Sailor et al. (2025), which outputs one of seven categories: Anger, Happy, Neutral, Sad, Surprise, Disgust, and Fear.

## VAD.

The valence, arousal, and dominance (VAD) scores are predicted as continuous values in the range $[0,1]$ using the SER model Sailor et al. (2025). Each dimension is then discretized into five equal-width intervals:
 $[0,0.2)$ (Very Low),
 $[0.2,0.4)$ (Low),
 $[0.4,0.6)$ (Medium),
 $[0.6,0.8)$ (High), and
 $[0.8,1]$ (Very High).

## A.3 Assessment Field Annotation

The Assessment field contains two attributes: Emotion Incongruence and Depression. Their annotation procedures are described below.

## Emotion Incongruence.

The Emotion Incongruence attribute indicates whether an inconsistency exists between the textual and acoustic emotional expressions of the speaker. The annotation procedure depends on the source dataset. For AudiPsy-Counsel, the ground-truth incongruence labels are obtained during dataset construction, and GPT-5 is employed to generate a natural-language rationale explaining the label using the system and user prompts in Appendix G.5.

For DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus, the dataset does not provide emotion incongruence labels. Therefore, GPT-5 is first required to determine whether emotion incongruence is present based on the transcript and acoustic emotion annotations, and then generate a brief rationale for its decision. We employ the system prompt described in Appendix G.6, together with the same user prompt used for AudiPsy-Counsel in Appendix G.5.

## Depression.

The Depression attribute indicates whether the speaker exhibits depressive symptoms and provides a corresponding explanation. For AudiPsy-Counsel, the dialogue topic serves as the depression label.
For DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus, the depression label is derived from the dataset questionnaire results. A skill informed by PHQ-8/SDS and the ECI theory is combined with GPT-5 to produce a natural-language explanation, with the prompts presented in Appendix G.7.

## A.4 Summary Field Annotation

The summary field provides a compact overview of each dialogue and its overall emotional characteristics.
The conversation summary is generated based on the complete dialogue till the current turn, using GPT-OSS-120B and the prompt presented in Appendix G.8.
The statistical attributes are computed by aggregating annotations across the conversation. For each emotional attribute, the corresponding statistic is reported as the ratio of utterances satisfying the criterion (e.g., negative sentiment or low valence) to the total number of utterances, yielding a set of conversation-level summaries that can be used for efficient filtering and retrieval.

## Appendix B AudiPsy-Counsel Dataset Counstruction

## B.1 Situational Augmentation and Affective Annotation

To construct the textual component of AudiPsy-Counsel, we collect source materials from two psychological counseling datasets: CounselChat Bertagnolli (2020) and CPsyCounR Zhang et al. (2024a). These datasets contain authentic mental health discussions and counseling reports, providing realistic scenarios for depression-oriented dialogue generation. Given each source instance, we prompt an LLM to rewrite it into a counseling dialogue between a seeker and a counselor in both English and Chinese. This process transforms static counseling records into interactive conversations suitable for subsequent speech synthesis and multimodal training.

In addition to the dialogue content, the LLM generates several affective annotations required by AudiPsy. For each seeker utterance, it produces a acoustic emotion description and an emotion incongruence label indicating whether the vocal emotion is consistent with the semantic content. To ensure sufficient supervision for emotion incongruence reasoning, each generated dialogue contains at least one incongruent seeker utterance. The LLM also infers the seeker’s gender (male, female, or unknown), which is later used for speaker assignment during speech synthesis. The resulting dataset therefore provides counseling conversations with rich affective annotations that support emotion perception, incongruence detection, and depression-oriented reasoning.

We use a unified prompt (Appendices G.1 and G.2) to convert each counseling record into a three-turn
dialogue with basic affective annotation. For other affective attribute annotation, please refer to Appendix A.

## B.2 Acoustic Realization

We convert the generated counseling conversations into speech using the prompt-controlled text-to-speech system Qwen3-TTS Hu et al. (2026). For each utterance, the dialogue text is used as the synthesis input, while the corresponding emotion description generated during dialogue construction is provided as an instruction to control the emotional speaking style.

Since Qwen3-TTS provides a fixed set of predefined voice identities, we assign speakers before speech synthesis. For seeker utterances, a voice is randomly sampled from the predefined speaker pool according to the predicted gender. Specifically, voices are sampled from the corresponding male or female speaker pool when the predicted gender is Male or Female, respectively, while utterances labeled as Unknown are assigned a random voice from the entire speaker pool. Counselor voices are sampled independently from the predefined speaker set. Once assigned, the same voice identity is used consistently for all utterances of the corresponding speaker throughout the dialogue.

Through this process, each bilingual counseling dialogue is transformed into a multi-speaker emotional speech interaction. Combined with the dialogue content, emotion descriptions, emotion incongruence labels, and speaker profiles, the resulting AudiPsy-Counsel dataset provides rich multimodal supervision for emotion perception, incongruence detection, and depression-oriented counseling analysis.

## B.3 Quality Check.

Since Qwen3-TTS generally produces reliable speech content and speaker identities, our quality-control procedure primarily focuses on verifying whether the synthesized speech conveys the intended emotion and preventing potential emotion hallucinations. Specifically, we first use an LLM to map each free-form emotion description into one of seven canonical emotion categories: Neutral, Happy, Sad, Angry, Surprised, Disgust, and Fear. We then apply a speech emotion recognition (SER) model Sailor et al. (2025) to the synthesized audio. If the emotion predicted by the SER model is inconsistent with the category derived from the emotion description, the audio is discarded and regenerated. This verification-and-regeneration process is repeated until the two emotion labels are consistent, with a maximum of 50 generation attempts per utterance.

## B.4 Dialogue Augmentation

The generated counseling conversations are initially constructed as three-turn conversations for efficient and consistent data generation. However, real-world counseling sessions, such as those in DAIC-WOZ, typically involve substantially longer interactions. To reduce this discrepancy, we further extend the conversations using two simple augmentation strategies.

First, we prepend several short rapport-building exchanges (e.g., greetings and opening questions) before the original dialogue to simulate the natural opening phase of counseling sessions. Second, we concatenate multiple generated conversations sharing the same counseling topic into a longer conversation. The transitions between adjacent dialogue segments are rewritten by the LLM to ensure supportive coherence and conversational continuity.

These dialogue augmentation strategies increase the diversity of dialogue lengths while preserving the original counseling content and affective annotations, enabling the synthesized corpus to better resemble real-world multi-turn counseling conversations.

## Appendix C IEMOCAP Annotation and Filtering

IEMOCAP Busso et al. (2008) is a multimodal conversational emotion dataset containing natural spoken interactions with manually annotated acoustic emotions, making it well suited for Stage-1 affective perception. Our Stage-1 training operates on individual utterances rather than question–answer conversations, so each utterance is treated as an independent training sample. Following the official split, Sessions 1–4 are used for training and Session 5 is reserved for evaluation.

We annotate the textual emotional polarity of every utterance using GPT-OSS-120B with three categories: positive, neutral, and negative. The annotation is performed solely based on the textual transcript, independent of the original acoustic emotion labels.

To obtain a balanced distribution over acoustic emotion and text polarity combinations, we perform class-wise downsampling within each text polarity category. Specifically, for each polarity, utterances belonging to the same acoustic emotion are randomly sampled to produce a more balanced distribution while preserving as much diversity as possible.
The resulting training set contains 1,031 utterances. Detailed statistics are shown in Table 8.

Since both the training set and the original Session-5 contains very limited samples for fear, disgust, and surprise, we evaluate only the four major acoustic emotions (angry, happy, neutral, and sad). For each acoustic emotion, we uniformly sample 10 utterances from each text polarity category, resulting in a balanced test set of 120 utterances.

## Appendix D Synthetic Emotion-Incongruence (SynEI) Dataset Construction

To expose the model to textual–acoustic emotion incongruence during Stage-1 training, we construct a Synthetic Emotion-Incongruence (SynEI) dataset using the training splits of DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus.

We first group all utterances according to their annotated text polarity (positive, neutral, and negative). Utterances are randomly sampled from each polarity category to obtain a balanced text polarity distribution. Each selected utterance is then synthesized with seven target acoustic emotions (neutral, happy, sad, angry, fearful, disgusted, and surprised) using IndexTTS2 Zhou et al. (2026). During synthesis, the speaker prompt is extracted from the original utterance so that the synthesized speech preserves the original speaker identity while modifying only the emotional expression.

To ensure the quality of the synthesized samples, we perform an automatic filtering step using a pretrained SER model Sailor et al. (2025). Only samples whose predicted acoustic emotion matches the intended target emotion are retained. We further downsample the retained samples within each acoustic emotion category to obtain a balanced distribution across the three text polarity categories. Tables 9 summarize the final Synthetic EI Dataset after SER-based filtering and downsampling.

## Appendix F Human Evaluation of Reasoning

## F.1 Evaluation Protocol

We recruited four practicing psychological counselors from established mental health counseling institutions as human experts to evaluate the reasoning generated by five methods. The experts were recruited through personal referrals and were compensated based on their standard hourly consultation rates. The compensation was aligned with their usual professional fees to ensure fair payment for their time and expertise.
All experts were informed of the evaluation task and compensation arrangements in advance and voluntarily agreed to participate. We collected only their evaluation scores and did not collect any personal or sensitive information.
The evaluation did not involve clinical interventions or the collection of patient data.

The evaluation set consists of 10 conversations randomly sampled from DAIC-WOZ, balanced between depressed and non-depressed cases.
Among the five methods, three follow the OAS paradigm and produce turn-level reasoning, while the other two generate a single dialogue-level reasoning output. We randomly sample five turn-level outputs from each OAS-based method, totaling 250 model outputs. Evaluators consider the sampled turn-level outputs jointly and assign one dialogue-level score to each method under each criterion. Thus, each evaluator provides scores for 50 method–dialogue pairs while examining 140 individual model outputs.

For each criterion, evaluators assessed dialogue-level reasoning using integer scores on a 1–5 Likert scale. They were instructed to make comparative judgments among the methods, assigning higher scores to stronger methods and lower scores to weaker ones. As a reference, a method judged slightly above the average quality of the compared methods received a 3, while one judged slightly below average received a 2. We use numerical ratings rather than rankings to capture both the relative ordering of methods and the perceived magnitude of their differences. Accordingly, we report mean-centered scores that reflect each method’s performance relative to the average of the compared methods.

For each method $m$ and criterion $c$ , we first aggregate its ratings across evaluators and dialogue samples:

 $S_{m,c}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}||\mathcal{D}|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}}s_{e,d,m,c},$ 

where $s_{e,d,m,c}$ denotes the rating assigned by evaluator $e$ to method $m$ on dialogue $d$ under criterion $c$ . We then report the mean-centered relative score

 $R_{m,c}=S_{m,c}-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}S_{m^{\prime},c},$ 

where $\mathcal{M}$ denotes the set of evaluated methods. A positive (negative) $R_{m,c}$ indicates that a method is rated above (below) the average across compared methods for criterion $c$ .

All four human evaluators were Chinese nationals and native Chinese speakers. Since the model outputs and DAIC-WOZ conversations were in English, we additionally provided GPT-5-generated Chinese translations for reference. The original English text was presented alongside the translation to facilitate understanding while preserving the original model outputs.

## F.2 Evaluation Criteria

We evaluate reasoning quality along four complementary dimensions. Evaluators assign higher scores when the generated reasoning better satisfies the corresponding criterion and lower scores otherwise.

## Evidence Grounding (EG)

This criterion evaluates whether the reasoning identifies depression-relevant evidence from the input speech, such as linguistic content, emotional expression, and vocal affect, and grounds its assessment in observable evidence rather than unsupported speculation. Stronger reasoning identifies relevant evidence and clearly connects it to the assessment.

## Clinical Plausibility (CP)

This criterion evaluates whether the reasoning is consistent with established psychological and clinical knowledge of depression. Stronger reasoning provides clinically plausible interpretations of observed symptoms, behaviors, and risk factors while avoiding unsupported or unreasonable clinical inferences.

## Multi-Modal Integration (MMI)

This criterion evaluates whether the reasoning integrates information across modalities, particularly linguistic content and vocal characteristics, rather than relying predominantly on a single modality. Stronger reasoning considers the consistency, complementarity, or discrepancy between modalities and incorporates these relationships into the assessment.

## Contextual Evidence Integration (CEI)

This criterion evaluates whether the reasoning integrates evidence across the dialogue context rather than drawing conclusions from isolated observations. Stronger reasoning connects information across dialogue turns and identifies cumulative evidence, recurring patterns, or changes over the course of the interaction.

## F.3 Instructions for Human Evaluators

The human evaluators were provided with detailed task instructions in Chinese, covering the evaluation procedure, dialogue materials, model output formats, and scoring process. The original instruction slides and criteria are included in the data supplementary materials to ensure transparency and reproducibility.

---

### Decision Principle (Strict)

- Answer **Yes** ONLY if there is a **clear and strong contradiction** between the textual emotion and the acoustic emotion.

- Answer **No** if:

 - the emotions are aligned,

 - OR only slightly different,

 - OR the acoustic emotion is weak, neutral, or ambiguous.

- Do NOT infer contradiction unless it is **obvious and unambiguous**.

- When in doubt, choose **No**.

---

### Emotion Grouping Rule (Very Important)

Treat emotions at a **coarse-grained level**, not fine-grained labels.

- Emotions with similar **polarity and affective direction** should be considered **congruent**, even if the labels differ.

Examples of SAME (congruent):

- sad, annoyed, frustrated, disappointed -> negative

- happy, excited, satisfied -> positive

- calm, neutral -> neutral / low-arousal

- Do NOT mark incongruence just because the labels are different.

---

### What counts as incongruence (Yes)

- Opposite polarity (e.g., clearly positive text vs clearly negative tone)

- Strong emotional contradiction (e.g., cheerful or excited tone for clearly sad or distressing content)

- Clear mismatch in emotional direction that cannot be explained by mild variation

---

### What does NOT count (No)

- Same polarity but different labels (e.g., sad vs annoyed)

- Neutral or weak acoustic emotion

- Mild intensity differences

- Slight category mismatch without polarity conflict

- Ambiguous or unclear emotional cues

---

### Reasoning Guidelines

- First determine the **polarity** (positive / negative / neutral) of both text and speech.

- Then consider **intensity (arousal)** and overall emotional direction.

- Base your reasoning on semantic meaning (text) and vocal cues (speech).

- Do not quote or restate the input.

- Do not mention labels, metadata, or feature names.

- Keep the explanation concise, natural, and coherent.

---

### Output Requirements

- Output exactly **two lines**.

- Do not include any extra text, symbols, or formatting.

---

# Depression Assessment Skill

## Core Principle

Assess depressive tendency using cumulative evidence rather than any single signal.

Prioritize evidence in the following order:

1. Persistent depressive symptoms and mood patterns in conversation content

2. PHQ-8/SDS symptom evidence

3. Positive emotional capacity and emotional recovery

3. Emotion Context Insensitivity (ECI)

4. Speech emotion indicators

Do not infer depression solely from temporary emotions such as sadness, frustration, anxiety, anger, or crying in response to a specific event.

Depressive tendency is primarily reflected by persistent symptoms and enduring mood patterns rather than isolated emotional reactions.

---

## Key Evidence

### 1. Depressive Symptoms

Look for evidence corresponding to PHQ-8 symptom domains, including:

* loss of interest or pleasure

* depressed mood or hopelessness

* sleep disturbance

* fatigue or low energy

* appetite changes

* guilt, worthlessness, or excessive self-blame

* concentration difficulties

* psychomotor slowing or agitation

Stronger evidence exists when symptoms are:

* persistent

* repeated across turns

* observed across different contexts

* reflected in both current and historical conversation content

---

### 2. Mood vs Emotion

Distinguish persistent mood from temporary emotion.

Evidence is stronger when negative experiences appear:

* ongoing rather than situational

* recurrent across conversations

* difficult to recover from

* present across multiple life domains

A single emotional reaction to a specific event should not be treated as evidence of depression by itself.

### 3. Positive Emotional Capacity and Recovery

Evidence against depressive tendency may be present when the seeker demonstrates preserved positive emotional responsiveness and emotional recovery.

Possible indicators include:

* vividly recalling positive experiences

* expressing genuine enjoyment, excitement, gratitude, or pride

* showing emotional variation across topics

* actively seeking solutions to current difficulties

* describing successful coping or self-regulation strategies

* recovering emotionally after setbacks

* maintaining motivation, hope, or future-oriented thinking

These indicators suggest that negative emotions may be situational rather than reflective of a persistent depressive state.

Particularly informative signs include:

* the ability to become emotionally engaged when discussing meaningful or positive experiences

* spontaneous discussion of activities that still bring enjoyment or satisfaction

* evidence that emotional distress improves with time, support, or coping efforts

Such evidence should be weighed against depressive indicators and may reduce confidence in a depression assessment when substantial.

---

### 4. Emotion Context Insensitivity (ECI)

ECI may provide supporting evidence.

Possible indicators include:

* emotionally flat responses

* little emotional variation across topics

* limited response to positive experiences

* reduced engagement with emotionally meaningful content

---

### 5. Speech Emotion

Speech emotion is Supportive evidence.

Potential indicators include:

* persistently low valence

* low arousal

* low dominance

* flattened affect

* monotonous emotional expression

Speech emotion alone is insufficient to determine depressive tendency.

When transcript content and acoustic emotion differ, prioritize conversation content unless the Depression Assessment Skill indicates otherwise.

---

## Reasoning

When generating the assessment rationale:

* Focus on the most informative evidence.

* Prioritize persistent patterns over isolated observations.

* Integrate content, symptom evidence, mood patterns, ECI, and speech signals into a single explanation.

* Mention uncertainty when evidence is limited, mixed, or borderline.

* Do not simply list symptoms or evidence categories.

The rationale should be concise and read like a natural assessment summary.
