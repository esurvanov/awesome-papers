Abstract.

Conversational recommender systems have recently adopted vision-language models to match user dialogue intents with multimodal product information. However, extending these methods to many product domains faces a dilemma: deploying a separately adapted VLM per domain scales cost linearly with the number of domains and requires routing each dialogue to the right model, while naively merging all domains into a single VLM degrades accuracy because product characteristics vary widely across categories. We propose LightCRS, a lightweight, query-conditioned framework for unified multi-domain multimodal conversational recommendation. LightCRS precomputes multimodal product representations once offline with frozen encoders. Online, a compact 5.3M-parameter module fuses this evidence for each dialogue via query-conditioned cross-attention, whose parameters are shared across all domains, and compares candidates jointly with an inter-candidate Transformer. To enable rigorous evaluation, we construct MM-R2A-25, a 25-domain benchmark pairing natural Reddit recommendation-seeking conversations with Amazon product listings. LightCRS achieves state-of-the-art average HR@1, NDCG@5, and MRR, improving HR@1 by 35.4% over the strongest baseline while reducing per-query latency by 266×. Joint multi-domain training itself improves HR@1 by 15.2% over separately trained single-domain models.

## 1. Introduction

Conversational recommender systems (CRSs) predict items that satisfy user intents expressed through natural-language dialogue (Xie et al., 2024; He et al., 2024; Yue et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2025; Jeon et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026a). Recent progress in CRSs has been driven by vision-language models (VLMs), which integrate visual and textual product evidence to improve product understanding (Jeon et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026a; Giahi et al., 2025). Meanwhile, e-commerce platforms host a large and growing number of product categories, and maintaining a separately adapted VLM for each category is untenable in practice. Therefore, a unified multimodal CRS that serves multiple domains is desirable.

Existing multimodal CRSs, however, face a dilemma when extended to many domains. Deploying per-domain models (Yue et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2025; Jeon et al., 2025) scales training and inference costs approximately linearly with the number of domains, and introduces a routing problem: the system must decide which model should handle each dialogue, which is difficult when the relevant domain is ambiguous. Conversely, naively merging all domains into a single VLM can degrade recommendation accuracy, because product attributes, visual characteristics, and recommendation criteria vary widely across categories (Jeon et al., 2025). Nor can task-specific training be skipped: general-purpose proprietary models and VLMs applied through zero-shot prompting perform poorly on conversational recommendation (Jeon et al., 2025; Xi et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024; Liao et al., 2026).

To address these limitations, we propose LightCRS, a lightweight, query-conditioned, multi-domain framework that resolves this dilemma. Following the candidate-based CRS setting of LaViC (Jeon et al., 2025), LightCRS ranks a set of candidates for each dialogue through two stages. Offline, since candidate information is static, each product is represented by five views: title, features, description, image captions, and visual tokens. These representations are precomputed once with frozen encoders, which removes VLM inference from the online path.
Online, a compact 5.3M-parameter trainable module scores candidates with two components: a query-conditioned cross-attention that fuses the five views for each dialogue across 25 domains, and an inter-candidate Transformer that lets candidates be compared jointly rather than scored in isolation.

The key observation behind our design is that which product evidence matters is determined by the dialogue itself: a user describing a product’s appearance needs visual signals, whereas one asking about CPU specifications needs textual attributes. Query-conditioned cross-attention therefore allows a single set of shared parameters to behave differently for every dialogue. This addresses all three obstacles at once. A single unified model eliminates per-domain replication and the routing mechanism it requires; offline precomputation with a compact online module keeps training and inference costs low; and sharing the attention parameters across categories turns joint multi-domain training from a liability into an asset, as our multi-domain model outperforms 25 separately trained single-domain counterparts by 15.2% in HR@1.
Overall, LightCRS improves average HR@1 by 35.4% over the strongest baseline while reducing per-query latency by 266×. Although designed for supervised multi-domain recommendation, LightCRS also provides modest generalization gains on unseen product categories.

To enable rigorous evaluation, we introduce MM-R2A-25, a 25-domain dataset linking conversational queries from Reddit recommendation communities to Amazon product listings. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first CRS benchmark designed to probe multi-domain visual and textual product understanding at scale.

Our main contributions are as follows:

• 

We propose LightCRS, a lightweight multi-domain multimodal CRS that decouples offline product representation from online scoring: five textual and visual product views are precomputed with frozen encoders, and a compact trainable module combines query-conditioned cross-attention, which adaptively fuses five product views for each query while sharing parameters across all 25 domains, with an inter-candidate Transformer that captures relative relationships among candidates.

• 

We release MM-R2A-25, a 25-domain multimodal CRS benchmark that links natural Reddit recommendation dialogues to Amazon product listings with verified images.

• 

Extensive experiments show that LightCRS achieves state-of-the-art average HR@1, NDCG@5, and MRR, improving HR@1 by 35.4% over the strongest baseline while reducing training and inference costs. Moreover, LightCRS benefits from joint multi-domain training, achieving a 15.2% HR@1 improvement over single-domain training, and exhibits modest generalization to unseen categories.

## 2. MM-R2A-25 Dataset

To evaluate multi-domain multimodal conversational recommendation at scale, we introduce MM-R2A-25, a large-scale benchmark that follows the Reddit-Amazon construction protocol (Jeon et al., 2025) while extending its domain coverage to 25 diverse product categories. Unlike existing CRSs datasets, which are often limited in scale, domain coverage, or multimodal richness, MM-R2A-25 provides aligned Reddit conversations, comprehensive Amazon product metadata, and verified product images spanning all 25 categories.

## 3. Method

Figure 1. Architecture of LightCRS. The offline stage extracts and caches multi-view product representations using frozen GTE-base and LLaVA-v1.6. The online stage encodes the query, performs query-conditioned cross-attention over candidate representations, models candidate interactions with an inter-candidate Transformer, and outputs relevance scores through an MLP scoring head.

## 3.1. Problem Statement

We formulate multi-domain multimodal conversational recommendation as a re-ranking problem.
Following LaViC (Jeon et al., 2025), we define the dialogue query at recommender turn $k$ as the prefix $q=C_{<k}=\{u_{t}\}_{t=1}^{k-1}$ containing every utterance before the recommender’s $k$ -th turn. The recommender’s $k$ -th utterance $u_{k}$ , which mentions the recommended product, is treated as the ground-truth label only and is excluded from $q$ , ensuring that the target product does not appear in the model input. Given $q$ , an SBERT retriever (Reimers and Gurevych, 2019; He et al., 2023) returns a candidate set $\mathcal{C}=\{c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{K}\}$ of $K=10$ candidates following the Reddit-Amazon setting (Jeon et al., 2025), where each candidate $c_{i}$ denotes a product from one of multiple e-commerce domains. Each candidate set contains exactly one ground-truth item $c^{+}$ and $K-1$ negative candidates. Conversations associated with multiple ground-truth items are split into multiple instances. In addition, if $c^{+}$ is initially absent in the candidates, we swap out one negative candidate for $c^{+}$ to ensure precisely one ground-truth item. After candidate-set construction, we randomly shuffle all $K$ candidate items to remove the original retrieval order while prevent positional bias.
Each candidate is associated with heterogeneous multimodal product evidence, including its title $\mathbf{t}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{txt}}}$ , features $\mathbf{f}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{txt}}}$ , description $\mathbf{d}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{txt}}}$ , image captions $\mathbf{I}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_{\mathrm{txt}}}$ , and visual tokens $\mathbf{V}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{5\times d_{v}}$ , where $d_{\mathrm{txt}}=768$ and $d_{v}=4096$ .
The goal is to learn a single multi-domain scoring model
 $s(c_{i}\mid q,\mathcal{C})\in\mathbb{R}$ that efficiently
integrates multimodal product evidence across diverse domains
and ranks candidates according to their alignment with the user’s
conversational intent, assigning the ground-truth item
 $c^{+}\in\mathcal{C}$ a higher rank than all remaining candidates.
Furthermore, we focus on improving multi-domain recommendation accuracy, rather than generating fluent dialogue (Jeon et al., 2025; Xie et al., 2024; He et al., 2024).

## 3.2. Proposed Framework

In this section, we propose LightCRS, a lightweight, query-adaptive framework that aligns user dialogue with product representations across diverse domains. As illustrated in Figure 1, LightCRS operates in two stages.

## 3.2.1. Offline Stage

To improve computational efficiency, we precompute static candidates’ multi-view representations offline. These cached representations are shared across training and evaluation. We next describe the encoding process for each view representation.

Text encoding.

All three textual views, including the title, features, and description, are encoded by GTE-base (Li et al., 2023). Each view is mean-pooled over its token sequence into a single $d_{\mathrm{txt}}$ -dimensional embedding:
title $\mathbf{t}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{txt}}}$ ,
features $\mathbf{f}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{txt}}}$ ,
description $\mathbf{d}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{txt}}}$ .

Image caption encoding.

Product images are captioned offline using LLaVA-v1.6. The resulting captions are then encoded by the same GTE-base model, mean-pooled per caption, and stacked to form
 $\mathbf{I}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_{\mathrm{txt}}}$ , where $N$ denotes the number of captions per product.

Visual-token extraction.

Visual features are extracted with the vision tower of LLaVA-v1.6. For one representative image, we divide it into five spatial crops and encode each crop independently. The resulting crop-level visual features are projected into the language-model hidden space, yielding one $d_{v}$ -dimensional visual token per crop. This yields five spatially grounded visual tokens, each summarizing a distinct region of the product image. The resulting visual representation is $\mathbf{V}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{5\times d_{v}}$ .

The actual cost of the offline stage is reported in Appendix F.

## 3.2.2. Online Stage

At this stage, LightCRS uses a shared cross-attention module to align the on-the-fly encoded query with each candidate’s precomputed multi-view representations, enabling query-adaptive fusion and knowledge sharing across all 25 domains. An inter-candidate Transformer then compares candidates each other, and a scoring MLP outputs the final logits.

Query encoding.

We tokenize the dialogue query with the same frozen GTE-base encoder into $T_{q}$ subword tokens, embed them as $\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{T_{q}\times d_{\mathrm{txt}}}$ , project to $\mathbb{R}^{T_{q}\times D}$ , and compress the resulting token sequence into a single vector $\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ via learned attention pooling:

(1)

 $\mathbf{q}=\operatorname{softmax}\Bigl(\frac{\mathbf{w}_{q}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}_{p})^{\top}}{\sqrt{D}}\Bigr)(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}_{p}),$ 

with $\mathbf{W}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{txt}}\times D}$ and $\mathbf{w}_{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times D}$ learnable, yielding $\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ .

Query-conditioned cross-attention.

For each candidate $c_{i}$ , we project all five feature views into a shared $D$ -dimensional space via learnable linear projections $\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i}=\mathbf{W}_{t}\mathbf{t}_{i}$ , $\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{i}=\mathbf{W}_{f}\mathbf{f}_{i}$ , $\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{i}=\mathbf{W}_{d}\mathbf{d}_{i}$ , $\hat{\mathbf{I}}_{i}=\mathbf{I}_{i}\mathbf{W}_{I}$ , and $\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{i}=\mathbf{V}_{i}\mathbf{W}_{V}$ , and concatenate them:

(2)

 $\mathbf{C}_{i}=\bigl[\,\hat{\mathbf{t}}_{i};\;\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{i};\;\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{i};\;\hat{\mathbf{I}}_{i};\;\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{i}\,\bigr]\in\mathbb{R}^{T_{c}\times D},$ 

where $T_{c}=3+N+5$ . A binary attention mask zeroes out padded positions for image-caption, handling the variable number of images per product.

The query vector is replicated across all $K$ candidates and processed through $L$ cross-attention layers, where each layer applies multi-head cross-attention followed by a feed-forward network, both with post-norm residuals:

(3)

 $\displaystyle\mathbf{q}^{(l)}_{i}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\mathrm{LN}\,\bigl(\mathbf{q}^{(l-1)}_{i}+\mathrm{MHA}\,\bigl(\mathbf{q}^{(l-1)}_{i},\mathbf{C}_{i},\mathbf{C}_{i}\bigr)\bigr),$ 

(4)

 $\displaystyle\mathbf{q}^{(l)}_{i}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\mathrm{LN}\,\bigl(\mathbf{q}^{(l)}_{i}+\mathrm{FFN}\,\bigl(\mathbf{q}^{(l)}_{i}\bigr)\bigr).$ 

Rather than committing a priori to a fixed weighting of modalities, the cross-attention splits its weights across all five views in proportion to how relevant each view is to the current dialogue. This lets the model adapt to each user’s intent within a domain: it attends more to visual tokens when a user describes the appearance of a product, and more to textual descriptions when the user asks about CPU or GPU type of a computer. The same product can therefore be scored differently depending on the information highlighted in the dialogue.

Beyond query-specific view weighting, cross-attention also serves as the shared mechanism for multi-domain knowledge fusion. All 25 domains jointly update the same attention parameters, allowing domain-specific modality preferences to be consolidated into a unified query-view alignment function.
For example, queries in text-oriented domains such as Software tend to emphasize titles, features, and descriptions, whereas queries in visually oriented domains such as Handmade place greater weight on image captions and visual tokens. Because all domains share the same cross-attention parameters, query–view alignment patterns learned from one domain can transfer to others, helping the model interpret query intent and identify the most relevant product views. This design enables a single cross-attention module to support all 25 domains.

Inter-candidate comparison.

The fusion above scores each candidate independently against the query. To decide which candidate is the best, the per-candidate fused representations $\mathbf{q}_{i}^{(L)}$ , which now carry the query-conditioned multi-view evidence of candidate $c_{i}$ , are exposed to each other through a Transformer encoder. Stacking these across the $K$ candidates gives $\mathbf{F}=[\,\mathbf{q}_{1}^{(L)};\;\mathbf{q}_{2}^{(L)};\;\dots;\;\mathbf{q}_{K}^{(L)}\,]\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times D}$ , which is fed to the encoder as

(5)

 $\mathbf{H}=\mathrm{TransformerEncoder}\,\bigl(\mathbf{F}\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times D},$ 

whose $i$ -th row $\mathbf{h}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ is the comparison-aware representation of candidate $c_{i}$ : it preserves the query-conditioned multi-view evidence from $\mathbf{q}_{i}^{(L)}$ and is refined through inter-candidate self-attention.

Scoring head.

The representation of each candidate is then mapped to a scalar logit by a two-layer MLP with GELU activation:

(6)

 $s_{i}=\mathbf{w}_{2}^{\top}\mathrm{GELU}\,\bigl(\mathbf{W}_{1}\mathbf{h}_{i}+\mathbf{b}_{1}\bigr)+b_{2},$ 

with $\mathbf{W}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{D/2\times D}$ , $\mathbf{b}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{D/2}$ , $\mathbf{w}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{D/2}$ , and $b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}$ learnable. Stacking the $K$ candidates yields the logit vector $\mathbf{s}=[s_{1},\ldots,s_{K}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$ , which is the model’s output $s(\cdot\mid q,\mathcal{C})$ for the given query and candidate set.

## 3.3. Training Objective

We train the full model end-to-end with a cross-entropy loss over the $K$ -way candidate set. The scoring head produces logits $\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$ , and we treat reranking as $K$ -way classification in which the target is the index $y^{+}$ of the ground-truth candidate $c^{+}$ :

(7)

 $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ce}}=-\log\frac{\exp(s_{y^{+}})}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}\exp(s_{j})}.$ 

## 4. Evaluations

## 4.1. Experimental Setup

Dataset.

We evaluate all methods on the newly constructed MM-R2A-25 dataset, which spans 25 product domains. Section 2 details the dataset construction, while Table 4 summarizes its statistics.
For each domain, we partition the conversations into disjoint training, validation, and test sets using an 8:1:1 ratio.

Baselines.

We compare our proposed method with three baseline categories: task-specific CRS methods, generative methods, and retrieval methods. Although earlier knowledge graph-based CRSs methods have shown effectiveness (Chen et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2020b; Wang et al., 2022), they rely heavily on domain-specific knowledge graphs and have mainly been evaluated in movie recommendation. Generalizing such approaches consistently to our 25 diverse product domains is non-trivial. Moreover, prior studies have shown that zero-shot large language models often outperform knowledge graph-based conversational recommender systems on Reddit dialogues (He et al., 2023). Therefore, we focus on these three baseline categories because they are relevant to candidate-based conversational recommendation and applicable across multiple product domains.

(1) Task-specific CRS methods.
These methods are specifically designed or adapted for conversational recommendation. Among the multimodal methods, LaViC (Jeon et al., 2025) distills visual knowledge from LLaVA and finetunes the resulting model with LoRA. MSCRS (Wei et al., 2025) integrates collaborative, textual, and visual semantic graphs through GNN-based prompt learning. PRIME (Yue et al., 2025) adopts a retrieval–reranking pipeline in which a Qwen2-VL-2B multimodal ranker reranks candidates using online preference feedback. Among methods without direct visual signals, RTA (He et al., 2024) reindexes item titles as individual tokens and calibrates item-level generation probabilities, while NBCRS (Xie et al., 2024) models collaborative preferences through item neighborhoods.

(2) Generative methods.
These methods take the conversation and candidate-item information as input and use shared prompt templates to select a single most relevant item from the candidate list.
MiniMax-M2.7 and MiniMax-M3 are proprietary models accessed through APIs. Vicuna-v1.5 (Chiang et al., 2023) is a text-only instruction-following LLM. LLaVA-v1.5 (Liu et al., 2024a) extends Vicuna through visual instruction tuning, while LLaVA-v1.6 (Liu et al., 2024b) further improves multimodal reasoning and multi-image processing.

(3) Retrieval methods.
These methods rank candidate items according to the similarity between the conversation and item titles. BM25 (Robertson and Zaragoza, 2009) performs lexical matching based on token overlap. SBERT (Reimers and Gurevych, 2019) generates sentence embeddings using a siamese BERT architecture. RoBERTalarge (Liu et al., 2019) uses representations from a large pretrained language encoder, while SimCSElarge (Gao et al., 2021) learns sentence embeddings through contrastive training.

Metrics.

We use HitRatio@1 (HR@1) , NormalizedDiscountedCumulativeGain@5 (NDCG@5), and MeanReciprocalRank (MRR) as recommendation accuracy metrics. HR@1 directly measures how often the model identifies the correct item as the best candidate. NDCG@5 evaluates whether the ground-truth item is placed within a top-ranked shortlist. MRR further considers the exact position of the ground-truth item across the entire candidate list. Beyond recommendation accuracy, we report both training and inference costs to evaluate whether performance improvements are achieved. Trainable parameters measure the optimization burden during task adaptation, whereas total parameters and on-disk model size reflect the complete deployment footprint. For inference, peak GPU memory and per-query MFLOPs characterize memory and computational demand, while wall-clock latency (ms/query) and throughput (queries/second) measure end-to-end serving efficiency.

Implementation Details.

All locally executed training and inference experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA DGX Spark workstation equipped with an NVIDIA GB10 Grace Blackwell Superchip and 128 GB of unified system memory. Using a single workstation provides a consistent hardware environment for comparing the computational costs of LightCRS and baselines. In addition, we obtain open-source pretrained models (SBERT, RoBERTalarge, SimCSElarge, Vicuna, and LLaVA) from the official HuggingFace33
 3

 https://huggingface.co/ repositories, while MiniMax-M3 and MiniMax-M2.7 are accessed through their official APIs and therefore are excluded from hardware-dependent efficiency comparisons. Moreover, for all methods, we report the mean and standard deviation of HR@1, NDCG@5, and MRR over three independent runs.
Retrieval methods (BM25, SBERT, RoBERTa, and SimCSE) are deterministic given fixed inputs, so we omit their zero standard deviations.
Hyperparameter details and model-specific configurations are provided in Appendix E to support reproducibility.

## 4.3. Ablation Study

## 4.3.1. Component Ablation.

To understand the contribution of each component in our framework, we conduct ablation experiments by removing one component at a time and evaluating the resulting recommendation performance, as shown in Table 2.

Inter-candidate comparison drives top-1 accuracy.

Although removing inter-candidate comparison (ICC) retains the strongest performance among all ablated variants (HR@1 of 0.1432, NDCG@5 of 0.3505, MRR of 0.3389), it still reduces HR@1 by 45.3% relative to the full model, confirming that direct candidate-to-candidate comparison is essential for accurate top-1 selection.

Cross-attention and visual tokens contribute most.

Removing cross-attention (CA) or visual tokens (VT) has the greatest impact on top-1 accuracy, with HR@1 dropping to 0.0998 and 0.0987, respectively, underscoring the importance of query-conditioned multimodal interaction and visually grounded representations. Image descriptions (ID) further supply useful visual semantics, though their contribution is smaller than that of the raw visual tokens.

Title is the most informative textual view.

Among the textual feature views, removing the title yields the lowest NDCG@5 (0.2832) and MRR (0.2871) among all ablated variants, while product descriptions and features contribute complementary signals.

## 5. Further Analysis

CRSs on unseen domains remains challenging, as even strong proprietary models achieve limited performance without task-specific adaptation (Jeon et al., 2025; Xi et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024; Liao et al., 2026).
A key challenge in unseen-domain CRSs is the domain-dependent semantics. For example, “light” may denote low weight in hardware, light wearing comfort, a thin texture in cosmetics, or low computational cost in software.
Although LightCRS is not explicitly designed for recommendation on unseen domains, we evaluate its transferability through 5-fold domain-split testing across 25 domains. In each fold, the model is trained on 20 domains and evaluated on 5 unseen domains.
Table 7 in Appendix C shows the comparison of LightCRS on unseen domains with retrieval and generative baselines, for which the evaluation domains are also unseen.
LightCRS slightly outperforms the strongest MiniMax-M3 baseline. However, the absolute performance remains relatively low, indicating that recommendation on unseen domains remains challenging.

## 6. Related Work

Recommendation systems have long studied how to infer user preferences and rank relevant items from behavioral signals. Conventional sequential recommendation focuses on predicting users’ next items from historical interaction sequences. Early methods model sequential transitions with Markov chains and recurrent networks (Rendle et al., 2010; Hidasi et al., 2015), followed by convolutional and self-attention architectures such as Caser (Tang and Wang, 2018), SASRec (Kang and McAuley, 2018), and BERT4Rec (Sun et al., 2019). More recent self-supervised and contrastive learning methods further improve sequence representations by exploiting auxiliary sequence-level signals (Zhou et al., 2020a; Xie et al., 2022). Along another line, candidate reranking methods refine an initially retrieved item list by modeling listwise context and candidate interactions. Representative methods include listwise context models (Ai et al., 2018), groupwise scoring functions (Ai et al., 2019), SetRank (Pang et al., 2020), and personalized reranking modules (Pei et al., 2019). Recent industrial generative recommender systems, such as OneMall (Zhang et al., 2026b), further reformulate large-scale e-commerce recommendation as autoregressive semantic-ID generation. Together, these studies provide important foundations for sequence modeling, candidate comparison, and industrial-scale recommendation, but they mainly rely on behavioral histories, retrieved item lists, or platform-specific interaction signals rather than natural-language conversational interactions.

CRSs extend recommendation by eliciting user preferences through multi-turn dialogue and refining recommendations according to the evolving conversational context. Early CRSs datasets and systems, such as ReDial (Li et al., 2018), GoRecDial (Kang et al., 2019), and TG-ReDial (Zhou et al., 2020c), established conversational recommendation as the task of predicting relevant items from natural-language interactions, mostly in narrow domains such as movies. With the development of neural approaches, CRSs began to jointly model dialogue context, user preference, external knowledge, and item recommendation (Chen et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2020b; Wang et al., 2022). More recently, LLM-based CRSs methods have enhanced conversational recommendation by leveraging the instruction-following ability and world knowledge of large language models (He et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2025; He et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2025b). Despite these advances, most existing CRSs methods remain primarily text-based and are not designed to exploit rich multimodal product evidence in e-commerce scenarios.

Recent studies have further extended CRSs from text-only interactions to multimodal product understanding by leveraging VLMs to jointly encode visual and textual product evidence (Jeon et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026a; Giahi et al., 2025). These methods show that visual signals can complement textual dialogue, especially in visually grounded e-commerce scenarios where product appearance, style, color, and packaging are important for user decisions. However, VLMs-based CRSs still face three major limitations when deployed across diverse e-commerce categories: (1) training and inference costs that scale approximately linearly with the number of product domains, (2) performance degradation when combining heterogeneous domains into a single VLM, and (3) an additional routing challenge when multiple domain-specific models must be selected per dialogue. These limitations motivate LightCRS, which precomputes multimodal product views offline and performs lightweight dialogue-conditioned reranking online, using query-conditioned cross-attention and inter-candidate comparison to improve multi-domain multimodal CRSs recommendation accuracy while maintaining low training and inference costs.

## 7. Conclusion

We presented LightCRS, a lightweight framework for unified multi-domain multimodal conversational recommendation. By moving product representation computation offline, LightCRS avoids repeatedly encoding static candidate information during training and inference. Its compact online design enables a single model to adapt to different dialogue intents, share knowledge across product domains, and compare candidates jointly. In addition, we introduce MM-R2A-25, a benchmark linking Reddit recommendation conversations to Amazon product listings across 25 categories. Experiments show that LightCRS provides state-of-the-art average recommendation performance compared to the evaluated baselines while using fewer trainable parameters and lower inference costs.

Limitations and Future Work.

Our method follows a candidate-based reranking pipeline, so its overall performance remains partly constrained by the quality of the first-stage retriever. Improving candidate retrieval could therefore yield further gains in recommendation performance.
On unseen domains, LightCRS still underperforms its in-domain supervised counterpart, while even strong proprietary models achieve limited performance. This indicates that achieving effective recommendation on unseen domains remains an important direction for future work.
Moreover, our model focuses on improving recommendation accuracy, while extending it to support dialogue generation remains another important direction for future work.

## Appendix A Dataset Details

Table 4 presents the full per-category breakdown of the MM-R2A-25 dataset. Table 5 compares it with existing CRSs benchmarks. Early efforts (e.g., ReDial (Li et al., 2018), GoRecDial (Kang et al., 2019), and TG-ReDial (Zhou et al., 2020c)) primarily focus on movies, often with crowd-sourced or synthetic dialogues. Recent datasets have expanded into broader domains (e.g., beauty, home and fashion) or used natural Reddit conversations (Rabiah et al., 2024; Jia et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023), but still lack multimodal product information. In contrast, MM-R2A-25 covers 25 diverse product categories, enabling comprehensive evaluations of multi-domain multimodal conversational recommender systems. Following LaViC (Jeon et al., 2025), we use SBERT to retrieve candidate sets for each conversation, with a Recall@10 of 0.6479.

## Appendix D Prompt Templates

This appendix consolidates the four prompt templates that govern our data curation and model training pipeline. Table 8 isolates Reddit threads that end in an explicit product recommendation, Table 9 elicits visually grounded captions, and Tables 10–11 drive the recommendation step in the text-only and the text-plus-image configurations, respectively.

Table 8. Prompt used to identify product-recommendation conversations. Only conversations labeled yes by GPT-3.5-turbo are retained in MM-R2A-25.

You are given a Reddit conversation containing multiple numbered messages. Each message follows the format
<message>(i) [content]</message>.
Determine whether the final message explicitly recommends one or more specific items in response to a recommendation request in an earlier message.
Answer yes if both conditions are satisfied, no otherwise, or uncertain if the conversation does not provide enough information.
Return only yes, no, or uncertain, without any explanation.
Conversation: {conversation}

Table 9. Prompt used to generate visually grounded product descriptions.

You are given an Amazon product title and image. Generate a detailed, visually grounded description that helps users evaluate the product. Focus on its appearance, visible features, and other visually informative details. Do not repeat or mention the product title.
Product title: {title}
Product image: {image}

Table 10. Prompt for text-only product recommendation, used by generative baselines.

You are given a conversation between a user requesting product recommendations, marked by <submission>, and other users providing responses, marked by <comment>. You are also given candidate products formatted as ID: title.
Select the candidate that best matches the user’s needs. Return only its ID without any explanation.
Conversation: {conversation}
Candidates: {list of candidates}

Table 11. Prompt for image-aware product recommendation, used by generative baselines.

You are given a conversation between a user requesting product recommendations, marked by <submission>, and other users providing responses, marked by <comment>. You are also given candidate products, each formatted as ID: title followed by its image.
Select the candidate that best matches the user’s needs based on the conversation, title, and image. Return only its ID without any explanation.
Conversation: {conversation}
Candidates: {list of candidates}

## Appendix E Hyperparameter Details

No domain-specific hyperparameter tuning was conducted. We used one fixed configuration across all 25 product domains and trained the model (Section 3) under this unified protocol.

The common projection dimension is $D=256$ . The query–candidate cross-attention stack has $L=2$ layers with 4 attention heads and a feed-forward dimension of 1024 ( $4\times D$ ), and the inter-candidate comparison Transformer mirrors this configuration (2 layers, 4 heads, 1024 FFN). The scoring head is a 2-layer MLP ( $D\to D/2\to 1$ ), and dropout is fixed at 0.1 across all trainable modules. Frozen encoder outputs feed in at their native hidden sizes: $d_{q}=768$ for the GTE-base text encoder and $d_{v}=4096$ for the LLaVA-v1.6 visual tokens, with a maximum of $N=15$ image descriptions and 5 visual tokens per candidate item. The training objective is a cross-entropy classification loss over the $K$ -way candidate set. Models are trained for up to 20 epochs with early stopping (patience $=2$ ) on validation loss, using a batch size of 16 and the AdamW optimizer ( $\text{lr}=1\times 10^{-4}$ , weight decay $1\times 10^{-5}$ ) under a constant learning-rate schedule with no warmup or decay. Gradient clipping (max norm 1.0) and BF16 mixed precision are applied throughout.

## Appendix F Offline Preprocessing Cost and Storage Footprint

LightCRS moves product-side multimodal encoding from online scoring to offline preprocessing (Section 3.2.1). This appendix reports the computational cost and storage overhead of this offline process.

Measurement methodology.

All measurements are conducted on a single NVIDIA DGX Spark workstation. We evaluate four offline processing steps: text encoding(title/features/descriptions), image caption generation, caption encoding, and visual-token extraction. Each step is measured independently. We sample 50 products from the Books category, measure the runtime and peak GPU memory after one warm-up pass, and linearly extrapolate the results to the full MM-R2A-25 catalog since each product is processed independently.

Offline preprocessing cost.

Table 12 reports the runtime, throughput, peak GPU memory, and extrapolated cost on the full catalog. Caption generation dominates the preprocessing cost, requiring 593.46 GPU-hours for generating captions of all product images, while the remaining encoding stages require less than 3 GPU-hours in total. This cost is incurred only once per catalog. After preprocessing, the online module operates only on cached representations without additional product-side encoding.

Storage footprint.

Table 13 summarizes the storage cost of cached product representations. We notice that visual tokens accounts for the majority of storage usage.
