## Mitigating Query Shortcuts in Chain-of-Thought

with Context-Aware Decoding

Shin’ya Yamaguchi

Affiliation: NTT

Email: shinya.yamaguchi@ntt.com

Abstract

While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting improves the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs), recent studies have shown that final answers do not always depend on the generated rationales. In particular, LLMs may rely on query-specific shortcuts rather than the information provided by their reasoning steps. In this paper, we investigate whether context-aware decoding (CAD), an existing contrastive decoding method, can mitigate such shortcut behavior when applied to CoT reasoning. We study this application as Rationale Contrastive Decoding (RCD), which contrasts the next-token distributions conditioned on the query with and without its rationale, amplifying tokens whose likelihood increases due to the rationale. We provide a theoretical interpretation of this decoding rule via a KL-regularized reward-maximization objective. Experiments across five reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that RCD improves answer accuracy over standard CoT. Furthermore, intervention analyses using ground-truth and random rationales reveal that RCD increases the behavioral dependence of final answers on the rationale content. Our findings suggest that contrastive decoding offers a simple, training-free approach to mitigating query shortcuts.

## 1 Introduction

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting Wei et al. (2022); Kojima et al. (2022) has emerged as an effective inference strategy for improving the reasoning performance of Large Language Models (LLMs).
By generating multiple intermediate reasoning steps, or rationales, before producing a final answer, CoT enables LLMs to solve complex problems.
Beyond improving task performance, these rationales are often interpreted as explanations of the model’s predictions.
However, recent studies have revealed that generated rationales do not necessarily reflect the actual decision-making process Turpin et al. (2023); Arcuschin et al. (2025); Lewis-Lim et al. (2025).
In particular, LLMs may rely on shortcut information directly from the input query, even when their rationales suggest a different conclusion.
Such query-shortcut behavior weakens the behavioral dependency between rationales and final answers, raising concerns about the reliability of CoT.

Prior research has investigated this issue by rationale interventions that measure whether changes to reasoning steps affect final predictions Lanham et al. (2023); Turpin et al. (2023) or improving rationale reliability with additional supervision or rationale selection Paul et al. (2024); Sun et al. (2026).
While these studies provide important insights, a complementary question is whether query-shortcut behavior can be mitigated directly during answer decoding, without modifying model parameters or employing external evaluators.
In standard CoT decoding, the query and generated rationale are jointly provided as context for next-token prediction.
However, this formulation does not explicitly distinguish the contributions of the query and the rationale, allowing the model to generate answers primarily from query-conditioned predictions.

In this work, we investigate the application of Context-Aware Decoding (CAD) Shi et al. (2024) to mitigate query-shortcut behavior in CoT.
CAD is an established contrastive decoding approach originally developed to prioritize contextual evidence over a model’s parametric knowledge.
We examine whether the same principle can encourage LLMs to rely more heavily on self-generated rationales during decoding.
Specifically, we study Rationale Contrastive Decoding (RCD), an application of CAD in which the context part is a CoT rationale rather than an external document.
RCD contrasts the next-token distributions conditioned on both the query and rationale with those conditioned only on the query.
By subtracting query-only logits, this decoding rule increases the relative preference for tokens supported by the rationale, potentially suppressing query-shortcut behavior without additional training.
We further interpret this established decoding formulation as a KL-regularized reward-maximization objective, in which the reward is the log-density ratio between the rationale-conditioned and query-only distributions.

We empirically investigate RCD through comprehensive experiments across multiple reasoning benchmarks.
First, intervention analyses with ground-truth and randomly substituted rationales demonstrate that RCD makes final predictions more sensitive to rationale content, providing behavioral evidence of increased rationale–answer dependency.
Second, RCD consistently improves answer accuracy over standard CoT across five reasoning benchmarks and multiple model families.
Third, an analysis of rationale correctness reveals that the performance gains primarily stem from correcting answers that fail to follow valid rationales, rather than repairing incorrect reasoning.
These findings suggest that applying contrastive decoding to CoT can mitigate query-shortcut behavior and improve the utilization of generated rationales.

## 2 Preliminary

## 2.1 Next-token Prediction in LLM

Consider an auto-regressive LLM, parameterized by $\theta$ .
Given a query $q$ , an LLM generates an output token sequence $\bm{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{L})\in\mathcal{V}^{L}$ following:

 $\displaystyle p(\bm{y}|q)=\prod^{L}_{i=1}p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q),$ 

(1)

where $L$ is token length, $\mathcal{V}$ is token vocabulary, and $\bm{y}_{<i}$ are preceding output tokens.
 $p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q)$ over $\mathcal{V}$ is computed by the softmax of the model’s output $\operatorname{logits}_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q)$ as follows.

 $\displaystyle p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q)=\sigma(\operatorname{logits}_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q))$ 

(2)

 $\displaystyle=\frac{\exp(\operatorname{logits}_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q))}{\sum_{{w}\in\mathcal{V}}\exp(\operatorname{logits}_{\theta}(y_{i}=w|\bm{y}_{<i},q))}.$ 

(3)

Each token $y_{i}$ is generated from $p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q)$ via a decoding strategy such as greedy decoding and sampling.
We define a function for an arbitrary decoding strategy:

 $\bm{y}=\operatorname{generate}_{\theta}(q).$ 

(4)

## 2.2 Chain-of-Thought (CoT)

CoT aims to enhance LLM reasoning by incorporating multi-step rationale generation during decoding Wei et al. (2022); Kojima et al. (2022).
First, LLMs generate a rationale $r$ from query $q$ with instruction prompt, e.g., Let’s think step-by-step Kojima et al. (2022), as follows.

 $r=\operatorname{generate}_{\theta}(q).$ 

(5)

Adding $r$ to the input context, LLMs generate the final answer by the next-token prediction, similar to Eq. (4):

 $\displaystyle\bm{y}=\operatorname{generate}_{\theta}(q,r).$ 

(6)

For simplicity, we assume that the rationale and answer generation steps are separated, and that the generated rationale can be intervened by alternative rationales, e.g., ground truth.

In Eq. (6), each answer token $y_{i}$ is decoded by the next-token distribution $p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)$ , and thus, it is not guaranteed that the output tokens are based on the rationale $r$ , i.e., LLMs can directly answer from the query $q$ .
In fact, previous works have reported the unfaithful behavior of CoT, such as post-hoc rationalizing by biasing toward the order of multiple-choice options for the answer Turpin et al. (2023), silent error correction, where the model implicitly fixes the computational or logical errors in the rationales due to shortcuts Arcuschin et al. (2025).
These findings indicate that standard CoT can exhibit weak or inconsistent behavioral dependence on its rationale.

## 3 Method

## 3.1 Rationale Contrastive Decoding (RCD)

We investigate whether context-aware decoding can mitigate query-shortcut behavior in CoT by increasing the influence of rationales during answer generation.
To this end, we study Rationale Contrastive Decoding (RCD), which applies Context-Aware Decoding (CAD; Shi et al. (2024)) to self-generated CoT rationales rather than external contexts (Fig. 1).
Following CAD, RCD instead reweights this distribution using the density ratio:

 $\hat{p}_{\theta}(y_{i})\propto p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)\times\left(\frac{p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)}{p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q)}\right)^{\alpha},$ 

(7)

where $\alpha$ controls the contrastive strength.
During decoding, this is equivalently implemented by the logit-level computation in a batch-parallel manner:

 $\displaystyle\hat{\operatorname{logits}}_{\theta}(y_{i}):=$ 
 $\displaystyle(1+\alpha)\operatorname{logits}_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)$ 

 $\displaystyle-\alpha\operatorname{logits}_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q).$ 

(8)

For $\alpha>0$ , the density ratio increases the relative preference for tokens whose probability rises when conditioning on the rationale.
Unlike logit addition, which reinforces tokens favored by both distributions, subtraction emphasizes the information contributed by the rationale beyond the query alone as shown in Section 4.
This aims to mitigate query-shortcut behavior rather than simply increase agreement with query-only predictions.

## 3.2 Theoretical Insight

Eq. (7) can be interpreted as the closed-form solution to the following KL-regularized reward maximization problem Schulman et al. (2015); Schulman et al. (2017):

 $\max_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[R]-\beta\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}[\pi||\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}],$ 

(9)

where $R=\log\frac{p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)}{p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q)}$ is reward function, $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}=p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)$ is reference policy, and $\beta$ is a hyperparameter modulating the KL-penalty term.

Sampling by Eq. (7) is equivalent to sampling from the optimal policy $\pi^{*}$ for Eq. (9).

Intuitively, maximizing Eq. (9) means finding the next-token distribution $\pi$ that yields a token clearly highlighting the difference from the query-only distribution while preserving the likelihood of the original query-rationale conditional distribution.
As a result, the LLM is expected to generate an answer by relying heavily on the CoT rationale $r$ .

## 4 Experiments

## 4.1 Settings

We used five representative reasoning-intensive benchmark datasets: GSM8K Cobbe et al. (2021), MATH Hendrycks et al. (), CommonsenseQA Talmor et al. (2019), AQuA-RAT Ling et al. (2017), and MMLU-Pro Wang et al. (2024).
For LLMs, we used Qwen3 Yang et al. (2025), Gemma3 Team et al. (2025), and Llama-3.1 Grattafiori et al. (2024).
To examine the effect of contrastive subtraction, we additionally evaluate Logit Addition, which sums the rationale-conditioned and query-only logits, corresponding to a Product-of-Experts combination.
This baseline allows us to investigate whether subtracting query-only predictions is more effective than reinforcing them.
We set $\alpha=1$ for RCD by grid-searching on the GSM8K train subset.
For sampling, we used the official default parameters (temperature: 0.7, top_k: 20, top_p: 0.8) for Qwen3.
CoT and RCD shared the same rationales.
All experiments were run three times on an NVIDIA H100 with 80 GB of VRAM.

## 4.2 Proof-of-Concept by Intervention Analysis

We conduct an intervention analysis on GSM8K by replacing the rationale $r$ in Eq. (6) with either ground-truth (GT) or random rationales.
If a decoding method sensitive to rationales, GT rationales should improve performance and irrelevant random rationales should degrade it.
We use the GT rationales provided in GSM8K and construct random rationales by shuffling them across examples.

Table 1 shows the intervention results. Standard CoT improves over direct answering, but still keeps non-trivial accuracy even with random rationales, suggesting that it can often ignore the given rationale and rely on shortcut biases.
In contrast, RCD achieves higher accuracy with GT rationales and substantially lower accuracy with random rationales, indicating stronger behavioral dependence on rationale content.
Notably, Logit Addition degrades accuracy with both self-generated and GT rationales while retaining higher accuracy with random rationales.
These contrasting results suggest that subtracting query-only logits, rather than reinforcing them, is effective in increasing rationale–answer dependency.

## 4.5 Analysis with Human Annotation

To examine how rationale correctness affects RCD, we annotated the rationales generated by Qwen3-8B on the GSM8K test set (1,319 samples) and analyzed prediction transitions from CoT to RCD. Table 3 summarizes the results.
Among 1,201 correct rationales, RCD corrects 10 of 33 CoT errors (30.30%) without corrupting any correct predictions.
In contrast, among 118 incorrect rationales, RCD neither corrects any of the 110 CoT errors nor changes any of the eight correct predictions.
These results indicate that RCD’s accuracy gains primarily stem from improving adherence to correct rationales, while increased reliance on incorrect rationales can occasionally be detrimental.

## 5 Related Work

Recent studies have examined CoT faithfulness through rationale interventions Lanham et al. (2023) and methods for measuring or improving rationale-answer alignment Paul et al. (2024); Sun et al. (2026).
These approaches primarily focus on faithfulness evaluation or require additional mechanisms (e.g., preference tuning and multiple-rationale evaluations), rather than directly providing comparable answer-decoding baselines.
In contrast, we investigate the application of existing Context-Aware Decoding Shi et al. (2024) to CoT rationales, without training or external evaluators.
We therefore focus on decoding baselines and separately examine compatibility with Self-Consistency Wang et al. (2023) in Appendix H.

## 6 Conclusion

In this work, we investigated the application of context-aware contrastive decoding to mitigate query-shortcut behavior in Chain-of-Thought reasoning.
By contrasting rationale-conditioned and query-only next-token distributions, Rationale Contrastive Decoding (RCD) encourages LLMs to generate answers that depend more strongly on their rationales.
Our experiments demonstrate increased behavioral rationale–answer dependency and consistent accuracy improvements over standard CoT across multiple reasoning benchmarks.
These findings suggest that existing contrastive decoding techniques can improve the utilization of CoT rationales without additional training or external evaluators.
Nevertheless, stronger rationale dependency does not guarantee faithful reasoning, particularly when the rationales themselves are incorrect.
Our study thus highlights both the potential and the limitations of decoding-level interventions for mitigating query shortcuts in CoT.

## Limitations

First, although our rationale interventions show that RCD increases the behavioral dependency of final answers on the provided rationales, they do not establish that the rationales faithfully reflect the model’s internal reasoning process.
In particular, changes in accuracy under ground-truth or randomly shuffled rationales measure sensitivity to rationale content but do not necessarily indicate more correct or faithful reasoning.
Therefore, our findings should be interpreted as evidence of stronger rationale–answer dependency rather than a general improvement in reasoning reliability.

Second, stronger rationale dependency can be detrimental when rationales are incorrect or incomplete.
Since RCD does not verify rationale correctness, it may amplify erroneous reasoning while suppressing useful information from the query-only distribution.
Future work could explore adaptive contrastive weighting based on rationale quality.

We further discuss potential societal and deployment risks in Appendix B.

## Appendix A Proof of Theorem 3.1

Proof.

Consider the general KL-regularized reward maximization objective Schulman et al. (2015); Schulman et al. (2017); Rafailov et al. (2023):

 $\max_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[R(s,a)]-\beta\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}[\pi(a|s)||\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a|s)],$ 

(10)

where $R$ is a reward function, $s$ is a state (input context), and $a$ is an action (output).
Obviously, Eq. (9) is a special case of Eq. (10).
From Appendix A.1 of Rafailov et al. (2023), the optimal policy $\pi^{*}(a|s)$ for this objective is given by

 $\pi^{*}(a|s)\propto\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a|s)\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}R(s,a)\right).$ 

(11)

In the maximization of Eq. (9), given $a=y_{i}$ and $s=(\bm{y}_{i<},q,r)$ , we can set each component in Eq. (10) as $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a|s)=p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)$ , $R(s,a)=\log\frac{p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)}{p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q)}$ , and $\beta=1/\alpha$ .
Substituting them into Eq. (11) yields

 $\displaystyle\pi^{*}(a|s)$ 

 $\displaystyle\propto p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)\exp\left(\alpha\log\frac{p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)}{p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q)}\right)$ 

 $\displaystyle=p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)\left(\frac{p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q,r)}{p_{\theta}(y_{i}|\bm{y}_{<i},q)}\right)^{\alpha}$ 

 $\displaystyle=\text{Eq.~\eqref{eq:rcd}}.$ 

Therefore, sampling by Eq. (7) is indeed equivalent to sampling from the optimal policy $\pi^{*}(a|s)$ for the maximization in Eq. (9).
∎

Note that while this theoretical framework is shared with RED Yamaguchi et al. (2026), which multiplies the separated image-conditioned and rationale-conditioned distributions as product-of-experts for multimodal CoT, our RCD and RED have different formalizations and objectives.

## Appendix B Potential Risks

First, RCD has potential dual-use risks. By making answers more consistent with explicit rationales, the method could be misused to produce more persuasive but misleading explanations, such as rationalized misinformation, deceptive arguments, or fabricated justifications. While our work does not target such applications, future deployment of rationale-sensitive decoding methods should consider monitoring, provenance tracking, and safeguards against persuasive misuse.

Second, the fairness and robustness implications of RCD remain underexplored. Our experiments focus on standard English reasoning benchmarks and do not evaluate multilingual, culturally grounded, or demographically sensitive scenarios. If the generated rationales encode social biases or culturally narrow assumptions, increasing the model’s reliance on them may reinforce biased outputs. Future work should evaluate RCD on more diverse languages, domains, and user populations before deployment in broad-facing applications.

## F.1 Ablation Study

Effects of $\alpha$ .
Figure 3 shows the validation performance when varying $\alpha$ .
We observed that increasing $\alpha$ enhances the reliance on the rationales, but accuracy actually decreases once a certain value is exceeded.
This is because if the query-only conditions in Eq. (8) are significantly reduced with larger $\alpha$ , the performance of the query itself also declines.

Model Generality.
We show the performance when varying the model size and families in Table 5.
RCD stably performed on multiple models.
The performance gain over CoT increases with model size, suggesting that RCD can significantly improve answer quality with stronger rationales when a model has strong reasoning capabilities.

## Appendix G Computational Analysis

RCD requires two model evaluations at each answer-decoding step. To quantify the practical cost, we measured end-to-end latency and peak VRAM on Qwen3-8B/GSM8K under the same hardware and decoding settings.
Table 6 shows the results.
Relative to CoT, RCD increases the observed end-to-end latency by only 1.5% and peak VRAM by 1.4% in our batch-parallel implementation. Thus, although RCD increases answer-stage model computation, its measured wall-clock and memory overhead are small in this setting.

## Appendix H Compatibility with Self-Consistency

We further examine whether RCD can be combined with Self-Consistency (SC; Wang et al. (2023)), which aggregates predictions from multiple sampled rationales.
Unlike SC, which improves answer selection through multiple reasoning paths, RCD modifies the answer decoding distribution conditioned on each rationale. We evaluate their combination on GSM8K using Qwen3-8B with three sampled rationales ( $k=3$ ).

Table 7 presents the results.
Standard SC achieves 89.58% accuracy, whereas combining SC with RCD improves accuracy to 90.79%.
Similarly, in the single-rationale setting, RCD improves standard CoT from 89.16% to 89.91%.
These results suggest that RCD is complementary to multi-sample aggregation.
Furthermore, RCD incurs only a 1.5% increase in end-to-end latency over standard CoT, indicating that its additional computational overhead is small in this setting.
