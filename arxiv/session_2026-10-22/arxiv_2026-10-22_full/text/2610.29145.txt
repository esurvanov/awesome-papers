Abstract

Large language models increasingly automate optimization modeling from natural language problems,
producing written mathematics, solver code and a returned report. Verifying one optimization problem
across these artifacts requires semantic comparison: unequal models can attain the same objective,
equivalent models can use different auxiliary variables, and mutually consistent artifacts can omit
the same source requirement. ORSMT provides a typed semantic interface and exact SMT relation queries.
It aligns observable decisions, projects auxiliary variables, proves or refutes feasible region and
objective relations, localizes refutations to implicated rows or domains and links source texts
when available. It then reconstructs one artifact from another, and each adopted candidate passes
the required relation checks.
Across 3,034 problems and six generator sizes, ORSMT improves the model–code match rate in all 72
benchmark and generator combinations before selection. Protected selection raises the aggregate
rate from 43.2% to 88.2% and the share that are both correct and matched from 25.2% to 40.2%. On the 1,905 refuted outputs,
certified voting under the shared fallback makes 50.9% of outputs correct and matched, compared with 44.8% for
ReLoop and 49.4% for Opt-Verifier. A separate one-round source revision followed by exact
reconstruction restores a model–code match for 84.4% of the refuted pairs.

## 1. Introduction

Optimization models support logistics, production planning, and energy scheduling by optimizing decisions under operational constraints. Decision quality depends on how natural-language requirements are translated into mathematical formulations, executable solver models, and reported decisions. Recent large language model (LLM) systems generate these artifacts together, while evaluation commonly focuses on solver execution and the final objective value. Establishing semantic agreement across these artifacts therefore motivates verification and repair at the level of the optimization problem itself.

Classical criteria such as feasibility and answer correctness evaluate the returned decision without assessing the agreement across the underlying artifacts (Ramamonjison et al., 2022; AhmadiTeshnizi et al., 2024b; Huang et al., 2025a; Jiang et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025). Different feasible regions can attain the same optimum, so a correct objective value does not identify the implemented problem. Conversely, equivalent models can differ in auxiliary variables, redundant constraints, and syntax. Agreement between written mathematics and executable code can also miss a source requirement from the natural language omitted by both. Output-based evaluation cannot distinguish these failures or establish whether a proposed repair restores the required relations.

Recent methods broaden feedback beyond the final value through behavioral tests, structural comparisons, model-based judgments, and counterexample searches (Zhang et al., 2026; Li and Hai, 2026; Zhai et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025; Lian et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026).
Although these methods provide useful repair signals, passing behavioral tests or receiving a favorable model-based judgment does not establish semantic agreement between the math formulation and its code implementation.
A program can produce the expected outputs on tested cases while admitting decisions excluded by the formulation, whereas structural comparisons can reject equivalent models that use different auxiliary constructions.
Verification must therefore examine whether the different artifacts admit the same feasible decisions and preserve their objective preferences.
It should also localize discrepancies to guide repair and verify that the repaired artifacts satisfy the required relations before adoption.

This paper proposes ORSMT, a satisfiability modulo theories (SMT)-based verification and repair framework for source requirements, written mathematics, instantiated solver models, and returned reports.
ORSMT translates the written model and captures the solver model from code in a common exact representation, aligning observable decisions and projecting auxiliary variables. Within the affine rational core, SMT queries check admitted source requirements, feasible regions, objective relations, and reports for a fixed instance. A satisfiable violation query returns a localized counterexample; an unsatisfiable query certifies the corresponding relation. The evidence supports direct reconstruction between mathematics and code or guides revision, and every adopted candidate passes the required checks again. This keeps diagnosis, repair, and acceptance as distinct stages of one formally checked process. Our contributions are summarized as follows:

(i) We introduce ORSMT, a semantic verification framework for source requirements, mathematical formulations, solver codes, and returned reports. Its common representation supports exact SMT checks within the admitted affine rational core, providing certificates or localized counterexamples for decided relations.

(ii) We develop a verification and repair loop that combines bidirectional reconstruction with counterexample-guided revision. Each adopted candidate passes the required relation checks, connecting diagnosis, repair, and acceptance through the same verifier.

(iii) We evaluate ORSMT on 3,034 problems from 12 benchmarks and 6 generator sizes. Model–code match rates improve in all 72 benchmark–generator combinations before selection. Under protected selection, the aggregate match rate rises from 43.2% to 88.2%, and the share of outputs with both a correct answer and a model–code match rises from 25.2% to 40.2%. ORSMT further improves correct-and-matched outputs when integrated with existing OptiMUS-0.3 and OptiMind pipelines, supporting its use as a modular verification and repair layer.

## 2. Related Work

Autoformulation and evaluation. NL4Opt (Ramamonjison et al., 2022) studies formulation.
OptiMUS and OptiMUS-0.3 build modular formulation and execution
pipelines (AhmadiTeshnizi et al., 2024b; AhmadiTeshnizi et al., 2024a), while OptiMind adds expert-guided training and inference (Zhang et al., 2025). ORLM, LLMOPT and ORThought generate and revise
mathematical models, code and solver outputs (Huang et al., 2025a; Jiang et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2026). MAMO,
OptiBench and OPT Engine broaden evaluation tasks (Huang et al., 2025b; Yang et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026).
Feasibility and reference objectives assess final answers, but do not establish that the written
model, instantiated code and report describe the same optimization problem.

Verification and repair. ReLoop tests program behavior under parameter
changes (Lian et al., 2026). Opt-Verifier judges structure and solutions (Liu et al., 2026),
and SAC Opt uses semantic anchors for revision (Zhang et al., 2026). EquivaMap and ORGEval compare
variable mappings or graph structure (Zhai et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025). Falsification uses solver-based testing under controlled instance transformations, whereas FLARE verifies MILP reformulations using theorem proving (Li and Hai, 2026; Robbins et al., 2026). ORSMT repairs instance-specific mathematical, executable and report artifacts. Therefore, repair comparisons use ReLoop and Opt-Verifier.

Formal semantic verification. Denotational tests evaluate program behavior beyond textual
similarity (Zhong et al., 2020). Z3 and cvc5 decide arithmetic formulas (de Moura and Bjørner, 2008; Barbosa et al., 2022), while neural symbolic methods translate proposed statements into formal
queries (Jha et al., 2023; Pan et al., 2023; Ye et al., 2023; Olausson et al., 2023). ORSMT verifies source
requirements, feasible regions, objective preferences and reports in this way. Its evidence identifies
missing, additional and redundant restrictions and supports repair from either usable model.

## 3. Problem Setting

An autoformulation pipeline maps a source statement $s$ and instance data to written
mathematics, a solver program $\widehat{P}$ , and a report
 $R=(\hat{x},\hat{z},\operatorname{status})$ , where $\hat{x}$ and $\hat{z}$ represent the returned solution and its objective value.
The written mathematics is lowered to the mathematical model $A$ .
The instantiated solver model is represented by
 $B=\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})$ . A source adapter supplies formal requirements
 $C=\mathcal{F}_{\rm src}(s)$ , each linked to its supporting
source text and variable roles, when that
adapter admits the source. Verification checks the available relations among $C$ , $A$ , $B$ , and $R$ .

Define a model as $A=(X_{A},U_{A},\Phi_{A},f_{A},\delta_{A})$ , where $X_{A}$ contains observable decisions,
 $U_{A}$ contains auxiliary variables, $\Phi_{A}$ contains domains and constraints, $f_{A}$ is the
objective, and $\delta_{A}\in\{\min,\max\}$ . The executable model $B=(X_{B},U_{B},\Phi_{B},f_{B},\delta_{B})$ is defined in the same way. A fixed correspondence aligns
the observable decisions of the two models. Their comparison projects out the auxiliary variables.
The projected feasible region and objective graph are

 $F_{A}^{X}=\{x:\exists u_{A}\ \Phi_{A}(x,u_{A})\},\qquad G_{A}^{X}=\{(x,z):\exists u_{A}\ \Phi_{A}(x,u_{A})\land z=f_{A}(x,u_{A})\}.$ 

(1)

When $G_{A}^{X}$ is single-valued, $v_{A}(x)$ denotes the unique objective value associated with $x\in F_{A}^{X}$ .The relation $x\succ_{A}y$ 
denotes a strict improvement according to $\delta_{A}$ , and $x\succeq_{A}y$ also permits equal
values. Therefore we define semantic model agreement as

 $A\equiv_{\rm sem}B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(F_{A}^{X},\succ_{A})=(F_{B}^{X},\succ_{B}).$ 

(2)

This relation preserves feasible decisions and their preference order. Absolute objective values are
checked separately. Source satisfaction requires a feasible model that entails every applicable
requirement in a nonempty admitted $C$ . Untranslated source units retain an undecided source
verdict rather than a vacuous certificate. The report $R$ passes when its returned decision is feasible,
correctly valued and optimal. Instance parameters are substituted before comparison, and partial
reports use existential completion over unreported coordinates.

The task is to return matched artifacts and evidence for each required relation. A mathematical model
can supply a missing implementation, a captured solver model can supply missing mathematics, and a
source counterexample can expose an omission shared by both. Every decided relation is certified or
accompanied by a concrete refuting assignment.

## 4.1. Model representation and bidirectional construction

Mathematical translation. The translator $\mathcal{L}$ lowers admitted mathematical formulation into the model
representation $A$ defined in Section 3. Structured declarations, solver objects, LP/MPS files provide
variables, contraints and objectives,
while mathematical prose is transcribed together with links to the source text from which each element is
derived.
Finite decimal coefficients are represented as exact rationals. The resulting representation explicitly records variable bounds and domains, contraints, and contraint rows. Appendix A.2 presents the detailed input formats.

Executable model and correspondence. ORSMT aligns the observable
decisions $X_{A}$ and $X_{B}$ of A and B through a one-to-one correspondence based on normalized scalar
or index-tuple identifiers, variable roles, and types. Auxiliary variables $U_{A}$ and $U_{B}$ are not matched directly: they are projected out when comparing the model objects in
Eq. (1). A contradiction, type mismatch, or unmatched observable decision prevents
a syntactic correspondence from being accepted and leaves the corresponding relation Undecided.
This separation allows semantically equivalent models to use different auxiliary constructions or
redundant constraints without requiring a one-to-one match of all solver variables.

Target and normalization.
ORSMT supports reconstruction in both directions. When the mathematical model $A$ is available, ORSMT uses it as the primary construction target;
otherwise, it uses the captured executable model $B=\mathcal{H}(\widehat{P})$ as the target for reconstructing missing mathematical
artifacts.
Before construction, ORSMT applies a normalization operator ( $\mathcal{N}$ ) that preserves
the projected feasible region and objective graph relevant to the observable decisions. The
normalization may make implicit domains explicit and derive redundant constraints or auxiliary
definitions needed for compilation, while preserving the semantic model objects used for
verification. The resulting normalized representation is then used as the construction target.

Mathematics to program.
For the mathematical target $T=A$ , ORSMT computes

 $\widetilde{A}=T,\qquad\widetilde{P}=\operatorname{Compile}(\mathcal{N}(T)),\qquad\widetilde{B}=\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{P}),\qquad\widetilde{R}=\operatorname{Solve}(\widetilde{P}).$ 

(3)

The generated solver code declares the normalized model and reports its
decision, objective value, and status. ORSMT then captures the instantiated executable model
( $\widetilde{B}$ ) from the generated program and verifies its semantic agreement with
( $\widetilde{A}$ ), together with the correctness of the returned report ( $\widetilde{R}$ ).
Consequently, this direction can be used either to synthesize missing solver code from a mathematical
model or to repair existing code that fails to match the available formulation.

Program to mathematics. When the executable model is available but the mathematical formulation is missing or incomplete,
ORSMT sets (T=B). It exports the domains, bounds, constraints, objective, and optimization direction
of B into a mathematical representation ( $\widetilde{A}$ ), and uses this representation as the
construction target. The resulting artifacts are compiled, executed, and captured as in Eq. (3), after which the same verification queries are applied to certify their
correspondence. Thus, either representation can serve as the construction source, while both
directions return to the same semantic verification procedure.

Proposition 1: Artifact Construction. If the normalization operator $\mathcal{N}$ preserves the projected feasible region and
objective graph of its input, and that $\mathcal{H}$ faithfully captures the model compiled from the
normalized representation,
Eq. (3) constructs artifacts whose projected model semantics
agree with the construction target, up to the fixed correspondence of observable decisions.
Appendix B gives the full proof and the guarantees for
constraint reduction.

## 4.2. Verification queries and diagnosis

Feasible regions. ORSMT encodes domains and rows as exact arithmetic formulas and
computes the two directional violation queries

 $Q_{A\setminus B}=\operatorname{SAT}(F_{A}^{X}\land\neg F_{B}^{X}),\qquad Q_{B\setminus A}=\operatorname{SAT}(F_{B}^{X}\land\neg F_{A}^{X}).$ 

(4)

A witness in $A\setminus B$ identifies a mathematical decision excluded by the implementation.
A witness in $B\setminus A$ identifies an implemented decision excluded by the mathematics.
Unsatisfiability in both directions establishes equality of the projected
feasible regions which includes the case where both regions are empty.
Therefore, model feasibility is checked separately before certifying semantic agreement.

Verdict and diagnosis.
Each verification query returns either a certificate or a counterexample. A satisfiable violation query refutes the corresponding relation and returns a concrete witness, whereas an unsatisfiable query certifies that the relation holds. After substituting instance parameters, ORSMT extracts the implicated constraints, domains, and linked source texts from the witness and, when applicable, the unsatisfiable core. Objective diagnoses additionally record the evaluated preference relation, while source diagnoses identify the violated requirement and its applicable scope. The resulting diagnostic payload is used for deterministic reconstruction or counterexample-guided language-model revision.

Objective relations.
For decisions that are feasible in both models, ORSMT verifies whether the two objectives induce the same preference ordering. It decides the directional violation query:

 $\begin{split}Q^{\rm pref}=\operatorname{SAT}\bigl(&x,y\in F_{A}^{X}\cap F_{B}^{X}\land\\
&[(x\succ_{A}y\land y\succeq_{B}x)\lor(x\succ_{B}y\land y\succeq_{A}x)]\bigr).\end{split}$ 

(5)

A satisfying pair exposes opposite ordering or a strict improvement that becomes a tie. Feasible
models and unsatisfiable relation queries certify Eq. (2). Absolute values use

 $Q^{\rm value}=\operatorname{SAT}\bigl(x\in F_{A}^{X}\cap F_{B}^{X}\land v_{A}(x)\neq v_{B}(x)\bigr).$ 

(6)

Unsatisfiability certifies equal values at common decisions, separately from preference agreement.

Source requirements.
Each source requirement in $C$ is represented as a guarded contract
 $(g_{k},\psi_{k},S_{k})$ , consisting of an applicability condition, a logical obligation, and its supporting source text. Variable identifiers are resolved through the typed source schema before a contract is admitted. Unresolved identifiers or untranslated source units remain undecided, and a source certificate is issued only when the admitted contract set is nonempty. For a feasible candidate, ORSMT decides

 $Q^{\rm source}_{A,k}=\operatorname{SAT}(\Phi_{A}\land g_{k}\land\neg\psi_{k}),\qquad Q^{\rm source}_{B,k}=\operatorname{SAT}(\Phi_{B}\land g_{k}\land\neg\psi_{k}).$ 

(7)

Unsatisfiability certifies that the guarded requirement is enforced whenever its applicability condition holds, whereas a satisfying assignment returns a violating decision. A shared witness on both artifacts identifies a common omission and links it to $S_{k}$ . This source relation is independent of pairwise model equivalence: two artifacts may agree semantically while violating the same source requirement.

Missing, additional and redundant constraints. Row entailment applies the same
violation test to individual mathematical and executable restrictions. A written row violated by an
implemented decision is missing from the implementation. An executable row violated by a written
decision adds a restriction. To test redundancy of a row $c$ , let $D_{A}$ collect domains and
 $K_{A}$ collect constraint rows. ORSMT asks

 $Q^{\rm redundant}_{A,c}=\operatorname{SAT}\!(D_{A}\land\bigwedge_{d\in K_{A}\setminus\{c\}}d\land\neg c).$ 

(8)

Unsatisfiability permits deletion of $c$ . Subsequent tests use the reduced model. A refuting
assignment is completed against the entire candidate before revision, and infeasible models receive
conflict diagnoses. Appendix C details the reduction and scalability.

Reported result. Let $O\subseteq X_{B}$ be the reported coordinates and $\tau\geq 0$ the
declared reporting tolerance. Let $B_{\tau}(\hat{x}_{O})$ as their tolerance box denote the corresponding tolerance box, with discrete
coordinates fixed to admissible integers; coordinates outside $O$ are existentially completed.
For minimization, ORSMT computes

 $\displaystyle Q^{\rm extend}_{\tau}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\operatorname{SAT}(\Phi_{B}\land B_{\tau}(\hat{x}_{O})),$ 

 $\displaystyle Q^{\rm bind}_{\tau}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\operatorname{SAT}(\Phi_{B}\land B_{\tau}(\hat{x}_{O})\land|f_{B}-\hat{z}|\leq\tau),$ 

(9)

 $\displaystyle Q^{\rm better}_{\tau}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\operatorname{SAT}(\Phi_{B}\land f_{B}<\hat{z}-\tau).$ 

(10)

Maximization reverses the last inequality.
Unsatisfiability of the extension and binding queries certifies, respectively, that no feasible completion matches the reported decision and value, while satisfiability provides a completion witness.
An unsatisfiable better-decision query certifies that no feasible decision improves on $\hat{z}$ beyond the declared tolerance.

## 4.3. Certificates and scalable exactness

Relation certificate.
With fixed observable correspondence, feasible models, and single-valued objective graphs, unsatisfiability of both region-difference queries in Eq. (4) and the preference query in Eq. (5) certifies $A\equiv_{\rm sem}B$ . A satisfying assignment to any query refutes the corresponding relation. The certificate covers all decisions admitted by the encoded models, while a refutation provides the separating decision or decision pair used for diagnosis. Appendix B proves this claim and the corresponding report guarantees.

Sound reduction.
Using the domain formula $D_{A}$ and active row set $K_{A}$ defined above, consider a compiled obligation $\psi$ and any $L\subseteq K_{A}$ :

 $\operatorname{UNSAT}(D_{A}\land\bigwedge_{c\in L}c\land\neg\psi)\ \Longrightarrow\ \Phi_{A}\models\psi.$ 

(11)

If the reduced query is unsatisfiable, the full violation query is also
unsatisfiable, certifying the obligation. If it is satisfiable, we test
whether its assignment can be extended to satisfy the omitted rows.
Only an extension that satisfies the full model and violates $\psi$ 
is reported as a counterexample; otherwise, the reduced query alone
does not refute the obligation.

Exact component slicing.
The bipartite row–variable incidence graph retains every connected component containing variables of $\psi$ . On a feasible base model, this slice is exact because disconnected components admit independent satisfying assignments. Smaller neighborhood slices retain the one-way guarantee in Eq. (11). Appendix B gives both proofs.

## 4.4. Revision and Selection

Revision.
Let $Y_{0}=(A,\widehat{P},R)$ be the current artifacts. Verification evidence
 $e$ records the failed relation, a counterexample when available, implicated
rows or domains, and linked source text. Given $s$ and $e$ , the reviser
proposes a program $\widehat{P}^{\prime}_{k}$ . ORSMT captures its executable model,
reconstructs the artifacts through Eq. (3), and rechecks
the required source, region, objective, and report relations.

The evidence guides revision: a decision in the mathematical region but not
the executable region must be admitted by the implementation, while a decision
only in the executable region must be excluded. Objective and source revisions
address the violated preference and linked requirement, respectively. For
source revision, plan review checks the model against the source through the
computed plan; row review checks individual requirements to localize the
mismatch.

Selection.
Only candidates passing every check required by the active policy are
eligible. Direct selection takes the first eligible candidate in a fixed
order. Certified voting groups eligible candidates by verified reported
value and selects from the largest group if at least two agree. Protected
selection also preserves the original finite result when available. If no
candidate qualifies, ORSMT retains $Y_{0}$ ; otherwise, it adopts the selected
model, program, report, and verification evidence together. Reference
objectives and statuses are used only for evaluation. Appendix B.4 gives
the complete procedure.

Proposition 2: Certified Adoption.
Every adopted candidate satisfies the checks required by its selection
policy; an undecided required check prevents adoption. Otherwise, the
original artifacts remain unchanged. Voting additionally certifies agreement
of at least two eligible candidates on the reported value, and protected
selection preserves an available original finite result.

Checks follow their prerequisites: admission and feasibility precede semantic
comparison, region agreement precedes objective preference, and report
binding precedes optimality. An undecided prerequisite leaves its dependent
check undecided.

Certificates are SMT-backed verdicts for the encoded relations, not
independently checked proof objects. They depend on translation, runtime
capture, observable correspondence, compiled source requirements, model
compilation, and the SMT backend. Unsupported translation, ambiguous
correspondence, timeout, or solver unknown yields Undecided.

## 5. Experiments

Benchmarks and protocol.
The experiments evaluate SMT checking effectiveness and cost, direct reconstruction,
revision from counterexamples, repair baselines and host integration. A correct answer passes the
benchmark or host evaluator. A model–code match means that the written mathematical model and
captured executable model satisfy the exact semantic relation. An artifact match is the corresponding
reference-blind check for host integrations. An output is correct and matched when its answer is correct
and the applicable match holds. Problems decided is the percentage of the 3,034 assigned problems that return a decision; unavailable executable models count as unresolved. Failures repaired
is the percentage of original failures that are repaired. All checks pass means a model–code match,
feasible models and a verified report. Source requirements are evaluated separately.

The twelve benchmarks contain 3,034 problems. NL4Opt, NLP4LP, MAMO, OptiBench and Text2Opt provide
the main formulation collections (Ramamonjison et al., 2022; AhmadiTeshnizi et al., 2024b; Huang et al., 2025b; Yang et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2026). IndustryOR, LogiOR, ComplexOR, OptMATH and BWOR extend the
coverage (Huang et al., 2025a; Yang et al., 2026; Xiao et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2025; Zhang, 2026).
For each of the 3,034 problems, one Qwen2.5-Instruct output at each of six sizes
(1.5B–72B) yields $3{,}034\times 6=18{,}204$ assigned outputs (Yang et al., 2024).
References comprise 3,032 numeric objectives and two
infeasibility statuses (BWOR-037 and BWOR-054) (Zhang, 2026). The controlled comparison fixes the 1,905
pre-repair refuted outputs, gives every feedback method the same Qwen2.5-14B reviser and permits
one temperature-zero revision with a 32,768-token context. A numeric
answer is correct when $|v-v^{\star}|\leq 10^{-6}\max(1,|v^{\star}|)$ .

## 6. Conclusion

ORSMT closes the verification–repair loop by combining exact certificates and concrete counterexamples with bidirectional reconstruction and rechecking every adopted artifact. Across 3,034 problems and six generator sizes (18,204 outputs), it raises the model–code match rate from 43.2% to 88.2% and the share of outputs that are both correct and matched from 25.2% to 40.2%. Among 1,905 refuted outputs, certified voting yields 50.9% correct-and-matched outputs, while one source revision followed by reconstruction restores model–code agreement for 84.4% of refuted pairs. The analysis also reveals systematic asymmetries in generated artifacts: code is unavailable nearly twice as often as mathematics and is more likely to introduce additional constraints than to omit existing ones. ORSMT decides 2,869 of 3,034 problems, remains sub-second through 99 constraints, and achieves a 29.3 $\times$ speedup from component slicing. Its gains on both host systems further support its use as a modular verification and repair layer for LLM-based optimization pipelines.

## A.2. Artifact representations and capture

Accepted declarations contain finitely many uniquely named real, integer or Boolean variables,
optional rational bounds, linear rows with relation $\leq$ , $\geq$ or equality, and an objective
with its optimization direction. Structured declarations, solver objects, LP and MPS files supply
these fields directly. Mathematical prose supplies a proposed transcription whose source text is
retained. Malformed declarations, unsupported operators and undeclared references return a typed
translation failure.

The historical panel represents each variable with a real arithmetic symbol and adds integrality
predicates for integer domains. Boolean domains intersect integrality with the bounds zero and one.
An absent bound remains unbounded, and each finite decimal denotes its exact rational value.
Its accepted declarations require a nonempty objective term list and nonempty terms in each row.
The row list itself may be empty. Region and preference comparisons use zero tolerance.

Runtime capture intercepts the optimization call, flushes pending modifications and records the
instantiated model after loops and instance expansion. Later solver calls produce separate
snapshots. Returned text, explicit objective and decisions, solver attributes and execution
exceptions occupy separate fields. The frozen model comparison aligns all declared variables by
unique normalized names, with no hidden auxiliaries. Missing or ambiguous correspondences retain
an unresolved state.

Source requirements. Each obligation records its supporting source text, quantified scope,
variable roles, exact coefficients, relation and applicability. Released reference formulations
supply formal obligations when their adapters admit them. Open natural language supplies proposed
obligations for review. Reference conventions such as symmetry constraints retain their provenance.
Published objective values and status labels remain separate and enter only scoring. When an
adapter admits a released formulation as a source contract, the resulting statement only checks that
contract. It does not independently recover the author’s intent.

Exact reconstruction. The compiler emits domains, rows, objective and direction from the
selected model. A bounded product $y=bx$ , where $b$ is Boolean and $L\leq x\leq U$ , has the exact
reformulation

 $Lb\leq y\leq Ub,\qquad x-U(1-b)\leq y\leq x-L(1-b).$ 

Bounded integer products use binary expansion. Added variables are existentially projected for
comparison. Sequential redundancy deletion retains the model after each proved deletion, and a
final relation query checks the reconstructed pair.

## Appendix B Formal Guarantees and Complete Procedures

This section establishes the certificate guarantees used by verification, slicing, reconstruction
and adoption. It then gives the complete procedures that instantiate those guarantees.

## B.1. Relation certificates

Proof of the relation certificate. The two unsatisfiable difference queries establish both inclusions and equality
of the projected feasible regions. The preference query excludes any common decision pair ordered
strictly by one model and tied or oppositely ordered by the other. Their strict preference relations
are equal. A satisfying query supplies the separating point or decision pair directly.

A feasible model entails a compiled source obligation when its violation formula is unsatisfiable.
The returned result queries use the model, report and tolerance from Eqs. (9)
and (10). Extension and value binding are existential feasibility checks. The absence
of a better decision certifies optimality to the declared margin. An empty model receives its
separate infeasibility outcome.

## B.2. Constraint reduction and witness completion

Proof of sound reduction. The full violation formula implies the reduced formula. The
reduced formula’s unsatisfiability therefore implies the full formula’s unsatisfiability. A complete
satisfying assignment lies in the candidate feasible region and outside the obligation. A partial
satisfying assignment is extended and checked against the full model before it supplies this witness.

Proof of exact component slicing. Omitted components share no variables with retained
components or the obligation. Base feasibility supplies a satisfying assignment in every omitted
component. Combining these assignments extends every satisfying retained assignment to the full
model. The reverse implication follows by restricting a full assignment to retained variables.

Neighborhood slices smaller than the component use Eq. (11) and full witness completion.
For a target with all variables observable, a directional difference decomposes into violations
of its individual rows and domains. Hidden auxiliaries retain the complete projected formula.
The evidence record stores the assignment, violated rows and relation used by the revision
operator in Section 4.4.

## B.3. Reconstruction and adoption

Proof of Proposition 1. Let $T$ be the selected target and
 $\mathcal{N}(T)$ its normalized representation. Preservation of the projected region and objective
graph gives $F_{\mathcal{N}(T)}^{X}=F_{T}^{X}$ and $G_{\mathcal{N}(T)}^{X}=G_{T}^{X}$ . Compilation constructs
the normalized tuple, and runtime capture recovers it from the generated program. Hence

 $F_{\widetilde{B}}^{X}=F_{\mathcal{N}(T)}^{X}=F_{T}^{X},\qquad G_{\widetilde{B}}^{X}=G_{\mathcal{N}(T)}^{X}=G_{T}^{X}.$ 

The exported mathematical model is $\widetilde{A}=T$ . Its region and objective graph agree with
those of the captured implementation. The final queries check both region inclusions and the
objective relation for these constructed artifacts.

Sequential redundancy deletion preserves equivalence by induction. At each step the remaining
rows entail the deleted row, and adding or removing that entailed row preserves the feasible region.
The objective is unchanged. Each new deletion uses the currently retained basis.

The adoption loop accepts a candidate only after its required relation checks succeed. After an
accepted construction, a failed proposal retains that certified pair and its evidence. Before such
acceptance, the return value retains the original verdicts. Source interpretation follows the compiled
requirements and retained provenance of the selected target.

## D.3. LLM feedback, ablations and paired repair

Protected selection retains every finite original result. It reconstructs a paired artifact around
that result and applies value changing revision when the original has no finite value. The
mathematical artifact is the default reconstruction target, and the captured program supplies the
target when the mathematical artifact is unavailable. Every route records the selected target.

The fixed 14B reviser receives the problem statement and evidence for its repair condition. All
single candidate revision calls use temperature zero with a 32,768 token context. Program, model, requirement
and judgment allowances are 3,072, 4,096, 8,192 and 800 tokens, respectively. Native optimization
receives 30 seconds, rational reconstruction five seconds, and final checks ten seconds per query
with 120 seconds per relation. The five candidate policy groups certified finite values by exact
equality, select the largest group and break ties by the fixed candidate order. Reference values enter
scoring after selection.

The matched experiment compares direct revision, which receives only a generic mismatch notice,

assignment. Both conditions use the same system instruction, problem statement, written model,
previous program, reviser and decoding budget. Bracketed fields below are filled from the stored

Comparison-feedback prompt variants

Thus the comparison requests differ in verifier feedback rather than in the task or output format.
Every returned program is recaptured and checked before it can be selected.

The implementation serializes at most 6,000 characters of the written model and 8,000 characters of

after which the complete returned program is executed, recaptured and checked.

## D.7. Source requirements after repair

An artifact match measures agreement between the generated mathematical and executable models;
source requirement checks separately measure whether that shared formulation contains the admitted
requirements from the original problem.
Two separate cohorts evaluate source requirements and revision. The 654-pair audit admits 4,614 typed
obligations and yields 293 complete certificates, 54 explicit-source refutations, 75 symmetry-only

candidates and the 231 adopted row revisions, rerunning original and proposed programs.

## D.7.1. Prompts for source review and revision

Both reviews receive the original problem, the checked model and its computed optimal plan. The plan
review asks for one overall requirement-by-requirement judgment. The row review makes the same task
explicit for every constraint, variable declaration and objective term. A flagged output is revised
from the reported findings and then reviewed again. The computed plan is evidence about the candidate
model; no benchmark answer or reference objective is included.

Prompts for plan review, row review and source revision

Common system: You are an operations-research modeling expert.
Inputs to both reviews: [problem statement] [certified mathematical model] [computed
objective value] [up to 200 nonzero variables in the computed optimal plan]
Plan review instruction: Check the model against the problem statement requirement by requirement:
every data value and coefficient, every bound and integrality condition, every stated constraint,
and the objective and its direction. Use the supplied optimal plan to test the requirements it
touches. Return exactly NO_VIOLATION when all requirements are represented; otherwise list
each misstated or omitted requirement, quoting its sentence and naming the variables involved. Do
not write code.
Row review instruction: For each constraint, quote the sentence it implements and compare every
coefficient and right-hand side. Check every variable type and bound, list each source requirement
implemented by no row, and compare the objective coefficients and direction. Mark discrepancies as
MISMATCH or MISSING, then end with VERDICT: NO_VIOLATION or
VERDICT: VIOLATION. Do not write code.
Revision inputs: [problem statement] [previous program] and one of [irreducible infeasible
rows and bounds], [unbounded model], or [certified model, computed plan and review findings].
Revision instruction: The problem statement is the implementation target. Correct the
program so that it implements the problem statement. Return complete corrected code only.

Both reviews use temperature zero. Plan review allows 1,024 output tokens and row review allows
2,048; revision allows 3,072. The plan field contains at most 200 nonzero variables. Plan review is
flagged unless its reply begins with NO_VIOLATION. Row review uses the final explicit
VERDICT; if that line is absent, the presence of MISMATCH or MISSING
flags the output. At most 40 flagged lines are passed to revision, with the final 1,500 characters
used as a fallback when no such line is present. These parsing rules are fixed before outcome scoring.
