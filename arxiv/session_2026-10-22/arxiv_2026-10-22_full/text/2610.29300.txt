## When Are We Near Nash?

Certifying Solutions in General-Sum Games

Boning Li

Affiliation: Institute for Interdisciplinary Information Sciences, Tsinghua University

 Correspondence: longbohuang@tsinghua.edu.cn

Longbo Huang

October 21, 2026

Abstract

Heuristic solvers for general-sum extensive-form games produce a stream of
iterates, and practice reports the NashGap of the returned profile.
A recent meta-learning benchmark reports the lowest NashGap along the
training trajectory, located by exact evaluation at every checkpoint, without
stating the selection rule or the evaluation budget behind that number. We ask
what a solver can know about its own iterates from the regret accumulators and
the terminal-distribution accumulators its updates already maintain.
In two-player games the NashGap of a weighted average strategy
equals, at the maximizing player, the weighted external regret plus a
signed value correction.
Bounding the regret by
the running counterfactual-regret accumulator gives an online certificate
whose cost per checkpoint is linear in the number of terminal histories.
The certificate stays sound under alternating updates and in games with three
or more players. The mismatch between what
alternation evaluates and what the averages record enters as one signed
comparator term, maximized in a single backward pass per player whose
comparator weight is nonzero. The bound is
read off the regret state, with no best response against the average. Run a
portfolio of training-free regret-matching solvers, output the iterate with the
smallest certificate, and verify that iterate once.
On the benchmark’s biased Leduc family a pool of sixteen training-free solvers,
seven original and nine recent variants, solves $89\%$ of the $64$ instances
at threshold $10^{-5}$ this way, matching the trajectory-minimum union of the
solvers. The same protocol solves $92\%$ of the $64$ pre-registered held-out
instances and $89\%$ again on a third set of $64$ instances that no earlier
stage used. The zero-best-response certificate certifies all $57$ in-sample
solves and all $57$ fresh ones, and $58$ of the $59$ held-out ones. Its
exact-comparator refinement certifies every solve in the three sets. At the
selected iterate the refinement tracks the true gap within a median factor of
 $1.4$ over the $36$ instances whose selected gap is above $10^{-12}$ . The
meta-trained method reports $96\%$ under the trajectory-minimum protocol.
That protocol leaves its selection rule and evaluation budget unstated. The
certificate states both and attributes its bound. Code is available at
https://github.com/lbn187/CertNE.

## 1. Introduction

Computing a Nash equilibrium of a general-sum game to precision
inverse-polynomial in the game size is PPAD-complete (Polynomial Parity
Arguments on Directed graphs), for two players
(Chen et al., 2009) and for three or more players
(Daskalakis et al., 2009). The no-regret dynamics
that solve two-player zero-sum games at scale
(Zinkevich et al., 2007; Tammelin, 2014; Li and Huang, 2026d; Li and Huang, 2026f)
guarantee only
coarse correlated equilibria in general (Anagnostides et al., 2022). Practice
therefore runs heuristic solvers and judges them afterwards by their
NashGap, the largest gain any player could obtain by deviating alone
(Lisỳ and Bowling, 2017; Davis et al., 2014).

Computing that gap exactly costs one best-response pass per player over the
game tree, the same order as one iteration for a tabular solver
(Table 4). The certificate below builds a bound from the
solver’s own regret state, without a best response. Under aligned two-player
play, where every accumulator refers to the same start-of-round profile, an
exact evaluator that also records the running realized value recovers the
same regret and value terms from its best-response values. Under alternation,
or with three or more players, the certificate adds a comparator term, the
signed functional of Section 5, which an exact evaluator
does not compute. With that term the raw regret state is a bound again.

A recent benchmark shows why the scoring protocol matters. Neural predictive
counterfactual regret minimization+ (NPCFR+)
(Sychrovský et al., 2026) meta-learns a predictive bias. On
biased two-player Leduc games it reports solving $96\%$ of instances to
NashGap $10^{-5}$ ; its untrained counterpart PCFR+ reports $16\%$ . The
metric is the lowest NashGap along the training trajectory, located
by exact evaluation at roughly $19$ checkpoints over $2^{18}$ iterations. The
reported number is therefore the output of a selection rule, the argmin of
the exact gap, and an evaluation budget of one gap per checkpoint. Neither
is part of the method or stated with the result
(Section 4). The quantity the same paper proposes for
bounding the gap without a best response combines a regret term with a
Pinsker-type correlation term. It is the natural candidate for such a rule,
and we find it sits three orders of magnitude above the true gap and grows.

We give an online NashGap certificate computed from the regret accumulators
and the terminal accumulators, together with an
account of which quantities the gap actually depends on. In two-player
games the correlation term is avoidable. The comparator term is
exact by bilinearity, and the value term becomes exact once the
distribution exchange is replaced by directly measurable quantities. The
inequality therefore becomes an identity (Theorem 4).
The NashGap of the weighted average strategy equals the weighted
external regret plus a signed value correction. Bounding the regret by the
running counterfactual-regret accumulator (Lemma 1)
gives a certificate whose per-checkpoint cost is linear in the number of
terminal histories.

The strongest solvers update players in alternation
(Tammelin, 2014; Brown and Sandholm, 2019; Burch et al., 2019). The regrets they
evaluate then refer to a partially updated opponent, while the averages
record the start-of-round profile. A second regret pass per iteration is
the usual remedy. The mismatch itself is one more measurable comparator
term, a signed linear functional maximized in one backward pass per player
with nonzero comparator weight, so no second regret pass is needed. The
certificate is therefore sound for any update
order and any number of players (Proposition 9). In
games with three or more players the same term is the coalition imbalance
that the correlation level is structurally blind to
(Remark 7). No bound on the coalition term vanishes with
the correlation level, so the Pinsker-type route fails beyond two players;
the comparator term measures it directly. The
certificate splits its bound into a regret term, a value correction and
comparator terms (Table 5).

The certificate turns the post-hoc protocol into an algorithm
(Figure 1). Run a portfolio of training-free
regret-matching solvers, weighted, predictive, discounted, scheduled and
stabilised variants
(Tammelin, 2014; Farina et al., 2021; Brown and Sandholm, 2019; Xu et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2026; Farina et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025),
and record the certificate at every checkpoint of every solver. Output the
iterate with the smallest certificate, so the selection rule and its budget
are explicit. The zero-best-response certificate ( $C^{0}$ ) reads accumulators
alone, and its exact-comparator refinement ( $C^{1}$ ) adds one signed backward
pass per player with nonzero comparator weight at each checkpoint. On biased
Leduc this is a single pass, costing about as much wall-clock as an exact gap
evaluation and less than one training iteration. The selected iterate can be
verified exactly in one further gap evaluation (Corollary 10).

Figure 1: Two selection protocols on the same $K$ solvers and $m$ 
checkpoints per solver. a, Exact evaluation scores every candidate
with $n$ best-response (BR) passes, costing $Kmn$ passes overall, and
outputs the minimum-gap strategy with its exact gap.
b, Online certification combines signed regret, value correction,
and comparator terms (Proposition 9). $C^{0}$ uses
accumulators alone; $C^{1}$ adds one signed backward pass per player with
nonzero comparator weight at each checkpoint. Either certificate selects
its own argmin across solvers and checkpoints and bounds the selected
strategy’s NashGap. Optional verification evaluates only that strategy,
with $n$ BR passes (Corollary 10). Nodes, trees and policy
glyphs are schematic; arrows on trees indicate backward traversal.

On the benchmarks of Sychrovský et al. (2026) this protocol
matches the trajectory-minimum rule with no exact-gap query during
selection; the cost it does pay is the accumulator bookkeeping
(Table 4). On biased
Leduc ( $64$ instances), certificate selection over sixteen training-free
solvers solves $89\%$ of instances at threshold $10^{-5}$ , the same fraction
as the trajectory-minimum union over the same solvers. The seven original
solvers alone reach $84\%$ , and the sixteen-solver
pool, extended by nine recent variants in a second pre-registered stage,
reaches $92\%$ on $64$ held-out instances. On a third instance set that no earlier stage
used, the protocol repeats at $89\%$ ( $57/64$ ).

The certificate itself is below the threshold on every in-sample solve, for
 $C^{0}$ read off accumulators alone and for $C^{1}$ with one signed backward
pass per checkpoint. Across the three sets $C^{1}$ certifies every solve, and
 $C^{0}$ all but one held-out solve. At the selected iterate $C^{1}$ is within a
median $1.4\times$ of the true gap over the $36$ instances whose selected gap
is above $10^{-12}$ . The aligned start-of-round certificate pays an extra
regret pass per player per iteration yet is looser ( $1.9\times$ ) and
certifies $86\%$ . On the single deterministic three-player Leduc game,
PDCFR+ reaches NashGap $1.17\times 10^{-6}$ while the certificate-selected
HS-PCFR+ run is certified at $2.83\times 10^{-6}$ , both below the reported
 $10^{-3}$ NPCFR+ comparator. Under our protocol the training-free
portfolio reaches $0.89$ against the reported $0.96$ . The certificate is
solver-agnostic and attaches to NPCFR+ or any other update sequence
unchanged. Our $7$ in-sample failures cluster in games with a low
tie-refund fraction (Section 7).

Our contributions are:

• 

Theory. We prove that in two-player games the NashGap of a
weighted average equals its weighted external regret plus a
signed value correction, an identity, not a bound
(Theorem 4). We build from it an online certificate whose
zero-best-response form reads accumulators alone and whose exact form
costs one signed backward pass per player with nonzero comparator
weight, sound for any update order and player count
(Proposition 9). We show that in games with three or
more players no correlation level controls the comparator term
(Remark 7), and the certificate measures it
directly. One exact gap evaluation verifies the selected iterate
(Corollary 10).

• 

Algorithm. We turn the certificate into the selection
rule: score every checkpoint of every solver, output the argmin, and
report the candidate with a stated budget and no exact-gap query
during selection. The argmin comes from accumulators alone under
 $C^{0}$ ; under $C^{1}$ it costs one signed backward pass per player with
nonzero comparator weight.

• 

Re-evaluation under a stated selection rule. On the NPCFR
benchmarks, we show that training-free certificate selection reaches
 $89\%$ in-sample, matching the trajectory-minimum union over the same
solvers, $92\%$ held-out and $89\%$ on a third, never-used set,
against the reported meta-trained $96\%$ . $C^{1}$ certifies every solve
in all three sets, and $C^{0}$ certifies all but one held-out solve.
The residual in-sample failures show the same lean.

## 2. Related Work

Meta-learning and steering.
Learning-not-to-regret meta-learns regret-minimizer internals for faster
convergence (Sychrovskỳ et al., 2024), following the general
meta-learning-in-games program of Harris et al. (2023). NPCFR
(Sychrovský et al., 2026) redirects this machinery at
general-sum Nash approximation by meta-learning a predictive bias, and is
the reference point for our re-evaluation;
Sychrovský et al. (2025) extends the line to self-play. An
alternative channel steers no-regret learners toward equilibria through
external payments (Zhang et al., 2024). Our core methods RM+ and
PRM+ are standard training-free algorithms; DPRM+ is also training-free
but modifies the PRM+ prediction with an analytic decorrelation term.
The portfolio-level selection among their iterates is new.

Certificates and gap evaluation.
Theorem 1 of Sychrovský et al. (2026) bounds the NashGap by a
regret term plus a term in the extensive-form marginalizability of the
empirical play distribution, via a Pinsker-type inequality, and states it
as an analysis tool. Our
Proposition 3 is its weighted-averaging analogue, and
Theorem 4 removes the distribution inequality entirely in
the two-player case. Outside these structural certificates, obtaining a NashGap means
computing it, one exact best-response pass per player. Small equilibrium
certificates exploit tree structure to compress the strategy witness
in large games
(Zhang and Sandholm, 2020), and accelerating the best-response computation
itself has long been an engineering goal there
(Johanson et al., 2011; Li and Huang, 2026b). Exploitability is likewise the
standard for judging deployed agents
(Davis et al., 2014; Lisỳ and Bowling, 2017; Lockhart et al., 2019; Balduzzi et al., 2018; Li et al., 2026a).

Regret-based certificates.
They survive only through zero-sum structure. There the
marginals of a coarse correlated equilibrium are $2\varepsilon$ -Nash
(MacQueen, 2023), and the average’s gap is bounded by total regret at
every step (Zinkevich et al., 2007). Since $u_{2}=-u_{1}$ forces
 $\Delta_{1}=-\Delta_{2}$ , that bound is the zero-sum case of
Theorem 4, in which the two signed value corrections cancel.
In general-sum play they do not, and the theorem measures the residual from
the running realized value. Faster regret rates, such as those of the $+$ and
predictive variants (Tammelin, 2014; Farina et al., 2021), shrink the
regret term and leave that residual untouched. AIVAT (Burch et al., 2018) estimates a fixed
agent’s value without bias, and anytime PSRO (McAleer et al., 2022) gates a growing
policy population on restricted-game exploitability.
None of these gives an online, best-response-free certificate
for iterate selection during training in general-sum extensive-form games.

Regret minimization and equilibria.

Counterfactual regret minimization (CFR) decomposes external regret over
infosets (Zinkevich et al., 2007). CFR+
(Tammelin, 2014; Tammelin et al., 2015) with alternating updates
(Burch et al., 2019; Li and Huang, 2026g) and linear averaging solved heads-up limit
poker (Bowling et al., 2015). Predictive regret matching+
(Farina et al., 2021), discounted regrets and weights
(Brown and Sandholm, 2019), their predictive combination PDCFR+
(Xu et al., 2024), hyperparameter schedules for the discount and
weight exponents (Zhang et al., 2026), the smooth, stable and
extragradient stabilisations of regret matching+ (Farina et al., 2023)
and scale-invariant IR-PRM+ (Zhang et al., 2025) are training-free
accelerants, and one configuration of each is in our pool. The last-iterate
linear convergence of the scale-invariant step is analyzed by
Li and Huang (2026h). All of
these guarantees concern regret, which
in general-sum games yields only (coarse) correlated equilibria
(Anagnostides et al., 2022; Celli et al., 2019). Even averaging with an
explicit approximation guarantee, as under fictitious play
(Conitzer, 2009), works only in restricted settings. Monte Carlo variants
(Lanctot et al., 2009; Li et al., 2026b) trade these exact terminal
distributions for sampling. Abstraction and pruning
(Li et al., 2024; Li and Huang, 2025; Li and Huang, 2026a; Li and Huang, 2026c) are a
complementary route to scale in
large games (Appendix F).

Portfolios and algorithm selection.
Running several solvers and selecting among their outputs is classical in
algorithm selection (Rice, 1976; Gomes and Selman, 2001; Xu et al., 2008).
Here the selection signal is a certificate with a soundness guarantee,
so the selected iterate comes with
a bound on its NashGap.
The task differs from optimizing a strategy portfolio, which is already NP-hard
in zero-sum games (Drabent et al., 2025), and from solver-dependent selection
among equal-value equilibria (Leal, 2026). Ours is a portfolio of no-regret
solvers, and the chosen iterate is scored by the certificate.

## 3. Preliminaries

Extensive-form games and sequence form.
We consider finite $n$ -player perfect-recall extensive-form games (EFGs).
Each has terminal set $\mathcal{Z}$ , information sets (infosets) $\mathcal{I}_{i}$ that group each
player’s decision points by what that player observes there, at most $A$ 
actions per infoset, and fixed chance reach probability $p_{c}(z)$ . Utilities are
 $u_{i}\colon\mathcal{Z}\to[-M,M]$ , where
 $M\coloneqq\max_{i,z}|u_{i}(z)|$ . In the sequence-form
representation (Koller et al., 1994; Koller et al., 1996),
player $i$ ’s strategy is a realization plan $x_{i}$ in a convex polytope
 $\mathcal{X}_{i}$ ; we write $x_{i}(z)\in[0,1]$ for the realization weight of player
 $i$ ’s last sequence on the path to $z$ . A profile
 $\sigma=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})$ induces the terminal distribution

 $d_{\sigma}(z)\;=\;p_{c}(z)\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}(z),\qquad u_{i}(\sigma)\;=\;\langle d_{\sigma},u_{i}\rangle\;=\;\textstyle\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}d_{\sigma}(z)\,u_{i}(z).$ 

(1)

Here and below $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the Euclidean inner product on
 $\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{Z}|}$ . The map $x_{i}\mapsto u_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})$ is linear in
each player’s realization plan separately, by multilinearity.

NashGap.
For a profile $\sigma$ ,

 $\mathrm{NashGap}(\sigma)\;=\;\max_{i}\;\Big[\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}u_{i}\big(x^{\prime},\sigma_{-i}\big)-u_{i}(\sigma)\Big],$ 

(2)

so $\sigma$ is an $\varepsilon$ -Nash equilibrium iff $\mathrm{NashGap}(\sigma)\leq\varepsilon$ .
Evaluating $\mathrm{NashGap}$ exactly requires one best-response (BR) computation per
player, i.e. $n$ backward passes over the game tree. Computing an
 $\varepsilon$ -Nash equilibrium of a general-sum game, for $\varepsilon$ inverse-polynomial
in the game size, is PPAD-complete
(Chen et al., 2009; Daskalakis et al., 2009). Regret-minimization
dynamics guarantee convergence only to coarse correlated equilibria, joint
play distributions that no player improves on by deviating before play
begins, in general (Anagnostides et al., 2022).

Weighted iterates and online accumulators.
An iterative solver produces profiles
 $\sigma^{t}=(x_{1}^{t},\dots,x_{n}^{t})$ , $t=1,\dots,T$ . Fix nonnegative
weights $w_{t}\geq 0$ with $W_{T}\coloneqq\sum_{t\leq T}w_{t}>0$ (we use
 $w_{t}=t^{p}$ , with $p=0$ uniform, $p=1$ linear and $p=2$ quadratic
(Tammelin, 2014; Brown and Sandholm, 2019)), and let
 $\rho_{t}=w_{t}/W_{T}$ . Define the following quantities.

• 

the weighted average strategy
 $\bar{x}_{i}=\sum_{t}\rho_{t}x_{i}^{t}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}$ , a realization plan by
convexity of $\mathcal{X}_{i}$ . Normalizing per infoset gives the behavioral
profile $\bar{\sigma}$ , whose terminal distribution
 $d_{\bar{\sigma}}=p_{c}\prod_{i}\bar{x}_{i}\eqqcolon\bar{\mu}$ equals that of
 $\bar{x}$ by realization equivalence. The behavioral profile and the
realization plan are distinct objects whenever player $i$ acts at
multiple infosets on a path, but they induce the same terminal
distribution.

• 

the weighted empirical terminal distribution
 $\bar{d}(z)=\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\,p_{c}(z)\prod_{i}x_{i}^{t}(z)$ , i.e. the
distribution of play when a round $t\sim\rho$ is drawn and all
players jointly use $\sigma^{t}$ ;

• 

the weighted external regret
 $\bar{R}_{i}=\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\big[u_{i}(x^{\prime},\sigma^{t}_{-i})-u_{i}(\sigma^{t})\big]$ ;

• 

the correlation level, measured by the Kullback–Leibler
divergence
 $\varepsilon\,\coloneqq\,\mathrm{KL}\!\big(\bar{d}\,\|\,\bar{\mu}\big)$ between the
empirical joint terminal distribution and the independent-play
distribution $\bar{\mu}=p_{c}\prod_{i}\bar{x}_{i}$ induced by the average
realization plans. It is
the weighted analogue of $\varepsilon$ -extensive-form marginalizability
( $\varepsilon$ -EFM) in Sychrovský et al. (2026), finite since
 $\bar{d}\ll\bar{\mu}$ .

Counterfactual values and regrets.
For an infoset $I\in\mathcal{I}_{i}$ and action $a\in A(I)$ , the
counterfactual action value at iteration $t$ is

 $v_{i}^{t}(I,a)\;=\;\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}(I,a)}p_{c}(z)\,x_{-i}^{t}(z)\,\pi_{i}^{t}(z\mid Ia)\,u_{i}(z),$ 

(3)

where $\mathcal{Z}(I,a)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}$ collects the terminals reached through $I$ 
after taking $a$ , $x_{-i}^{t}(z)=\prod_{j\neq i}x_{j}^{t}(z)$ is the
opponents’ realization weight on the path to $z$ , and
 $\pi_{i}^{t}(z\mid Ia)$ is the product of player $i$ ’s behavioral
probabilities from $Ia$ to $z$ . The counterfactual convention therefore
excludes own reach before $I$ and includes own continuation
after the action, as in
Zinkevich et al. (2007, Theorem 3). The own reach
 $\pi_{i}^{x^{\prime}}(I)\in[0,1]$ of a realization plan $x^{\prime}$ is the product of its
behavioral probabilities along the path to $I$ . The instantaneous
counterfactual regret of $a$ at $I$ against the current play is
 $ir_{i}^{t}(I,a)\;=\;v_{i}^{t}(I,a)-\sum_{a^{\prime}\in A(I)}\sigma^{t}(a^{\prime}\mid I)\,v_{i}^{t}(I,a^{\prime}).$ 

These are byproducts of a counterfactual-regret-minimization (CFR) style
loop (Zinkevich et al., 2007). The accumulators $\bar{x}$ and
 $\bar{d}$ and the cumulative counterfactual regrets are running sums updated
in $O(|\mathcal{Z}|)$ 
per iteration, and $\bar{R}_{i}$ is bounded online without a best response
(Lemma 1). Averaging destroys independence across
players, so $\bar{d}\neq\bar{\mu}$ in general, and that distinction separates
Nash from coarse correlated play (Anagnostides et al., 2022).

Regret matching variants.
A $+$ superscript denotes the regret-matching+ step
(Tammelin, 2014), which floors the accumulated regret at zero
before matching. We reserve CFR+/PCFR+/NPCFR+ for the reference
study’s nomenclature, where PCFR+ is predictive CFR+ and NPCFR+
its meta-learned counterpart. Our solvers are RM+, the core of CFR+
(Hart and Mas-Colell, 2000; Tammelin, 2014; Tammelin et al., 2015), PRM+,
predictive RM+ (Farina et al., 2021), and DPRM+, PRM+ with the
analytic
decorrelation term of Appendix E, all with simultaneous or
alternating updates (Burch et al., 2019) and weight exponent
 $p\in\{0,1,2\}$ . The nine solvers added in the second stage keep their
published names. DCFR (Brown and Sandholm, 2019), DCFR+ and PDCFR+
(Xu et al., 2024), HS-PCFR+ and HS-DCFR (Zhang et al., 2026),
Smooth PRM+, Stable PRM+ and ExRM+ (Farina et al., 2023), and
IR-PRM+ (Zhang et al., 2025) are training-free, as are the original
seven.

## 4. The Oracle Gap in Trajectory-Minimum Evaluation

The protocol and why it matters.
The NPCFR benchmark studied here reports the trajectory minimum:
given a checkpoint grid $t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{m}$ , the reported score of
a run is

 $g^{\star}\;=\;\min_{k\leq m}\;\mathrm{NashGap}\big(\bar{\sigma}^{\,t_{k}}\big),$ 

(4)

and a game counts as “solved” if $g^{\star}\leq 10^{-5}$ 
(Sychrovský et al., 2026). The original study uses checkpoints
at $2^{k}$ (about $19$ evaluations through $T=2^{18}$ on biased Leduc); our
experiments use a $128$ -point dense grid for selection and diagnostics. Locating either grid’s minimizer requires an exact gap
evaluation ( $n$ best-response passes) at every checkpoint, so $g^{\star}$ 
is the output of a selection rule, a running argmin of the exact gap,
that is implementable online but is neither part of the method nor stated
with the result. Two consequences
follow. First, post-hoc selection. The directly available outputs of a run
are the final average or an iterate chosen by an online rule. For
plus-family algorithms the trajectory of $\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma}^{\,t})$ is not
monotone (on the benchmark of Section 7, our
PRM+ control routinely reaches its minimum and then rebounds by an order of
magnitude), so $g^{\star}$ can be far below the final average a run
returns. Obtaining it takes the same-grid exact selector of
Section 7. Second, misalignment. The protocol compares trajectory
optima, which a run yields only by spending $m$ exact gap queries and which
its reported output, the final average, does not attain. Any conclusion of
the form “method A solves $X\%$ of games” silently credits every method
with those queries.

What a certificate should do.
We require the selection rule to be algorithmic: an online quantity
 $C_{t}$ computable from the run itself such that
(i) soundness: $C_{t}\geq\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma}^{\,t})$ , so certifying
 $C_{t}\leq\varepsilon$ proves the iterate is an $\varepsilon$ -Nash equilibrium;
(ii) no best response against the reported profile: $C^{0}$ reads
the regret accumulators and the terminal-distribution accumulators kept
alongside the updates, and $C^{1}$ adds one signed
backward pass per player with nonzero weight, so a checkpoint of either is a
fraction of one training
iteration; (iii) tightness: $C_{t}$ tracks
 $\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma}^{\,t})$ within a small factor on the trajectories at hand, so that
 $\arg\min_{t}C_{t}$ preserves the solved-instance decisions of the exact
selector at the thresholds tested; and
(iv) comparability: certificates of different runs are mutually
comparable, enabling portfolio-level selection. Section 5
constructs such a certificate and shows that requirement (iii) fails for the
natural Pinsker-based candidate, whose correlation term sits three orders
of magnitude above the true gap. It holds empirically for the exact
reformulation. The certificate tracks the gap within a median $1.27\times$ 
over the $127{,}849$ biased-Leduc checkpoints whose gap is above $10^{-12}$ ,
and within $1.4\times$ at the selected
iterate over the $36$ instances whose selected gap is above $10^{-12}$ .
Soundness (i) is the theorem.

## 5. Online Certificates from an Exact Decomposition

All statements below are made only at checkpoints with $W_{T}>0$ ; throughout,
a bound $B\geq\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})$ is sound, and it is tight to a
factor $c$ when $B\leq c\,\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})$ . No multiplicative tightness holds
for all games (Appendix A.9). Then $\bar{\sigma}$ , $\bar{d}$ , $\bar{\mu}$ , $\bar{R}_{i}$ , and
 $\varepsilon=\mathrm{KL}(\bar{d}\|\bar{\mu})$ are the normalized weighted quantities of
Section 3, accumulated through $T$ , and we suppress $T$ in
the notation.
Proofs not given inline are in Appendix A.
All soundness statements are in exact arithmetic. The experiments evaluate
the bounds in double precision without outward rounding, so a “certified”
count means that the computed bound is below the threshold;
Section 7 reports the smallest computed certificate-minus-gap
residual and the smallest threshold margin on each instance set.

The weighted external regret $\bar{R}_{i}$ involves a maximization over
 $\mathcal{X}_{i}$ and is not observable without a best response; the
counterfactual-regret decomposition bounds it online.

Lemma 1 (Weighted regret decomposition).

Let $v_{i}^{t}(I,a)$ and $ir_{i}^{t}(I,a)$ be the counterfactual action value and
the instantaneous counterfactual regret of Section 3. Then

 $\bar{R}_{i}\;\leq\;\widehat{R}_{i}\;\coloneqq\;\frac{1}{W_{T}}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\Big[\max_{a}\sum_{t\leq T}w_{t}\,ir_{i}^{t}(I,a)\Big]^{\!+},$ 

(5)

where $[x]^{+}\coloneqq\max\{x,0\}$ .

The proof in Appendix A applies the laminar expansion of
Zinkevich et al. (2007, Theorem 3) per iteration, sums with the weights
 $w_{t}$ , and only then takes the per-infoset maximum and positive part.
 $\widehat{R}_{i}$ uses a raw signed accumulator $Q_{i}(I,a)=\sum_{t\leq T}w_{t}ir_{i}^{t}(I,a)$ of the regrets the update evaluates, kept apart from the solver’s
clipped state; the lemma holds for whatever opponent sequence those regrets
were measured against.

Lemma 2 (Distribution exchange).

For any $f\colon\mathcal{Z}\to[-M,M]$ and terminal measures $p,q$ ,
 $\;|\langle p-q,f\rangle|\leq M\,\lVert p-q\rVert_{1}$ ; if $p,q$ are
probability distributions then
 $\lVert p-q\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{2\,\mathrm{KL}(p\|q)}$ (Pinsker; Cover et al. (1991)).

The Pinsker certificate and its bottleneck.

The natural certificate is the weighted analogue of the
 $\varepsilon$ -EFM-based bound of Sychrovský et al. (2026). It combines
the two lemmas.

Proposition 3 (Pinsker certificate, $n=2$ ).

For two-player games and any nonnegative weights,

 $\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})\;\leq\;\max_{i}\bar{R}_{i}\;+\;M\sqrt{2\varepsilon}.$ 

(6)

The proof in Appendix A handles the comparator term
exactly by bilinearity and relaxes only the value term through
Lemma 2. Its
deployable form replaces $\bar{R}_{i}$ by $\widehat{R}_{i}$ 
(Lemma 1).

Proposition 3 is sound but overestimates the true NashGap
by roughly three orders of magnitude on our benchmark (median
 $1.3\times 10^{3}\times$ ). The regret term is $\sim\!1\times$ tight; the
distribution-exchange relaxation dominates, since late in training $\varepsilon\approx 10^{-9}$ 
yet $\sqrt{2\varepsilon}\approx 10^{-4.5}$ , orders above a gap of
 $10^{-6}$ – $10^{-8}$ .

An exact decomposition for two players.

The proof of Proposition 3 relaxes only the value term.
That term needs no relaxation either: it is a signed, directly measurable
quantity, and saying so turns the inequality into an identity.

Theorem 4 (Exact NashGap decomposition, $n=2$ ).

For two-player games and any nonnegative weights,

 $\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})\;=\;\max_{i}\big[\bar{R}_{i}+\Delta_{i}\big],\qquad\Delta_{i}\;\coloneqq\;\langle\bar{d},u_{i}\rangle-u_{i}(\bar{\sigma}).$ 

(7)

where $\Delta_{i}$ is the signed value correction, the difference between
player $i$ ’s weighted average
realized value and the value of the average strategy profile. It may be
negative.

Proof.

Fix $i$ and add and subtract $\sum_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(\sigma^{t})$ : the comparator
term is then handled exactly by bilinearity (valid because $n=2$ ), the
value term satisfies $\sum_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(\sigma^{t})=\langle\bar{d},u_{i}\rangle$ , and
taking $\max_{i}$ yields (7). The algebra is in
Appendix A.
∎

Corollary 5 (Online certificate, simultaneous updates, $n=2$ ).

For $n=2$ define $C^{\mathrm{tight}}\coloneqq\max_{i}\big[\widehat{R}_{i}+\Delta_{i}\big]$ .
When the raw signed regret accumulators, averages, and terminal accumulators
refer to the same profile sequence, $C^{\mathrm{tight}}\geq\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})$ .
Every ingredient is online: $\widehat{R}_{i}$ from the raw weighted accumulator
of Lemma 1, an $O(\sum_{i}|\mathcal{I}_{i}|A)$ scan;
 $\langle\bar{d},u_{i}\rangle$ from the running realized-value sum; and
 $u_{i}(\bar{\sigma})=\langle\bar{\mu},u_{i}\rangle$ from the average realization plans, $O(|\mathcal{Z}|)$ 
per checkpoint and no best response. The only slack relative to
(7) is $\widehat{R}_{i}-\bar{R}_{i}$ .

Lemma 2 gives $\Delta_{i}=\langle\bar{d}-\bar{\mu},u_{i}\rangle\leq M\lVert\bar{d}-\bar{\mu}\rVert_{1}\leq M\sqrt{2\varepsilon}$ .
So $C^{\mathrm{tight}}$ is no larger than the distribution-exchange
certificate termwise whenever both sides use the same regret proxy $\widehat{R}_{i}$ ,
 $C^{\mathrm{tight}}=\max_{i}[\widehat{R}_{i}+\Delta_{i}]\leq\max_{i}[\widehat{R}_{i}+M\sqrt{2\varepsilon}]$ .
For simultaneous two-player runs this is the deployable certificate. The
alternating updates used by the strongest solvers need one further comparator
term, supplied by Proposition 9 below, a signed linear
functional over the terminal weights that measures the mismatch between the
profile a regret was evaluated on and the profile the averages record.

The coalition term for three or more players.

For $n\geq 3$ the comparator step of Theorem 4 fails.
Indeed $\prod_{j\neq i}\bar{x}_{j}\neq\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\prod_{j\neq i}x_{j}^{t}$ in
general, because averaging destroys independence within the opposing
coalition. The deviation is again directly measurable. Define the
coalition imbalance

 $D_{-i}\;\coloneqq\;\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}p_{c}(z)\,\Big|\bar{c}_{-i}(z)\;-\;\prod_{j\neq i}\bar{x}_{j}(z)\Big|,$ 

(8)

where $\bar{c}_{-i}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\prod_{j\neq i}x_{j}^{t}(z)$ 
is maintained online by accumulating the coalition products (cost
 $O(n|\mathcal{Z}|)$ per iteration).

Proposition 6 (Coalition certificate, general $n$ ).

For any number of players and nonnegative weights,

 $\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})\;\leq\;\max_{i}\Big[\,\bar{R}_{i}+\Delta_{i}+M\,D_{-i}\,\Big],$ 

(9)

and for $n=2$ , $D_{-i}\equiv 0$ , recovering the bound of
Theorem 4.

The deployable coalition certificate is

 $C^{\mathrm{coal}}\coloneqq\max_{i}[\widehat{R}_{i}+\Delta_{i}+MD_{-i}],$ 

(10)

sound when the raw signed regret, average-strategy, empirical-terminal,
and coalition accumulators all describe the same profile sequence. The
 $\bar{R}_{i}$ form in the proposition is a theoretical bound, and the
certificate uses the measured signal.

The correlation level $\varepsilon$ is structurally blind to $D_{-i}$ , which is not a
function of $\varepsilon$ . The certificate therefore measures
 $D_{-i}$ directly. Remark 7 and
Appendix A give the details.

Remark 7 ( $\varepsilon$ is blind to the coalition term).

A bound on $D_{-i}$ that vanishes with $\varepsilon=\mathrm{KL}(\bar{d}\|\bar{\mu})$ 
cannot follow from $\varepsilon$ alone: at terminals $z$ with $\bar{x}_{i}(z)=0$ ,
both $\bar{d}(z)$ and $\bar{\mu}(z)$ vanish whatever the opposing coalition
does there, so two configurations differing only in the coalition’s
correlation at such $z$ share the same $\varepsilon$ and differ in $D_{-i}$ , while
a deviation $x^{\prime}$ can still reach $z$ . The coalition term has to be measured
directly, since the correlation level cannot bound it.
Appendix A.10 gives a three-player game with $\varepsilon=0$ ,
all regrets $0$ and $\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})=\tfrac{1}{2}$ .

The factor $M$ in Proposition 6 is the only remaining
relaxation of the comparator term, and it can be removed exactly.

Proposition 8 (Exact coalition comparator).

Define the signed imbalance weight
 $w_{i}(z)\coloneqq p_{c}(z)\,u_{i}(z)\big(\prod_{j\neq i}\bar{x}_{j}(z)-\bar{c}_{-i}(z)\big)$ 
and $L_{-i}\coloneqq\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\langle x^{\prime},w_{i}\rangle$ . Then for any
number of players and nonnegative weights,

 $\displaystyle\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\;\max_{i}\Big[\,\bar{R}_{i}+\Delta_{i}+L_{-i}\,\Big],$ 

(11)

 $\displaystyle L_{-i}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\;M\,D_{-i}.$ 

and $L_{-i}$ can be negative.

 $L_{-i}$ is one backward pass per player over the logged imbalance weight
 $w_{i}$ , a signed linear functional maximized over $\mathcal{X}_{i}$ . It also
attributes the zero-best-response certificate’s slack at the certificate-maximizing
player $i$ , $C^{\mathrm{coal}}-\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})=(\widehat{R}_{i}-\bar{R}_{i})+(MD_{-i}-L_{-i})+\xi_{i}$ , where the residual $\xi_{i}$ is the slack of splitting the maximum
over $x^{\prime}$ in the proof, less $\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})-\mathrm{gap}_{i}$ . On three-player
games the comparator relaxation
 $MD_{-i}-L_{-i}$ accounts for $96$ – $99\%$ of that slack
(Section 7).

Any update order.

The statements so far measure player $i$ ’s regret against the profile
sequence $\sigma^{t}$ that the averages use. Alternating updates
(Burch et al., 2019), which the strongest solvers rely on, evaluate
player $i$ after players $1,\dots,i-1$ have moved, so its regrets refer to
a partially updated opponent and the $\widehat{R}_{i}$ read off them need not
dominate $\bar{R}_{i}$ . A second regret pass per iteration against the
start-of-round profile is the usual remedy. The mismatch itself is one
more measurable comparator term, so no second pass is needed. Write
 $y^{(i),t}_{-i}$ for the opponent
profile against which $ir_{i}^{t}$ was computed ( $y^{(i),t}_{j}=x_{j}^{t+1}$ for
 $j<i$ and $x_{j}^{t}$ for $j>i$ under alternation; $x^{t}_{-i}$ under
simultaneous updates) and accumulate, when that regret is computed,

 $\bar{d}^{(i)}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\,p_{c}(z)\,x_{i}^{t}(z)\prod_{j\neq i}y^{(i),t}_{j}(z),\qquad\bar{c}^{(i)}_{-i}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\prod_{j\neq i}y^{(i),t}_{j}(z).$ 

(12)

Proposition 9 (Certificate along the evaluation sequence).

For any number of players, nonnegative weights, and evaluation sequences,
with $\widehat{R}_{i}$ the bound of Lemma 1 applied to the
regrets $ir_{i}^{t}$ measured at $(x_{i}^{t},y^{(i),t}_{-i})$ ,

 $\displaystyle\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\;\max_{i}\big[\,\widehat{R}_{i}+\Delta^{(i)}_{i}+L^{(i)}_{-i}\,\big],\qquad\Delta^{(i)}_{i}\coloneqq\langle\bar{d}^{(i)},u_{i}\rangle-u_{i}(\bar{\sigma}),$ 

(13)

 $\displaystyle w^{(i)}_{i}(z)$ 
 $\displaystyle\coloneqq p_{c}(z)\,u_{i}(z)\Big(\prod_{j\neq i}\bar{x}_{j}(z)-\bar{c}^{(i)}_{-i}(z)\Big),\qquad L^{(i)}_{-i}\coloneqq\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\langle x^{\prime},w^{(i)}_{i}\rangle,$ 

and $L^{(i)}_{-i}\leq\lVert w^{(i)}_{i}\rVert_{1}\leq MD^{(i)}_{-i}$ with
 $D^{(i)}_{-i}\coloneqq\sum_{z}p_{c}(z)\,|\prod_{j\neq i}\bar{x}_{j}(z)-\bar{c}^{(i)}_{-i}(z)|$ .

The proof in Appendix A is that of
Proposition 8 with $\bar{c}_{-i}$ replaced by
 $\bar{c}^{(i)}_{-i}$ . The comparator identity holds for any sequence. The
regret step is Lemma 1 on the sequence player $i$ 
was evaluated on. The value term is $\langle\bar{d}^{(i)},u_{i}\rangle$ by

Propositions 6–8. Under
two-player alternation the first mover’s weight vanishes and the second
mover’s is a drift between consecutive iterates of the first,

 $w^{(2)}_{2}=p_{c}\,u_{2}\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\big(x_{1}^{t}-x_{1}^{t+1}\big),\qquad\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\big(x_{1}^{t}-x_{1}^{t+1}\big)=\rho_{1}x_{1}^{1}+\sum_{t=2}^{T}(\rho_{t}-\rho_{t-1})\,x_{1}^{t}-\rho_{T}x_{1}^{T+1},$ 

(14)

so for nondecreasing weights the coefficients $\rho_{1}$ and
 $\rho_{t}-\rho_{t-1}$ are nonnegative and sum to $\rho_{T}$ , and
 $\sum_{t}\rho_{t}(x_{1}^{t}-x_{1}^{t+1})=\rho_{T}(\tilde{x}_{1}-x_{1}^{T+1})$ with
 $\tilde{x}_{1}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}$ a convex combination of the iterates. Hence for
every deviation $x^{\prime}$ ,
 $\langle x^{\prime},w^{(2)}_{2}\rangle=\rho_{T}\big(u_{2}(x^{\prime},\tilde{x}_{1})-u_{2}(x^{\prime},x_{1}^{T+1})\big)$ ,
so $|L^{(2)}_{-2}|\leq 2M\rho_{T}$ , which is $O((p+1)/T)$ for $w_{t}=t^{p}$ ,
the scale of one iteration’s weight, against the $O(1/\sqrt{T})$ 
worst-case regret bound of CFR (Zinkevich et al., 2007). The drift therefore decays faster than
that bound at long horizons. This is a statement about the bound, not about
the observed regret or gap. This drift is the
alternation correction that Burch et al. (2019, Theorem 1) write as a trailing
sum for zero-sum uniform averages and discard through an improved-strategy monotonicity
argument. We keep it as a measured signed term: no such monotonicity holds in
general-sum play, and at every finite $T$ the certificate reports it. Every
ingredient of (13) is a byproduct of the update: the
opponent reach $p_{c}\prod_{j\neq i}y_{j}$ is formed to evaluate $ir_{i}^{t}$ , so
 $\bar{d}^{(i)}$ and $\bar{c}^{(i)}_{-i}$ cost two $O(|\mathcal{Z}|)$ accumulations
per player per iteration and no tree pass. We call

 $C^{0}\coloneqq\max_{i}\big[\widehat{R}_{i}+\Delta^{(i)}_{i}+\lVert w^{(i)}_{i}\rVert_{1}\big],\qquad C^{1}\coloneqq\max_{i}\big[\widehat{R}_{i}+\Delta^{(i)}_{i}+L^{(i)}_{-i}\big]$ 

(15)

the zero-best-response certificate and its exact-comparator refinement.
 $C^{1}$ costs one signed backward pass per player with $w^{(i)}_{i}\neq 0$ at
each checkpoint, a single pass under two-player alternation, and never a best
response against the average. The certificate is a stated rule whose terms
attribute its bound. The same backward pass, applied to the raw regret
table and $w^{(i)}_{i}$ together, returns the exact gap
(Proposition 11,
Appendix A.7); $C^{1}$ keeps the terms apart.

Corollary 10 (One-shot best-response verification).

Consider the protocol: run $K$ solvers; freeze one stored snapshot
 $\hat{\sigma}$ using an online signal, without consulting its exact gap;
then compute one exact best response per player at that snapshot,
obtaining $\hat{g}=\mathrm{NashGap}(\hat{\sigma})$ . The pair $(\hat{\sigma},\hat{g})$ reports
its exact NashGap. The selection signal and
the verification are operationally separate, so the signal may be any online rule.
The cost is one gap evaluation of $n$ best-response passes, against one at
every checkpoint in trajectory-minimum reporting, and the result holds for
any certificate slack, update scheme and player count.

Three layers recur throughout. Layer I is the exact identity of
Theorem 4: it contains $\bar{R}_{i}$ and is an analysis statement.
Layer II is the online certificate of
Proposition 9, a theorem-backed upper bound for every
update scheme and player count. $C^{0}$ is read off the regret accumulators and
the terminal accumulators kept alongside the update, and $C^{1}$ adds one signed backward pass per player with
nonzero comparator weight at each checkpoint.
Layer III is one-shot verification, $n$ best-response passes on the
selected snapshot alone. The start-of-round aligned certificate,
which logs a second regret pass per iteration so that every accumulator
refers to $\sigma^{t}$ , is also a Layer II bound. Section 1
finds it looser than $C^{1}$ on the same trajectories
(Table 5).

Per checkpoint, the certificate’s own cost stays low. $C^{0}$ costs about
three quarters of an exact gap, and the $C^{1}$ backward pass is comparable to
one. Inside a full run these evaluations are negligible either way. The
 $128$ validation evaluations cost $19$  ms in total, about $0.02\%$ of the
run, and in situ each checkpoint costs $1.2$ – $1.7\times$ its isolated
figure. What the certificate does cost is the accumulator bookkeeping,
 $25.4$  s, which is $21.7\%$ of an instrumented run and $27.6\%$ on top of the bare
solver. $C^{0}$ is no cheaper than an exact gap, and the four certificate-state
policies sit within run-to-run variation of each other.

The aligned variant spends the portfolio budget. It adds a second regret pass
on every iteration, $0.14$  ms on a training iteration of $0.37$  ms.
The $7\times 64$ aligned grid of the original solvers is $24.4$ core-h and the
 $9\times 64$ grid of the added solvers $12.8$ core-h, $37.2$ core-h for the
 $1024$ runs in total, measured end to end on the cluster including the
diagnostic evaluations. The added solvers run at $54$ – $60$  s per $2^{18}$ 
iterations, except ExRM+ ( $161$  s, two projections per iteration) and
IR-PRM+ ( $134$  s).

Alternating updates record the $i$ -th updater’s regret against a partially
updated opponent, whereas the averages use the start-of-round profile.
Proposition 9 absorbs the mismatch into the comparator
weight $w^{(i)}_{i}$ , so $C^{0}$ and $C^{1}$ are sound without an extra regret
pass per iteration. On the two-player grid they were above the exact gap
at every one of
the $131{,}072$ checkpoints, as the theorem requires. For comparison we also
paid the aligned pass across the full portfolio. Start-of-round regret
logging on all $16\times 64$ runs (a rerun on identical trajectories for the
seven original solvers, the same run for the nine added ones) was likewise sound
at every checkpoint but certified fewer instances (Table 1).
The three-player runs report $C^{1}$ directly (Table 2).

Stage 1 document released alongside the code. Stage 1 ran the seven original
solvers with

What the comparator term does.

The aligned start-of-round certificate is sound at all $131{,}072$ 
checkpoints, and among the four certificates of Figure 2
 $C^{1}$ is the tightest at $98.5\%$ of quantile levels and never looser than
 $C^{0}$ . On the sixteen-solver pool it selects the same $57/64$ as $C^{1}$ 
but certifies $55/64$ against $57/64$ , and it is looser where the gap is
resolvable. Median tightness at the selected iterate is $1.9\times$ against
 $1.4\times$ for $C^{1}$ over the $36$ instances whose selected gap is above
 $10^{-12}$ , and over the $127{,}849$ checkpoints with a resolvable gap a
median of $1.37\times$ against $1.27\times$ (Figure 2). On the seven original solvers alone
the contrast was wider, selection $51/64$ and certification $44/64$ against
 $54/64$ and $48/64$ , with medians $2.7\times$ against $1.8\times$ at the
selected iterate and $1.75\times$ against $1.34\times$ over all checkpoints.
The term $|L^{(2)}_{-2}|$ is $0.02\times$ the true gap at the median checkpoint
and $0.31\times$ at the $90$ th percentile, and its zero-best-response bound
 $\lVert w^{(2)}_{2}\rVert_{1}$ is $0.27\times$ the gap at the median. On the
sixteen-solver pool $C^{0}$ certifies the same $57$ instances as $C^{1}$ in-sample
and one fewer held-out. The certified counts are not knife-edge. The smallest
 $C^{1}$ certificate margin at $10^{-5}$ is $4.178\times 10^{-6}$ ( $42\%$ of the
threshold) over the $57$ in-sample solves (ExRM+, seed $35$ ),
 $4.760\times 10^{-6}$ ( $48\%$ ) over the $59$ held-out solves (DPRM+), and
 $8.235\times 10^{-6}$ ( $82\%$ ) over the $57$ fresh solves (Stable PRM+).
The smallest computed $C^{1}$ -minus-gap residual over all checkpoints is
 $-4.221\times 10^{-16}$ in-sample, $8.638\times 10^{-17}$ held-out, and
 $6.077\times 10^{-16}$ on the fresh set; the negative value is double-precision
rounding noise within the $10^{-12}$ soundness gate.

Uncertainty.
All solved fractions in Table 1 are counts over the same $64$ 
instances, and the head-to-head claims rest on few discordant pairs, so the
effective unit is the instance. A Wilson $95\%$ interval on that unit puts
the sixteen-solver $C^{1}$ -selection fraction $57/64$ between $0.79$ and $0.95$ ,
and the in-sample certified counts share it. The two paired contrasts that
carry the claims are regret-only selection against $C^{1}$ on the
sixteen-solver pool ( $8$ discordant instances, McNemar exact two-sided
 $p=0.008$ ) and the certified gain of the extension ( $9$ instances
certified by the sixteen-solver pool and not by the seven-solver pool, none
the other way, $p=0.004$ ). The remaining
paired contrasts, including the seven-solver selection gain over certification
(six instances, $p=0.031$ ), rest on at most six instances each, so we
state them as counts.

## 8. Conclusion

The NashGap of a two-player weighted average admits an exact decomposition.
Its accumulator-based bound is sound for any update order and any number
of players, with the alternation mismatch entering as one signed comparator
term. The certificate turns a reported trajectory minimum into a stated
rule. Run a portfolio, score every checkpoint from quantities the updates
maintain, and output the argmin. On biased Leduc that rule matches the solvers’
trajectory-minimum union over sixteen training-free solvers, solving $89\%$ 
of instances at $10^{-5}$ in-sample, $92\%$ on pre-registered held-out
instances and $89\%$ again on a third, never-used set. It certifies every
solve in all three sets through $C^{1}$ , and all but one held-out solve
through $C^{0}$ , reading the accumulators alone. On three-player Leduc it
certifies a gap $354\times$ below the reported comparator. We prove the
identity, build the certificate that defines the selection rule, and
re-evaluate the benchmark under it. Selection costs no best-response pass, and one exact evaluation verifies the
chosen profile. Because the certificates read only the accumulators, any
regret-matching solver that maintains them can be scored. A solved fraction should report its
selection rule and its verification budget. The certificate supplies both.

## A.1. Proof of Theorem 4

Fix $i$ and add and subtract $\sum_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(\sigma^{t})$ :

 $\displaystyle\mathrm{gap}_{i}={}$ 
 $\displaystyle\Big[\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}u_{i}(x^{\prime},\bar{x}_{-i})-\sum\nolimits_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(\sigma^{t})\Big]$ 

 $\displaystyle+\Big[\sum\nolimits_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(\sigma^{t})-u_{i}(\bar{\sigma})\Big]$ 

 $\displaystyle={}$ 
 $\displaystyle\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\sum\nolimits_{t}\rho_{t}\,\big(u_{i}(x^{\prime},x_{-i}^{t})-u_{i}(\sigma^{t})\big)+\Delta_{i}$ 

 $\displaystyle={}$ 
 $\displaystyle\bar{R}_{i}+\Delta_{i},$ 

using bilinearity for the comparator term (valid because $n=2$ ) and
 $\sum_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(\sigma^{t})=\langle\bar{d},u_{i}\rangle$ for the value term. Taking
 $\max_{i}$ yields (7).

## A.2. Proof of Lemma 1

Fix player $i$ and a comparator $x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}$ ; by perfect recall we may
identify $x^{\prime}$ with behavioral probabilities $x^{\prime}(a\mid I)$ and own-reach
weights $\pi^{x^{\prime}}_{i}(I)\in[0,1]$ . Write $v_{i}^{t}$ and $ir_{i}^{t}$ for the
counterfactual action value and the instantaneous counterfactual regret of
Section 3.
The laminar (telescoping) expansion of
Zinkevich et al. (2007, Theorem 3) over player $i$ ’s infoset tree is,
for each fixed $t$ , an identity:

 $\displaystyle u_{i}(x^{\prime},\sigma_{-i}^{t})-u_{i}(\sigma^{t})$ 

(16)

 $\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\pi^{x^{\prime}}_{i}(I)\sum_{a\in A(I)}x^{\prime}(a\mid I)\,ir_{i}^{t}(I,a).$ 

(Both sides are affine in $x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}$ and linear in the leaf-payoff
vector: by the sequence-form convention
 $\pi^{x^{\prime}}_{i}(I)\,x^{\prime}(a\mid I)=x^{\prime}(Ia)$ is the realization-plan coordinate
of the child sequence $Ia$ , so the right side is
 $\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\sum_{a\in A(I)}x^{\prime}(Ia)\,ir_{i}^{t}(I,a)$ , linear in $x^{\prime}$ ;
the left side adds only the constant $-u_{i}(\sigma^{t})$ . Both sides agree on
every pure $x^{\prime}$ by direct telescoping, and the pure strategies are the
extreme points of the polytope $\mathcal{X}_{i}$ , whose convex hull is all of
 $\mathcal{X}_{i}$ , so affine linearity extends the identity to all of $\mathcal{X}_{i}$ .)
Multiply (16) by $w_{t}\geq 0$ and sum over $t$ ;
since $x^{\prime}$ does not depend on $t$ , the right side becomes
 $\sum_{I}\pi^{x^{\prime}}_{i}(I)\sum_{a}x^{\prime}(a\mid I)\,Q_{i}(I,a)$ with
 $Q_{i}(I,a)\coloneqq\sum_{t}w_{t}\,ir_{i}^{t}(I,a)$ .

Before relaxing, note what the weighted sum already is. Writing
 $x^{\prime}(Ia)=\pi^{x^{\prime}}_{i}(I)\,x^{\prime}(a\mid I)$ for the realization-plan coordinate
of the child sequence,

 $r_{i}(x^{\prime})\;\coloneqq\;\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\big(u_{i}(x^{\prime},\sigma_{-i}^{t})-u_{i}(\sigma^{t})\big)\;=\;\frac{1}{W_{T}}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\sum_{a\in A(I)}x^{\prime}(Ia)\,Q_{i}(I,a),$ 

(17)

a linear functional of $x^{\prime}$ with coefficients read off the raw table. Hence
 $\bar{R}_{i}=\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\frac{1}{W_{T}}\sum_{I,a}x^{\prime}(Ia)\,Q_{i}(I,a)$ 
exactly: the raw accumulator loses no information about the weighted
external regret. What the relaxation below trades away is the single
maximization over $\mathcal{X}_{i}$ , replaced by a sum of independent positive local
maxima.

Now bound in three steps,
in this order:

 $\displaystyle\sum_{a}x^{\prime}(a\mid I)Q_{i}(I,a)$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\max_{a}Q_{i}(I,a)$ 

 $\displaystyle\leq\big[\max_{a}Q_{i}(I,a)\big]^{+},$ 

(18)

because $x^{\prime}(\cdot\mid I)$ is a probability distribution, and then
 $\pi^{x^{\prime}}_{i}(I)\big[\max_{a}Q_{i}(I,a)\big]^{+}\leq\big[\max_{a}Q_{i}(I,a)\big]^{+}$ , which is valid because the positive
part has already made the bracket nonnegative (dropping
 $\pi^{x^{\prime}}_{i}\in[0,1]$ before taking the positive part would be unsound:
a bracket that is negative at some infoset would flip the inequality).
Summing over $I$ , dividing by $W_{T}$ , and maximizing over $x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}$ 
yields the claim. Note that the maximum is taken over the
weighted cumulative regrets $Q_{i}$ , which is exactly the structure
of $\widehat{R}_{i}$ , and that the aggregation over $t$ happens strictly before
the maximum and the positive part.
 $\square$ 

## Remark on the raw signed accumulator.

The certificate requires a separate table
 $Q(I,a)=\sum_{t}w_{t}ir_{i}^{t}(I,a)$ with no RM+ clipping. The algorithm’s
clipped internal table cannot in general provide $\widehat{R}_{i}$ . The raw table
accumulates the very regrets the update evaluates, so it costs no extra
pass and one array of $|\Sigma_{i}|$ entries per player, the same order as
the sequence-form policy it sits beside;
Proposition 9 accounts for the profile sequence
those regrets refer to.

## A.3. Proof of Proposition 3

Fix $i$ and write
 $\mathrm{gap}_{i}=\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}u_{i}(x^{\prime},\bar{x}_{-i})-u_{i}(\bar{\sigma})$ .
For the comparator term, $n=2$ means the opponent is a single player, so

exactly. For the value term,
 $u_{i}(\bar{\sigma})=\langle\bar{\mu},u_{i}\rangle\geq\langle\bar{d},u_{i}\rangle-M\sqrt{2\varepsilon}=\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\,u_{i}(\sigma^{t})-M\sqrt{2\varepsilon}$ by

## A.4. Proof of Proposition 6

For the comparator term, for any $x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}$ ,

 $\displaystyle\left|u_{i}(x^{\prime},\bar{x}_{-i})-\sum\nolimits_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(x^{\prime},x_{-i}^{t})\right|$ 

 $\displaystyle\quad=\left|\sum\nolimits_{z}p_{c}(z)x^{\prime}(z)u_{i}(z)\left(\prod\nolimits_{j\neq i}\bar{x}_{j}(z)-\bar{c}_{-i}(z)\right)\right|$ 

 $\displaystyle\quad\leq MD_{-i}.$ 

since $x^{\prime}(z)\in[0,1]$ and $|u_{i}(z)|\leq M$ ; the bound is uniform over
 $x^{\prime}$ , so it passes through the maximum. The value term is handled
exactly as in Theorem 4 (it never used $n=2$ ). Combining
the two gives $\mathrm{gap}_{i}\leq\bar{R}_{i}+MD_{-i}+\Delta_{i}$ .
 $\square$ 

## A.5. Proof of Proposition 8

The display in the proof of Proposition 6 is an
identity before any bound is taken: for every $x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}$ ,

 $\displaystyle u_{i}(x^{\prime},\bar{x}_{-i})-\sum\nolimits_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(x^{\prime},x_{-i}^{t})$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{z}x^{\prime}(z)\,w_{i}(z)$ 

 $\displaystyle=\langle x^{\prime},w_{i}\rangle.$ 

Let

 $r_{i}(x^{\prime})\coloneqq\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\big(u_{i}(x^{\prime},x_{-i}^{t})-u_{i}(\sigma^{t})\big).$ 

Adding and subtracting $\sum_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(\sigma^{t})$ as in
Theorem 4 and splitting the maximum over the sum,

 $\displaystyle\mathrm{gap}_{i}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\big[r_{i}(x^{\prime})+\langle x^{\prime},w_{i}\rangle\big]+\Delta_{i}$ 

 $\displaystyle\leq\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}r_{i}(x^{\prime})+\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\langle x^{\prime},w_{i}\rangle+\Delta_{i}$ 

 $\displaystyle=\bar{R}_{i}+L_{-i}+\Delta_{i}.$ 

Since $x^{\prime}(z)\in[0,1]$ and $|u_{i}(z)|\leq M$ ,
 $\langle x^{\prime},w_{i}\rangle\leq\sum_{z}|w_{i}(z)|\leq MD_{-i}$ for every $x^{\prime}$ , hence
 $L_{-i}\leq MD_{-i}$ . The maximum of the linear functional
 $\langle\cdot,w_{i}\rangle$ over the polytope $\mathcal{X}_{i}$ is a sequence-form
best-response computation on the signed terminal weight $w_{i}$ (one
backward pass for player $i$ ); $L_{-i}<0$ precisely when
 $\langle x^{\prime},w_{i}\rangle<0$ for every $x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}$ , for instance when every
pure plan reaches only terminals with negative $w_{i}$ . For $n=2$ ,
 $w_{i}\equiv 0$ and $L_{-i}=0$ , recovering Theorem 4.
The two inequalities of the chain are the only slack of the deployable
certificate: for the player $i$ maximizing $C^{\mathrm{coal}}$ , since
 $\Delta_{i}$ appears on both sides,

 $\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{coal}}-\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})$ 
 $\displaystyle=(\widehat{R}_{i}-\bar{R}_{i})+(MD_{-i}-L_{-i})+\xi_{i},$ 

 $\displaystyle\xi_{i}$ 
 $\displaystyle\coloneqq\Big[\bar{R}_{i}+L_{-i}-\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\big(r_{i}(x^{\prime})+\langle x^{\prime},w_{i}\rangle\big)\Big]-\big[\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})-\mathrm{gap}_{i}\big].$ 

The first bracket is the slack of splitting the maximum; it is
nonnegative and vanishes exactly when $r_{i}$ and $\langle\cdot,w_{i}\rangle$ share a
maximizer over $\mathcal{X}_{i}$ , which agreement of the maximizing player does not
imply. The second bracket is nonnegative and vanishes when $i$ also
maximizes $\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})$ .
 $\square$ 

## A.6. Proof of Proposition 9

Fix $i$ and write $\tau^{t}\coloneqq(x_{i}^{t},y^{(i),t}_{-i})$ for the
profile at which $ir_{i}^{t}$ was computed; by construction
 $\sum_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(\tau^{t})=\langle\bar{d}^{(i)},u_{i}\rangle$ and
 $\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\prod_{j\neq i}y^{(i),t}_{j}(z)=\bar{c}^{(i)}_{-i}(z)$ .
For every $x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}$ , multilinearity gives the identity

 $u_{i}(x^{\prime},\bar{x}_{-i})-\sum\nolimits_{t}\rho_{t}\,u_{i}(x^{\prime},y^{(i),t}_{-i})=\sum\nolimits_{z}p_{c}(z)\,x^{\prime}(z)\,u_{i}(z)\Big(\prod\nolimits_{j\neq i}\bar{x}_{j}(z)-\bar{c}^{(i)}_{-i}(z)\Big)=\langle x^{\prime},w^{(i)}_{i}\rangle,$ 

which uses nothing about how the sequence $y^{(i),t}$ was generated.
Adding and subtracting $\sum_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(\tau^{t})$ ,

 $\displaystyle\mathrm{gap}_{i}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\Big[\sum\nolimits_{t}\rho_{t}\big(u_{i}(x^{\prime},y^{(i),t}_{-i})-u_{i}(\tau^{t})\big)+\langle x^{\prime},w^{(i)}_{i}\rangle\Big]+\langle\bar{d}^{(i)},u_{i}\rangle-u_{i}(\bar{\sigma})$ 

 $\displaystyle\leq\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\sum\nolimits_{t}\rho_{t}\big(u_{i}(x^{\prime},y^{(i),t}_{-i})-u_{i}(\tau^{t})\big)+L^{(i)}_{-i}+\Delta^{(i)}_{i}\;\leq\;\widehat{R}_{i}+L^{(i)}_{-i}+\Delta^{(i)}_{i}.$ 

The last step is Lemma 1 applied to the sequence
 $\tau^{t}$ : the laminar identity (16) holds at each
 $\tau^{t}$ with $ir_{i}^{t}$ the counterfactual regrets computed there, and the
three relaxation steps are unchanged. The chain
 $L^{(i)}_{-i}\leq\lVert w^{(i)}_{i}\rVert_{1}\leq MD^{(i)}_{-i}$ is
 $x^{\prime}(z)\in[0,1]$ followed by $|u_{i}(z)|\leq M$ . Taking $\max_{i}$ gives
(13). For the two-player alternating drift (14),
 $\sum_{t}\rho_{t}(x^{t}-x^{t+1})$ telescopes as stated. With $\rho_{t}$ 
nondecreasing the coefficients $\rho_{1}$ and $\rho_{t}-\rho_{t-1}$ are
nonnegative and sum to $\rho_{T}$ , so $\rho_{1}x_{1}^{1}+\sum_{t\geq 2}(\rho_{t}-\rho_{t-1})x_{1}^{t}=\rho_{T}\tilde{x}_{1}$ with $\tilde{x}_{1}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}$ 
by convexity. Then $\langle x^{\prime},w^{(2)}_{2}\rangle=\rho_{T}\langle d_{(x^{\prime},\tilde{x}_{1})}-d_{(x^{\prime},x_{1}^{T+1})},u_{2}\rangle=\rho_{T}\big(u_{2}(x^{\prime},\tilde{x}_{1})-u_{2}(x^{\prime},x_{1}^{T+1})\big)\in[-2M\rho_{T},2M\rho_{T}]$ because both utilities lie in
 $[-M,M]$ . The earlier $L^{1}$ bound $\lVert w^{(2)}_{2}\rVert_{1}\leq 2M\rho_{T}\sum_{z}p_{c}(z)$ follows from the same decomposition and
 $|x_{1}(z)|\leq 1$ , but carries the factor $\sum_{z}p_{c}(z)$ that the bound on
the exact term does not need.
 $\square$ 

## A.7. The logged state determines the exact gap

The two relaxations of Proposition 9, the per-infoset
positive parts in $\widehat{R}_{i}$ and the split of one maximum into two, can both
be undone at the cost of one linear maximization per player.

Proposition 11 (Exact evaluator from the logged state, general $n$ ).

For any number of players, nonnegative weights and evaluation sequences,
with $Q_{i}(I,a)=\sum_{t}w_{t}\,ir_{i}^{t}(I,a)$ the raw signed accumulator of the
regrets measured at $\tau^{t}=(x_{i}^{t},y^{(i),t}_{-i})$ ,

 $\mathrm{gap}_{i}(\bar{\sigma})\;=\;\Delta^{(i)}_{i}\;+\;\max_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\Big[\frac{1}{W_{T}}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{i}}\sum_{a\in A(I)}x^{\prime}(Ia)\,Q_{i}(I,a)\;+\;\langle x^{\prime},w^{(i)}_{i}\rangle\Big],$ 

(19)

for every player $i$ , and $\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})=\max_{i}\mathrm{gap}_{i}(\bar{\sigma})$ .

Proof.

In the proof of Proposition 9, the first display is an
identity in $x^{\prime}$ before any inequality is taken:
 $\mathrm{gap}_{i}=\max_{x^{\prime}}[\,r_{i}(x^{\prime})+\langle x^{\prime},w^{(i)}_{i}\rangle\,]+\Delta^{(i)}_{i}$ 
with $r_{i}(x^{\prime})=\sum_{t}\rho_{t}(u_{i}(x^{\prime},y^{(i),t}_{-i})-u_{i}(\tau^{t}))$ .
Identity (17), applied at each $\tau^{t}$ and summed
with the weights, gives $r_{i}(x^{\prime})=W_{T}^{-1}\sum_{I,a}x^{\prime}(Ia)\,Q_{i}(I,a)$ .
Substituting proves (19).
∎

The bracket in (19) is one linear functional of the
realization plan: the coefficient of sequence $Ia$ is $Q_{i}(I,a)/W_{T}$ plus the
sum of $w^{(i)}_{i}(z)$ over the terminals $z$ whose last player- $i$ sequence is
 $Ia$ . Its maximum over $\mathcal{X}_{i}$ is one sequence-form backward pass for player
 $i$ , the same pass that computes $L^{(i)}_{-i}$ . Thus the pass that refines
 $C^{0}$ into $C^{1}$ can instead return the exact gap; $C^{1}$ is the choice that
keeps the three terms separate so that the bound attributes its slack, and
the exact-gap selector of Table 4 is the same pass with the
two linear functionals added before maximizing. The certificate’s cost
advantage is $C^{0}$ , which needs no pass at all.

Remark 12 (The target profile is free).

Nothing in the proofs of Propositions 9
and 11 uses that the target is the average of the
evaluated own strategies. Replace $\bar{x}_{j}$ by the realization plans
 $\hat{x}_{j}$ of any profile $\hat{\sigma}$ in $w^{(i)}_{i}$ and $u_{i}(\bar{\sigma})$ by
 $u_{i}(\hat{\sigma})$ in $\Delta^{(i)}_{i}$ ; both statements then bound or
evaluate $\mathrm{gap}_{i}(\hat{\sigma})$ , provided $Q_{i}$ and $\bar{d}^{(i)}$ were
accumulated from the same evaluated own strategies $x_{i}^{t}$ . The evaluated
own strategy may also differ from the averaged one (as in extragradient or
multiple-evaluation updates); only the matching between $Q_{i}$ , $\bar{d}^{(i)}$ 
and the evaluated profile matters.

## A.8. A zero-pass comparator term no larger than $\lVert w\rVert_{1}$ 

Write $a^{(i)}_{i}(z)\coloneqq p_{c}(z)\big(\prod_{j\neq i}\bar{x}_{j}(z)-\bar{c}^{(i)}_{-i}(z)\big)$ , so that $w^{(i)}_{i}=u_{i}\,a^{(i)}_{i}$ .

Lemma 13 (Positive part and shift invariance).

For every $x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}$ and every constant $c\in\mathbb{R}$ ,

 $\langle x^{\prime},w^{(i)}_{i}\rangle\;=\;\langle x^{\prime},(u_{i}-c)\,a^{(i)}_{i}\rangle\;\leq\;\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\big[(u_{i}(z)-c)\,a^{(i)}_{i}(z)\big]^{+}\;\leq\;\lVert(u_{i}-c)\,a^{(i)}_{i}\rVert_{1}.$ 

(20)

Consequently $L^{(i)}_{-i}\leq\min_{c}\sum_{z}[(u_{i}(z)-c)\,a^{(i)}_{i}(z)]^{+}\leq\sum_{z}[w^{(i)}_{i}(z)]^{+}\leq\lVert w^{(i)}_{i}\rVert_{1}$ , and the
certificate obtained from $C^{0}$ by replacing $\lVert w^{(i)}_{i}\rVert_{1}$ with
 $\min_{c}\sum_{z}[(u_{i}(z)-c)\,a^{(i)}_{i}(z)]^{+}$ is sound and never larger than
 $C^{0}$ .

Proof.

Shift invariance: $\sum_{z}x^{\prime}(z)\,p_{c}(z)\prod_{j\neq i}\bar{x}_{j}(z)=1$ is the
total mass of the terminal distribution of $(x^{\prime},\bar{x}_{-i})$ , and
 $\sum_{z}x^{\prime}(z)\,p_{c}(z)\,\bar{c}^{(i)}_{-i}(z)=\sum_{t}\rho_{t}\sum_{z}p_{c}(z)\,x^{\prime}(z)\prod_{j\neq i}y^{(i),t}_{j}(z)=\sum_{t}\rho_{t}=1$ likewise; hence
 $\langle x^{\prime},a^{(i)}_{i}\rangle=0$ and the constant $c$ drops out. The first
inequality uses $x^{\prime}(z)\in[0,1]$ termwise, keeping only terminals where the
coefficient is positive; the second drops the positive-part restriction.
The minimum over $c$ exists because the map $c\mapsto\sum_{z}[(u_{i}(z)-c)a^{(i)}_{i}(z)]^{+}$ is convex, piecewise linear and coercive whenever
 $a^{(i)}_{i}\neq 0$ (it is identically zero otherwise). Soundness of the
modified certificate follows by substituting the bound for
 $L^{(i)}_{-i}$ in (13).
∎

The minimization over $c$ is one-dimensional over the breakpoints
 $\{u_{i}(z)\}$ and needs no tree pass.

## A.9. Tightness is not universal

Soundness holds for every game and trajectory; no multiplicative tightness
guarantee does. Let player 1 choose Out (payoff $0$ ) or In, after which
player 1 chooses Bad (payoff $-1$ ) or Good (payoff $\delta\geq 0$ ); the
opponent has no decision. Repeat for $T$ rounds the behavioral profile
that plays Out at the root and Bad at the In-infoset. The average profile
is the same profile, so $\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})=\delta$ and $\Delta_{1}=0$ (a single
decision-maker produces no correlation). At the root, $v(\text{Out})=0$ 
and $v(\text{In})=-1$ (the continuation plays Bad), so the cumulative
regret of In is negative and $[\max_{a}Q_{1}(\text{root},a)]^{+}=0$ . At the
In-infoset the counterfactual convention removes the own reach of $0$ , so
 $v(\text{Good})=\delta$ , $v(\text{Bad})=-1$ and $Q_{1}(\text{In},\text{Good})/W_{T}=1+\delta$ . Hence $\widehat{R}_{1}=1+\delta$ and
 $C^{0}=C^{1}=1+\delta$ while the gap is $\delta$ : at $\delta=0$ the
profile is an exact equilibrium certified only to $1$ , and the ratio
 $C^{1}/\mathrm{NashGap}$ is unbounded as $\delta\downarrow 0$ . The slack is the
positive part at the root, which discards the negative upstream regret
that would cancel the off-path term; the exact evaluator of
Proposition 11 returns $x^{\prime}(\text{In})=x^{\prime}(\text{In}\cdot\text{Good})=1$ 
and the value $-1+(1+\delta)=\delta$ . Tightness on the benchmarks of
Section 7 is therefore a property of the trajectories
regret matching produces there, not of the certificate alone.

## A.10. Additional details for Proposition 6

The value-term step used in Theorem 4 and
Proposition 6 is the identity
 $\sum_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(\sigma^{t})=\langle\bar{d},u_{i}\rangle$ , which holds for any $n$ :
each $u_{i}(\sigma^{t})=\sum_{z}p_{c}(z)\prod_{j}x_{j}^{t}(z)\,u_{i}(z)$ , and
exchanging the (finite) sums over $t$ and $z$ gives the weighted empirical
terminal distribution $\bar{d}$ by definition. Similarly
 $u_{i}(\bar{\sigma})=\langle\bar{\mu},u_{i}\rangle$ with
 $\bar{\mu}=p_{c}\prod_{j}\bar{x}_{j}$ by Kuhn’s theorem. Hence
 $\Delta_{i}=\langle\bar{d}-\bar{\mu},u_{i}\rangle$ is measurable from the two running
terminal distributions.

The cumulative sums stored by the implementation are unnormalized:
 $J_{T}(z)=\sum_{t\leq T}w_{t}d_{\sigma^{t}}(z)$ ,
 $X_{i,T}=\sum_{t\leq T}w_{t}x_{i}^{t}$ , and
 $H_{i,T}(z)=\sum_{t\leq T}w_{t}\prod_{j\neq i}x_{j}^{t}(z)$ . They are divided by
 $W_{T}>0$ only when a checkpoint is evaluated. Thus
 $\bar{c}_{-i}=H_{i,T}/W_{T}$ and
 $D_{-i}=\sum_{z}p_{c}(z)|H_{i,T}(z)/W_{T}-\prod_{j\neq i}(X_{j,T}(z)/W_{T})|$ .
This is algebraically equivalent to normalized online maintenance but avoids
changing all previous entries when $W_{T}$ grows.

## An explicit game for Remark 7.

Three players. Player 1 chooses Out, ending the game with payoff $0$ to
everyone, or In; after In, players 2 and 3 simultaneously choose a bit.
Player 1 receives $1$ if the bits differ and $0$ otherwise; players 2 and
3 always receive $0$ . Take two equally weighted rounds in which player 1
plays Out in both and the opponents play $(0,0)$ in the first and $(1,1)$ 
in the second. Both rounds end at Out, so $\bar{d}=\delta_{\text{Out}}$ ;
the average plan of player 1 is Out with probability $1$ , so
 $\bar{\mu}=\delta_{\text{Out}}$ as well and $\varepsilon=\mathrm{KL}(\bar{d}\|\bar{\mu})=0$ . Every weighted external regret is $0$ : player 1 deviating to In earns
 $0$ against $(0,0)$ and $0$ against $(1,1)$ , and players 2 and 3 have
constant payoff. Yet the average profile has $\bar{x}_{2}=\bar{x}_{3}=(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})$ , so deviating to In earns player 1 the value
 $\tfrac{1}{2}$ : $\mathrm{NashGap}(\bar{\sigma})=\tfrac{1}{2}$ . The coalition term sees it:
 $\bar{c}_{-1}$ puts mass $\tfrac{1}{2}$ on each of $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$ while
 $\bar{x}_{2}\bar{x}_{3}$ puts $\tfrac{1}{4}$ on each of the four bit pairs, so
 $D_{-1}=4\cdot\tfrac{1}{4}=1$ , Proposition 6 gives
 $\mathrm{NashGap}\leq 0+0+MD_{-1}=1$ , and the exact comparator of
Proposition 8 gives $w_{1}=\tfrac{1}{4}$ on $(0,1)$ 
and $(1,0)$ , $0$ elsewhere, so $L_{-1}=\tfrac{1}{2}$ and the bound is
exact. A bound that vanishes with $\varepsilon$ cannot hold here: $\varepsilon=0$ and
the regrets are $0$ while the gap is $\tfrac{1}{2}$ , so for $n\geq 3$ the
coalition term is not a function of $\varepsilon$ and the regrets.

## Sheriff of Nottingham.

The bribery negotiation game of Farina et al. (2019)
(their Appendix F). The Smuggler secretly loads
 $n\in\{0,\dots,n_{\max}\}$ illegal items, then $r$ bribe rounds
follow, in which the Smuggler offers $b_{t}\in\{0,\dots,b_{\max}\}$ and the
Sheriff answers accept/reject, with only the final-round decision
binding. If the Sheriff accepts, payoffs are $(v\cdot n-b_{r},\,b_{r})$ ;
if he rejects and $n\geq 1$ , $(-p\cdot n,\,p\cdot n)$ ; if he rejects
and $n=0$ , $(+s,\,-s)$ .11
 1

 The conference version of
Farina et al. (2019) prints the accept payoff as
 $p\cdot n-b_{r}$ ; the arXiv version and the utility values in their
Table 1 give $v\cdot n-b_{r}$ , which we implement. The accept branch
has payoff sum $v\cdot n\neq 0$ , making the game general-sum; there
are no chance moves, and the Sheriff never observes $n$ . We fix
 $n_{\max}=5$ , $b_{\max}=2$ , $r=2$ ( $216$ terminals; $43$ Smuggler
and $21$ Sheriff infosets) and draw instances as $v\sim U(1,5)$ ,
 $p\sim U(0.5,2)$ , $s\sim U(0.5,2)$ . Our implementation reproduces
the sequence-count formulas and the numerical examples of the reference
paper.

## General-sum Goofspiel GS( $5$ ).

We use Goofspiel (Ross, 1971) in the full-information
family studied by Lanctot et al. (2013), with $5$ cards per player and
a general-sum twist: each round, player $1$ 
bids a card (hidden), then
player $2$ bids, then both are revealed; the higher card wins the
round’s prize, and on a tie the prize evaporates: neither
player collects, so total payoff is not constant. Bids are revealed
after each round (the “full information” variant of
Lanctot et al. (2013) except for the within-round hiding of
player $1$ ’s bid); there are no chance moves. The game has
 $(5!)^{2}=14{,}400$ terminals and $18{,}426$ infosets per player.
Instances draw the prize sequence as a seeded random permutation of
 $\{1,\dots,5\}$ perturbed elementwise by $U(0.8,1.2)$ .

## Appendix D OpenSpiel Cross-Validation

To reduce the cross-implementation uncertainty discussed in
Appendix F without access to the reference paper’s
code, we checked selected aspects of our implementation against OpenSpiel
(Lanctot et al., 2019) (v1.6.3). A second implementation of
biased Leduc was written directly from the reference paper’s game
description as an OpenSpiel Python game, sharing no code with our
engine. NashGap on the OpenSpiel side is computed from the per-player
improvements the library reports per player in general-sum games, taking
their maximum (its headline scalar is their sum, the NashConv
convention, a different quantity from our $\max_{i}$ NashGap. Sum-form
certification requires the per-player bounds $\widehat{R}_{i}+\Delta_{i}$ summed).
The following checks validate selected aspects of our own rules and
evaluator implementation. They are not a reproduction of NPCFR or its
reported PCFR+ baseline. Three checks follow, in increasing strictness of what
they exercise.

• 

Rules. At $\beta=1$ biased Leduc reduces to standard
Leduc. Running OpenSpiel’s own CFR+ solver for $512$ iterations on
our reimplementation and on the library’s own Leduc poker game gives
identical NashGap values at all ten logged checkpoints (relative
difference $0.0$ ; both games have $936$ infosets).

• 

Evaluator. We exported final average strategies from our
engine for $12$ instance–solver pairs ( $\beta\in\{1.0\}\cup$ three
sampled benchmark instances, $\times$ three solvers spanning
RM+/PRM+/DPRM+) and injected them into OpenSpiel tabular
policies (information-state key sets verified equal). OpenSpiel’s
maximum per-player improvement matches our NashGap on all $12$ pairs with
relative error $\leq 3.9\times 10^{-7}$ ; the worst pair has a true gap of
 $1.7\times 10^{-8}$ , i.e. the error is at floating-point noise level,
and all other pairs agree to $\leq 3\times 10^{-9}$ .

• 

Trajectories. Running OpenSpiel’s CFR+ for $2^{12}$ 
iterations on three benchmark instances and comparing against our
matched solver’s logged trajectory gives NashGap ratios from $0.65$ to $1.24$ 
at the aligned checkpoints ( $5$ of $6$ inside a $[2/3,3/2]$ acceptance
band, one marginally outside at $0.6497$ ). A fine-grid diagnostic shows
the two trajectories agree to relative difference $\sim 10^{-11}$ at
 $t=64$ on all three instances. This is a trajectory diagnostic. It tests
none of solver equivalence, update bookkeeping, or long-horizon behavior.
The difference then grows monotonically to $24$ – $35\%$ at
 $t=4096$ ; we do not use this diagnostic to identify its cause.

The first two checks support the limited conclusion used here.
The game rules and NashGap evaluator in our implementation agree with an
independent implementation on the tested cases. They leave the reference
solver, its averaging conventions, and the source of the cross-paper
discrepancy untested. The scripts, exported policies and logs behind this
cross-validation are not part of the code released upon acceptance, and its manifest
states what is included.

## Appendix E Implementation Details

## Engine.

All games are compiled to sequence form, with terminal arrays
 $(p_{c},u_{1},\dots,u_{n})$ over $\mathcal{Z}$ plus per-player sequence trees.
Realization, backward regret sums, best responses, and normalization are
vectorized by tree depth, so a CFR iteration is $O(\mathrm{depth})$ NumPy
calls over $O(|\mathcal{Z}|)$ arrays. Three-player Leduc compiles to
 $1{,}043{,}952$ terminals and $8{,}600$ infosets per player
( $\approx 195$  ms per PRM+ iteration on one CPU core).

## Accumulators and certificates.

Each run maintains, with averaging weights $w_{t}$ , a separate raw signed
counterfactual-regret accumulator (for $\widehat{R}_{i}$ ), distinct from the clipped
RM+ state, together with the weighted average realization
plans $\bar{x}_{i}$ , the weighted empirical terminal distribution $\bar{d}$ 
(one fused multiply-add over $\mathcal{Z}$ per iteration), and for $n\geq 3$ the
per-player coalition products for $D_{-i}$ ( $O(n|\mathcal{Z}|)$ ). At each
checkpoint the aligned tight certificate is
 $\max_{i}[\widehat{R}_{i}+\langle\bar{d},u_{i}\rangle-\langle\bar{\mu},u_{i}\rangle]$ and the
coalition certificate adds $MD_{-i}$ inside the max; both are a few dot
products. Algorithm 1 writes $H_{i}$ without the chance
factor so that $H_{i}/W_{T}=\bar{c}^{(i)}_{-i}$ exactly as defined in
Proposition 9. The implementation folds $p_{c}$ into the
stored accumulator, whose raw coalition sum is
 $\sum_{t}w_{t}p_{c}(z)\prod_{j\neq i}y^{(i),t}_{j}(z)$ , and forms the imbalance
weight as $w^{(i)}_{i}=u_{i}\,(p_{c}\prod_{j\neq i}\bar{x}_{j}-\mathrm{coal}_{i}/W_{T})$ ,
where $\mathrm{coal}_{i}$ is that chance-weighted sum, so $p_{c}$ appears
exactly once. The comparator identity
 $u_{i}(x^{\prime},\bar{x}_{-i})-\sum_{t}\rho_{t}u_{i}(x^{\prime},y^{(i),t}_{-i})=\langle x^{\prime},w^{(i)}_{i}\rangle$ is an identity of linear functionals of $x^{\prime}$ . Both sides
reduce to $\sum_{z}w^{(i)}_{i}(z)\,x^{\prime}(z)$ , so it holds for every $x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}$ 
without enumerating deviations. The implemented weight was checked against
that closed form on biased Leduc, whose fold and showdown terminals carry
different $p_{c}$ : applying $p_{c}$ twice moves the residual from
 $1.9\times 10^{-16}$ to $1.2\times 10^{-1}$ .

## DPRM+.

At zero-based iteration $t$ , the engine realizes $x^{t}$ , sets
 $w_{t}=(t+1)^{p}$ , and inserts $w_{t}x^{t}$ and $w_{t}d_{\sigma^{t}}$ into the
running totals before forming the look-ahead proxy, so the passed totals
 $(W_{t},J_{t},M_{t})$ already include the current iterate:

 $\displaystyle p_{t}(z)$ 
 $\displaystyle=\frac{J_{t}(z)+w_{t}d_{\sigma^{t}}(z)}{W_{t}+w_{t}},$ 

(21)

 $\displaystyle q_{t}(z)$ 
 $\displaystyle=p_{c}(z)\prod_{j}\frac{M_{j,t}(z)+w_{t}x_{j}^{t}(z)}{W_{t}+w_{t}},$ 

(22)

with $M_{j,t}(z)=\sum_{s\leq t}w_{s}x_{j}^{s}$ player $j$ ’s running weighted
realization total at the sequence that leads to $z$ . The proxy doubles the
current iterate’s effective weight; it is a finite-difference estimate of
the next profile’s direction. With
 $F^{t}_{\mathrm{proxy}}=\sum_{z}p_{t}(z)\log[p_{t}(z)/q_{t}(z)]$ differentiated with
respect to player $i$ ’s current terminal reach $x_{i}^{t}(z)$ at fixed
 $(W_{t},J_{t},M_{t})$ , the literal gradient carries the prefactor
 $w_{t}/(W_{t}+w_{t})$ ; the implemented direction returns
 $g^{t}_{\mathrm{term}}=F_{t}\,\nabla F^{t}_{\mathrm{proxy}}$ with
 $F_{t}=(W_{t}+w_{t})/w_{t}$ , so the effective pre-clipping multiplier relative to
the literal gradient is $\lambda F_{t}$ , which grows like $t/(p+1)$ . The
runs use the own-reach-excluded counterfactual convention, so $g_{i}^{t}$ is a
prediction direction in regret space. The actual prediction is

 $m_{i}^{t+1}=ir_{i}^{t}+\operatorname{clip}(-\lambda g_{i}^{t},-B_{t},B_{t}),\qquad B_{t}=8(t+1)^{1/4}.$ 

(23)

The clip applies to the decorrelation bias only; $ir_{i}^{t}$ and the raw
certificate accumulator enter unclipped. In alternating mode, player $i>1$ 
is re-realized after earlier players update, so its prediction and regret
use a partially updated profile; the accumulators $\bar{d}^{(i)}$ and
 $\bar{c}^{(i)}_{-i}$ of Proposition 9 are taken from that
same profile, while the averages use the start profile. A finite-difference
test in the own-reach-included mode checks the analytic gradient of the
doubled-proxy objective against central differences, to relative error
 $<10^{-5}$ . No parameter is learned: the decorrelation scale is drawn from
the fixed candidate set $\lambda\in\{0.1,0.3\}$ , protocol configuration
shared by all reported runs.

## Added solvers.

The nine solvers of the second stage are local regret minimizers plugged into
the same engine; the certificate accumulators read the instantaneous regret
at the profile actually played and never the optimizer’s discounted or
projected state, so Proposition 9 applies unchanged. DCFR
discounts the accumulated regret by $t^{\alpha}/(t^{\alpha}+1)$ for positive and
 $t^{\beta}/(t^{\beta}+1)$ for negative entries with $(\alpha,\beta)=(1.5,0)$ ;
DCFR+ applies the positive discount and the $+$ floor, and PDCFR+ adds
the one-step prediction of Table 1 of Xu et al. (2024); both use the
corresponding power weights in the average ( $p{=}2$ , $4$ and $5$ ). HS-PCFR+
and HS-DCFR follow the linear schedule of equation (4) of
Zhang et al. (2026) with the total iteration count $n=T$ substituted,
 $\gamma_{s}=\gamma_{0}-5s/n$ for the weight exponent (and the discount exponents
for HS-DCFR); the resulting weights reach $e^{369}$ at $T$ and stay inside
double precision, and the configuration check rejects schedules that would
not. Smooth PRM+ projects the regret state onto $\{R\geq 0,\lVert R\rVert_{1}\geq 1\}$ per infoset, Stable PRM+ restarts an infoset’s state at the unit
level when it falls below it, and ExRM+ takes the extragradient step of
Algorithm 5 of Farina et al. (2023); the step $\eta$ rescales the
regrets against that unit level, and because counterfactual regrets in
biased Leduc carry chance reach of about $1/30$ the grid was
 $\eta\in\{10,10^{2},10^{3},10^{4}\}$ , above the matrix-game values of the
paper. ExRM+ exists only with simultaneous updates. IR-PRM+ is
Algorithm 2 of Zhang et al. (2025) with the previous utility vector as
prediction. Each family was run on seeds $0$ – $9$ over a grid of $35$ 
configurations in total (discount exponents, schedules, $\eta$ , averaging
exponent, update order), and one configuration per family was selected by a
rule written before the pilot: the highest oracle fraction at $10^{-5}$ ,
ties broken by the smaller median trajectory minimum. The selected
configurations are the nine solvers reported everywhere; seeds $0$ – $9$ are
part of the in-sample set, so the sixteen-solver in-sample figures include this
selection step. The two held-out stages use the same $64$ instances
(seeds $64$ – $127$ ): Stage 1 touched them first, and the second
preregistration was frozen before the Stage 2 runs, so the Stage 2 figures
reuse the Stage 1 instances and are not fresh data with respect to Stage 1.
Stage 3 uses a separate set (seeds $128$ – $191$ ) that no earlier stage had
touched. Six added solvers ran on three-player
Leduc by a rule stated before the two-player results were read: the three
discounting-family solvers, plus every added solver whose two-player oracle
fraction reached $0.60$ (HS-PCFR+, Smooth PRM+, Stable PRM+).

## Portfolio and reported rows.

The pool of $K=16$ solvers contains RM+ and PRM+ with linear ( $p{=}1$ )
and quadratic ( $p{=}2$ ) weights, three DPRM+ solvers that add a
clipped, scaled, tree-chained decorrelation direction from a
doubled-current-iterate proxy, and the nine added solvers above, all
training-free. “Selection”
rows of Table 1 report the exact NashGap of the iterate chosen
by a certificate. “Certified” rows report the fraction whose selected
certificate is itself below the threshold, the number a deployment can state
without an exact best-response pass against the average. The reference’s
three-player benchmark is the fixed game of its Table 6.

## Protocol.

Biased Leduc: $\beta\sim U(0,\tfrac{1}{2})$ per instance drawn from
seeds $0$ – $63$ , paired across solvers, $T=2^{18}$ , our own dense grid of $128$ evenly spaced
checkpoints; the reference instead reports checkpoints at $2^{k}$ , about
 $19$ evaluations through this horizon. The three control solvers of
Table 1 (uniform RM+/PRM+ and simultaneous PRM+) use
the same paired seeds. Three-player Leduc: $T=2^{16}$ , deterministic, with $64$ checkpoints spaced
every $1024$ iterations. Every run records $C^{0}$ and $C^{1}$ from the
accumulators of Proposition 9; the two-player grid and the
preselected three-player solver additionally log the aligned start-of-round
certificate (extra regret pass) for the comparison in
Section 7. The aligned three-player rerun
(rerun in strict logging mode into a fresh output directory) reproduced
the original trajectory to floating-point precision; the solver was fixed
before it. All headline numbers are recomputed from the stored per-run
logs by the analysis scripts.

## Appendix F Future Work

The results point to the following experiments and technical extensions.

## Reproducing the reported baselines.

Our re-evaluation compares our runs against the reported numbers
of Sychrovský et al. (2026): no code accompanies their paper,
which has no repository link or reproducibility statement and no public
release, so rerunning their solver is not possible. The paper does
not fully specify the averaging weights of the
plus-family baselines. We could not reproduce their PCFR+ row under
any configuration we tried. Our uniform PRM+ measures $0.00$ 
at $10^{-5}$ and $0.77$ at $10^{-4}$ against their reported $0.16$ and
 $0.48$ , mismatched in both directions, and our uniform-average runs remain
above $10^{-5}$ at this horizon for RM+ and PRM+. To test our own
rules and NashGap evaluator as the sources of this mismatch, we
cross-validated them against OpenSpiel (Lanctot et al., 2019). A
reimplementation of biased Leduc written independently from the paper’s
text matches OpenSpiel’s built-in Leduc exactly at $\beta=1$ 
(identical CFR+ NashGap values at every logged checkpoint), and
injecting our exported average strategies into OpenSpiel’s general-sum
gap computation reproduces our NashGap to relative error
 $\leq 3.9\times 10^{-7}$ on all $12$ instance–solver pairs tested
(Appendix D). These checks validate our own rules and
evaluator; the reference solver’s configuration remains an open
reproduction target, and obtaining it would enable a controlled rerun.

## A paired low- $\beta$ comparison.

At threshold $10^{-5}$ on biased Leduc, NPCFR+’s reported $0.96$ 
exceeds our certificate-selected $0.89$ , and the two sides use
independently drawn instance samples. Our portfolio’s $7$ in-sample failures
lean towards low $\beta$ and its $5$ held-out failures do not. A shared
instance set would test whether the reported aggregate margin concentrates
in the low- $\beta$ region. The NPCFR instance draws are unpublished, so the
comparison currently rests on aggregates.

## Aligned logging as an ablation.

Proposition 9 makes the second regret pass against the
start-of-round profile unnecessary, but we paid it across the full
two-player biased-Leduc portfolio to compare the two certificates on
identical trajectories. Both are sound at all $131{,}072$ checkpoints; on
the seven original solvers the aligned certificate selects $0.80$ and certifies
 $0.69$ at $10^{-5}$ against $0.84$ and $0.75$ for $C^{1}$ , and on the
sixteen-solver pool it certifies $0.86$ against $0.89$ 
(Section 7). The
comparison isolates a design principle. The certificate should follow the
regrets the algorithm actually minimizes and account for the resulting
profile mismatch explicitly. Forcing every accumulator onto one profile
costs tightness. The certified-anchor pattern also transfers beyond
regret-matching loops. In a side experiment on Leduc, a regularized
entropy-descent solver advancing a fixed-average reference maintained an
aligned certificate next to its working strategy, and all $13$ anchor
updates over $5{,}000$ iterations were exactly re-audited as sound (final
anchor NashConv $0.0139$ against its stored bound $0.0205$ ; the per-move
audit will be released with the code). That
deployment certifies the sum-form NashConv, so the per-player bounds
 $\widehat{R}_{i}+\Delta_{i}$ enter as a sum
(Appendix D).

## Certificates for selection and for control.

Using the certificate to control the solver is a different regime
with a known failure mode. In the same side experiment, gating reference
advances on certified progress of a single sequence froze the dynamics
after four advances (final NashConv $0.109$ versus $0.022$ for the
ungated schedule). Forcing advances past the gate violated the gate’s own
monotone-threshold invariant at every forced step. The stored acceptance
threshold no longer bounded the reference’s NashConv, because an anchor
advance replaces the reference that bound was evaluated on. All $18$ 
forced advances in the pilot run violated the stored bound. Throughout,
the certificate
inequality $\sum_{i}(\widehat{R}_{i}+\Delta_{i})\geq\mathrm{NashConv}$ held for the
anchor actually in force, whose audit logs will be released with the code.
These results separate certificate-gated control from certificate-based
selection; our protocol keeps the certificate out of the update loop.

## Tighter multi-player certificates.

On three-player Leduc the $MD_{-i}$ form of the certificate is
 $12$ – $387\times$ loose at $T$ across the six original solvers, $C^{0}$ is
 $2.9$ – $105\times$ , and the exact comparator $C^{1}$ is $1.4$ – $8.2\times$ 
(Table 2); on each of them the comparator relaxation
 $MD_{-i}-L_{-i}$ is essentially the whole slack of the $MD$ form
(median share $0.98$ – $1.0$ ; Figure 3 shows the PRM+,
 $p{=}2$ solver). The added HS-PCFR+ solver is the exception that confirms the
attribution: its averaging weights sit almost entirely on the last iterates,
the coalition imbalance is near zero, and the three forms coincide at
 $1.3\times$ . Closing the remaining gap between $C^{0}$ and $C^{1}$ therefore requires
a zero-best-response surrogate for the comparator optimum $L_{-i}$ , since
evaluating $L_{-i}$ costs a backward pass per player per checkpoint.
One-shot evaluation remains the general final-verification tier when an
exact guarantee is needed.

## Broader benchmark coverage.

The benchmarks come from the paper we re-evaluate and are
Leduc variants, deliberately so because the target claim concerns those
benchmarks. Appendix C adds two structurally different
general-sum EFGs, where selection and certification carry over. Sheriff is near-saturated for the predictive solvers, Goofspiel leaves
the DPRM+ certificates visibly loose, and all games remain small
tabular benchmarks. The three-player result is a single deterministic
run, as in the reference protocol. Larger and more varied games are the
next test of mechanism transfer; so are payoffs other than chips, such as
tournament prize equity (Li and Huang, 2026e). Agents that maintain no regret
accumulator, such as LLM poker agents grounded in a rule library
(Li et al., 2026c), fall outside the certificate’s scope.

## Sampled and amortized certificates.

The certificate consumes exact weighted terminal distributions, an
 $O(|\mathcal{Z}|)$ object per checkpoint, and is therefore incompatible with
Monte Carlo CFR-style sampling (Lanctot et al., 2009) as it stands.
Relatedly, the DPRM+ solver’s $O(|\mathcal{Z}|)$ decorrelation gradient per
iteration was infeasible at full length on three-player Leduc
( $\approx 3.5$  s per iteration), so the three-player portfolio ran
without DPRM+ solvers. Sampled estimators or amortized terminal
accumulators are the natural next step.
