## ABSTRACT
We aim to address the problem of Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Mode Averaging, a critical problem that emerges when distilling Flow Matching (FM) based behavior policies in offline Reinforcement Learning (RL): When learning over multi-modal datasets, standard distillation inadvertently forces the policy to interpolate across different behavioral modes, causing the generated actions to collapse into invalid, OOD regions. To resolve this, we propose Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD), a novel framework that reformulates offline policy distillation as a value-guided generative trajectory alignment problem. By employing a dynamic coupling mechanism, VRD actively rectifies flow dynamics to explicitly decouple distinct behavioral modes, cleanly isolating high-value actions. Specifically, we instantiate VRD through two complementary algorithms: VRD-Q, which utilizes value-quantile behavior distillation to statistically filter out low-value behaviors, and VRD-R, which reorganizes the latent space to geometrically isolate high-value generative trajectories from the FM-based behavior policy. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that VRD improves existing generative baselines across diverse continuous control and planning tasks on the D4RL, OGBench, and Minari benchmarks, especially over those multi-modal, non-expert datasets.

## BODY (first 18k chars)
Abstract

We aim to address the problem of Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Mode Averaging, a critical problem that emerges when distilling Flow Matching (FM) based behavior policies in offline Reinforcement Learning (RL): When learning over multi-modal datasets, standard distillation inadvertently forces the policy to interpolate across different behavioral modes, causing the generated actions to collapse into invalid, OOD regions. To resolve this, we propose Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD), a novel framework that reformulates offline policy distillation as a value-guided generative trajectory alignment problem. By employing a dynamic coupling mechanism, VRD actively rectifies flow dynamics to explicitly decouple distinct behavioral modes, cleanly isolating high-value actions. Specifically, we instantiate VRD through two complementary algorithms: VRD-Q, which utilizes value-quantile behavior distillation to statistically filter out low-value behaviors, and VRD-R, which reorganizes the latent space to geometrically isolate high-value generative trajectories from the FM-based behavior policy. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that VRD improves existing generative baselines across diverse continuous control and planning tasks on the D4RL, OGBench, and Minari benchmarks, especially over those multi-modal, non-expert datasets.

## 1 Introduction

Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) aims to derive a high-capacity policy from a fixed, pre-collected dataset without further environment interaction [17, 15], which is particularly vital for the applications where online exploration is costly or hazardous. Recently, generative models, particularly Flow Matching (FM) [18], have emerged as a powerful alternative to traditional Diffusion-based methods [9, 2, 5], where FM-based policies, such as Flow Q-Learning (FQL) [21], offer significant advantages in modeling complex, multi-modal behavior distributions while circumventing the training instabilities typically associated with backpropagating through ODE solvers.

Figure 1: Problem of Latent Fusion (left) and the OOD Mode Averaging problem caused by FQL-style behavior distillation (right).

Despite their empirical promise, we identify a fundamental pathology in how current flow-based RL frameworks distill high-value actions from multi-modal behavior policies. Specifically, when learning from multi-modal data, our analysis reveals that Flow Matching networks inherently suffer from Latent Fusion: the topological entanglement between the generative trajectories of optimal and sub-optimal behavioral modes, caused by the vector field’s training dynamics, as is shown in Figure 1 (left). In previous works, like FQL [21], it first pre-trains a behavior flow policy to capture the full multi-modal behavior distribution [4] from the dataset, and subsequently applies Q-value guidance to distill the optimal policy. However, because such distillation operates on a continuous vector field where trajectories are already topologically entangled by the MLE pre-training, the Q-guided objective, as is shown in Figure 1 (right), inadvertently forces the sampled actions to linearly interpolate across distinct modes during inference, which triggers the OOD Mode Averaging disaster, where it is risky for the generated actions to collapse into low-density regions of the state-action manifold, ultimately causing performance degradation. Although recent approaches like Guided Flow Policy (GFP) [24] incorporate reward information to amplify optimal behavior modes in FM-based behavior policy, their FQL-style distillation mechanism still suffers from OOD Mode Averaging.

To address this issue, our core insight is that during the distillation phase, policy refinement must go beyond simply cloning from FM-based behavior policy; rather, it inherently requires rectifying the flow dynamics to explicitly decouple distinct behavioral modes along the data manifold. Therefore, we introduce Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD), a novel framework that reformulates offline policy distillation as a value-guided trajectory alignment problem, of which the core lies in a dynamic coupling mechanism that precisely modulates the flow dynamics to explicitly decouple and exclude sub-optimal behavioral modes, ensuring that the generated actions are confined to the optimal action manifold during inference.

To instantiate this unified framework, we derive two distinct algorithms from complementary mathematical perspectives: VRD-Q (Statistical Rectification): this variant performs in-place vector field alignment by employing asymmetric weight modulation based on value quantiles, which statistically filters sub-optimal regions without altering the underlying flow topology. VRD-R (Geometric Rectification): this variant actively reshapes the flow’s topology, which constructs a value-gravity field that transports the generative trajectories toward a highly compact optimal support set, geometrically isolating them from sub-optimal modes.

In summary, our primary contributions include: 1) Tracing OOD Mode Averaging: We theoretically trace the root cause of the OOD Mode Averaging problem in FQL-style distillation mechanism, revealing how its distillation objective interacts with latent fusion to generate invalid OOD actions on multi-modal data. 2) The VRD Framework: We propose Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD) with two implementations VRD-Q and VRD-R, reformulating offline policy distillation as a value-guided trajectory alignment problem to safely extract high-value policies without mode averaging. 3) Empirical Validation: Extensive evaluations demonstrate that VRD-Q and VRD-R effectively mitigate this OOD issue, outperforming or performing competitively with existing generative baselines across various continuous control and planning tasks.

## 2 Preliminaries and motivation

## 2.1 Offline RL and Flow Q-Learning

Suppose a Markov Decision Process $\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},r,\rho,p)$ , then the goal of offline RL is to learn a policy $\pi$ that maximizes the expected cumulative discounted return using a static dataset $\mathcal{D}$ collected by a behavioral policy. Previous work adopted the FQL framework [21] to avoid the instability of backpropagating through ODE solvers. FQL consists of three components:

Critic: A value function $Q_{\phi}(s,a)$ trained to minimize the Bellman error.

BC Flow Policy: A flow-matching policy $\mu_{\theta}(s,z)$ defined by a vector field $v_{\theta}$ , trained purely on Behavioral Cloning (BC) to capture the dataset distribution:

 $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{Flow}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{s,a\sim\mathcal{D},t\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1),x^{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})}[\|v_{\theta}(t,s,x^{t})-(a-x^{0})\|_{2}^{2}]$ 

(1)

where $x^{t}=(1-t)x^{0}+ta$ .

One-Step Actor Policy: An expressive feed-forward network $\pi(s,z):\mathcal{S}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathcal{A}$ that maps latent noise $z$ directly to actions.

In standard FQL, the actor $\pi$ is trained to maximize $Q$ -values while being regularized via distillation from the frozen flow policy $\mu_{\theta}$ . The standard objective uses an unstructured Gaussian prior $z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})$ for this coupling:

 $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\pi}=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D},z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})}[-\lambda\cdot Q_{\phi}(s,\pi(s,z))+\|\pi(s,z)-\mu_{\theta}(s,z)\|_{2}^{2}]$ 

(2)

, while the Q-networks $Q_{\phi}$ are learned by minimizing a standard double Temporal Difference (TD) objective:

 $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\phi_{i})=\mathbb{E}_{(s,a,r,s^{\prime})\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\left(Q_{\phi_{i}}(s,a)-r-\gamma\min_{j=1,2}Q_{\bar{\phi},j}(s^{\prime},\pi(s^{\prime},z))\right)^{2}\right],\ z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ 

(3)

This implies that the latent space of the actor is isotropic; the position of $z$ carries no semantic information regarding the quality of the action.

## 2.2 Latent fusion and OOD mode-averaging problems of FQL in multi-modal data

According to Flow Q-Learning, we can fit the action distribution of the behavior policy $\pi_{\beta}(a|s)$ , by the flow model $\mu_{\theta}(s,z)$ , where $z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ . Then we define the support action set given $s$ , as $\mathcal{A}(s)=\{a|\pi_{\beta}(a|s)>0\}$ , and OOD action set $\bar{\mathcal{A}}=\{a|\pi_{\beta}(a|s)=0\}$ . Furthermore, given a Q-function $Q(s,a)$ as the evaluator, we can define the optimal and suboptimal action sets as,

 $\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{*}_{Q}(s)$ 
 $\displaystyle=\{a|a\in\mathcal{A}(s),Q(s,a)\geq\max_{a^{\prime}}Q(s,a^{\prime})-\epsilon_{opt}\}$ 

(4)

 $\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{-}_{Q}(s)$ 
 $\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}(s)/\mathcal{A}^{*}_{Q}(s)$ 

(5)

In our work, we focus on the setting of multi-modal data, which has raised attention in the field [3, 10] recently. Specifically, in our work, we focus on the isolated multi-modal data as,

 $\displaystyle\inf_{a^{*}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}_{Q}(s),a^{-}\in\mathcal{A}^{-}_{Q}(s)}\|a^{*}-a^{-}\|_{2}=D>0$ 

(6)

which means that there exists a gap between the optimal and sub-optimal data modes. Under such kind of data, the first problem we encounter in flow-based offline RL is the Latent Fusion for the flow policy $\mu_{\theta}$ learned by $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Flow}}(\theta)$ in Eq.(1):

Proposition 1 (Latent Entanglement of OOD Generation).

Suppose the latent space $\mathcal{Z}$ (support of $z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ ) is connected, and the flow mapping $\mu_{\theta}(s,z)$ is $L$ -Lipschitz continuous w.r.t. $z$ . Let $\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{Q}(s)$ , $\mathcal{Z}^{-}_{Q}(s)$ , and $\mathcal{Z}_{OOD}(s)$ denote the latent preimages of $\mathcal{A}^{*}_{Q}(s)$ , $\mathcal{A}^{-}_{Q}(s)$ , and $\bar{\mathcal{A}}(s)$ , respectively. If the model expresses both optimal and sub-optimal modes ( $\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{Q}(s)\neq\emptyset,\mathcal{Z}^{-}_{Q}(s)\neq\emptyset$ ), the following holds:
(1) The optimal latent boundary is non-empty ( $\partial\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{Q}(s)\neq\emptyset$ );
(2) The OOD latent set is strictly non-empty ( $\mathcal{Z}_{OOD}(s)\neq\emptyset$ );
(3) Any $\epsilon$ -neighborhood of a boundary point $z_{bd}\in\partial\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{Q}(s)$ intersects non-optimal regions: $\forall\epsilon\!>\!0,\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}(z_{bd})\cap\big(\mathcal{Z}^{-}_{Q}(s)\cup\mathcal{Z}_{OOD}(s)\big)\neq\emptyset$ .

Proof is shown in Appendix A.1. Proposition 1 formalizes the Latent Fusion dilemma. Because $\mu_{\theta}$ is continuous and $\mathcal{Z}$ is connected, the model cannot safely isolate optimal latents. Property (2) proves that to bridge the physical gap $D>0$ , any continuous curve connecting the modes must structurally intersect the OOD space (a topological necessity, though without quantifying probability mass). Furthermore, Property (3) shows $\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{Q}(s)$ is inextricably entangled with sub-optimal/OOD regions at its boundary.

While Proposition 1 leverages the potent multi-modal modeling capabilities of Flow matching for targeted policy extraction, the extreme topological entanglement within the latent space, as characterized by the Latent Fusion in Proposition 1, renders such distillation and mapping via continuous, smooth networks susceptible to mode-averaging, as shown in Theorem 1. This phenomenon forces the policy to generate interpolated, OOD actions ( $\overline{a}\notin\mathcal{A}(s)$ ) that collapse into off-manifold regions.

Theorem 1 (OOD Mode Averaging in FQL).

Suppose the Q-function satisfies $\mu$ -strong concavity in the region bridging the modes and is $L$ -Lipschitz smooth.
Consider the FQL-style distillation that minimizes the regularized objective in Eq.(2).
Under multi-modal data with gap $D>0$ as Eq.(6) and any non-zero value regularization penalty $\lambda>0$ ,
FQL generates OOD actions with a minimum expected error bounded by $D^{2}/4$ .

Proof is shown in Appendix A.2. Theorem 1 reveals a fundamental limitation of FQL: under the latent mode entanglement established in Proposition 1, the explicit Euclidean regularization (e.g., $-\lambda Q+\|a-\mu_{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}$ ) blindly forces policy updates to perform linear interpolation across the action space. Because multi-modal data manifolds are topologically separated by a physical gap $D>0$ , this shortest Euclidean path traverses low-density OOD valleys, which triggers the Mode Averaging disaster with a strict expected error bounded by $D^{2}/4$ . Consequently, we conclude that action-space Euclidean constraints are structurally incompatible with multi-modal offline RL. Besides, assuming local $\mu$ -strong concavity in the valley between modes serves as an analytical abstraction to capture the optimization tension in FQL.

In other words, in probability density estimation, a smoothly connected set of feasible actions (like slightly different stable walking postures) topologically constitutes a single, broad, and continuous mode. Flow Matching naturally and gracefully captures this continuous variance. However, we suggest that arises specifically when the behavioral distribution contains structurally distinct peaks separated by low-density valleys (OOD regions).

Motivation. The observation that standard Euclidean distillation is vulnerable to mode averaging on multi-modal datasets motivates our Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD) framework. As analyzed in this section, directly penalizing the action difference can inadvertently pull the actor into OOD regions. To effectively mitigate this issue, policy extraction must move beyond simple action-space regularization. Instead of linearly interpolating actions across empty spaces, VRD rectifies the continuous latent mapping by dynamically aligning the flow dynamics with value guidance, hence encouraging the policy to safely extract high-value actions along the data manifold.

## 3 Methodology

## 3.1 Unified framework: kernel-coupled value rectification

In Flow Q-Learning, while the initial behavior cloning phase effectively captures the multi-modal data distribution via the flow model $\mu_{\theta}$ , it absorbs suboptimal behavioral modes (as discussed in Section 2). To distill $\mu_{\theta}$ into an optimal policy $\pi$ that exclusively outputs actions within the optimal set $\mathcal{A}^{*}_{Q}$ , a principled rectification mechanism is essential. Rather than treating this process as isolated heuristic regularizations, we cast the value rectification as a latent distribution alignment problem.

To this end, we propose a generalized Value-Rectified Functional Space. We define the rectification objective $\Psi(\pi;\mu_{\theta})$ as an expected kernel-coupled divergence over the joint latent space $\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}$ :

 $\Psi(\pi;\mu_{\theta})=\int_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}(z)\left[\int_{\mathcal{Z}}\kappa_{Q}(z,z^{\prime}\mid s,\mu_{\theta},\pi)\cdot\|\mu_{\theta}(s,z)-\pi(s,z^{\prime})\|_{2}^{2}\,dz^{\prime}\right]dz$ 

(7)

where $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}(z)$ is the prior distribution of the pre-trained latent space, and $\kappa_{Q}(z,z^{\prime}\mid\cdot):\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}$ is defined as the Value-Guided Alignment Kernel (VGAK). Specifically, in our work, we focus on a Non-merging transport kernel $\kappa_{Q}(z,z^{\prime}\mid\cdot)=w(z)\delta(z^{\prime}-T(z))$ for a measurable weight $w\geq 0$ and a measurable map $T:\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}$ satisfying mode separation $T(\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}^{*})\cap T(\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}^{-})=\emptyset$ and fiber-wise optimal preservation (i.e., for any $z^{\prime}\in T(\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}^{*})$ , the induced conditional barycenter over the pre-image fiber $T^{-1}(\{z^{\prime}\})$ remains within $\mathcal{A}_{Q}^{*}(s)$ ). The core insight of this unified functional is that the transition from a behavior-cloned flow to an optimal policy is essentially a latent-to-latent coupling process. The kernel $\kappa_{Q}$ mathematically governs both the coupling strength (how much weight a sample carries) and the coordinate mapping (where the latent variable is transported) between the source latent variable $z$ and the target latent variable $z^{\prime}$ .

Then, by plugging Eq. (7) into Eq. (2), the rectification-based policy optimization objective is:

(8)

where $\lambda$ is the balance coefficient, and $\mathcal{P}_{test}$ is a prior distribution of the latent variable $z^{\prime}$ used at test.

## CONCLUSION
This paper proposes the Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD) framework, reformulating policy distillation as a value-guided trajectory alignment process to solve the latent fusion problem in offline RL over multi-modal dataset. VRD-Q and VRD-R mitigate OOD Mode Averaging problem via statistical filtering and geometric reshaping respectively. Experiments show VRD successfully decouples the high-value behavior mode from the multi-modal data, which enables VRD outperform baselines on various benchmarks and datasets, demonstrating exceptional robustness for sub-optimal data noise.

Limitations (see Appendix D).

The proposed VRD relies on the underlying value network’s accuracy, where severe Q-value overestimation may mislead the alignment process. Future work could explore more robust value estimation or extend this framework to discrete action spaces.
