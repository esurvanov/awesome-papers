## ABSTRACT
Abdominal organ segmentation is essential in modern medical applications. While existing deep learning approaches demonstrate promising performance, their heavy reliance on large-scale image data raises concerns over generalization capability and privacy risks. This motivates the development of generalizable segmentation training paradigms based on domain-randomized image synthesis, facilitating generalization across unseen real image domains. However, existing paradigms either suffer from limited segmentation performance when applied to abdominal organs, or require dataset-specific finetuning. To address these limitations, we propose ReSeg, a novel two-stage retraining-free framework for domain-agnostic zero-shot abdominal organ segmentation. At the first stage, our organ-agnostic synthetic data-driven pretraining approach exploits semantically and visually diverse synthetic images generated by a dedicated data engine to optimize a Generalizable Semantic Representation Network (GSRNet). The network is thereby capable of learning semantic features robust against variations in anatomical structure, imaging contrast, resolution and spatial context, which provide universal representation capabilities that serve as a foundation for segmentation learning. Subsequently, in the second stage, we continue to train an Organ-specific Encoder-decoder Segmentation Network (OESNet), whose encoder is initialized with the pretrained weights. This training process is facilitated by another dedicated data engine, which generates diversified visual images while strictly retaining the inherent spatial layout of abdominal organs. Comprehensive evaluation on seven abdominal organ segmentation datasets across three different modalities including CT, MRI, and PET demonstrates that ReSeg consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods while eliminating the need for real training images.

## BODY (first 18k chars)
Abstract

Abdominal organ segmentation is essential in modern medical applications including computer-aided diagnosis, therapy planning, and surgical navigation. While existing deep learning approaches demonstrate promising performance, their heavy reliance on large-scale patient image data covering multiple domains raises concerns over generalization capability and privacy risks. This motivates the development of generalizable segmentation training paradigms based on domain-randomized image synthesis, where networks are trained using synthesized images with diverse contrasts and resolutions, facilitating generalization across unseen real image domains. However, existing paradigms either suffer from limited segmentation performance when applied to abdominal organs, or require dataset-specific finetuning. To address these limitations, we propose ReSeg, a novel two-stage retraining-free framework for domain-agnostic zero-shot abdominal organ segmentation. At the first stage, our organ-agnostic synthetic data-driven pretraining approach exploits semantically and visually diverse synthetic images generated by a dedicated data engine to optimize a Generalizable Semantic Representation Network (GSRNet). The network is thereby capable of learning semantic features robust against variations in anatomical structure, imaging contrast, resolution and spatial context, which provide universal representation capabilities that serve as a foundation for segmentation learning. Subsequently, in the second stage, we continue to train an Organ-specific Encoder-decoder Segmentation Network (OESNet), whose encoder is initialized with the pretrained weights. This training process is facilitated by another dedicated data engine, which generates diversified visual images while strictly retaining the inherent spatial layout of abdominal organs. To enhance organ-relevant region perception, OESNet further incorporates a Semantics-guided Segmentation (SS) decoder, which hierarchically integrates rich semantic priors derived from the well-trained GSRNet. Comprehensive evaluation on seven abdominal organ segmentation datasets across three different modalities including CT, MRI, and PET demonstrates that ReSeg consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods while eliminating the need for real training images. The source code will be publicly available at https://github.com/Kendra9999/ReSeg.

## 1 Introduction

Abdominal organ segmentation plays a pivotal role in medical imaging by enabling precise organ localization and volumetric analysis, serving as a foundation for computer-aided diagnosis (Chan et al., 2020), therapy planning (Chen et al., 2021), and surgical navigation (Okamoto et al., 2015). For instance, liver segmentation aids focal lesion detection and liver fibrosis staging (Tong et al., 2015; Gao et al., 2023), and kidney segmentation from preoperative scans is a prerequisite for building 3D models that support augmented reality navigation in laparoscopic partial nephrectomy (Zhang et al., 2019). Manual segmentation of abdominal organs is time-consuming, labor-intensive, and error-prone, thus spurring the need for accurate automatic segmentation algorithms.

Automated segmentation of abdominal organs from volumetric medical images is a challenging task. First, soft tissues usually have low contrast characteristic in abdominal imaging, leading to weak boundaries between adjacent anatomical structures (Wang et al., 2019). Second, the abdominal cavity presents a complex background with highly variable morphological structures, whose shapes and sizes exhibit significant heterogeneity among patients (Ma et al., 2021). Third, abdominal images acquired from different clinical settings often vary substantially in terms of field of view (FOV), introducing inconsistencies in the contextual information associated with organs.

The field has witnessed remarkable progress through deep learning approaches to meet the challenges mentioned above (Isensee et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025). This progress has shifted the research focus from dataset-specific models towards universal foundation models, which offer improved performance while enhancing generalization capability. Some studies leverage approaches inspired by Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining (CLIP) (Liu et al., 2023) or Segment Anything Model (SAM) (Du et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024; He et al., 2025) to handle diverse segmentation tasks, while others advance representation learning methods to pretrain networks with powerful feature extraction capability. These include reconstruction-based methods like MAE (He et al., 2022), SimMIM (Xie et al., 2022), and MiM (Zhuang et al., 2025a), as well as contrastive learning approaches such as VoCo (Wu et al., 2024), UAE (Bai et al., 2025), and SuperCL (Zeng et al., 2026). Finetuning these pretrained networks on downstream segmentation datasets has yielded promising results.

Despite these advantages, most existing biomedical foundation segmentation methods face a fundamental limitation: they require large amounts of medical image datasets covering multiple domains to achieve excellent generalization performance and inherently have high reliance on real medical images for training (Liu et al., 2023; Du et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024; He et al., 2025). Such reliance introduces not only privacy risks but also modality-specific biases, as significant domain gaps persist with large-scale data (Huang et al., 2025), leading models to learn suboptimal feature representations. This results in limited generalization in real clinical scenarios, particularly when processing images from entirely unseen biomedical scenarios with variant imaging modalities, such as Computed Tomography (CT), Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), and Positron Emission Tomography (PET). Hence, there is an urgent need for a domain-agnostic zero-shot segmentation approach, where the model can segment images from unseen domains with cross-modality discrepancies and intra-modality variations.

Recently, there have been research efforts dedicated to developing generalizable segmentation training paradigms based on domain-randomized image synthesis strategies in brain MR image analysis (Billot et al., 2023; Ayaz et al., 2024; Gopinath et al., 2025). These approaches train Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) exclusively on synthetic images sampled from a generative model conditioned on label maps, eliminating the requirement for real training images. By leveraging a domain randomization strategy, they generate pseudo training images with varying contrasts and resolutions, exposing models to extensive variability during training. This forces the models to learn domain-independent features, enabling effective generalization to real clinical scans across diverse domains during inference. Beyond brain imaging, such a paradigm has also been extended in existing literature to segmentation tasks targeting other anatomical structures, including vascular structures (Chollet et al., 2024) and cellular nuclei (Dey et al., 2024). Building on the domain randomization concept, Anatomix (Dey et al., 2025) further introduced a general-purpose representation learning framework powered by a dedicated data engine, which synthesizes highly variable training samples from compositions of organ shape templates. This enables the pretraining of a network that serves as a strong, dataset-agnostic initialization for finetuning on new segmentation datasets.

Despite significant progress achieved so far, to achieve generalizable segmentation of abdominal organs without access to real training images, several challenges persist. First, most existing approaches are designed for structures with relatively fixed anatomical topology and stable tissue intensity distribution such as the brain (Billot et al., 2023), the vascular structures (Chollet et al., 2024), and the cellular nuclei (Dey et al., 2024), and thus fall short in segmentation performance when applied to abdominal organs (as demonstrated by the experimental results presented in Fig. 1), which exhibit large morphological variations among patients. Second, it is challenging to generate realistic synthetic abdominal data due to the lack of standardized anatomical atlases and refined annotated templates, resulting in severe domain gaps between synthetic and real clinical scans. Third, the existing domain-randomization-based representation learning method Anatomix (Dey et al., 2025) focuses on acquiring appearance-agnostic representations, yet overlooks the contextual consistency in the learned features. This may lead to diminished performance when segmenting images across varying FOVs where contextual information changes. Finally, although the existing representation learning method Anatomix (Dey et al., 2025) enhances domain-agnostic features, additional finetuning on downstream datasets is required for adapting the pretrained network to task-specific segmentation, limiting their applicability in retraining-free settings.

To address these limitations, we first propose a Generalizable Appearance (GA) model to generate diversified pseudo-images based on organ shape templates, thereby eliminating the reliance on real annotated images for network training. This model realizes full randomization of image contrast and resolution via domain randomization. On this basis, we further develop two dedicated data engines, namely GenRep and GenSeg, which are respectively devised for organ-agnostic semantic representation learning and abdominal-organ-specific segmentation learning. Specifically, GenRep generates image samples with rich semantic layouts and visual variations to capture organ-agnostic, generalizable semantic representations. In contrast, GenSeg yields appearance-variant pseudo-images while retaining inherent anatomical spatial configurations. Combined with background simulation empowered by a newly designed Label Processing (LP) model, it enables optimization for abdominal organ segmentation without any real training images.

Built upon these two data engines, we propose a two-stage retraining-free framework, referred to as ReSeg, for real-training-image-free abdominal organ segmentation, as visualized in Fig. 1-(a). Specifically, at the first stage, our organ-agnostic synthetic data-driven pretraining approach exploits semantically and visually diverse synthetic images generated by the GenRep data engine to optimize a Generalizable Semantic Representation Network (GSRNet). The network is thereby capable of learning semantic features robust against variations in anatomical structure, imaging contrast, resolution and spatial context, which provide universal representation capabilities that serve as a foundation for segmentation learning. This is done by designing a novel Multi-patch Context-aware Contrastive (MCC) loss that encourages domain-invariant feature alignment amidst contextual variations. Subsequently, in the second stage, we continue to train an Organ-specific Encoder-decoder Segmentation Network (OESNet), whose encoder is initialized with the pretrained weights. This training process is facilitated by the GenSeg data engine, which generates diversified visual images while strictly retaining the inherent spatial layout of abdominal organs. To enhance organ-relevant region perception, OESNet further incorporates a Semantics-guided Segmentation (SS) decoder, which hierarchically integrates rich semantic priors derived from the well-trained GSRNet. During inference, ReSeg can segment real clinical images from multiple domains without retraining or finetuning. ReSeg achieves superior performance compared with existing state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods on seven abdominal segmentation datasets across three different modalities (see Fig. 1-(b)). Our primary contributions are summarized as follows:

1. 

We introduce a Generalizable Appearance (GA) model that can synthesize images with diverse contrasts and resolutions from given organ shape templates. Based on this model, we further introduce two dedicated data engines, referred to as GenRep and GenSeg, respectively designed to support organ-agnostic semantic representation learning and abdominal-organ-specific segmentation learning.

2. 

Built upon these two data engines, we propose ReSeg, a novel two-stage retraining-free framework for real-training-image-free abdominal organ segmentation.

3. 

In the proposed framework, the generalizable semantic representation learning at the first stage is achieved by designing a novel Multi-patch Context-aware Contrastive (MCC) loss that encourages domain-invariant feature alignment amidst contextual variations, while the abdominal-organ-specific segmentation learning at the second stage is facilitated by the pretrained weights and by incorporating semantic priors into a Semantics-guided Segmentation (SS) decoder.

4. 

Comprehensive experiments on seven abdominal organ segmentation datasets across three different modalities including CT, MRI and PET demonstrate the superior performance of our proposed ReSeg over the SOTA approaches.

## 2 Related work

## 2.1 Dataset-specific models for medical image segmentation

The unprecedented developments of deep learning have revolutionized the field of medical image segmentation, driving a paradigm shift from traditional methods (Wolz et al., 2013; Tong et al., 2015) toward deep learning-based approaches (Isensee et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2022). The U-shaped encoder-decoder framework, exemplified by U-Net (Ronneberger et al., 2015) with skip connections and multi-scale feature extraction, has become the dominant architecture. Numerous variants have been developed to further improve segmentation accuracy for various anatomical structures, like abdominal organs (Ma et al., 2021; Ji et al., 2022). One strategy integrates attention mechanisms to recalibrate feature responses and emphasize target regions, thereby mitigating interference from complex abdominal background and enhancing boundary delineation of ambiguous organs (Wang et al., 2019; Zheng et al., 2025). Transformer-based architectures have also been adopted to capture long-range spatial dependencies across volumetric abdominal images, complementing local CNN features with a global perspective (Cao et al., 2022; Ding et al., 2026). More recently, Mamba-based models have emerged as promising alternatives, offering long-range modeling with linear complexity and thus balancing performance and computational efficiency for volumetric abdominal image segmentation (Ruan et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025; Tan et al., 2026). Despite these advances, these methods are typically trained on a single dataset and thus remain dataset-specific, lacking the ability to generalize across heterogeneous imaging domains.

## 2.2 Self-supervised learning for medical image segmentation

To improve model generalization capability, remarkable progress has been witnessed in developing foundation models pretrained on large-scale medical image datasets (Butoi et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Du et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024; He et al., 2025). A key direction within this effort involves designing self-supervised proxy tasks to enhance representation learning. These approaches typically fall into two categories. The first employs reconstructive losses to model anatomical structures and their spatial relationships, with representative works including MAE (He et al., 2022), SimMIM (Xie et al., 2022), and MiM (Zhuang et al., 2025a). The second category, by contrast, utilizes contrastive losses to learn consistent semantic representations. This is achieved by enforcing feature similarity within a predefined semantic space, as exemplified by VoCo (Wu et al., 2024), UAE (Bai et al., 2025), and SuperCL (Zeng et al., 2026). Despite significant progress, existing representation learning methods remain limited by their high reliance on heuristics tailored to their target datasets and typically learn feature similarity within a single imaging modality. This leads to compromised generalization when applied to new biomedical domains. Although UAE (Bai et al., 2025) extends feature learning to cross-modality CT-MRI scenarios, its iterative training process is computationally intensive, and the learned features remain insufficient under significant domain shifts (e.g. CT or MRI to PET). ## CONCLUSION
Domain-randomized image synthesis offers a promising way to eliminate the need for real training images and support generalizable segmentation learning. However, it remains underexplored whether such synthetic data-driven training paradigms can be effectively applied to generalizable abdominal organ segmentation without requiring domain-specific adaptation. To mitigate the gap, we propose ReSeg, a two-stage retraining-free framework for zero-shot segmentation of abdominal scans. Built upon two delicately designed data engines, our proposed two-stage network first learns organ-agnostic, generalizable semantic features, and then transitions to acquiring abdominal-organ-specific semantic features. We conducted comprehensive experiments on seven abdominal segmentation datasets encompassing different imaging modalities (CT, MRI, and PET), varying spatial resolutions, and diverse patient demographics to evaluate the generalization capability of ReSeg to various unseen domains. Across all evaluation datasets, our method consistently outperformed the competing SOTA approaches, as evidenced by the results presented in Tables 1, 2, and 3, and Figures 8, 9, 10, and 11. To further investigate the effectiveness of the proposed GA model, the MCC loss, and the architectural components of the proposed OESNet, we conducted comprehensive ablation studies. The experimental results presented in Tables 4, 5, and 6 provide a detailed assessment of how different components of the proposed method contribute to the segmentation generalization performance.

Direct application of existing domain-randomization-based segmentation training paradigms like SynthSeg (Billot et al., 2023) to abdominal organ segmentation proved ineffective (Tables 1, 2, and 3). Thus, we develop two dedicated data engines with the newly designed GA model to enhance feature learning in the complex scenarios of volumetric abdominal images. In particular, the GA model uses a dynamic lower bound for the mean parameters in the GMMs to better capture the large contrast variations encountered in abdominal imaging. This strategy significantly enhances the generalization capability of the segmentation network, as evidenced by the improved performance shown in Table 4. Our delicately designed data engines enable generalizable segmentation learning for abdominal anatomy, which exhibit high variations in appearance and background among patients and thus pose significant zero-shot segmentation challenges.
