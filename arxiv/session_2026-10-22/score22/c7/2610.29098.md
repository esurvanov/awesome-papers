Authors: Derong Yu, Qingsong Zhao, Yanbing Yue, Yikai Li, Zhi Zhang, Guoyan Zheng

## ABSTRACT
One-shot landmark localization in volume scans with varying fields of view (FOVs) is challenging. While existing self-supervised learning methods have shown promising performance, they rely on contrastive learning built upon augmented views of the same image, which inherently limits their generalization in cross-modality scenarios. This motivates research on cross-modality embedding learning using paired multi-modality images. However, existing methods suffer from not only limited training data, but also the requirement of multi-stage iterative training. To address these limitations, we propose UNIVERS, an end-to-end trainable framework that learns UNIversal Voxel-wise Embedding via Randomized Synthesis. Our framework leverages diverse pseudo-data synthesized from organ shape templates, with randomization applied to semantic layout, visual appearance, FOV, and spatial pose. Coupled with cross-domain discriminative voxel-wise contrastive learning, this domain randomization strategy forces the network to disregard domain-specific biases and learn features that are robust against variations in imaging contrast, resolution, and spatial context. Consequently, the trained model enables one-shot landmark localization in multi-modality volume scans with arbitrary FOVs. In this study, the efficacy of the proposed method is demonstrated by applying it to the task of localizing the adrenal glands, which are paired, small, and irregular endocrine organs. In particular, comprehensive experiments are conducted on three challenging adrenal gland datasets. The experimental results demonstrate that UNIVERS consistently outperforms the state-of-the-art (SOTA) one-shot landmark localization approaches, with particularly prominent advantages in cross-modality scenarios. The source code is publicly available at https://github.com/Kendra9999/UNIVERS.

## I Introduction

The adrenal glands are paired, small, irregular endocrine organs located superior to both kidneys [1]. By secreting essential hormones, they play vital roles in regulating stress response, metabolism, electrolyte homeostasis, and sympathetic nervous system [2, 3]. Adrenal disorders, such as adrenal hyperplasia and adrenal tumor, are frequently encountered in routine radiological imaging [4]. Such abnormalities can disrupt hormonal balance, resulting in hypertension and other hormone-related symptoms [5]. Computer-aided interpretation of adrenal imaging offers a noninvasive and effective approach, playing an increasingly critical role in the clinical management of adrenal-related diseases. Due to the extremely small size of the adrenal glands, precise localization of their centers in volumetric medical images becomes an essential preprocessing step for many downstream computer-aided tasks, such as adrenal segmentation [3] and lesion classification [6]. Existing landmark localization methods mostly rely on supervised learning [7, 8], which requires large amounts of manually annotated training data. Unfortunately, annotating medical images is laborious, costly, and requires clinical expertise.

To reduce the annotation burden, recent research has shifted towards one-shot landmark localization, which requires only a single annotated support image to identify corresponding landmarks in query images [9, 10, 11, 12]. An emerging paradigm to find such landmark correspondences is achieved by learning discriminative voxel-wise anatomical embeddings. Typically, two augmented, overlapping views are extracted from the same image and fed into a Siamese network to generate voxel-wise embeddings. Then, self-supervised contrastive learning is applied, which encourages corresponding voxels across the two views to have similar embeddings while pushing non-corresponding voxels apart in the embedding space. Given the anatomical structural consistency, the learned embeddings map corresponding landmarks to similar feature representations. Consequently, annotations from a single support image can be transferred to query images via feature similarity matching. Building on this concept, several strategies have been proposed to enhance landmark localization performance, including negative sampling [10], coarse-to-fine architectures [9, 10, 11], and semantic embedding learning [12]. More recently, Universal Anatomical Embedding (UAE-M) [12] has extended this line of work to cross-modality landmark matching between Computed Tomography (CT) and Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI). This method learns voxel correspondences across unaligned multi-modality images via an iterative training process.

Despite significant progress, several limitations persist. First, most existing methods [9, 10, 11] build upon the heuristic that corresponding voxels across augmented views of the same image should yield similar embeddings, while their training is restricted to single-modality data. As a result, these methods struggle to match corresponding anatomical structures across multi-modality images. Second, while UAE-M [12] addresses cross-modality landmark matching, it relies on paired multi-modality images of the same subject. Such image pairs are usually unaligned across modalities, necessitating multi-stage iterative training to establish reliable voxel correspondences. More importantly, existing methods [9, 10, 11, 12] rely heavily on real clinical images, yet it is challenging, if not impossible, to collect large-scale multi-modality datasets. Accordingly, networks are exposed to a limited spectrum of domain variations in anatomical structure, imaging contrast, spatial resolution, field of view (FOV), and patient pose. Such insufficient data diversity results in suboptimal feature representations that lack robustness to large domain shifts in real clinical scenarios.

To address these limitations, we propose UNIVERS, an end-to-end trainable framework that learns UNIversal Voxel-wise Embedding via Randomized Synthesis for generalizable one-shot landmark localization. Instead of relying on real training images, our learning process utilizes synthetic data generated from organ shape templates, where randomization is introduced in semantic layout, visual appearance, FOV, and spatial pose. This strategy exposes the network to a wide range of variability during contrastive learning, forcing it to learn features that are robust to variations in imaging contrast, resolution, and spatial context. Discriminative voxel-wise embedding learning is then achieved via a Cross-domain Discriminative Voxel-wise Contrastive (CDVC) loss, which encourages the voxel-wise discriminability of domain-invariant features amidst contextual variations. During inference, UNIVERS relies exclusively on anatomical structural consistency to distinguish voxels, generating domain-invariant voxel-wise embeddings for real clinical images from multiple domains without retraining or finetuning. UNIVERS achieves superior performance compared with existing state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods under three one-shot evaluation settings: intra-dataset, cross-dataset intra-modality, cross-dataset cross-modality. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

• 

We propose UNIVERS, an end-to-end trainable framework for learning UNIversal Voxel-wise Embedding via Randomized Synthesis. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to learn generalizable, discriminative voxel-wise embeddings exclusively from pseudo-data synthesized via domain randomization.

• 

We design a novel Cross-domain Discriminative Voxel-wise Contrastive (CDVC) loss, which operates on multi-domain pseudo-patch quadruplets with multiple positives to drive the network toward learning domain-invariant and discriminative embeddings.

• 

We develop a coarse-to-fine one-shot adrenal gland localization framework built upon a single frozen UNIVERS network, which enables landmark localization in multi-modality volume scans with arbitrary FOVs.

• 

We conduct comprehensive experiments under three one-shot settings across three distinct datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that our proposed UNIVERS consistently outperforms the SOTA approaches.

## II Related work

## II-A One-shot landmark localization

To mitigate the annotation burden, one-shot landmark localization has garnered increasing research interest. Traditional solutions rely on image registration to establish voxel correspondences, typically employing a two-stage alignment pipeline: a global affine or rigid transformation [13] followed by a deformable registration [14, 15, 16]. By deriving a deformation field that warps the query image into the support image space, registration methods can propagate support landmarks to the query image. However, such methods often incur high computational costs and struggles with large inter-subject variations, including anatomical deformations and FOV shifts.

Alternatively, a growing body of work has explored embedding-learning-based paradigms on 3D CT images [9, 10, 11, 12]. These methods seek to learn discriminative voxel-wise embeddings, so that corresponding landmarks between the support and query images can be matched based on feature similarity. A representative framework is Self-supervised Anatomical eMbedding (SAM) [10], which employs a coarse-to-fine architecture and designs a hard and diverse sampling strategy for effective negative selection. However, SAM primarily captures contextual appearance information and lacks explicit semantic understanding, which hinders its ability to differentiate organs with similar appearances. This limitation motivated UAE-S [12], which extends SAM with an additional semantic head trained via a prototypical supervised contrastive loss. Another line of work is Localize Anything Model for 3D Medical images (MedLAM) [11], which introduces unified anatomical mapping and multi-scale similarity learning. To estimate landmark localization, it applies Relative Position Regression (RPR) [9] followed by multi-scale feature similarity matching. Despite these advances, these methods are trained on datasets within the same modality and contrast voxels across augmented views of the same image, inherently limiting their generalization across heterogeneous imaging domains.

To enable landmark matching in cross-modality scenarios, UAE-M [12] extends the embedding-learning-based paradigm to CT-MRI settings via a two-stage learning process. At the first stage, a modality-agnostic network is trained on single-modality images with strong contrast augmentation. Subsequently, at the second stage, this modality-agnostic network initializes an iterative training process, which alternates between learning cross-modality embeddings from aligned multi-modality images and performing cross-modality registration to align those images. Despite its success, UAE-M relies on computationally expensive multi-stage iterative training to establish robust voxel correspondences between CT and MR images. By contrast, our method offers a simple, end-to-end trainable framework that learns domain-invariant voxel-wise embeddings from a distinct perspective by training on synthetic data generated via domain randomization.

## II-B Domain randomization for medical image analysis

Domain-randomization-based generative models aim to bridge all domain gaps via extreme augmentation beyond realism, eliminating the need for real training images [17, 18]. These methods fully randomize generation parameters to produce highly diverse training data, forcing networks to learn domain-independent features. For brain segmentation, methods like SynthSeg [19] use visually diverse pseudo-images synthesized from brain label maps to train networks, which can be applied to real brain MR scans across multiple domains without retraining. This paradigm has subsequently been extended to other segmentation tasks, including vascular structures [20], cell nuclei [21], and abdominal organs [22]. For image registration, SynthMorph [23] proposed a generative strategy to produce diverse synthetic label maps and images, enabling networks to learn contrast-invariant registration. In the realm of representation learning, Anatomix [24] proposed a data engine that randomizes the spatial configuration of organ shape templates to create paired image samples with distinct appearances. These samples then train a network robust to nuisance imaging variations that do not alter image semantics. However, none of these works were designed for generalizable one-shot landmark localization. This task imposes a unique requirement: each voxel must be encoded with a discriminative embedding that sets it apart from other anatomical regions. Learning such universal discriminative voxel-wise embeddings is the core focus of our work, which is not directly addressed by existing domain-randomization-based methods.

## III Method

## III-A Learning universal voxel-wise embedding

Fig. 1: A schematic overview of UNIVERS. (a) We construct an image pair database and a patch quadruplet database from shape templates. (b) UNIVERS consists of a shared CNN encoder with a Domain-invariant Discriminative (DD) decoder and a Domain-invariant Semantic (DS) decoder. During training, patch quadruplets are randomly sampled to train the DD decoder via a Cross-domain Discriminative Voxel-wise Contrastive (CDVC) loss, while image pairs are randomly sampled to optimize the DS decoder via a Cross-domain Prototypical Semantic Contrastive (CPSC) loss. (c) During inference, UNIVERS can generate Domain-invariant Voxel-wise (DV) embeddings for multi-modality volumes with arbitrary FOVs. Embeddings are reduced to 3 dimensions via PCA and visualized as RGB images. RGA: Random Geometric Augmentation. Embed.: Embedding.

UNIVERS learns domain-invariant voxel-wise embeddings without requiring any real training images. For training, we rely solely on freely available organ shape templates from the TotalSegmentator CT dataset [25], which comprises 1,204 volumes with 104 annotated anatomical structures, to synthesize pseudo-training data. To encode precise location information for each voxel, UNIVERS is designed to acquire both high-level semantic understanding to distinguish different organs and low-level discriminative capacity to differentiate adjacent structures. To this end, we generate two specific types of pseudo-data, forming an image pair database and a patch quadruplet database (see Fig. 1-(a)). Each image pair consists of two pseudo-images that share a randomized semantic layout but differ in appearance, thereby facilitating domain-invariant semantic feature learning. In contrast, each patch quadruplet is derived from an image pair and comprises four overlapping pseudo-patches with distinct FOVs and spatial poses, encouraging local discriminability amidst varying contexts. UNIVERS then employs a dual-decoder network design: it comprises a shared Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) encoder with a Domain-invariant Semantic (DS) decoder and a Domain-invariant Discriminative (DD) decoder (see Fig. 1-(b)). The DS decoder is optimized on the image pairs by leveraging a Cross-domain Prototypical Semantic Contrastive (CPSC) loss, while the DD decoder is trained with the patch quadruplets and supervised via a Cross-domain Discriminative Voxel-wise Contrastive (CDVC) loss.

## III-A1 Data generation

For training data, we first build the image pair database, based on which we further construct the patch quadruplet database.

Image pair generation. Randomized image pairs with rich semantic layouts and appearances are synthesized from shape templates via a data engine (see Fig. 1-(a1)). Following the pipeline introduced in [24], the data engine first creates diverse randomized label maps with a fixed size of $S_{I}\times S_{I}\times S_{I}$ voxels using randomly sampled templates. For each randomized label map $\mathcal{M}$ , it then synthesizes two pseudo-images, $I^{1}$ and $I^{2}$ , which share an identical semantic layout but exhibit distinct appearances, effectively simulating aligned scans from two different domains. Such image synthesis involves sampling intensities from Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) with randomized parameters, followed by intensity and geometric augmentations. To preserve semantic alignment while maximizing appearance variance within an image pair, intensity augmentations are applied independently to $I^{1}$ and $I^{2}$ , whereas geometric augmentations are shared. These pseudo-data constitutes the image pair database $\{(I^{1},I^{2}|\mathcal{M})\}$ .

Patch quadruplet generation. Randomized patch quadruplets with diverse FOVs and spatial poses are generated from the randomized image pairs. Specifically, to construct a patch quadruplet, we first randomly sample an image pair $(I^{1},I^{2})$ , where $I^{1}$ and $I^{2}$ are spatially aligned. We then extract two patches from each image— $P_{1}^{1}$ and $P_{2}^{1}$ from $I^{1}$ , and $P_{1}^{2}$ and $P_{2}^{2}$ from $I^{2}$ . As shown in Fig. 1-(a2), we propose two distinct patch quadruplet generation pipelines that separately simulate different sources of spatial variation, and randomly select one per quadruplet:

• 

Random cropping pipeline: This pipeline predominantly simulates patches with large FOV variations. Each patch is randomly cropped from an image at a random location with a random size of $S_{R}\times S_{R}\times S_{R}$ voxels, where $S_{R}$ is sampled from a uniform distribution $U(S_{L},S_{U})$ . Here $S_{L}$ and $S_{U}$ respectively denote the lower bound and the upper bound of $S_{R}$ , which are constrained to ensure that the four cropped patches spatially overlap (see Section III-C for detailed parameter values). All patches are subsequently resized to $S_{P}\times S_{P}\times S_{P}$ voxels.

• 

Random geometric augmentation pipeline: This pipeline primarily simulates patches with large spatial pose variations.

## CONCLUSION
In this paper, we proposed an end-to-end trainable framework that learns UNIversal Voxel-wise Embedding via Randomized Synthesis, referred to as UNIVERS, for generalizable one-shot landmark localization in multi-modality volume scans of arbitrary FOVs. For model training, a domain-randomization-based data generation strategy was delicately designed to synthesize pseudo-image pairs and pseudo-patch quadruplets covering a wide range of appearances and spatial contexts. Built upon these pseudo-training data, a cross-domain voxel-wise discriminative contrastive learning strategy was then introduced to establish a domain-invariant embedding space that enables effective differentiation of anatomical structures. For accurate adrenal center localization at inference time, a coarse-to-fine one-shot localization framework was further developed by integrating a single frozen UNIVERS network. Extensive experiments across three challenging datasets, conducted under three one-shot settings with increasing difficulty, consistently demonstrated the superior performance of UNIVERS over the SOTA methods. These findings validate the strong generalization capacity of the proposed framework and position UNIVERS as a promising approach for one-shot landmark localization in real clinical applications.
