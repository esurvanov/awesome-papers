# 2610.29135 — LaVGuard: Selective Decoding for Contextual Value Drift
Authors: Jiale Dai, Liuxian Ma, Hongcan Deng, Xiaoke Niu, Wenjing Zhang, Zhaoxiang Liu, Shiguo Lian, Guojie Song

Categories: cs.AI

## ABSTRACT
A content-safe response can still promote priorities that conflict with its task: an assistant may demand compliance when asked to preserve autonomy. We study contextual value drift as a task-relative decoding problem with an explicit allowance for change. LaVGuard separates an authorized ten-dimensional profile from a frozen estimate of the values expressed in the visible prefix. A profile-relative density gate selects when to evaluate candidate continuations; their signed progress determines a logit tilt constrained by both changed-position and cumulative-KL budgets. We establish local surrogate improvement, pathwise budget feasibility, and a bound on response-distribution change. Profile conditioning raises boundary TPR at 5% FPR from .58 to .72. At the same .12 mean intervention ratio, the selection study reports risk-safety scores of .88 for LaVGuard and .72 for position-matched edits. The resulting interface makes task priorities actionable while keeping the extent of intervention explicit and bounded.

## 1 Introduction

An assistant helping a team organize a project is asked to preserve each member’s autonomy. “Assign everyone a role and require compliance” contains no prohibited content, yet privileges conformity over the requested freedom of choice. “Offer clear roles and let members choose” respects both coordination and autonomy. What matters is not just which values an answer expresses, but whether their emphasis fits the authorized task. Figure 1 illustrates this distinction.

Figure 1: Task-relative selective decoding. The authorized profile and the frozen probe’s visible-prefix state meet in the residual; the two-dimensional region illustrates the fitted geometry. Boundary exceedance and remaining position and KL allowances enable candidate evaluation. The paired continuations express contrasting priorities under a shared content guard. Along the decoding ribbon, cinnabar marks a changed sampling kernel, counted by $I_{t}$ ; $K_{t}$ is that step’s conditional KL cost. Unchanged kernels consume neither allowance. The task, trajectory, responses, and ribbon are constructed illustrations; profile and state cells indicate dimensions.

We call a departure from the task’s authorized priorities contextual value drift. Values such as security, achievement, and self-direction can each be appropriate; their intended emphasis depends on the task. Value taxonomies provide a vocabulary for these co-occurring priorities (Schwartz, 1992; Yao et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2024). A task-relative controller therefore needs to distinguish the priorities expressed by a prefix from the priorities the application requests. It must also decide how much of the ongoing generation to change: acting at every position can spend intervention on language that already fits the task.

Existing methods make several parts of this problem tractable. ConVA selectively activates value directions (Jin et al., 2025); Values as Style learns a semantic–value editing interface for low-damage steering (Dai et al., 2026); AIMES adapts value-specific activation strengths from online observations (Bhattacharjee and Tandon, 2026). Controlled Decoding and Anchored Decoding connect generation to KL-regularized objectives and sequence-level divergence budgets, respectively (Mudgal et al., 2024; He et al., 2026). We address the joint decision these tools motivate: when does the current language depart from this task’s priorities, and how should a finite change allowance shape its continuation?

LaVGuard couples a task-relative trigger with two explicit resources: the number of positions changed and the cumulative divergence from the base decoder. An authorized profile is supplied before generation. A frozen, prefix-causal probe estimates ten value intensities from visible language, without reading the numeric profile. Subtracting the profile translates a learned residual geometry into the task’s activation region. At an active position, the model evaluates likely one-token continuations, favors progress toward a mixture-derived reference, and chooses a feasible tilt by scalar KL search. The content guard is shared with the base decoder.

Changing the authorized profile while holding the language state fixed can change whether intervention is warranted, without retraining the probe. Appendix J follows this decision through candidate scoring, sampling, and budget exhaustion.

Our evaluation tests profile conditioning, position selection, and budget control. Profile conditioning raises boundary TPR at 5% FPR from $.58$ to $.72$ (Table 1). At a shared mean intervention ratio of $.12$ , LaVGuard reaches a risk-safety score of $.88$ versus $.72$ for position-matched selection (Table 3). Removing the position budget increases the edit ratio to $.34$ , with risk safety of $.89$ and benign safety of $.73$ , compared with $.88$ and $.81$ for the full controller. These comparisons motivate selecting where to spend the allowance, as well as limiting its total size.

Our contributions are:

• 

A task-relative control interface. We separate authorized priorities from observed prefix values, making the same language state evaluable under different task profiles.

• 

Selective decoding with two resources. A profile-residual gate, candidate scoring, and a feasible scalar update jointly control changed positions and accumulated conditional KL.

• 

An analysis of what this control establishes. We connect the local candidate surrogate to the tilt, prove budget and response-distribution bounds, and evaluate the roles of profile conditioning, position selection, and the two allowances.

## 2 Related Work

## Values and task context.

Schwartz’s values describe co-occurring motivational priorities (Schwartz, 1992). FULCRA and ValueBench evaluate these priorities in language models (Yao et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2024); Social Chemistry and Moral Stories ground normative judgments in context (Forbes et al., 2020; Emelin et al., 2021). Value Alignment Tax studies cross-value effects (Chen and Shen, 2026). More recently, Liscio et al. (2026) track competing value orientations across conversations, showing that early steering effects can fade or reverse. LaVGuard makes the task reference explicit: an authorized profile and the observed prefix state remain separate inputs to a residual-based decision.

## Attribute and reward guidance.

PPLM, GeDi, DExperts, and FUDGE guide generation using attribute models or auxiliary distributions (Dathathri et al., 2020; Krause et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2021; Yang and Klein, 2021). FUDGE predicts an eventual attribute from a prefix; our probe estimates values expressed in the visible text. DeAL uses reward-guided lookahead, IVG combines token- and chunk-level value functions, and Controlled Decoding trains prefix scorers for KL-regularized reward optimization (Huang et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2024; Mudgal et al., 2024). LaVGuard uses candidate reweighting with a task-relative trigger and a local prototype-progress score.

## Value editing and semantic preservation.

Representation Engineering, ITI, and CAA expose behavioral directions in hidden states (Zou et al., 2023a; Li et al., 2023; Rimsky et al., 2024). Values as Style learns a semantic–value interface with one-way semantic grounding; editing the value code while holding the semantic code fixed produces a residual-stream update (Dai et al., 2026). Selective Steering uses norm-preserving rotations at discriminatively selected layers (Dang and Ngo, 2026). These designs improve the intervention interface. LaVGuard controls the sampling kernel and accounts for its cumulative change relative to the base decoder.

## Selective and adaptive intervention.

ConVA gates value-vector activation with query and token classifiers (Jin et al., 2025). MAT-Steer learns token gates and multiple attribute directions with sparsity and orthogonality objectives (Nguyen et al., 2025). AIMES uses intermediate-layer vocabulary readouts to adapt each requested value’s steering strength during generation (Bhattacharjee and Tandon, 2026). These controllers adapt activation edits to the current input or state. LaVGuard combines a profile-translated residual boundary with explicit allowances for changed positions and conditional KL. Appendix L compares the observation, actuation, and accounting interfaces.

## Safety and divergence budgets.

SIREN detects harmfulness from internal features, while SafeDecoding, SafeInfer, and SaP target content safety (Jiao et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2024; Banerjee et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025). Anchored Decoding budgets trajectory-wide divergence from a separate safe model for copyright-risk control (He et al., 2026). At the agent level, VoS learns intervention value from counterfactual continuations and calibrates a harm-budgeted trigger (Li et al., 2026). LaVGuard instead accounts for changed token positions and conditional KL against its base decoder along each response, while sharing the content guard with uncontrolled generation.

## 3.1 Profile, language state, and drift

Given prompt $x$ and context $c$ , a frozen autoregressive model generates $y_{1},\ldots,y_{T}$ with next-token distribution

 $p_{t}=p_{M}(\cdot\mid x,c,y_{<t})=\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_{t}).$ 

(1)

Here $p_{t}$ is the actual sampling kernel. Context describes the task, affected parties, and stated constraints. Before generation, an authorized user or policy owner supplies a profile $\boldsymbol{\pi}(c)\in[0,1]^{10}$ . Each coordinate specifies a value intensity; the coordinates need not sum to one.

Let $\mathbf{q}(y,c)$ be an independently annotated value vector and $\mathcal{A}(c)$ the set of value expressions admissible for the task. Contextual drift is the event $\mathbf{q}(y,c)\notin\mathcal{A}(c)$ . Online control uses a learned prefix estimate $\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{<t}$ and a fitted admissible set $\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(c)$ . Evaluation uses independent compatibility judgments, keeping the intended behavior distinct from the controller’s estimate.

## Common content-safety policy.

A shared guard $H$ checks the empty prefix and each sampled prefix. If it activates, a common safe-completion rule replaces the response and terminates generation. Base and controlled decoders use this same rule. Write their conditional kernels as $p_{t}^{H}$ and $\tilde{p}_{t}^{H}$ ; they coincide on guard-stop transitions and otherwise equal $p_{t}$ and $\tilde{p}_{t}$ . This separates explicit content safety from control of priorities among permissible continuations.

## 3.2 An explicit allowance for decoder change

For each generated response, track changed positions and accumulated conditional KL:

 $\displaystyle I_{t}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}[\tilde{p}_{t}^{H}\neq p_{t}^{H}],\quad C_{t}^{tok}=\sum_{j\leq t}I_{j},$ 

(2)

 $\displaystyle C_{t}^{KL}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq t}D_{KL}(\tilde{p}_{j}^{H}\|p_{j}^{H}).$ 

The controller obeys

 $\begin{gathered}C_{T}^{tok}\leq B_{tok},\qquad C_{T}^{KL}\leq B_{KL},\\
D_{KL}(\tilde{p}_{t}^{H}\|p_{t}^{H})\leq\epsilon_{KL}.\end{gathered}$ 

(3)

 $B_{tok}$ is an integer budget; $C_{T}^{tok}/T$ is the realized intervention ratio. Common guard-stop transitions cost zero. A sampled token that triggers the guard retains its incurred cost, including when the output is subsequently replaced. These quantities account for LaVGuard’s changes to the generation process.

## 4 The LaVGuard Controller

## 4.1 Read the visible value trajectory

The probe pools the last $W$ visible positions of $[x;c;y_{<t}]$ at four depth-quartile layer taps $\mathcal{L}$ . Affine projections $P_{\ell}$ produce a shared-width representation:

 $\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\phi}_{<t}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\operatorname*{concat}_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}P_{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{|J_{t}|}\sum_{j\in J_{t}}\mathbf{h}_{t,j}^{(\ell)}\right),$ 

(4)

 $\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{<t}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sigma(W_{v}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{<t}+\mathbf{b}_{v}).$ 

 $J_{t}$ contains the last $\min(W,n_{t})$ input positions. All features precede the sampling of $y_{t}$ . Numeric profile coordinates and annotation labels are excluded from the probe input; the probe reads the visible language, while the profile enters through the controller.

Training uses visible-prefix annotations at 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of each response. Annotators assign intensities in $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$ ; their mean divided by four supplies a soft target, with an adjudicated vector taking precedence when present. Soft-label binary cross-entropy fits the probe while the backbone remains frozen. Validation MAE selects the probe, which is then frozen for boundary fitting and decoding (Appendix B).

## 4.2 Locate the state relative to the profile

Let $\mathbf{r}_{<t}=\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{<t}-\boldsymbol{\pi}(c)$ . A $K$ -component diagonal Gaussian mixture fitted to admissible training residuals defines

 $\displaystyle p_{adm}(\mathbf{r})$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\omega_{k}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{r};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k},\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k}),$ 

(5)

 $\displaystyle D(\mathbf{r})$ 
 $\displaystyle=-\log p_{adm}(\mathbf{r}),\quad b_{<t}=D(\mathbf{r}_{<t})-\tau.$ 

Diagonal variances are shrunk toward the global training variance. A threshold on family-max validation scores is selected under a benign-family FPR constraint, yielding

 $\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(c)=\{\mathbf{v}\in[0,1]^{10}:D(\mathbf{v}-\boldsymbol{\pi}(c))\leq\tau\}.$ 

(6)

The profile translates a shared residual geometry. Thus the same estimated state can lie inside the boundary for one task and outside it for another. Context enters this decision through the declared profile; the probe continues to measure the visible language. Appendix J gives a complete paired calculation of this contrast.

For candidate scoring, the mixture also defines a prototype reference:

 $\mathbf{v}^{*}_{<t}=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\left(\boldsymbol{\pi}(c)+\sum_{k}a_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{<t})\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k}\right),$ 

(7)

where $a_{k}$ are posterior responsibilities. The density level set determines when to act; distance to this prototype determines which candidates to favor. These distinct geometric roles are developed in Appendix C.

## 4.3 Evaluate likely next-token continuations

When $b_{<t}>0$ and both budgets remain, take the top $M$ tokens $\mathcal{C}_{t}$ under $p_{t}$ . Append each candidate $w_{i}$ to the same prefix, evaluate the extended prefixes in batches, and score

 $\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{t,i}^{cand}$ 
 $\displaystyle=f_{\theta}(x,c,y_{<t}\oplus w_{i}),$ 

(8)

 $\displaystyle\Delta_{t,i}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\|\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{<t}-\mathbf{v}^{*}_{<t}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\mathbf{v}_{t,i}^{cand}-\mathbf{v}^{*}_{<t}\|_{2}^{2},$ 

 $\displaystyle s_{t,i}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\Delta_{t,i},&i\in\mathcal{C}_{t},\\
0,&i\notin\mathcal{C}_{t}.\end{cases}$ 

This score measures one-step progress toward the prototype. Its zero extension defines the surrogate on unscored tokens. With a fixed positive scale $\sigma_{b}$ and clipping level $c_{z}$ , the proposed logit direction is

 $d_{t,i}=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{[b_{<t}]_{+}}{\sigma_{b}}s_{t,i},-c_{z},c_{z}\right).$ 

(9)

Boundary exceedance scales the proposal, while candidate progress supplies its direction.

## 4.4 Allocate the remaining change budget

The two budgets regulate distinct decisions. The integer allowance determines whether another position can change; the KL allowance limits how far its sampling distribution can move. For the fixed candidate direction, consider the normalized exponential tilt

 $q_{\alpha}(i)=\frac{p_{t}(i)e^{\alpha d_{t,i}}}{Z_{t}(\alpha)},\quad Z_{t}(\alpha)=\sum_{j}p_{t}(j)e^{\alpha d_{t,j}}.$ 

For a nonconstant direction with budget available, choose

 $\displaystyle\eta_{t}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\min\{\epsilon_{KL},B_{KL}-C_{t-1}^{KL}\},$ 

(10)

 $\displaystyle\alpha_{t}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\max\{\alpha\in[0,1]:D_{KL}(q_{\alpha}\|p_{t})\leq\eta_{t}\}.$ 

Otherwise return $p_{t}$ . The scale searches a one-dimensional family with a fixed direction, rather than recomputing candidate preferences as the budget changes. Sample from $\tilde{p}_{t}=q_{\alpha_{t}}$ and update the counters. Constant directions preserve the distribution and consume no position budget. The implementation uses log-sum-exp normalization and the feasible lower endpoint of a bisection bracket; $J$ iterations bound its width by $2^{-J}$ .

KL is computed between the actual sampling kernels. The reference configuration uses full-support softmax with unit temperature. Any alternative temperature or support convention must be included in the kernels before budget selection. Tokens outside $\mathcal{C}_{t}$ retain their relative probabilities and share the tilt’s normalization factor.

## CONCLUSION
LaVGuard turns an authorized task profile into a selective decoding rule. The profile determines how to interpret an observed value state; candidate progress supplies a local preference; position and KL allowances constrain its effect on generation. The analysis establishes what the local update optimizes and how its expenditure composes into a response-distribution bound. The profile, selection, and budget comparisons support treating where to intervene and how much to change as connected design choices. This yields an explicit interface for task-relative value control while keeping the base decoder and common content guard fixed.
