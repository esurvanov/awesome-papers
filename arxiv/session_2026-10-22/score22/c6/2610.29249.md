## ABSTRACT
Omni-modal assistants must remember not only what users say, but also what they show and convey. Existing long-term memory benchmarks are mostly text-and-image or report a single end-to-end score, so they cannot locate whether evidence is lost when it is represented, retrieved, delivered, or read. We introduce MOSAIC, a benchmark of 923 questions over persona-grounded histories of 11-24 sessions spanning text, image, video, and audio. MOSAIC constructs targeted spatial, temporal, and prosodic signals rather than decorative media, with the strongest causal validation for spatial information and selector-level validation for annotated prosody, and pairs them with a matched diagnostic protocol that freezes the question, reader, prompt, and budget while intervening on representation, retrieval, and delivery. Matched decomposition shows that representation loss is modality-dependent, residual reading dominates even under gold evidence, and abstention remains weak on these controls: strong answerable scores coexist with failures to refuse. MOSAIC thus turns omni-modal memory evaluation from a leaderboard into a diagnosis of write, retrieve, deliver, and read failures.

## 1 Introduction

Omni-modal assistants such as GPT-4o, Gemini, Qwen-Omni, and MiMo (OpenAI et al., 2024; Team et al., 2025; Qwen Team, 2026; Xiaomi LLM-Core Team, 2026) are moving from single-turn demos to persistent, multi-session deployments. A user may share a product photo in January, leave a dietary voice note in March, and later expect both to inform a recommendation. Answering requires not only retrieving temporally scattered evidence, but chaining it across modalities (Figure 1). Prior conversational memory benchmarks omit video/audio, treat images as optional, or stop at text–image co-reference; none combine four-modality multi-session dialogue with a matched stage-diagnostic protocol (Table 1).

Multi-hop text QA (Yang et al., 2018; Trivedi et al., 2022) likewise lacks this multimodal, temporal setting, and coupled system comparisons obscure where evidence is lost. We therefore cast the pipeline as four stages—write, retrieve, deliver, and read (Figure 2)—and diagnose them with matched interventions under a frozen reader, prompt, budget, and judge.

Each stage fails in its own way, motivating four research questions:

• 

RQ1 (Write). Does the stored representation keep the spatial,
temporal, and prosodic detail the answer will later depend on?

• 

RQ2 (Retrieve & Deliver). Is every required unit found,
and does it survive packing into the reader’s request? Media caps can
drop even top-ranked artefacts, so the two are measured separately.

• 

RQ3 (Read). Given the complete delivered chain, can the reader
integrate it across modalities and sessions?

• 

RQ4 (Abstain). Does the system abstain precisely when
required evidence is absent—and only then?

MOSAIC has 923 questions in 11–24-session haystacks with
construction-targeted modality gaps (§3.2). Headline
findings: textualization costs $\sim$ 9 points (spatial-heavy); retrieval costs
 $\sim$ 8 points and packing $\sim$ 4 at production budgets (far more under tight
media caps); $\sim$ 15 points remain under gold symbolic facts; abstention
remains weak on these controls. Under the matched protocol, each paired drop is
associated with the intervened stage; within-item carrier contrasts further
localize signal-specific loss, with the strongest causal validation for spatial
information and additional selector-level validation for annotated prosody
(§6.2).

## 2 Related Work

## Conversational memory benchmarks.

From single-session persona grounding (Zhang et al., 2018) to multi-session suites, text-only benchmarks probe extraction, multi-session and temporal reasoning, knowledge updating, recall, and persona consistency (Zhong et al., 2023; Ou et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025; He et al., 2024; Du et al., 2025). Multimodal extensions remain limited: LoCoMo images are often non-essential (Maharana et al., 2024; Bei et al., 2026); Mem-Gallery and MemLens make images task-critical but stop at text+image and emphasise finding rather than cross-modal chaining (Bei et al., 2026; Ren et al., 2026); ATM-Bench aggregates personal multi-source evidence rather than sequential conversational chaining (Mei et al., 2026); and MMRC, MMDU, MM-NIAH/MMNeedle, Mementos, and M3-Bench add image/video reasoning without multi-session dialogue structure (Xue et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2024b; Wang et al., 2024a; Wang et al., 2024b; Zhou et al., 2026). MOSAIC is the first to require retrieving and chaining evidence across all four primary modalities in multi-session conversations (Table 1).

## Multi-hop and cross-modal reasoning.

HotpotQA, MuSiQue, MultiModalQA, WebQA, and CRIT target compositional multi-hop QA over text and images (Yang et al., 2018; Trivedi et al., 2022; Talmor et al., 2021; Chang et al., 2022; Sung et al., 2026); long-context suites such as BABILong, RULER, and MMLU-Reason chain facts in static corpora (Kuratov et al., 2024; Hsieh et al., 2024; Tie et al., 2025). MOSAIC combines multi-hop reasoning with session-scattered multimodal retrieval.

## Memory-augmented LLM systems.

Memory architectures virtualize or structure long context (Packer et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2023; Sarthi et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024a), integrate graphs with retrieval (Guo et al., 2025; Gutiérrez et al., 2025; Paul et al., 2026), or provide agentic/OS-inspired layers (Xu et al., 2025; Kang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025). They lack a standard omni-modal benchmark for controlled comparison—the role MOSAIC fills.

## RAG and memory diagnosis.

Prior work localizes failures via claim-level RAG diagnostics (Ru et al., 2024), tests of visual evidence use (Hu et al., 2025), controlled document pools (Peng et al., 2026), AMemGym’s write/read/utilization split (Jiayang et al., 2026), and MemFail’s text-memory stages (Garg et al., 2026); localization itself is not novel. We differ in how stages are separated: instead of stressing a still-coupled pipeline, MOSAIC makes each stage substitutable under a frozen reader, prompt, packer, budget, and judge. This measures deliver apart from retrieve and attributes loss to carrier-level signals—to our knowledge the first substitutable four-stage protocol for omni-modal conversational memory.

## 3 Preliminaries

Every MOSAIC item is a persona-grounded, multi-modal query embedded in a chronological haystack of user–assistant sessions. Three orthogonal axes define it, each worked through end-to-end in Appendix A: the question type fixes the reasoning skeleton (§3.1), the modality gap defines what each non-text modality must contribute (§3.2), and the reasoning chain composes Narrower, Selector, and Answer facts into a deterministic retrieval path (§3.3).

## 3.2 Modality Gaps

A modality gap is information that survives only because its native
modality preserves it, while a text-only or caption-based agent would lose it.
Required non-text facts are assigned a gap strategy
(Appendix A): image (background detail, fine-grained
attribute, spatial configuration), video (action ordering, state
transition, repetition count), or audio (happy/unhappy prosody with
neutral wording over $\geq 2$ candidates); Appendix D
breaks down tag coverage. Audio prosody is the most frequent
annotated strategy (450 facts / 399 of 784 answerable items), so Caption-Text /
ASR write paths lose the tone discriminator by design—a pipeline exposure,
not a gold-delivery perception cost (§6.2).

## Challenge by construction.

A media attachment is insufficient to establish multimodal reasoning,
since a caption or transcript may preserve the entire answer. MOSAIC therefore
assigns a challenge before dialogue and media realization, specifying the
native relation that carries a fact together with constraints that keep that
relation out of the surrounding text; every answerable item contains at least
one required challenge-bearing fact (Table 2). Action
Repetition Count is representative: a fixed-camera clip repeats one action with
no cuts and no on-screen counter, and the dialogue says only “a few times,” so
a generic caption preserves the event but loses the selector
needed to continue the chain (per-challenge specifications:
Appendix A).

## 3.3 Reasoning Chains and Fact Roles

Every answerable item has a reasoning chain of required facts with roles
Narrower (NAR; reduces candidates), Selector (SEL; identifies
an entity), and Answer (ANS; carries the final value), in the pattern
 $\text{NAR}^{*}\;\text{SEL}^{*}\;\text{ANS}$ .
Valid chains are bridge-entity-linked and deletion-tested
(§4): removing any required fact makes the
question unanswerable, and audio/video discriminators choose among
 $\geq 2$ same-type entities. A chain is cross-modal when required
facts span at least two modalities; gap-bearing detail usually sits in a
Narrower or Selector, and the Answer’s final
value is always a short text-expressible string (enabling judge
scoring), though the fact carrying it may itself be multimodal (full
constraints and templates: Appendix A).

## 4 The MOSAIC Generation Pipeline

MOSAIC is produced by a fully automated, config-driven four-stage pipeline
(Figure 7; details in Appendix B). Each item
is grounded in a Nemotron-Personas persona (Meyer and Corneil, 2025).
An LLM designs a caption-distinct primary entity, then builds the chain
question-first under the bridge-entity and entity-pair constraints
(§3.3). A validate-and-repair loop combines programmatic checks
with a per-fact deletion test against twelve failure modes; unrepaired chains
are discarded, and Answer Refusal items strip required facts after
auditing. Surviving facts become chronological evidence and filler sessions
under leak guards. Stage 4 synthesises media (FLUX-2-pro / Kling–Seedance /
Qwen3-TTS). Filtering then yields the exact lineage
 $1{,}050\!\rightarrow\!1{,}004\!\rightarrow\!879$ (automated) before a
three-annotator audit of 100 items (94.3% well-posed) rescues 44 probe false
negatives, giving MOSAIC-Eval: 923 items (784 answerable +
139 refusal). Reweighting that audit with a 100-item probe-YES follow-up
estimates $\approx$ 97.8% gold-correct and $\approx$ 96.6% evidence-sufficient
on the 784 answerable releases (Appendix I); the 784/864
(90.7%) figure remains a filtering retention rate, not a human
answerability ceiling (Appendix E).

The release spans 70 personas (11–24 sessions, mean 17.5), 10,765 multimodal
artefacts (Table 10), and $\sim$ 18.8M tokens
( $\sim$ 269K/persona). Among answerable items, 85.2% need $\geq$ 2 facts and
75.0% need $\geq$ 2 modalities; required evidence appears in image, audio,
video, and text for 99.5%, 54.1%, 23.5%, and 35.3% of items (overlapping),
so we report per-challenge strata rather than claiming uniform modality
balance; Appendix D collects the remaining haystack
statistics.

The main paired contrasts are symbolic versus native (perception cost),
structured versus generic text (avoidable write-time compression), gold native
versus retrieved-all-delivered (retrieval loss), and all-delivered versus
production-capped requests (delivery loss). Delivery is additionally traced as a
budget curve over tighter packer allowances (top-10 units; top-5 with one
media item per modality). Residual errors under gold symbolic facts bound
answer integration and grading error.

## Frozen configuration.

Matched conditions use Gemini 3.5 as the fixed omni-modal reader (temperature 0;
extended thinking disabled), held out from dataset construction: MiMo-V2.5 is
the answerability probe, so using it as the primary reader would couple
selection with evaluation. The trade-off is disclosed—gold-native failures
mix perception error with bounded item noise
(§6.1). Retrieval replays a frozen top-50 ranked
manifest; the production budget delivers the top 20 units with at most three

## LIMITATIONS SECTION
MOSAIC’s personas, evidence sessions, and media are synthetic; evaluation on
organically collected multimodal histories remains future work. The primary
reader is held out from filtering, and stage contrasts are paired, but stage
labels are matched-protocol associations rather than fully isolated mechanisms.
Video nulls are underpowered (Appendix F). The raw-audio versus
ASR gap is inconclusive for this reader, and textualized audio carriers often
retain paralinguistic lexical cues while other gold hops already expose the
answer value—so gold-delivery audio nulls do not establish acoustic recovery
(Appendix G). Swapped gold prosody tags
nonetheless show selector-level sensitivity
(Appendix K.4). RQ4 uses length-matched refusal
controls with residual form cues (Appendix H).
Further caveats on modality coverage and backend scope are in
Appendix G.

## CONCLUSION
MOSAIC separates omni-modal memory failures into write,
retrieve, deliver, and read. Under the matched
protocol and fixed reader, reading accounts for the largest residual loss
under gold facts; retrieval and delivery constrain native pipelines, textualization text-derived ones. These are protocol associations, not universal bottlenecks. Spatial structure has the
clearest causal validation and no adequate text surrogate; annotated prosody
tags show selector-level sensitivity, while temporal and raw-audio versus ASR
contrasts remain inconclusive. No system both answers and refuses reliably:
progress requires preserving modality-specific signal, delivering complete
chains, and reading them faithfully.
