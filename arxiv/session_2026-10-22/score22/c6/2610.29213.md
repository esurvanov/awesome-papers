## ABSTRACT
Satellite precipitation products face a persistent trade-off: real-time estimates are timely but less accurate, whereas the most reliable products are often released months after observation. This delay keeps high-quality precipitation information outside the decision window for flood warning and disaster response. Here we report Huayu-Global, a cross-constellation deep-learning system that combines three geostationary sensor streams from five satellites through a common retrieval framework and generates coherent quasi-global precipitation fields every thirty minutes. Across more than a million independent station records from two periods outside the training years, Huayu-Global improves event detection and precipitation-amount estimation relative to the real-time products evaluated and reaches delayed-product-level skill in key metrics. Huayu-Global therefore turns operational geostationary observations into a timely, high-skill precipitation layer for flood forecasting, water management and disaster response. Its cross-constellation design also provides a scalable foundation for incorporating future geostationary missions into hydrological and weather-prediction systems.

## 1 Introduction

Figure 1: Data processing workflow for the Huayu-Global precipitation monitoring framework. Representative Geostationary Operational Environmental Satellite (GOES), Meteosat, and FengYun-4B (FY-4B) sector views are transformed into Advanced Baseline Imager (ABI), Spinning Enhanced Visible and Infrared Imager (SEVIRI), and Advanced Geostationary Radiation Imager (AGRI) cloud-feature sequences, then passed through the Huayu models for the Americas, Europe-Africa, and Asia-Pacific domains. Sector outputs are merged to generate coherent quasi-global grid precipitation products using only satellite inputs available at the inference time.

Precipitation is a primary driver of the water cycle, flood hazard, agricultural risk, and hydrological extremes. Operational monitoring therefore requires products that are both timely and quantitatively reliable. Ground gauges, weather radar, and gauge–satellite merged analyses provide essential constraints on precipitation fields, but their global operational use is limited by uneven station density, radar-network gaps, terrain blockage, oceanic coverage gaps, and differences in data-sharing conditions (Shen et al., 2014; Fulton et al., 1998; Sun et al., 2018; Kirschbaum et al., 2017). These limitations are most acute when precipitation information is needed rapidly and consistently across continents and ocean-adjacent regions.

Satellite precipitation products have reduced this coverage gap, but operational users still face a hard choice between speed and accuracy. Real-time streams such as Global Satellite Mapping of Precipitation NOW (GSMaP_NOW) and Precipitation Estimation from Remotely Sensed Information using Artificial Neural Networks–Cloud Classification System (PERSIANN-CCS) provide prompt coverage but show lower detection and quantitative skill (real-time products achieve critical success index (CSI) values between 0.23 and 0.33 in our evaluation, whereas the gauge-adjusted Final Run of Integrated Multi-satellitE Retrievals for Global Precipitation Measurement (IMERG FR), released 3.5 months after observation, reaches 0.42) (Kubota et al., 2020; Nguyen et al., 2018; Hong et al., 2004). More extensively processed products such as the Climate Prediction Center morphing technique (CMORPH) and IMERG can improve accuracy, but their highest-quality streams may arrive months later (Joyce et al., 2004; Huffman et al., 2015; Kidd et al., 2020; Tan et al., 2019; Huffman et al., 2023). Global IMERG assessments also show that residual errors remain dependent on region, season, and precipitation regime, even after multi-sensor merging and gauge adjustment (Tan et al., 2016; Pradhan et al., 2022). Information delivered after the decision window cannot support flood warning or other time-critical action. A useful quasi-global system must therefore combine real-time availability with delayed-product-level precipitation skill.

High-frequency geostationary observations offer a route to this goal because they continuously sample cloud evolution across broad domains. Decades of geostationary meteorological satellite development, from early operational systems to modern multispectral imagers, have increased the temporal and spectral information available for rainfall inference (Blersch and
Probert, 1991; Schmit et al., 2005; Bessho et al., 2016). The difficulty is that cloud-top radiances do not map uniquely to surface rainfall, and the relationship varies with region, season, terrain, storm type, and sensor geometry. The challenge is compounded across satellite platforms: the GOES ABI, Meteosat SEVIRI, and FY-4B AGRI differ in spectral coverage, native spatial resolution, and full-disk scan cadence, yet an operational quasi-global product must seamlessly combine their outputs. Recent studies have shown that nonlinear and multispectral models can improve geostationary precipitation retrieval in regional settings (Song et al., 2026; Kühnlein
et al., 2014; Sehad et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2022a; Ma et al., 2022b; Zhang et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2024; Ji et al., 2025). The remaining challenge is to extend this idea from single-sensor or regional retrievals to a cross-constellation system that can operate across the Americas, Europe-Africa, and Asia-Pacific sectors while preserving a common output grid.

Here we present Huayu-Global, a cross-constellation framework for real-time quasi-global precipitation retrieval. Extending the earlier regional Huayu framework (Song et al., 2026), it coordinates sensor-adapted models for GOES ABI, Meteosat SEVIRI, and FY-4B AGRI within a common architecture and merges their outputs on a unified 0.05∘ grid (Fig. 1). Huayu-Global thereby converts operational geostationary observations into coherent precipitation fields every 30 minutes across most low- and mid-latitude regions. The system is developed from 59.7 TB of satellite data and evaluated against 5,395 independent ground stations contributing 1.85 million matched three-hourly records from July–December 2022 and January–June 2025, both outside the 2023–2024 training years. It leads the evaluated real-time products in event detection and error magnitude and reaches delayed-product-level skill in key station-based metrics. By moving high-skill precipitation information from retrospective archives into the operational decision window, Huayu-Global establishes a scalable observation layer for flood forecasting, water management, disaster response and future satellite-enabled weather prediction.

The full-sample metrics in Table 1 show the same pattern across complementary diagnostics. Across 1,847,967 matched 3-hour station-product records, Huayu-Global achieves the highest CSI (0.441) and coefficient of determination (R2, 0.362), and the lowest root-mean-square error (RMSE, 1.961 mm). Huayu-Global also exceeds the 3.5-month IMERG FR product in CSI, R2, and RMSE in this independent station comparison. Daily-scale diagnostics (Extended Data Fig. 2) show that this aggregate advantage is not confined to a small number of validation dates, while also indicating that the strongest non-Huayu baseline varies by metric and day.

## 2.2 Representative spatial case

Figure 3: Representative spatial comparison of precipitation estimates for one selected evaluation case. Panels show (a) GSMaP_NOW, (b) PERSIANN-CCS, (c) PERSIANN, (d) CMORPH, (e) IMERG FR, and (f) Huayu-Global. Each panel presents a quasi-global precipitation field with four enlarged regional views; station markers indicate the validation locations in the zoomed regions. The annotations report case-specific CSI, CC, R2, and RMSE for the displayed case only and should not be interpreted as the full-sample scores in Table 1.

The selected case in Fig. 3 illustrates the spatial character of the retrievals. All six products locate the main precipitation systems in broadly similar areas, but Huayu-Global preserves connected rain bands and internal intensity gradients across the mosaic, whereas several baselines produce fragmented, block-like, or isolated features. The four enlarged regions show the same contrast for both elongated rain bands and localized precipitation centres.

The case-specific metrics are consistent with the visual result: Huayu-Global has the highest CSI (0.595), CC (0.686), and R2 (0.455), together with the lowest RMSE (1.528 mm), among the six displayed products. Because this is a single selected scene and some uncertainty ranges overlap, it illustrates rather than establishes the product-wide ranking; that conclusion rests on the full-sample evaluation in Table 1.

## 2.4 Performance across validation conditions

Figure 5: Stratified performance across validation conditions. Rows show CSI, ACC, POD, FAR, CC, R2, and RMSE; columns show validation month, station elevation, longitude sector in 2022 and 2025, and observed 3-hour precipitation amount. Solid teal lines denote Huayu-Global, and dashed grey lines denote the strongest non-Huayu baseline selected separately within each bin and metric. Metric–bin combinations that are undefined or uninformative by construction are omitted.

Figure 5 resolves the aggregate comparison across temporal, geographic, and precipitation regimes. Across validation months, Huayu-Global generally remains close to or exceeds the best-baseline envelope, although the size of the difference varies by metric and month. The 2022 longitude profiles are comparatively stable, whereas the 2025 profiles are more heterogeneous; Huayu-Global nevertheless remains among the leading products across most valid longitude sectors.

Stratification also identifies the most challenging evaluation regimes. CSI and POD generally decrease with station elevation while FAR increases, indicating weaker event detection over high terrain. RMSE rises sharply with observed precipitation amount, identifying heavy precipitation as a prominent regime of increasing magnitude error. Because the strongest baseline is selected separately within each bin and metric, the grey series is an upper-envelope diagnostic rather than a comparison with one fixed product; amount-conditioned categorical metrics likewise describe the selected precipitation bins rather than an independent threshold-sensitivity test.

## 3 Discussion

Huayu-Global extends the earlier regional Huayu framework (Song et al., 2026) from a single-sensor regional retrieval to a three-sensor quasi-global system covering approximately 85% of Earth’s land surface between 60∘S and 60∘N. It demonstrates that high-frequency geostationary imagery can deliver real-time-input precipitation estimates with skill previously associated with delayed post-processed products. At independent station locations, Huayu-Global achieves the highest CSI and R2 and the lowest RMSE among the evaluated products. Relative to GSMaP_NOW, it increases CSI by 34.9% and reduces RMSE by 31.8%. The central advance is the conversion of continuously available cloud-evolution observations into high-skill precipitation information within the operational decision window.

## CONCLUSION
Huayu-Global extends the earlier regional Huayu framework (Song et al., 2026) from a single-sensor regional retrieval to a three-sensor quasi-global system covering approximately 85% of Earth’s land surface between 60∘S and 60∘N. It demonstrates that high-frequency geostationary imagery can deliver real-time-input precipitation estimates with skill previously associated with delayed post-processed products. At independent station locations, Huayu-Global achieves the highest CSI and R2 and the lowest RMSE among the evaluated products. Relative to GSMaP_NOW, it increases CSI by 34.9% and reduces RMSE by 31.8%. The central advance is the conversion of continuously available cloud-evolution observations into high-skill precipitation information within the operational decision window.

This capability connects satellite retrieval directly to time-critical environmental workflows. Huayu-Global generates a new 0.05∘ precipitation field every 30 minutes, and data loading and inference require less than 5 minutes per timestep after model initialization on a single workstation (Methods). The resulting fields can provide a timely observation layer for flood nowcasting, hydrological forecasting, agricultural monitoring and disaster response. They also complement recent deep-learning nowcasting and weather-prediction systems, which require rapidly updated precipitation information to initialize, constrain and evaluate forecasts (Sønderby et al., 2020; Ravuri et al., 2021; Espeholt et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023).

The cross-constellation design makes this capability scalable. The three sector models share the same Head–Geographic Attention Block (GeoAB)–Tail architecture while using separate parameters for ABI, SEVIRI, and AGRI streams. This combination applies a consistent representation pathway to cloud evolution while adapting to differences in spectral coverage, resolution, scan frequency and viewing geometry. All three models achieve useful retrieval skill within their respective domains (Table 2), enabling their outputs to form a coherent quasi-global product. The same design provides a practical path for incorporating Meteosat Third Generation, FY-4C, and Geostationary Extended Observations (GeoXO) missions as the global geostationary constellation evolves.
