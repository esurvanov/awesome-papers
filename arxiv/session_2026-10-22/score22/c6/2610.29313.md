## ABSTRACT
Multimodal ocean modeling remains challenging due to substantial heterogeneity in data structures, physical semantics, and spatiotemporal characteristics across satellite imagery, gridded oceanographic fields, and buoy observations. Existing approaches largely focus on task-specific ocean modeling, while learning transferable representations from heterogeneous observations remains an open problem. To address this challenge, we propose YingLan, a multimodal ocean foundation model that integrates physics-attribute-conditioned dynamic encoding, Spatiotemporal Increment Coupling (STIC), and ocean-state evolution-aware pretraining. Specifically, the dynamic encoding mechanism generates adaptive input projections conditioned on physical semantics and observation characteristics, enabling flexible representation of heterogeneous ocean variables. STIC models multiscale spatiotemporal dependencies through cross-scale interactions of latent temporal increments. The pretraining strategy further combines masked observation reconstruction with temporal evolution objectives to facilitate the learning of coherent ocean-state representations across different prediction horizons. YingLan is implemented using MindSpore and trained on Huawei Ascend 910B NPUs, demonstrating its compatibility with domestic AI computing infrastructure. In a seven-day sea surface salinity (SSS) forecasting task conducted in the Shenzhen coastal waters, YingLan achieves an average RMSE of 0.687508 PSU, corresponding to relative error reductions of 0.58%, 5.31%, and 12.42% compared with LangYa, XiHe, and GloNet2, respectively. These results provide initial evidence of the model's effectiveness in regional ocean forecasting, while broader cross-task and cross-regional evaluations remain necessary to assess its generalization capabilities.

## 1 Introduction

Accurate characterization and prediction of the marine environment are fundamental to oceanographic research and marine environmental monitoring, with important implications for marine ecosystem conservation, marine resource management, and ocean hazard early warning [1]. Advances in satellite remote sensing, ocean numerical modeling, and in situ observation technologies have expanded the spatial coverage, temporal resolution, and range of physical variables represented in multi-source ocean observation data, providing an essential basis for multiscale characterization and dynamic analysis of the marine environment [2]. Specifically, satellite imagery captures the spatial distribution of ocean surface conditions, oceanographic products provide spatiotemporal information on variables such as temperature, salinity, and ocean currents, and in situ observations from buoys and other platforms record continuous variations in local marine conditions [3]. These observation sources are complementary in terms of spatial coverage, temporal sampling, and physical information. However, substantial differences in data structure, variable semantics, and spatiotemporal scales pose challenges to joint modeling of heterogeneous observations and knowledge transfer across downstream tasks. Consequently, large-scale pretraining on heterogeneous ocean observations to learn generalizable representations with cross-modal adaptability and spatiotemporal generalization has emerged as a promising research direction in ocean intelligence [4].

In recent years, deep learning has made substantial progress in ocean environmental forecasting and the development of ocean foundation models. For spatiotemporal ocean modeling, XiHe employs local and global spatial interactions to perform high-resolution [5], multivariate global ocean forecasting, whereas WenHai integrates air-sea flux computation with ocean-dynamics-informed network design, highlighting the importance of spatial dependency modeling and the incorporation of domain knowledge in ocean prediction [6]. In the development of foundation models, AI-GOMS explores a pretraining and downstream adaptation paradigm for multiple ocean-related tasks, demonstrating the potential of shared representations to support diverse applications [7]. Meanwhile, ocean remote sensing research has increasingly investigated the joint use of complementary observations during pretraining. The Sentinel-3 ocean-colour foundation model jointly pretrains on ocean-colour reflectance and sea surface temperature through masked modeling and evaluates the resulting representations on ocean-colour-related tasks [8]. OceanMAE combines multispectral imagery with auxiliary ocean descriptors for self-supervised reconstruction and assesses the transferability of its learned representations on ocean segmentation and bathymetry regression [9]. Despite these advances in ocean forecasting, multi-source information utilization, and domain-specific pretraining, differences in the physical attributes, spatiotemporal scales, and temporal dynamics of heterogeneous observations continue to pose challenges for unified representation learning and joint modeling. Effectively encoding heterogeneous ocean observations, characterizing dependencies across spatial scales and temporal horizons, and learning ocean-state evolution through pretraining remain important open research questions for multimodal ocean foundation models.

Despite the progress of existing studies, developing multimodal foundation models that jointly leverage satellite imagery, oceanographic fields, and buoy observations remains challenging in terms of heterogeneous representation learning, multiscale spatiotemporal modeling, and the learning of ocean-state dynamics. First, these observation types differ substantially in data structure, physical semantics, and sampling characteristics [10, 11]. Satellite imagery primarily captures the spatial distribution and textural characteristics of ocean surface conditions, oceanographic fields describe the spatial structures of variables such as temperature, salinity, and current velocity, while buoy observations record localized temporal variations at specific locations. Fixed input representations may not adequately accommodate these differences in observational attributes [12, 13], potentially limiting the effective utilization of heterogeneous information within a shared representation space. Second, marine environments exhibit multiscale spatiotemporal variability, in which local spatial structures, broader environmental contexts, and temporal variations over different horizons are interrelated. Single-scale feature extraction may therefore be insufficient to characterize these complex dependencies. Furthermore, pretraining based solely on reconstructing observed data does not necessarily encourage models to learn ocean-state evolution patterns that are predictive of future conditions [14]. Consequently, further research is needed to develop transferable multimodal ocean foundation models that effectively represent heterogeneous observations, capture multiscale spatiotemporal dependencies, and learn the temporal evolution of ocean states through pretraining.

## 2 Related Work

## 2.1 Ocean Foundation Models and Pretraining Paradigms

Existing deep learning studies on ocean modeling have primarily focused on ocean state estimation and evolution forecasting, aiming to characterize ocean physical processes and their spatiotemporal variability through data-driven approaches. Eeltink et al. [15] proposed a hybrid approach that integrates recurrent neural networks with a nonlinear wave evolution model, learning data-driven correction terms to represent wave-breaking processes and illustrating the integration of physical models with neural networks for ocean dynamics modeling. Ham et al. [16] proposed a global ocean data assimilation framework based on partial convolutions and generative adversarial learning, reconstructing ocean states by incorporating observational information and improving the handling of incomplete ocean observations. Furthermore, Cui et al. [6] introduced WenHai, which employs a Swin Transformer-based architecture to model multivariate ocean state evolution and incorporates air–sea flux information and multi-step forecasting strategies. These studies advance ocean deep learning from complementary perspectives, including physical process modeling, ocean state reconstruction, and spatiotemporal forecasting. Nevertheless, their training objectives remain primarily tied to specific ocean variables and predefined estimation or forecasting tasks, motivating the exploration of pretrained representations that can be transferred across diverse ocean applications.

Recent ocean foundation models have explored diverse pretraining strategies tailored to different types of ocean data. Xiong et al. [7] proposed AI-GOMS, which learns ocean-state representations from HYCOM reanalysis fields through Fourier-based masked autoencoding and supports downstream adaptation across ocean modeling tasks. Glaser et al. [17] introduced WV-Net, using contrastive self-supervised learning on large-scale Sentinel-1 SAR imagery to obtain transferable representations for ocean remote sensing. Complementarily, Bi et al. [18] developed OceanGPT through ocean-science corpus pretraining and domain-specific instruction tuning, while Xue et al. [19] introduced OceanPile, a multimodal ocean-science corpus designed to support foundation model training across marine imagery, sonar data, and scientific text. These studies span physical-field pretraining, image-based representation learning, and language-oriented ocean knowledge modeling, illustrating the diversity of data and objectives in ocean-domain pretraining. However, their learning frameworks address different data modalities and task requirements, leaving the unified representation of heterogeneous ocean observations an important direction for further investigation.

## 2.2 Heterogeneous Ocean Representation and Multiscale Spatiotemporal Learning

Ocean observations differ in their spatial resolution, sampling characteristics, and physical information, motivating the development of methods that can effectively integrate complementary data sources. Thiria et al. combined sea surface height (SSH) and sea surface temperature (SST) observations to reconstruct mesoscale ocean currents through CNN-based downscaling [20]. Wang et al. proposed ODF-Net, which fuses in situ measurements, satellite observations, and three-dimensional model and reanalysis data to reconstruct ocean temperature fields [21]. Champenois and Sapsis developed a machine learning framework that combines historical reanalysis with real-time satellite and buoy measurements for regional four-dimensional ocean temperature reconstruction [22]. Under sparse observation conditions, Lei et al. investigated multimodal fusion-based spatiotemporal incremental learning for ocean environment perception [23]. Collectively, these studies explore ocean data fusion through spatial downscaling, multi-source field reconstruction, and multimodal spatiotemporal learning.

Existing ocean spatiotemporal modeling methods primarily employ hierarchical feature extraction, attention mechanisms, and multiscale feature fusion to characterize spatial dependencies and temporal variations in ocean variables. Zhao et al. proposed HSSSA, a hierarchical encoder-decoder network that integrates spatial and temporal self-attention for multiscale SST forecasting [24]. Bai et al. developed MUSTAN, which progressively fuses features across spatial scales and applies spatial and temporal attention to predict SST variations [25]. Pan et al. introduced an effective attention model for global SST prediction at daily and monthly temporal resolutions, explicitly modeling spatiotemporal correlations in historical observations [26]. Tao et al. investigated multiscale spatial features and temporal attention for El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) prediction, demonstrating the use of these mechanisms in modeling large-scale ocean variability [27]. These studies illustrate complementary architectural strategies for capturing ocean dynamics across spatial scales, temporal horizons, and physical processes.

## 3 Method

## 3.1 Overall Architecture

We propose YingLan, a multimodal ocean foundation model designed for unified representation learning from heterogeneous ocean observations. The overall architecture is illustrated in Fig. 1. YingLan leverages large-scale satellite remote sensing imagery, gridded oceanographic fields, and buoy time-series observations to address heterogeneity in physical semantics, data structures, and spatiotemporal resolutions across diverse observational sources. The model aims to learn generalizable ocean representations that capture both shared cross-modal information and ocean-specific spatiotemporal dynamics. Its architecture comprises three core components: Physics-Attribute-Conditioned Dynamic Encoding, Spatiotemporal Increment Coupling (STIC), and Ocean-State Evolution-Aware Pretraining.

Figure 1: Overall architecture of YingLan.

YingLan first employs Physics-Attribute-Conditioned Dynamic Encoding to construct adaptive feature projections conditioned on the physical semantics and observational characteristics of individual variables. Modality-specific input adapters are used to map ocean observations with different data structures into a shared feature space. The resulting representations are subsequently processed by the STIC module, which integrates multiscale spatial representations with scale-specific temporal modeling. By coupling latent temporal increments across spatial scales, STIC captures dynamic interactions between local ocean structures and large-scale environmental contexts, producing unified spatiotemporal representations.

During pretraining, YingLan jointly optimizes masked observation reconstruction, horizon-conditioned latent increment prediction, and temporal compositional consistency. These complementary objectives enable the model to capture structural dependencies among heterogeneous observations while learning ocean-state variations across multiple temporal horizons. Following pretraining, the shared encoder is adapted to regional ocean monitoring and prediction tasks through task-specific prediction heads and parameter fine-tuning, allowing the transferability of YingLan’s pretrained representations to be evaluated in regional marine environments.

## CONCLUSION
This work investigates unified representation learning from heterogeneous ocean observations and presents YingLan as a multimodal foundation model for ocean environmental modeling. By incorporating physical semantics and observation conditions into adaptive feature encoding, YingLan provides a flexible representation mechanism for ocean variables with diverse observational characteristics. Its multiscale spatiotemporal architecture further integrates temporal variations across spatial hierarchies, while the joint pretraining objectives encourage the learning of both observational structures and ocean-state dynamics. These designs establish a unified modeling framework for integrating satellite imagery, gridded oceanographic fields, and buoy time-series observations. To evaluate its applicability to regional ocean forecasting, we conduct a downstream seven-day sea surface salinity (SSS) forecasting task in the Shenzhen coastal waters. YingLan achieves an average RMSE of 0.687508 PSU, yielding the lowest reported error among the evaluated methods. Beyond predictive performance, YingLan is implemented using MindSpore and trained on Huawei Ascend 910B NPUs, demonstrating the feasibility of developing and training multimodal ocean foundation models within a domestic AI computing environment. Overall, this study provides a methodological basis for learning transferable ocean representations while supporting the development of ocean intelligence models on domestic computing infrastructure. Future research will investigate broader downstream task adaptation, cross-regional generalization, and robust modeling under incomplete and heterogeneous ocean observations.
