## ABSTRACT
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), particularly through group-relative policy optimization (GRPO), has emerged as a central paradigm for the post-training of large language models (LLMs). While RLVR is highly effective at enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, it commonly induces \textit{distribution sharpening}, whereby the policy distribution becomes excessively peaked and rare-yet-correct trajectories are progressively suppressed, thus diminishing sample diversity. To this end, we promote flat-reward optima in RLVR to smooth the policy distribution. We theoretically link flat reward RLVR to reduced distribution sharpening, and empirically show improved sample diversity while maintaining single-attempt accuracy. Our method, called \texttt{Flat}-RLVR, searches for flat reward regions via sharpness-aware minimization during LLM post-training, combining this with GRPO variants to achieve better trade-offs between sampling efficiency and reasoning capacity.

## 1 Introduction

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), especially through group-relative methods, has emerged as a predominant methodology for large language model (LLM) post-training (Shao et al., 2024; Lambert et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2025). Its prominence stems from two complementary strengths: first, rewarding only the correctness of the final answer is sufficient to elicit valid reasoning behaviors (Guo et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2026); and second, the group-relative method eliminates the value network required by proximal policy optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017), substantially reducing the memory burden of maintaining a critic of comparable size to the policy (Shao et al., 2024). This critic-free design—first realized as group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) and subsequently extended in its variants—has rapidly become the de facto standard for RLVR.

Figure 1: RLVR and Flat-RLVR on LLM reasoning capacity. Gray nodes indicate paths that are unlikely to be sampled by the model, black nodes indicate paths that are likely to be sampled, i.e., each path consists of a sequence of outputs, later formulated by $o_{j}$ , where $j$ is the path index. Green nodes indicate correct solutions, and red nodes indicate incorrect.

However, a critical doubt has been raised, stemming from empirical observations that GRPO impairs its efficacy when evaluated across multiple samples by checking whether any one succeeds; so-called Pass@ $K$ performance metric (Shao et al., 2024). Specifically, base LLM models without post-training even surpass their RLVR-tuned counterparts under Pass@ $K$ with sufficiently large $K$ (Yue et al., 2025).
This misgiving has recently raised a hypothesis that RLVR merely improves sampling efficiency at the expense of the model’s underlying reasoning capacity driven by LLM’s sample diversity (Nguyen et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025). Further evidence reinforces this view: RLVR has been shown to amplify only responses that already carry high probability mass while neglecting the long tail of rare-yet-correct samples, thereby reducing sample diversity (He et al., 2025); and to concentrate exclusively on regions already solvable by the base model, failing to acquire novel capabilities (Yue et al., 2025). Taken together, these findings further support the interpretation that the current RLVR mechanism induces distribution sharpening—a phenomenon in which the model reinforces only the easier, already-solved instances and loses its exploratory capacity.

This is particularly problematic in light of the emerging paradigm of inference-time compute scaling, which leverages sample diversity for effective LLM deployment (Wang et al., 2023; Brown et al., 2024; Chow et al., 2025; Handa et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026b). These strategies operate on a simple principle—generate many candidate solutions and rely on a verifier to identify the correct ones—and are particularly well aligned with RLVR’s domains of mathematics and code, where deterministic verifiers naturally enable reliable selection (Cobbe et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2021; Brown et al., 2024).
In this regard, distribution sharpening from RLVR, which is observed to trade output diversity for efficiency, directly undermines LLM’s benefits of inference-time computation. Figure 1 presents LLM-generated output trajectories (i.e., reasoning paths), the base model covers sometimes-incorrect but wide variety of answers, but RLVR narrows the path to a few answers discarding diversity.

In response to this growing need for sample diversity, we apply reinforcement learning (RL) to flatten the reward surface of the RLVR framework, thereby smoothing policy distributions and diversifying the generated candidate samples.
Recently, it has been shown that a flatter maximum on the reward surface implies a robust policy against uncertain perturbations on actions, states, and rewards (Lee and Yoon, 2025).
Motivated by the flat RL concept, our key insight is that, rather than updating the LLM toward steepest loss descent, steering it to flatten the surface may smooth the distribution sharpening.

In this paper, we find that sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) variants (Foret et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2022; Hassan et al., 2025) applied to RL optimization within the RLVR paradigm indeed mitigate distribution sharpening by yielding a flatter reward
(as illustrated in Figure 1).
In addition, we formalize how the flat-search optimization mathematically lifts suboptimal candidates by smoothing the reward surface. Our contributions are highlighted as follows:
i) We propose a novel strategy to alleviate the distribution sharpening of GRPO by borrowing the concept of flat-minima search. ii) We provide a theoretical analysis of how a flat-inspired GRPO elevates suboptimal candidates, leading to the smoothed output distribution. iii) We suggest a Flat-RLVR framework by integrating SAM into GRPO methods. Flat-RLVR, instantiated by combining with GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), and CISPO (Chen et al., 2025), consistently improves Pass@ $256$ mathematical reasoning capacity while preserving accuracy with our LLM post-training adaptations.

## 2 Related Work

## 2.1 Group-Relative RLVR

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) trains a large language model on binary rewards provided by deterministic verifiers, eliciting strong reasoning behavior without human-preference data (Guo et al., 2025; Lambert et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2026). Within this paradigm, group-relative methods have become dominant, yielding computation benefits by normalizing rewards across a group of rollouts sampled from the same prompt and thereby removing the need for a separate value network (Shao et al., 2024). The original instance, GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), has since been extended in several directions. DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) introduces asymmetric clipping bounds that allow low-probability tokens to bring larger upward updates, together with dynamic sampling that discards groups whose advantages collapse to zero. CISPO (Chen et al., 2025) clips the importance-sampling weight to prevent dropping tokens that carry essential information.

Our work integrates a sharpness-aware optimization perspective into the reinforcement learning loop, showing a general applicability across the group-relative RLVR family.

## 2.2 Distribution Sharpening in RLVR

While the distribution sharpening phenomenon of RLVR is now well documented (Yue et al., 2025; Nguyen et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025), existing mitigation strategies all intervene at the level of the reward or token distribution. F-GRPO (Plyusov et al., 2026) applies a Focal Weight to down-weight easy prompts, the unlikeliness reward (He et al., 2025) amplifies signals from rare correct trajectories, and clipping-based variants such as DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) relax token-level updates to preserve low-probability tokens.

We address distribution sharpening via optimization geometry itself, biasing updates toward flat regions of the loss landscape via SAM—a direction is novel and orthogonal to the above approaches.

## 2.3 Advances in SAM

Sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) has been improved as a primary driver of flat-minima search by addressing its drawbacks. The first concern is its sensitivity to parameter re-scaling: ASAM (Kwon et al., 2021) addresses this by introducing a scaling operator that adaptively adjusts the perturbation radius according to parameter magnitudes. The second issue is SAM’s vulnerability to noisy gradients, particularly during the early stages of training; GCSAM (Hassan et al., 2025) mitigates this through Gradient Centralization, which removes the mean of the gradient to yield a more reliable perturbation direction. The third concern is the computational overhead of SAM; LookSAM (Liu et al., 2022) amortizes this cost by computing the SAM update only periodically, while Momentum-SAM (Becker et al., 2025) integrates Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (NAG) into SAM to directly improve computational efficiency. Lastly, the observation that SAM does not invariably converge to flat minima leads to GSAM (Zhuang et al., 2022) adopting a surrogate gap formulation for the sharpness metric.

Our work adopts the idea of several SAM-based variants to build the Flat-RLVR framework; applying the SAM update periodically, inspired by LookSAM (Liu et al., 2022) and Gradient Centralization, inspired by GCSAM (Hassan et al., 2025). Notably, Flat-RLVR is not limited to a certain type of SAM approaches.

## 2.4 Flat-Minima and Generalization

Flat minima have been studied as being linked to improved generalization (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber, 1997; Keskar et al., 2017) emphasized in domain generalization (Cha et al., 2021) and adversarial robustness (Wei et al., 2023) with underlying principles (Zhang et al., 2024; Yoo and Yoon, 2026).
Researchers have extended flat-minima toward various learning frameworks, including generative modeling (Lee et al., 2025), federated learning (Qu et al., 2022; Lee and Yoon, 2024), and reinforcement learning (RL) (Lee and Yoon, 2025).
Notably, RL with flatter reward yields more robust policies to action, state, and reward uncertainties (Lee and Yoon, 2025). Such flat reward insight makes us anticipate potential benefits in RLVR, but it is nontrivial to identify how flatness-aware RL steers LLM behavior.

Our work is the first attempt to employ SAM in LLM post-training, revealing its benefits in relieving distribution sharpening in RLVR.

## 3 Preliminaries

## 3.1 Group Relative Policy Optimization

GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) removes the value function, then instead calculates advantages relative to a group of sampled rollouts. For a prompt $q\sim Q$ with $G$ rollouts $\{o_{i}\}^{G}_{i=1}$ , and rewards $\{R_{i}\}^{G}_{i=1}$ , the group-relative advantage for $i$ -th rollout is

 $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i}=\frac{R_{i}-\bar{R}}{\sigma_{R}+\varepsilon},$ 

(1)

where $\bar{R}:=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i}R_{i}$ , $\sigma_{R}=\textrm{std}(\{R_{j}\})$ and $\varepsilon>0$ .

Let $o_{i}=(y_{i,1},...,y_{i,|o_{i}|})\in O$ denote the token sequence for rollout $i$ computed by the old policy $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(O|q)$ from a prompt $q$ . GRPO optimizes policy $\pi_{\theta}$ to maximize the following clipped surrogate objective based on group-relative advantage $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i,\tau}$ :

 $J_{\text{GRPO}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{q\sim P(Q),\{o_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(O|q)}\bigg[\\
\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{|o_{i}|}\sum_{\tau=1}^{|o_{i}|}\min\Big\{\frac{\pi_{\theta}(o_{i,\tau}\mid q,o_{i,<\tau})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(o_{i,\tau}\mid q,o_{i,<\tau})}\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i,\tau},\\
\mathrm{clip}\big(\frac{\pi_{\theta}(o_{i,\tau}\mid q,o_{i,<\tau})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(o_{i,\tau}\mid q,o_{i,<\tau})},1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon\big)\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i,\tau}\Big\}\bigg],\\
$ 

(2)

where $o_{i,\tau}=y_{i,\tau}$ , $o_{i,<\tau}=\{y_{i,1},\cdots,y_{i,\tau-1}\}$ , and $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i,\tau}$ is the $i$ -th rollout advantage given with a set of partial sequences $\{o_{i,\leq\tau}\}_{i=1}^{G}$ . In RLVR, all tokens within the same rollout share an identical advantage value; $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{i,\tau}=\hat{\mathcal{A}_{i}}$ for all $\tau$ . $\mathrm{clip}(\cdot,a,b)$ clips input to be in interval $(a,b)$ , where $a<b\in\mathbb{R}$ .

We drop the Kullback-Leibler regularization term adopted by GRPO by following recent findings that the regularization hinders explorations in reasoning (Zhou et al., 2026).

Flat-RLVR converges to a flatter minimum in the loss landscape by jointly optimizing for loss reduction and sharpness minimization, whereas RLVR is drawn to a sharper minimum driven solely by reward maximization.

## 3.2 Sharpness-Aware Minimization

SAM (Foret et al., 2021) seeks a minimum with both small loss and curvature, biasing optimization toward flat regions of the loss landscape. Formally, SAM solves the following min-max objective:

 $\underset{\theta}{\min}\ \underset{\|\epsilon\|_{2}\leq\rho}{\max}\ \mathcal{L}(\theta+\epsilon),$ 

(3)

where $\mathcal{L}(\theta)$ is the loss function parametrized by $\theta$ , and $\epsilon$ is a parameter perturbation constrained to lie within an $\ell_{2}$ ball of radius $\rho>0$ . The inner maximization admits a first-order approximation so that the worst-case perturbation becomes

 $\epsilon^{*}=\rho{\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}(\theta)}/{\lVert\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}(\theta)\rVert}.$ 

(4)

By minimizing the worst-case loss within this perturbation neighborhood, SAM penalizes sharp minima and encourages convergence to flat ones.

## 4 Analysis on Flatness in RLVR

Based on the GRPO and SAM objectives, we analyze how a sharpness-aware RLVR, so-called Flat-RLVR, affects distribution sharpening over LLM outputs. Without loss of generality, we use a local sequence-level unclipped surrogate induced by a fixed rollout group (Ge et al., 2026). For a prompt $q$ , let $J_{q}(\theta)$ denote the local RLVR objective, which drops the expectation and clipping in Eq. (2).
Set $\mathcal{L}_{q}(\theta)=-J_{q}(\theta)$ ; maximizing RLVR objective is equivalent to minimizing $\mathcal{L}_{q}$ .
Figure 2 previews the key components of our analysis that follows.

Definition 4.1 ( $\rho$ -Flat-RLVR).

Given prompt $q$ and the computed local sequence-level RLVR loss $\mathcal{L}_{q}(\theta)$ , let $\rho$ -sharpness of $\mathcal{L}_{q}$ at parameter $\theta$ be

 $\operatorname{Sharp}_{\rho}(\theta;q)=\max_{\|\epsilon\|_{2}\leq\rho}\left[\mathcal{L}_{q}(\theta+\epsilon)-\mathcal{L}_{q}(\theta)\right],$ 

(5)

where $\epsilon$ is a parameter perturbation and $\|\cdot\|_{2}$ is Euclidean norm.
Then, the $\rho$ -Flat RLVR objective is defined as the expected sharpness-aware loss:

 $\min_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{q\sim Q}\left[\mathcal{L}_{q}(\theta)+\operatorname{Sharp}_{\rho}(\theta;q)\right]\\
=\min_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{q\sim Q}\left[\max_{\|\epsilon\|_{2}\leq\rho}\mathcal{L}_{q}(\theta+\epsilon)\right].$ 

(6)

Following SAM, Flat-RLVR optimizes Eq. (6) by a two-step update at parameters $\theta_{t}$ , where $t$ is an update index: it first ascends to the worst-case perturbation $\epsilon_{t}^{*}=\rho\,\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{q}(\theta_{t})/\|\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{q}(\theta_{t})\|_{2}$ , then descends using the gradient at the perturbed point,

 $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_{t}-\alpha\,\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{q}(\theta_{t}+\epsilon_{t}^{*}).$ 

(7)

The perturbation $\epsilon_{t}^{*}$ is what distinguishes Flat-RLVR from the standard update, which cannot account for loss sensitivity to perturbations.

Definition 4.2 (Correct-set Distribution Sharpening).

Given prompt $q$ and policy distribution $\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|q)$ over outputs $o\in O$ , let $\mathcal{C}_{q}=\{o:R(o)=1\}$ be a verifier-correct set, whose elements show $1$ -reward.
For two correct outputs $o_{h},o_{l}\in\mathcal{C}_{q}$ satisfying

 $\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(o_{h}|q)>\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(o_{l}|q),$ 

(8)

let us formulate their correct-set log-probability gap at parameter $\theta$ as

 $D_{h,l}(\theta;q)=\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(o_{h}|q)}{\pi_{\theta}(o_{l}|q)}.$ 

(9)

An update from $\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$ to $\theta_{\mathrm{new}}$ is defined to sharpen the correct-set distribution on $(o_{h},o_{l})$ if

 $D_{h,l}(\theta_{\mathrm{new}};q)-D_{h,l}(\theta_{\mathrm{old}};q)>0.$ 

(10)

Here, $o_{h}$ denotes a correct reasoning path with higher probability under the old policy, while $o_{l}$ denotes a lower-probability but still correct reasoning path.
We now analyze how the policy update via $\rho$ -Flat-RLVR affects the confidence gap $D_{h,l}$ .

## CONCLUSION
In this work, we present a way to mitigate the distribution sharpening in group-relative RLVR, by utilizing flatness-seeking optimization. Concretely, we provide a mathematical analysis of the one-step parameter update in RLVR, showing that flat-minima search dampens the abrupt increase in probability, thereby mitigating distribution sharpness of LLM post-training. We propose the Flat-RLVR framework by integrating sharpness-aware minimization into RLVR.
Throughout experiments, Flat-RLVR implemented as Flat-GRPO/DAPO/CISPO mitigates the distribution sharpening induced by RLVR, consistently improving the diversity of the output distribution while preserving accuracy with our LLM post-training adaptations. These imply flatness serves as the central catalyst to mitigate the distribution sharpening.
