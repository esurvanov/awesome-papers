## ABSTRACT
As-built BIM reconstruction from point clouds demands semantic, volumetric, parametric, and editable building components, beyond 2D layouts or planar abstractions. Yet existing scan-to-BIM pipelines often rely on handcrafted rules, heuristic optimization, or manual correction, while learning-based methods fall short of recovering wall thickness, opening geometry, and component-level BIM structure. We introduce CRAB, a BIM-ready cuboid reconstruction framework that encodes walls, doors, windows, beams, and columns as compact architectural cuboids with IFC-compatible semantics and parameters. CRAB bridges point clouds and editable BIM by combining ProposalNet, which predicts coarse component cuboids, with AssemblyNet, a relation-aware Flow Matching model that refines geometry, predicts structural relationships, and enables automatic structural correction and human-guided editing. To address limited cuboid-level supervision, we extend Structured3D with component-wise cuboid annotations and construct cuboid-based procedural scenes. Experiments show CRAB surpasses floor plan and plane-based alternatives in component completeness, geometric accuracy, and generalization. For interior wall thickness estimation, CRAB achieves a mean error of 2.80 cm, with 79.50% of matched walls within 3 cm. The project page is available at https://vcc.tech/research/2026/CRAB.

## 1. Introduction

3D scanning is widely used on construction sites to capture the geometric structure of real-world buildings and environments. The resulting point clouds often provide noisy, incomplete, and purely geometric surface measurements. In contrast, Building Information Modeling (BIM) represents buildings as structured collections of semantic component instances with volumetric geometry, parametric attributes, and explicit relationships(Zbirovský and Nežerka, 2025; Liu et al., 2025b). Consequently, BIM aims to reconstruct editable as-built architectural components rather than merely producing a geometric reconstruction of the scanned scene. For architectural components, this requires inferring wall thickness, wall-opening relationships, and editable instance identities that are not directly observable from raw scans.

Current methods rarely recover such BIM components directly from point clouds.
Manual modeling remains labor-intensive, while classical Scan-to-BIM pipelines rely on multi-stage processing, including segmentation, primitive fitting, plane extraction, and topology optimization, often requiring scene-specific assumptions and carefully tuned parameters (Zbirovský and Nežerka, 2025; Liu et al., 2025b).
Learning-based approaches improve automation, but most focus on intermediate geometric abstractions such as floor plans, room polygons (Yue et al., 2023), or 3D planes (Mao et al., 2025).
Although these representations support spatial understanding, they describe
layouts or surfaces rather than explicit volumetric component instances.
They do not directly couple component geometry, limiting their
extensibility to other BIM attributes such as load-bearing status and
materials.
A reconstruction representation should therefore expose these attributes as primary variables, rather than deriving them from layouts or planes after prediction.

This mismatch calls for a volumetric, component-aware representation.
Cuboid and primitive assemblies are a natural candidate because they encode compact solids with interpretable parameters and support editable 3D modeling.
However, existing cuboid-based methods primarily target object decomposition(Ye et al., 2025b), shape generation (Jones et al., 2020), or furniture arrangement (Feng et al., 2025), rather than architectural reconstruction from incomplete scene-level point clouds with ambiguous component boundaries.
Moreover, their generic cuboid parameterizations are not tailored to highly
elongated architectural components. This motivates a BIM-oriented volumetric
representation that explicitly models parametric component geometry and
architectural relations.

To this end, we introduce CRAB (Cuboid-based Representation for As-built BIM), a BIM-ready proposal-and-assembly framework for indoor point clouds. CRAB is designed precisely to move
beyond layout and plane abstractions toward volumetric BIM components,
thereby demonstrating the value of a BIM-oriented cuboid representation for
AI-based Scan-to-BIM. It reconstructs walls, doors, windows, beams, and
columns in single-floor scenes and represents each scene as centerline-based
semantic cuboids and explicit topological relationships, jointly encoding
parametric component geometry, semantics, wall adjacency, and opening-wall
affiliation.

To recover these cuboids and relationships from noisy and incomplete point
clouds, CRAB adopts a two-stage design. ProposalNet first generates coarse semantic cuboids from point cloud features, while AssemblyNet further refines their geometry and predicts inter-component relationships through relation-aware Flow Matching. This refinement improves structural consistency while preserving editable cuboid parameters for user-guided modification. The resulting components can be directly instantiated as Industry Foundation Classes (IFC) elements, enabling compatibility with the open BIM data-exchange standard.

A further challenge is the lack of cuboid-level supervision for scan-to-BIM learning.
Existing indoor datasets typically provide floor plans, meshes, semantic labels, or layout annotations, but lack component-wise volumetric cuboids aligned with BIM elements.
To support training and evaluation, we extend the public Structured3D (Zheng et al., 2020) dataset with cuboid annotations for architectural components, and further construct a cuboid-based synthetic dataset with procedurally generated scenes and simulated point cloud acquisition.

In summary, our contributions are threefold:

• 

We recast scan-based indoor BIM reconstruction as semantic cuboid assembly, using centerline-based cuboid parameters to explicitly model component semantics, wall thickness, opening height, and editable volumetric geometry.

• 

We construct CrabSet, a scalable cuboid-level supervision dataset for Scan-to-BIM, by enriching Structured3D with component-wise cuboid annotations and procedurally synthesizing diverse architectural scenes with simulated point-cloud acquisition.

• 

We introduce CRAB, a proposal-and-assembly framework that couples ProposalNet for semantic cuboid generation with AssemblyNet for relation-aware flow-matching refinement, producing accurate, structurally coherent, and editable BIM-ready models.

## 2. Related Work

## 2.1. Structured Indoor Modeling

Indoor structural modeling is often cast as floor plan or layout estimation.
Existing methods reconstruct 2D graphs, room polygons, or polygonal loops from RGB-D scans and point-density maps (Chen et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2022a; Stekovic et al., 2021; Yue et al., 2023), with recent variants improving polygon generation, room representation, graph reasoning, segmentation, and edge continuity (Chen et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025a; Liu et al., 2025a).
While compact, these floorplan-oriented outputs do not directly specify wall thickness, vertical extent, opening height, or component volume, which are required by CAD/BIM workflows.

Recent 3D structured modeling methods move beyond 2D layouts by predicting walls, openings, oriented boxes, or parametric layout quads from images or point clouds (Avetisyan et al., 2024; Mao et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2022b; Bieri et al., 2025).
For planar representations, recovering volumetric semantic component
instances from predicted paired parallel planes requires additional plane
pairing and instance association to infer wall thickness, opening affiliation,
and component identity. Such associations can be ambiguous when a single
plane spans multiple walls, limiting extensions to instance-specific BIM
attributes such as materials and load-bearing status.
We instead reconstruct cuboid assemblies for walls, doors, windows, beams, and columns, with explicit centerline, thickness, height.

## 2.2. BIM-Ready Cuboid Reconstruction

Scan-to-BIM converts sensed geometry into editable building models.
Existing systems recover walls, slabs, openings, rooms, and IFC-compatible outputs from point clouds (Murali et al., 2017; Ochmann et al., 2019; Zbirovský and Nežerka, 2025), and BIMNet highlights the need for domain-specific data and BIM-aware geometric and topological evaluation (Liu et al., 2025b).
Yet many pipelines still rely on geometric rules, segmentation, optimization, or software-specific workflows, making them sensitive to clutter, occlusion, and incomplete scans (Wu et al., 2023; Han et al., 2023; Wong et al., 2025).
Moreover, they primarily recover final component geometry without explicitly modeling relations, limiting coordinated refinement.

Cuboid and primitive assemblies offer compact, editable structures.
ShapeAssembly represents shapes as programs over cuboid parts (Jones et al., 2020), while recent primitive and procedural methods learn structured decompositions or editable programs from data (Ye et al., 2025b; Raji et al., 2026).
CASAGPT uses cuboid primitives for indoor scene synthesis to reduce mesh-level collisions (Feng et al., 2025).
Existing methods mainly target object decomposition, shape generation, or
furnished scene synthesis rather than architectural reconstruction from
incomplete scene-level point clouds. They typically use generic cuboids
parameterized by center, size, and orientation, which are not tailored to
highly elongated architectural components. CRAB instead adopts
centerline-based semantic cuboids and explicit relations, supporting coordinated refinement and
editable component reconstruction.

## 2.3. Local Structural Refinement

Recent LLM/VLM agents have moved generation and editing from one-shot prompting to closed-loop planning, tool use, evaluation, and revision.
SceneCraft refines Blender-code scenes with rendered feedback (Hu et al., 2024), and VIGA reconstructs editable graphics programs through a code-render-inspect loop (Yin et al., 2026).
Similar feedback-driven loops appear in visual editing (Yao et al., 2026), while BIM agents invoke APIs, manipulate IFC data, or operate authoring software (Du et al., 2026; Nithyanantham et al., 2025; Deng et al., 2025).
Xie et al. (Xie et al., 2025) further show the value of local correction for missed or misplaced 3D layout elements.
Motivated by these local correction paradigms, our cuboid representation and AssemblyNet support user-guided refinement, enabling controllable local edits and global correction for improved structural consistency.

## 3. CrabSet Construction

Learning BIM-ready cuboid reconstruction requires component-level volumetric supervision, including the geometry and relations of walls, doors, windows, and other elements.
However, as summarized in Table 1, most existing indoor datasets are designed for scene understanding, embodied AI, or layout estimation, and mainly provide 2D floor plans, room layouts, semantic labels, or structured planar annotations.
Only a few datasets contain explicit volumetric building components, and they are limited in scale or scene diversity.

To this end, we construct CrabSet, a cuboid-level dataset for indoor BIM reconstruction from point clouds.
Each sample contains an input point cloud, component-wise volumetric cuboid annotations, and pairwise relations among architectural components.
Each cuboid records its semantic category, instance identity, position, orientation, and dimensions.
As shown in Fig. 2, CrabSet combines component-wise cuboid annotations derived from Structured3D with procedurally synthesized scenes, covering both residential layouts and broader architectural configurations. For synthetic scenes, point clouds are generated through virtual scanning.

## S3D-derived.

This subset is derived from Structured3D, which provides diverse residential layouts with room partitions, openings, non-Manhattan structures, and occlusions.
Using its planar faces, normals, semantic labels, and outer contours, we convert walls, doors, and windows into component-wise volumetric cuboids.
This subset anchors CrabSet to a public residential benchmark.

## Boundary-varied.

This subset increases local geometric diversity by varying outer contours, wall thicknesses, and non-Manhattan wall configurations.
It encourages the model to recover accurate cuboid geometry and wall thickness.

## Scale-varied.

This subset expands spatial scale and component complexity through large architectural spaces with service shafts, columns, and multi-layer window configurations.
It improves the model’s ability to handle dense non-residential scenes with large variations in component size and spatial extent.

## Corridor-centered.

This subset focuses on circulation-driven layouts, including straight, bent, notched, H-shaped, T-shaped, and ring-like corridors.
Rooms and openings are arranged along corridor boundaries, covering large building configurations that are rarely represented in residential datasets.

## 4. Methodology

## 4.1. Problem Formulation

Our goal is to reconstruct as-built BIM components, such as walls, doors, windows, beams, and columns, from an input point cloud. We represent them as parameterized semantic cuboids along with their explicit topological relationships.

Formally, we define the input point cloud as $P=\{p_{i}\}$ , where each point feature $p_{i}$ contains spatial coordinates and color information. The reconstructed architectural components are represented by a set of semantic cuboids

 $C=\{c_{i}\},\quad c_{i}=[g_{i},s_{i}],\quad g_{i}=[x_{1},y_{1},z_{1},x_{2},y_{2},z_{2},w,h],$ 

where the geometric parameters $g_{i}$ define the bottom central axis connecting endpoints $(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})$ and $(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})$ , together with cuboid thickness $w$ and height $h$ , while $s_{i}$ denotes the semantic category.

The relationships between cuboids are further represented by:

 $R=\{r_{ij}\mid c_{i},c_{j}\in C\},$ 

where $r_{ij}$ encodes structural interactions between components, including attachment relationships (e.g., doors or windows belonging to a wall) and adjacency relationships between neighboring walls.

The overall reconstruction task is therefore formulated as

 $(C,R)=\mathcal{F}(P),$ 

where $\mathcal{F}$ denotes the proposed mapping function that reconstructs cuboids and their relationships from the input point cloud.

## 4.2. ProposalNet

We argue that the primary objective of the first stage is to generate accurate individual architectural components, since reliable prediction of each cuboid forms the foundation for subsequent relationship reasoning and structural assembly. Therefore, this module focuses exclusively on cuboid geometry and semantic prediction. Formally, we aim to learn the conditional distribution $p_{\theta}(C\mid P)$ , from which the initial cuboid proposals are generated:

 $C_{\text{init}}\sim p_{\theta}(C\mid P).$ 

Specifically, we employ Sonata (Wu et al., 2025) as the point cloud encoder and Qwen2.5-0.5B (Yang et al., 2024) as the auto-regressive cuboid generator.
The Sonata encoder is initialized with pretrained weights and further trained on our point cloud and cuboid pairs.
For each input scene, we voxel-downsample the point cloud at $2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and randomly keep at most $800{,}000$ points for training and inference.

The point cloud encoder and autoregressive cuboid decoder are connected by a lightweight MLP Adapter, which maps point cloud features into the decoder token embedding space.
Conditioned on the adapted point cloud tokens, the decoder sequentially generates the initial semantic cuboids.

## LIMITATIONS SECTION
CRAB still has several limitations.
(1) Missing component recovery.
CRAB can automatically correct continuous geometric errors, local misalignments, and redundant components, but entirely missing components may still require coarse user initialization. The supplementary material further shows AssemblyNet’s ability to correct geometric errors and local misalignments, while removing redundant components through component alignment.
(2) Irregular non-Manhattan geometry.
While CRAB handles standard Manhattan layouts and moderate non-Manhattan variations, highly irregular or curved structures remain challenging: cuboids can capture the dominant organization, but may oversimplify fine-scale deviations.
(3) Sparse infrequent components.
Beams, columns, stairs, and service shafts appear sparsely in current real-world test scenes, limiting comprehensive evaluation.
Although CRAB can model these elements in principle, robust reconstruction requires richer component-level supervision and broader BIM benchmarks.

## CONCLUSION
We presented CRAB, a cuboid-based approach to indoor Scan-to-BIM reconstruction.
The key observation is that BIM reconstruction requires component-level volumetric structure, not only layout curves or planar surfaces.
Accordingly, CRAB represents walls, doors, windows, beams and columns as semantic cuboids with explicit centerline, thickness, height, and opening parameters.
This representation makes architectural components directly editable and provides a natural interface for downstream BIM conversion.

The proposed framework follows a proposal-and-assembly design.
ProposalNet predicts coarse architectural cuboids from point-cloud observations, while AssemblyNet refines their geometry and relationships through relation-aware Flow Matching. Together, the two-stage system is capable of correcting geometric errors and local misalignments while removing redundant components through component alignment.
To support learning and evaluation, we constructed CrabSet, which provides cuboid-level supervision from Structured3D-derived annotations and procedurally generated architectural scenes.
Experiments on synthetic and real-world scans show that cuboid assembly yields more accurate wall-thickness estimation, more stable refinement, and more flexible BIM instantiation than floor-plan- or plane-based alternatives. Importantly, these gains in volumetric reconstruction do not come at the expense of layout or plane quality, where CRAB remains competitive with representation-specific baselines.

To address the limitations discussed earlier, future work will primarily broaden CRAB’s practical capabilities and application scope. We will explore (1) complex non-Manhattan geometry and richer primitives, including cylinders and curved surfaces, (2) broader BIM categories and structural relations, together with semantic plane cues as auxiliary geometric evidence for robust reconstruction under real-scene domain shifts, and (3) interactive or agent-assisted workflows, reducing manual effort and enabling more automated Scan-to-BIM reconstruction.
