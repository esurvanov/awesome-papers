## ABSTRACT
Direct preference optimization (DPO) incurs training costs in output scoring, sequence execution, and backpropagation, so reducing supervision alone does not necessarily save computation. We introduce a unified framework for stochastic pruning of the DPO computation graph that independently samples response positions for scoring, response prefixes for execution, and preference branches for backpropagation. By making these selections before the corresponding operations, the framework omits computation at each stage of a training step. Our analysis characterizes the objective changes induced by position and sequence pruning and uses inverse-probability weighting to obtain a conditionally unbiased single-branch gradient estimator for the sampled objective. Controlled measurements show that position and sequence pruning primarily reduce peak memory, with most output-memory savings attributable to an implementation that retains full response supervision. Branch pruning yields a $1.426\times$ step-time speedup over a concatenated DPO baseline and reduces training time by about 30\% relative to stock DPO across two model families, with slightly lower mean accuracy and benchmark-dependent changes in open-ended scores. These findings relate the resource benefits of stochastic graph pruning to the computation omitted and the resulting changes in the training objective.

## 1 Introduction

Direct preference optimization (DPO) formulates alignment as a logistic comparison of policy–reference likelihood ratios, with gradient contributions from both chosen and rejected responses (Rafailov et al., 2023). In large-vocabulary language-model training, materialized logits can dominate memory consumption (Wijmans et al., 2025), and attention I/O and operator fusion also affect memory use and training throughput (Dao et al., 2022; Hsu et al., 2025). These costs motivate our question of which computations a preference-training step can omit.

Figure 1: Stochastic interventions on the DPO computation graph. The two response branches share policy parameters. Rewards average policy–reference log-ratios over scored response positions. Fixed-reference computations use matching prefixes and positions and are omitted. Sampling outcomes and counts are schematic.

Prior work changes token supervision through confidence-based token selection or weighting (Yoon et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025), reward-based selection (Yang et al., 2025b), and weighting from contrastive model predictions (Liu et al., 2025), while memory-efficient kernels change output-score computation (Wijmans et al., 2025; Hsu et al., 2025). This distinction motivates our question: which parts of a DPO step can be omitted, and how do their resource savings relate to changes in the optimization objective?

Preference-objective structure affects optimization (Chen et al., 2026), while shared-parameter updates can couple response likelihoods (Yuan et al., 2025) and reduce preferred-response probability (Razin et al., 2025). Our token-level analysis examines this coupling (Appendix C.1), and Proposition 1 separates reward preservation in expectation from the nonlinear loss.

We introduce a framework that samples the DPO computation graph to control training cost (Fig. 1). Each training step jointly selects the executed response prefixes, the positions used to estimate rewards, and the branches that carry gradients. These selections precede sequence execution, vocabulary projection, and backpropagation, allowing computation to be omitted throughout the step. This view unifies position, sequence, and branch pruning through their effects on the sampled objective and its gradient. Our analysis characterizes the objective changes induced by reward sampling, while inverse-probability weighting makes the branch gradient conditionally unbiased for that objective.

Our contributions are:

• 

a unified framework for sampling DPO computation across response execution, token scoring, and backpropagation, with separately configurable sampling decisions.

• 

a characterization of the position and prefix objective changes, tolerance conditions that relate their optimal gaps to the retention rate and the truncation level, and a conditionally unbiased branch estimator with a stated convergence rate.

• 

controlled comparisons showing that full supervision retains most output-memory savings, that branch pruning reduces training time across model families, and that mean accuracy is slightly lower while open-ended score changes vary by benchmark (Secs. 4.2, 4.4 and 4.5).

## 2 Related Work

## Token-level credit in preference optimization.

A growing family of methods changes where DPO assigns credit through confidence-based token selection (Yoon et al., 2025), confidence-dependent weighting of varied tokens (Jiang et al., 2025), trained oracles for token rewards (Yang et al., 2025b), and contrastive token weights (Liu et al., 2025). Other approaches use optimal-transport reweighting (Li et al., 2025a), gradient-attribution priors (Yang et al., 2026), prefix-level ratio matching (Nguyen et al., 2026), implicit-reward credit (Zhao et al., 2026), attention-derived weights (Huang et al., 2026), minimal-edit emphasis in clinical dialogue (Lai et al., 2026a), or token-level KL control (Zeng et al., 2024). Our framework places supervision selection within the computation of a complete DPO step. Matched selection controls motivate uniform membership (Sec. E.1), and full-retention controls distinguish supervision deletion from implementation savings (Tab. 1).

## Efficient preference training.

Cut Cross-Entropy avoids materializing the token-by-vocabulary logit matrix (Wijmans et al., 2025), and Liger-Kernel uses fusion and chunked cross-entropy computation to reduce memory and improve training throughput (Hsu et al., 2025). FlashAttention relates attention efficiency to memory accesses rather than FLOP counts alone (Dao et al., 2022), while activation checkpointing trades recomputation for lower activation storage (Chen et al., 2016). Related efficiency work covers flow training (Du et al., 2022) and diffusion inference (Li et al., 2025b). SePO reports reduced GPU training hours through offline token selection (Yang et al., 2025b). Our framework considers how sampling throughout a DPO step changes both executed computation and the training objective. Controlled profiles separate implementation savings from the effects of reduced supervision and execution (Sec. 4.2).

## Stochastic computation and optimization dynamics.

Dropout randomly removes units (Srivastava et al., 2014) and stochastic depth bypasses sampled layers (Huang et al., 2016), while importance sampling can reduce stochastic-gradient variance using gradient-dependent probabilities (Katharopoulos and Fleuret, 2018). Preference-optimization analyses study gradient entanglement (Yuan et al., 2025), likelihood displacement (Razin et al., 2025), logit-space squeezing (Luo et al., 2026), and gated rejected contributions (Mouiche, 2026). Density-ratio estimation provides a distribution-comparison perspective (Chen et al., 2025) for preference objectives such as DDRO (Higuchi and Suzuki, 2025), whose update structure is analyzed alongside margin-based losses (Chen et al., 2026). Our analysis separates objective changes from gradient-estimation noise under composed sampling (Propositions 2 and 3.5). Shared-parameter response changes are analyzed in Appendix C.1.

## 3 Stochastic Graph Pruning

## 3.1 Formulation

Let $\mathcal{D}=\{({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}_{w},{\bm{y}}_{l})\}$ contain prompts ${\bm{x}}$ with chosen and rejected responses ${\bm{y}}_{w},{\bm{y}}_{l}$ . Write $\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}$ for the policy with trainable parameters ${\bm{\theta}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ for the fixed reference (Rafailov et al., 2023). For side $b\in\{w,l\}$ , let $T_{b}\geq 1$ count response tokens, excluding prompt and padding. At position $t$ , define the target-token log-ratio

 $u_{b,t}=\log\frac{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}({y}_{b,t}\mid{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}_{b,<t})}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}({y}_{b,t}\mid{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}_{b,<t})}.$ 

(1)

Here ${y}_{b,t}$ is the target token and ${\bm{y}}_{b,<t}$ its preceding response prefix. With mean rewards $R_{b}=T_{b}^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{b}}u_{b,t}$ , margin $\Delta=R_{w}-R_{l}$ , and scale $\beta>0$ , DPO minimizes the loss (Rafailov et al., 2023):

 $F({\bm{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}[\log\bigl(1+e^{-\beta\Delta}\bigr)].$ 

(2)

This length normalization parallels SimPO (Meng et al., 2024), but retains policy–reference log-ratios.

Fig. 1 separates three costs: dense vocabulary projection materializes $T_{b}\times V$ logits for vocabulary size $V$ (Wijmans et al., 2025), sequence execution computes attention and stores or recomputes activations (Dao et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2016), and backpropagation includes both responses (Rafailov et al., 2023).

Write ${\mathbb{I}}_{M}=\{1,\ldots,M\}$ . On side $b$ , sequence pruning selects length $M_{b}\in{\mathbb{I}}_{T_{b}}$ through cutoff fraction $\rho_{b}\in(0,1]$ , retaining $\tilde{\bm{y}}_{b}={\bm{y}}_{b,1:M_{b}}$ . Position pruning selects ${\mathbb{S}}_{b}\subseteq{\mathbb{I}}_{M_{b}}$ with $k_{b}=|{\mathbb{S}}_{b}|$ controlled by retention $\alpha\in(0,1]$ . Branch pruning selects $b^{\star}\in\{w,l\}$ with probabilities $q_{w},q_{l}>0$ , $q_{w}+q_{l}=1$ . All sampling laws are parameter-independent, and rewards average over selected positions. We use $\sigma$ for the sigmoid and $\sg$ for stop-gradient, which preserves values and blocks derivatives. Appendix A summarizes the notation.

Standalone position pruning uses $M_{b}=T_{b}$ . Define the selected-position reward

The subset is drawn before the language-model head, without computing policy probabilities. Applying the head only at ${\mathbb{S}}_{b}$ evaluates Eq. 4 exactly, skipping $M_{b}-k_{b}$ vocabulary projections, their logits, and their backward traffic.

Uniform sampling preserves the mean reward, but the nonlinear loss introduces a gap.

Proposition 1.

For a fully executed response, let ${\mathbb{U}}_{k}$ be a uniform size- $k$ subset of ${\mathbb{I}}_{T}$ and ${\bm{\phi}}_{t}=\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\log\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}({y}_{t}\mid{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}_{<t})$ .  $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[R({\mathbb{U}}_{k})]$ 
 $\displaystyle=R({\mathbb{I}}_{T}),$

(5)

and

 $0\leq\mathbb{E}[\ell(\Delta_{U})]-\ell(\Delta_{\mathrm{full}})\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{8}\mathrm{Var}(\Delta_{U}),$ 

(6)

where $\mathrm{Var}$ is over the sampled masks and $\mathrm{Var}(\Delta_{U})=\sum_{b:T_{b}>1}\frac{T_{b}-k_{b}}{k_{b}(T_{b}-1)}\,v_{b}$ . Here $\bar{u}_{b}=T_{b}^{-1}\sum_{t}u_{b,t}$ and $v_{b}=T_{b}^{-1}\sum_{t}(u_{b,t}-\bar{u}_{b})^{2}$ are the population mean and variance. Single-token sides contribute zero variance.

At $\alpha=1$ , selected-head execution preserves the full-response objective while avoiding projection outside valid response positions (Tab. 1).

To bound the sampled optimum, assume throughout this subsection and Secs. B.5 and B.6 that $|u_{b,t}|\leq U$ , $\|\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}u_{b,t}\|\leq G$ , $v_{b}\leq\bar{v}$ , and $T_{b}\geq T_{0}\geq 2$ on the analyzed region. The objectives are twice differentiable there and attain their minima.

Corollary 1 (Optimality gap of position pruning).

Let ${\bm{\theta}}^{*}$ minimize the full-response objective $F$ and ${\bm{\theta}}_{A}^{*}$ minimize $F_{A}({\bm{\theta}})=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{E}_{S}[\ell(\Delta_{U})]$ . Then

 $0\leq F({\bm{\theta}}_{A}^{*})-F({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{8}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\bigl[\mathrm{Var}_{S}(\Delta_{U})\bigr].$ 

(7)

The variance is evaluated at ${\bm{\theta}}^{*}$ over masks, then averaged over data. Since $k_{b}\geq\alpha T_{b}$ , Proposition 1 gives $\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}[\mathrm{Var}_{S}(\Delta_{U})]\leq 2\bar{v}\,r_{A}$ , where

 $r_{A}:=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha(T_{0}-1)}.$ 

(8)

Thus the bound decays as $1/T_{0}$ at fixed retention and vanishes at $\alpha=1$ . A sufficient condition for loss tolerance $\varepsilon_{F}>0$ is

 $\alpha\geq\frac{1}{1+4\varepsilon_{F}(T_{0}-1)/(\beta^{2}\bar{v})},$ 

(9)

A second-order expansion also gives $\|\nabla F_{A}-\nabla F\|=\mathcal{O}(r_{A})$ (Appendix B.6). If $\nabla^{2}F\succeq\mu I$ , $\mu>0$ , along the segment joining interior stationary points of $F_{A}$ and $F$ within the analyzed region, their displacement is $\mathcal{O}(r_{A})$ rather than $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{r_{A}})$ . The loss bound requires no curvature assumption. Sufficient curvature conditions appear in Appendix B.7.

Sequence pruning truncates each response before policy and reference execution. For minimum retained fraction $\rho_{\min}\in(0,1)$ , draw $(\tilde{\bm{y}}_{b},M_{b})\sim\prefixsample({\bm{y}}_{b},\rho_{\min})$ by

 $\displaystyle\rho_{b}$

 $\displaystyle M_{b}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\max(1,\lceil\rho_{b}T_{b}\rceil),$ 

 $\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{y}}_{b}$

Both models repack the prompt with $\tilde{\bm{y}}_{b}$ , removing suffix attention, activations, and recomputation. The reward is $R_{b}=M_{b}^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{b}}u_{b,t}$ , and $\rho_{b}\equiv 1$ recovers the full-response objective. Sec. 4.3 evaluates $\rho_{\min}$ .

Prefix inclusion and length normalization favor earlier positions:

Lemma 1 (Implicit temporal weighting).

For one response of length $T$ , let $M\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$ be the sampled cutoff, with law $q(m)=\Pr(M=m)$ and prefix reward $R_{M}=M^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{M}u_{t}$ . Define the temporal weights $w_{t}:=\sum_{m=t}^{T}q(m)/m$ . Then

 $\mathbb{E}_{M}[R_{M}]=\sum_{t=1}^{T}w_{t}u_{t}.$ 

(11)

The weights satisfy $w_{1}\geq\cdots\geq w_{T}\geq 0$ and $\sum_{t=1}^{T}w_{t}=1$ . Uniform weights $w_{t}=1/T$ occur exactly when $M=T$ almost surely.

Appendix B.2 proves this identity and characterizes the cutoff laws. The nonlinear loss adds a Jensen gap:

Lemma 2 (Prefix Jensen gap).

Let $\Delta_{M}$ be the random-prefix margin, $\mu_{\mathrm{pref}}=\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{M}]$ its mean, and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pref}}=\mathbb{E}[\ell(\Delta_{M})]$ the expected prefix loss with Jensen gap $J_{\mathrm{pref}}$ . Then

 $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pref}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\ell(\mu_{\mathrm{pref}})+J_{\mathrm{pref}},$ 

(12)

 $\displaystyle 0\leq J_{\mathrm{pref}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{8}\mathrm{Var}(\Delta_{M}).$ 

Unlike position pruning, sequence pruning can shift the mean margin. The shift $\ell(\mu_{\mathrm{pref}})-\ell(\Delta_{\mathrm{full}})$ has no fixed sign, whereas $J_{\mathrm{pref}}\geq 0$ vanishes for deterministic cutoffs or $\beta=0$ (Appendix B.3). Parameter-independent cutoffs make $\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ell(\Delta_{M})$ unbiased for $\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pref}}$ , generally different from the full-response gradient. Sec. 4.4 evaluates the resulting performance.

Assume causal execution with the prompt retained, so prefix token scores and gradients match full execution. For truncation fraction $h_{b}=1-M_{b}/T_{b}$ , Eq. 10 gives $\mathbb{E}[h_{b}]\leq(1-\rho_{\min})/2$ . Prefix and full mean rewards differ by at most $2Uh_{b}$ , and their gradients by $2Gh_{b}$ . The $\beta$ -Lipschitz loss therefore yields

 $|F_{P}({\bm{\theta}})-F({\bm{\theta}})|\leq 2\beta U(1-\rho_{\min}),$ 

(13)

where $F_{P}=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pref}}]$ . Consequently, $F({\bm{\theta}}_{P}^{*})-F({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\leq 4\beta U(1-\rho_{\min})$ . Unlike Eq. 7, this bound has no response-length decay. At the tested $\rho_{\min}=1/2$ , the optimality bound equals the full loss range over $|\Delta|\leq 2U$ , making it vacuous. This comparison concerns theoretical bounds, not measured deviations. Appendix B.8 also derives gradient and displacement bounds.

 $\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}=-\lambda\,\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}R_{w}+\lambda\,\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}R_{l}.$

## LIMITATIONS SECTION
Evaluation covers parameter-efficient text preference training with an online reference on one eight-accelerator setup. Savings may vary with hardware, batch geometry, and reference execution.

## CONCLUSION
We separate output scoring, sequence execution, and backward computation to locate where stochastic DPO computation saves resources and how it changes optimization. Most output-memory savings come from the implementation rather than from supervision deletion, the branch-pruning time benefit reproduces across Qwen seeds and Mistral, and both accompany lower mean accuracy with mixed open-ended results, so configuration choice should follow the resource constraint and the acceptable objective change.
