## ABSTRACT
Existing world models typically perceive a scene through current visual frame or a narrow history window. However, these observations may not reveal the physics properties that determine transition dynamics, such as an object's mass distribution, joint damping, or surface friction. Without calibrating these physics factors for world models, predictions tend to regress toward an empirical prior and induce severe drift in novel test scenes. To this end, we propose PhysiCalWorld, which calibrates action-conditioned world models to the instance-specific dynamics via in-context prompting, without fine-tuning or analytical physics modeling. Our method encodes an exploratory interaction clip collected within the test scene into a compact physics descriptor token, which grounds subsequent predictions in the scene's actual physical behavior. PhysiCalWorld is trained end-to-end with the latent prediction loss alone, without physics labels. At inference time, the descriptor token is extracted once and can be reused across rollouts with negligible compute overhead. We evaluate PhysiCalWorld across four environments covering diverse physics variations and show that it substantially improves rollout prediction accuracy and visual planning success rate. The learned descriptors also form a physically structured latent space and generalize beyond the physics ranges seen during training.

## 1 Introduction

World models predict how a scene evolves given its current state and
the intended actions of the agents within it.
Trained on internet-scale data, video-based world
models (Bruce et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023; Team et al., 2026; Agarwal et al., 2026)
are now able to generate photorealistic visual predictions of complex scene dynamics that are difficult to model with traditional physics engines.
Modeling the world in image space is thus becoming a promising alternative to hand-built physics modeling in such areas as robotics (Hou et al., 2026) and autonomous driving (Feng et al., 2025).

Existing world models usually generate predictions from the current frame or a short visual history. However, such observations may leave the underlying physical properties unconstrained, forcing the world models to guess a future state among multiple possible ones. For example, an opaque bottle appears identical whether or not it is filled with water, yet it will respond differently under identical actuation. A world model that relies only on empirical priors over object category or appearance will produce incorrect predictions in such scenarios, limiting its applicability in downstream applications that require accurate dynamics (Zhou et al., 2025; Quevedo et al., 2026). This motivates calibrating the world model to the hidden physical properties of each scene.

A natural way to resolve this physics ambiguity is to physically interact with the scene to infer its hidden properties, similar to how we humans would do for an object with unknown properties.
Inspired by this,
prior system identification methods (Wu et al., 2015; Yu et al., 2017; Kumar et al., 2021) attempt to construct accurate physical models with closed-form equations and fit the predefined physics parameters to the in-situ interaction data. However, real-world systems exhibit complex, coupled physics factors, thus building a geometric model or formulating closed-form equations can be challenging.
Hence, to adapt to each deployment setting, existing world models (Gao et al., 2026) are typically finetuned on a small set of in-house data, which is computationally expensive.

To this end, we propose PhysiCalWorld, a method for in-context Physics Calibration for World models.
Inspired by the in-context learning capabilities of large sequence models (Brown et al., 2020; Bar et al., 2022), we prompt world models with a sequence of observation-action pairs
collected in the test environment (i.e., an environment whose physics configuration is unknown) for physics calibration.
Our in-context prompting method sidesteps both the need for human effort to build analytical physics simulators for system identification and expensive model finetuning. Our method requires only an exploratory interaction clip without any gradient descent steps.
PhysiCalWorld compresses the interaction sequence into a compact physics descriptor token that conditions the world model. The entire system is trained end-to-end with only a video prediction objective, requiring neither ground-truth physics parameters nor a simulator.

At test time, PhysiCalWorld first extracts the descriptor from a few exploratory interactions with the test scene (e.g., via random exploration or scripted policies).
This descriptor then conditions all subsequent rollouts in that scene, enabling the world model to generate physically consistent predictions in novel configurations without any test-time weight updates.

In summary, our contributions are three-fold:

• 

In-Context Physics Calibration: We formulate physics calibration of generative world models as in-context conditioning: from a short interaction sequence, the model adapts zero-shot to instance-specific dynamics, with no test-time weight updates.

• 

Physics Descriptors Learned from Video Alone: We introduce a physics descriptor token that grounds the world model in hidden physical properties. It is learned end-to-end from video prediction alone, requiring neither ground-truth physics parameters nor a simulator.

• 

Performance and Analysis: Across four environments with single and coupled physics variations, PhysiCalWorld consistently improves rollout accuracy and planning success on two world model backbones. Its single-token descriptor matches or exceeds raw-context conditioning at a fraction of the cost. Without physics labels, the descriptors form a physically structured latent space and extrapolate beyond the training parameter range.

## 2 Related Work

World Models for Robotics.

World models predict future states conditioned on current observations and agent actions, serving as an increasingly foundational framework for robotics (Li et al., 2025c; Hou et al., 2026). For example, they are scalable neural simulators, enabling low-cost policy evaluation without real-world execution (Guo et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026e; Team et al., 2025; Tseng et al., 2025).
Through future imagination, world models can also substantially improve sample efficiency in reinforcement learning (Hafner et al., 2019; Hansen et al., 2022; Seo et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2023) and facilitate policy learning (Saxena et al., 2026; Luo & Du, 2025).
Moreover, optimizing action sequences with world models enables embodied agents to complete novel tasks via visual trajectory planning (Zhou et al., 2025; Assran et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024).
However, existing methods typically condition only on the current frame or a narrow observation history, assuming that these observations suffice to identify the underlying transition dynamics of the test scene.
As a result, predictions tend to suffer from severe drift if latent physical properties vary across test scenes.
In this work, we ground model predictions in the actual test-scene dynamics by introducing an additional in-context interaction prompt collected within the test scene as input to the world model, substantially enhancing the prediction accuracy and visual planning performance.

In-Context Learning in Physical AI.

Context can serve embodied agents in various forms. For example, it can adapt a robot policy to test-scene dynamics by conditioning directly on execution history or by explicitly recovering physical parameters (Lee et al., 2020; Kumar et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). Another role is in-context policy learning, where task demonstrations steer actions either through structured representations such as keypoints and graphs (Di Palo & Johns, 2024; Vosylius & Johns, 2025; Hwang et al., 2024) or raw video-action pairs (Fu et al., 2024; Shah et al., 2025; Ding et al., 2026),
and in-context reinforcement learning (Lee et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2023).
Most recently, context has been applied to action-conditioned world models by prepending context prefixes to adapt to new camera viewpoints or embodiments (Yang et al., 2026).
However, prior works focus on policy adaptation, require privileged physical priors, or prepend raw context frames at high computational cost. In contrast, we use context to enable physics-grounded future prediction in world models, introducing a compact physics descriptor token to minimize computational overhead while maintaining high prediction accuracy and rollout efficiency.

Physics-Aware Video Generation.

Video generative models have advanced rapidly (Yang et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026), with widespread commercial deployment (Seedance et al., 2026; Wan et al., 2025).
However, their adherence to physical laws remains limited (Motamed et al., 2026; Upadhyay et al., 2026; Bansal et al., 2025; Meng et al., 2024).
Recent efforts seek to improve physical awareness in video models by incorporating explicit physics equations or parameters in the generation process (Zhang et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2026a; Yuan et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026b),
coupling with external physics simulators (Liu et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2025; Foo et al., 2026),
leveraging foundation models (Xue et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026b), or optimizing handcrafted rewards (Zhang et al., 2026a; Li et al., 2025a).
However, these approaches either rely on analytical formulations and are restricted to a pre-defined set of physics properties, or focus on learning general physics and tend to fail in scenarios that require fine-grained instance-level physics grounding.
In comparison, our work focuses on calibrating action-conditioned world models to the instance-specific dynamics of a test scene from an exploratory interaction prompt, operating entirely end-to-end without prior assumptions or ground-truth supervision of the environment’s underlying physics.

## 3 Method

We consider an embodied agent interacting with an environment over discrete timesteps $t$ . At each step, the agent receives a high-dimensional visual observation $o_{t}\in\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}$ (and an optional proprioceptive state $p_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{p}}$ ) and executes a control action $a_{t}\in\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{a}}$ . Our objective is to learn an action-conditioned world model that predicts future observations directly in a compact latent space and enables both long-horizon rollout and visual planning.

Latent World Models.

While our proposed in-context prompting framework is a plug-and-play module applicable to different world model architectures, we base the proposed method on two recent latent world models, DINO-WM (Zhou et al., 2025) and LeWM (Maes et al., 2026), due to their efficiency and strong trajectory planning performance.
In these latent world models, each input RGB frame $o_{t}$ is encoded by a visual encoder $E_{\text{v}}$ into a set of visual tokens $z_{t}$ :

 $z_{t}=E_{\text{v}}(o_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{z}\times d_{z}},$ 

(1)

where $N_{z}$ denotes the number of visual tokens per frame and $d_{z}$ is the feature dimension.
Meanwhile, actions $a_{t}$ are mapped into action embeddings $e_{t}^{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{a}^{\prime}}$ via a learned MLP $E_{\text{a}}$ :

 $e_{t}^{a}=E_{\text{a}}(a_{t}).$ 

(2)

The latent dynamics predictor $f_{\theta}$ (a causal transformer with weights $\theta$ ) models temporal transitions over a history window of $K$ steps. At step $t$ , the predictor takes a sequence of past visual tokens and action embeddings to predict the next latent tokens:

 $\hat{z}_{t+1}=f_{\theta}\left(\{z_{i},e_{i}^{a}\}_{i=t-K+1}^{t}\right).$ 

(3)

For visual evaluation and pixel-space rollouts, we train a lightweight convolutional decoder $D_{\text{v}}$ that reconstructs images $\hat{o}_{t}$ from latent tokens, $\hat{o}_{t+1}=D_{\text{v}}(\hat{z}_{t+1})$ , while downstream visual planning can be conducted directly within the latent space $\mathcal{Z}$ .

## 3.1 Physics Calibration via Physics Descriptor Token

Physics Ambiguity in Visual World Models.

The forward transition function $\mathcal{T}$ of an environment depends not only on the current states and actions, but also on a set of underlying physics properties $\xi\in\mathcal{X}$ (e.g., mass, surface friction, or joint damping).
Because many of these physics properties rarely correlate with surface appearance,
a static visual frame $o_{t}$ or a brief observation window can hardly identify $\xi$ and hence leaves the true transition dynamics underdetermined.

Consequently, action-conditioned future prediction becomes an ill-posed, one-to-many problem.
Without sufficient interaction data from the test scene to calibrate the future predictor $f_{\theta}$ , its prediction may collapse to the expectation over the training distribution with compounding dynamic drift when the test environment deviates from the nominal mean.
While one could explicitly estimate and feed the test-scene physics parameters to $f_{\theta}$ , this usually relies on ad-hoc analytical modeling (as one must know in advance which physics properties determine the dynamics) and also introduces cascading estimation errors.
An alternative strategy is to scale up the history window $K$ of world models, so that the future predictor $f_{\theta}$ can directly extract the physical transition dynamics from previous interaction.
However, self-attention complexity in transformers scales quadratically with sequence length, making long-history conditioning computationally prohibitive. Furthermore, continuously carrying long raw frame histories (e.g., thousands of tokens) in every inference step introduces substantial redundancy, as the underlying physics configuration can usually be represented far more compactly.

Physics Descriptor Token.

To address these challenges, our method calibrates world models through a single compact representation, termed physics descriptor token $\mathbf{z}_{\text{phy}}$ , which is extracted from an exploratory interaction sequence collected in the test scene.
Formally, given an interaction clip $\mathcal{C}=\{(o_{i}^{c},a_{i}^{c})\}_{i=1}^{T_{c}}$ of length $T_{c}$ , the context encoder $g_{\phi}$ maps it to a descriptor token
 $\mathbf{z}_{\text{phy}}=g_{\phi}(\mathcal{C})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{phy}}}$ .
Internally, a lightweight visual encoder $E_{c}^{v}$ extracts per-frame visual features and an action encoder $E_{c}^{a}$ maps the control actions into action embeddings.
At each timestep $i$ , the two are concatenated along the channel dimension into a joint transition token $[E_{c}^{v}(o_{i}^{c})\mid E_{c}^{a}(a_{i}^{c})]\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{v}+d_{a}^{\prime}}$ .
A transformer $E_{c}^{\text{fuse}}$ then fuses these transition tokens across time and pools them via a learnable query token $\mathbf{z}_{\text{cls}}$ :

 $\mathbf{z}_{\text{phy}}=g_{\phi}(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq E_{c}^{\text{fuse}}\Bigl(\mathbf{z}_{\text{cls}},\;\bigl\{\bigl[\,E_{c}^{v}(o_{i}^{c})\;\big|\;E_{c}^{a}(a_{i}^{c})\,\bigr]\bigr\}_{i=1}^{T_{c}}\Bigr)_{0},$ 

(4)

where $[\,\cdot\mid\cdot\,]$ denotes channel-wise concatenation and $(\cdot)_{0}$ denotes the output at the query-token position. We use $\phi$ to collectively denote the parameters of $g_{\phi}$ , including $E_{c}^{\text{fuse}}$ , $E_{c}^{v}$ , $E_{c}^{a}$ , and $\mathbf{z}_{\text{cls}}$ .
The world model’s latent dynamics predictor $f_{\theta}$ is subsequently conditioned on
 $\mathbf{z}_{\text{phy}}$ alongside the default observation-action history window $K$ , predicting future latents $\hat{z}_{t+1}$ as:

 $\hat{z}_{t+1}=f_{\theta}\Bigl(\bigl\{(z_{i},e_{i}^{a})\bigr\}_{i=t-K+1}^{t},\;\mathbf{z}_{\text{phy}}\Bigr).\vskip-5.0pt$ 

(5)

Efficiency and Reusability. The design of physics descriptor token provides two practical advantages. (1) minimal rollout overhead: adding a single additional token $\mathbf{z}_{\text{phy}}$ into the predictor $f_{\theta}$ introduces only a constant and almost negligible computational cost, circumventing the quadratic attention overhead compared to using longer history or simply prepending raw context frames to $f_{\theta}$ (see +Long-History and +Context-Prefix in Section 4 and Figure 6 for comparison);
(2) one-pass extraction and in-context reuse: $\mathbf{z}_{\text{phy}}$ is computed via a single forward pass of a lightweight context encoder and can then be cached.

## CONCLUSION
Limitations.
Our approach has several limitations.
First, training PhysiCalWorld requires grouping trajectories by the physics configurations under which they were collected. While partitioning data by environment identity is far less demanding than deriving analytical equations, curating grouped data from in-the-wild video remains non-trivial. One potential direction to relax this is leveraging self-supervised representation learning to automatically
cluster latent transition dynamics into environment groups.
Moreover, the proposed test-time calibration depends on the quality of the interaction prompt given to the model. While we find that simple playful or even random policies often suffice, future work could explore active or curiosity-driven exploration to collect more informative prompts.
Finally, our framework assumes the physics properties are stationary: the dynamics remain unchanged within an episode. Extending this approach to non-stationary environments (e.g., fluctuating wind fields) may require additional techniques such as reactive prompting or online descriptor updates.

Conclusion.
We believe that world models should go beyond generic visual fidelity toward instance-specific physics-grounded prediction.
In this work, we take a step toward this goal by introducing an in-context calibration framework that grounds action-conditioned world models in the actual physics dynamics of novel test scenes.
We achieve this by prompting the model with an interaction sequence collected within the test scene, enabling zero-shot calibration without finetuning or analytical physics priors.
Evaluations across diverse environments demonstrate that our approach substantially improves predictive accuracy and downstream visual planning.
