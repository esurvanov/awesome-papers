## ABSTRACT
Reinforcement learning (RL) enables adaptive and autonomous decision-making in embedded, edge, and heterogeneous computing systems. Field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs) and integrated circuits (ICs) offer low latency, energy efficiency, and architectural flexibility for RL deployment, while introducing challenges in resource constraints, implementation complexity, and hardware-algorithm co-design. This review surveys more than 100 studies on RL implementations across FPGA and IC platforms, emphasizing architectures, implementation strategies, performance evaluation, and applications. The literature is organized by RL family, including model-based Markov decision processes, classical value-based methods, deep RL, actor-critic, policy-gradient, and multi-agent approaches. We further classify studies by abstraction level, hardware realization strategy, and application domain, examining how RL algorithms map onto reconfigurable and fixed hardware. Unlike surveys restricted to particular algorithms or platforms, this work provides a unified analysis of FPGA and IC implementations, covering acceleration, reconfigurable architectures, energy-aware control, and system-level optimization. Comparative assessments consider resource utilization, latency, throughput, power consumption, and reported performance gains. Finally, we identify recurring architectural patterns, practical limitations, open challenges, and research directions for efficient RL-driven hardware systems. The survey offers a consolidated reference for researchers developing and evaluating RL solutions on specialized hardware.

Abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for enabling adaptive, autonomous, and self-optimizing behavior in modern computing systems, particularly as applications increasingly migrate toward embedded, edge, and heterogeneous hardware platforms. Field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs) and integrated circuits (ICs) offer compelling advantages for RL deployment, including low latency, energy efficiency, and fine-grained architectural customization, but they also introduce unique design challenges that differ substantially from conventional CPU- and GPU- based implementations. This review presents a comprehensive survey of RL implementations on FPGA and IC platforms, with a particular emphasis on architectural design, performance measurement, and hardware–algorithm co-design. A broad body of more than one hundred studies is systematically analyzed and organized into major RL families, ranging from model-based Markov decision processes and classical value-based methods to deep RL, Actor-critic (AC), policy-gradient (PG), and multi-agent approaches. The review further categorizes existing works according to abstraction levels, hardware realization strategies, and application domains, highlighting how different RL algorithms map onto reconfigurable and fixed hardware substrates. In contrast to prior surveys that focus narrowly on specific algorithms or platforms, this work provides a unified perspective spanning both FPGA and IC implementations, encompassing acceleration techniques, reconfigurable hardware approaches, energy-aware control, and system-level optimization. Detailed comparisons are provided in terms of resource utilization, latency, throughput, power consumption, and achieved performance gains. Additionally, this review identifies recurring architectural patterns, discusses practical limitations, and outlines open research challenges and future directions for RL-driven intelligent hardware systems. By consolidating design insights, evaluation metrics, and application trends, this review aims to serve as a comprehensive reference for researchers and practitioners working at the intersection of RL and hardware platforms.

## 1 Introduction

Recent years have witnessed a growing shift toward intelligent, autonomous decision-making embedded directly within computing systems, with increasing emphasis on assessing the real-world applicability and feasibility of algorithms that demonstrate strong performance under simulation-based evaluations. As workloads become increasingly dynamic and heterogeneous—particularly in networking and edge computing, cyber–security, robotics [8], natural language processing, intelligent sensing, and embedded environments—traditional static control and optimization techniques, such as proportional–integral–derivative control and fixed-rule heuristic approaches [108] or meta-heuristic-based optimization methods [127], often struggle to cope with variability in data rates, power budgets, timing constraints, and environmental conditions. These approaches are typically designed around fixed operating assumptions and lack the adaptability required to respond effectively to non-stationary workloads or unforeseen runtime disturbances.

RL (RL), as a core paradigm within ML (ML), has gained prominence in this context because it provides a principled framework for sequential decision-making under uncertainty. By continuously interacting with an environment and updating its policy based on observed rewards, an RL agent can learn to adapt its behavior online, even when system dynamics are partially unknown or change over time. This capability makes RL particularly attractive for modern computing platforms that must balance competing objectives such as performance, energy efficiency, latency, and reliability under tight operational constraints. However, translating RL from software-centric implementations to practical hardware platforms introduces significant challenges. Many RL algorithms rely on iterative updates, stochastic exploration, and memory-intensive computations that are difficult to reconcile with the strict latency, power, and determinism requirements of embedded systems. Implementations based purely on general-purpose CPU or GPU often suffer from high energy consumption, unpredictable execution timing, and limited scalability, which restrict their suitability for real-time or resource-constrained applications.

FPGA (FPGA)s and IC provide an alternative hardware substrate that fundamentally reshapes how ML, particularly RL, can be realized in practice. FPGA provide fine-grained parallelism, customizable datapaths, and reconfigurability, allowing RL algorithms to be mapped onto hardware architectures tailored to specific workloads and constraints. IC-based realizations, including ASIC and SoC platforms, further extend these advantages by enabling deterministic execution, compact form factors, and ultra-low power operation. At the same time, these platforms impose stricter design constraints, limited flexibility after deployment, and increased development complexity compared with software-based solutions. As a result, the intersection of RL with hardware design has evolved into a multidisciplinary research area encompassing algorithm–hardware co-design [64], hardware acceleration [90, 41, 58], architectural specialization [25], numerical precision optimization [5], and system-level control, including digital twin–based frameworks [106]. In this emerging landscape, RL plays a dual role: it can be treated as a workload to be accelerated on hardware, and it can also serve as a control mechanism for the hardware itself, governing tasks such as dynamic voltage and frequency scaling, resource allocation, scheduling, partial reconfiguration, and energy management. This duality blurs the traditional boundary between learning algorithms and hardware platforms, creating new opportunities as well as new design challenges. Despite rapid progress, the existing literature on RL implementations for FPGA and IC platforms remains fragmented.

Motivated by the increasingly practical shift of RL from a purely algorithmic concept to a hardware-constrained workload, this review consolidates and structures the fast-growing body of RL implementations on FPGA and IC platforms.
In particular, this survey emphasizes real-time pattern recognition and perception-driven decision-making systems, where hardware constraints critically affect learning efficiency, latency, energy consumption, and deployment feasibility.
Instead of revisiting algorithmic fundamentals, it adopts a hardware-centric lens to clarify how design and implementation choices shape real-world realizations of RL.
The review organizes the literature into coherent categories and distills recurring design patterns, trade-offs, and limitations, thereby clarifying the current state-of-the-art and highlighting open challenges that guide future research at the intersection of RL, pattern recognition, and intelligent hardware.

## 1.1 Related work and our contribution

Several surveys have examined the relationship between RL and reconfigurable or heterogeneous hardware, yet significant gaps remain. The survey in [118] provides an extensive overview of domain-specific RL architectures, with a primary focus on FPGA-based accelerators. However, it does not integrate IC implementations, unified abstraction layers, nor does it cover the complete spectrum of RL algorithmic families such as SARSA (SARSA), MC (MC), AC (AC), and PPO (PPO). Similarly, the work in [44] concentrates on ML applications within FPGA EDA (EDA) tools. While valuable, its treatment of RL is brief, lacks architectural depth, and does not relate RL methods to accelerator design or runtime optimization. The review in [162] centers on ML techniques for control and monitoring but does not address RL-driven FPGA or IC architectures, nor broader comparisons across RL categories. Additionally, the heterogeneous-chip scheduling survey in [99] identifies RL primarily as a future research direction without analyzing existing RL implementations, hardware mapping strategies, or design-flow implications. Consequently, none of these studies provide a unified RL-centric perspective that spans both FPGA and IC platforms while simultaneously comparing all major RL types, synthesizing research areas and applications, and identifying cross-cutting architectural and methodological limitations. ## 1.2 Review methodology

To ensure methodological rigor, transparency, and reproducibility, this review adopts a systematic literature search and selection strategy centered on peer-reviewed studies addressing the implementation of RL on FPGA and IC platforms. The primary search was conducted using Scopus as the main indexing engine, which provides broad coverage of major scientific publishers and digital libraries, including IEEE Xplore, Elsevier ScienceDirect, ACM Digital Library, Web of Science, Wiley, Springer, and IET Digital Library, among others. To consistently retrieve works at the intersection of RL algorithms and hardware realization, a structured Boolean query was formulated and applied across databases using title, abstract, and keyword fields. The search logic was designed to capture both classical and deep RL methods, while explicitly constraining results to hardware-oriented implementations rather than purely algorithmic or simulation-based studies. The generic query structure can be expressed as:

Selected papers =
FROM (“Title” OR “Abstract” OR “Keywords”)
SELECT (References WHERE
(RL OR DRL OR “Reinforcement Learning” OR “Deep Reinforcement Learning”) AND (FPGA OR “Field-Programmable Gate Array” OR ASIC OR IC OR SoC OR “Integrated Circuit”)) FILTER (Language = “English” AND Year BETWEEN 2015 AND 2026 AND DocumentType = (“Journal Article” OR “Conference Paper”))

The logical operators AND and OR ensure precise targeting of studies that explicitly combine RL with FPGA or IC hardware platforms. The selected time window reflects the emergence of practical RL hardware realizations, from early tabular and MDP (MDP)-based accelerators to modern deep and AC architectures.

To balance completeness with technical focus, the retrieved studies were further screened using relevance and quality criteria. Seminal RL hardware-acceleration works were retained when they directly supported the FPGA/IC-centered taxonomy, architectural discussion, performance comparison, or application-level analysis of this review. However, earlier works already extensively covered in prior surveys were not repeated exhaustively unless they established a necessary technical baseline or motivated a later research direction. This avoids redundant historical coverage while preserving the foundational context required for the proposed survey.

Redundant studies, papers with insufficient hardware-implementation details, works lacking meaningful evaluation results, and studies only weakly related to RL-based FPGA/IC platforms were excluded. Priority was given to recent studies that provide clear implementation details and contribute to emerging directions such as energy-efficient RL acceleration, quantized RL hardware, FPGA/IC-based runtime optimization, RL-assisted synthesis, MARL (MARL) hardware, and application-specific deployment.

## LIMITATIONS SECTION
Despite the significant progress achieved by RL-based FPGA and IC accelerators in terms of latency, energy efficiency, and real-time capability, several fundamental limitations remain that hinder their broader adoption and long-term scalability. These limitations span algorithmic, architectural, system-level, and methodological dimensions and are consistently observed across the surveyed works.

## CONCLUSION
This review has provided a comprehensive and hardware-centric synthesis of RL implementations on FPGA and IC platforms, spanning more than two decades of research and over one hundred representative studies. By systematically organizing the literature across RL algorithmic families, hardware abstraction levels, architectural design strategies, and application domains, the survey clarifies how learning-based decision-making has progressively transitioned from software-centric experimentation to practical, resource-aware hardware realizations.

A central outcome of this review is the identification of recurring architectural patterns that underpin successful RL deployment on FPGA and IC substrates. Across classical tabular methods, deep value-based approaches, and modern AC and policy-gradient algorithms, effective designs consistently exploit fine-grained parallelism, pipelined datapaths, quantized arithmetic, and tightly coupled on-chip memory hierarchies. At the same time, the review highlights a clear divergence between FPGA-oriented solutions—favoring flexibility, reconfigurability, and rapid prototyping—and IC-based realizations, which prioritize determinism, compactness, and ultra-low power operation. The unified abstraction framework presented in this work demonstrates that, despite these differences, coherent design flows can bridge FPGA prototyping and IC deployment when hardware–algorithm co-design principles are applied early. From an algorithmic perspective, the survey reveals that no single RL paradigm dominates across all hardware and application contexts. Lightweight tabular methods such as QL and SARSA remain attractive for real-time embedded control due to their simplicity and predictable timing, whereas deep RL approaches, including DQN, DDPG, PPO, and AC variants, enable scalability to high-dimensional sensing and control problems at the cost of increased architectural complexity. MARL further extends these capabilities to distributed and large-scale systems but introduces new challenges related to coordination, non-stationarity, and interconnect overhead. These trade-offs emphasize that algorithm selection must be driven jointly by task requirements and hardware constraints rather than by learning performance alone.
