## ABSTRACT
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) frequently integrates knowledge graphs (KGs) for complex multi-hop question answering. However, their performance is increasingly bottlenecked by the knowledge structuring and reasoning processes. (i) Severe semantic loss: Distilling continuous texts into discrete symbols strips away contextual nuances and narrative coherence. (ii) Pronounced modality mismatch: Fragmented graph structures clash with the continuous texts LLMs are pre-trained on, while rewriting them into natural language introduces generative noise and omits information. To address these limitations, we propose ThreadRAG, a semantic-topology-based RAG framework for context-preserving knowledge structuring and modality-native evidence reasoning. ThreadRAG introduces a Semantic Topology Mapper (STM), which organizes retrieved documents into context-intact Semantic Nodes and constructs a Semantic Topology Graph (STG) through narrative, semantic, and lexical edges. It further incorporates Energy-Driven Pathfinding (EDP), which identifies query-relevant subgraphs via energy diffusion and deterministically linearizes them into coherent textual evidence paths. Experiments on three multi-hop QA benchmarks demonstrate that ThreadRAG consistently outperforms strong baselines. Notably, it achieves a 14.4% EM improvement over text-centric methods on HotpotQA and surpasses the best baseline by 5.8% EM on 2WikiMultihop, while reducing token cost to 23.3% of the strongest baseline.

## 1 Introduction

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has become a powerful paradigm for enhancing large language models (LLMs) on knowledge-intensive tasks by grounding generation in external evidence Gao et al. (2023). However, complex multi-hop question answering (QA) remains challenging for existing RAG systems Lewis et al. (2020); Izacard et al. (2022); Trivedi et al. (2023). Unlike single-hop QA, multi-hop QA requires models to identify scattered evidence across multiple documents, connect them through latent relations, and perform multi-step reasoning Shao et al. (2023).

Graph-enhanced RAG methods address this challenge by introducing structural priors over retrieved knowledge. Despite their promise, existing graph-RAG paradigms often suffer from a mismatch between how knowledge is structured and how LLMs perform reasoning. As illustrated in Figure 1, this mismatch manifests in two key limitations. First, existing graph construction pipelines can incur substantial semantic loss. Triplet-based knowledge graphs compress continuous text into rigid symbolic tuples, often discarding contextual nuances, while chunk-based graphs split documents into isolated segments, leading to semantic truncation and noisy boundaries. Such fragmentation weakens the continuous contextual pathways required for multi-hop reasoning. Second, existing graph representations introduce a modality mismatch at inference time. LLMs are primarily optimized to consume coherent natural language sequences, whereas graph-RAG methods typically provide fragmented graph paths or rely on generative verbalization to convert subgraphs into text. These conversions can introduce redundancy, hallucinated transitions, or omission of critical evidence, thereby limiting the effectiveness of structural priors.

Figure 1: Comparison of graph-RAG paradigms. Left: Our proposed ThreadRAG (a) structures raw texts into a Semantic Topology Graph (STG) and extracts Semantic Topology Paths (ST-Paths), preserving semantic context and aligning modality for LLM deduction. Right: Existing paradigms, Triplet KG (b) and Chunk KG (c), fragment texts into discrete components, introducing severe semantic loss and modality mismatch.

To address these limitations, we propose ThreadRAG, a semantic-topology-based RAG framework that preserves textual context during knowledge structuring and converts graph evidence into LLM-native reasoning sequences. ThreadRAG consists of two key components: a Semantic Topology Mapper (STM) for high-fidelity knowledge structuring, and an Energy-Driven Pathfinding module (EDP) for query-aware evidence selection and topological linearization.

A Semantic Topology Mapper for high-fidelity knowledge structuring.
To mitigate semantic loss introduced by conventional graph construction, STM structures retrieved documents into an LLM-oriented Semantic Topology Graph (STG). Instead of extracting symbolic triplets or relying on isolated chunks, STM organizes retrieved documents into self-contained Semantic Nodes that preserve local textual context. It then connects these nodes through complementary topological relations, including narrative, semantic, and lexical edges. Narrative edges preserve the original textual order, while semantic and lexical edges capture cross-node associations beyond surface adjacency. This design allows STG to retain the semantic continuity of raw text while exposing the relational structure needed for evidence navigation. These relations transform fragmented retrieved evidence into a navigable topology without discarding the natural context required for multi-hop reasoning.

An Energy-Driven Pathfinding module for modality-native knowledge representation.
To bridge the modality mismatch between discrete graph structures and sequence-based LLM reasoning, EDP converts query-relevant topology into continuous textual evidence paths. Given a query, EDP first identifies relevant Semantic Nodes as reasoning anchors, and then performs energy-driven propagation and dynamic pruning over the STG to obtain a compact, query-specific evidence subgraph. Instead of directly prompting the LLM with discrete graph paths or using free-form generation to verbalize the subgraph, EDP deterministically linearizes the selected nodes according to their topological and narrative structure. This process preserves the structural priors of the graph while presenting evidence in a form naturally compatible with LLM pretraining. The resulting Semantic Topology Paths provide continuous, context-preserving, and sequence-native evidence, enabling more coherent and reliable multi-hop reasoning.

We conduct comprehensive evaluations across three multi-hop QA benchmarks. ThreadRAG consistently surpasses the best mainstream graph-enhanced paradigms across all three datasets (e.g., achieving a 3.4% EM improvement over KnowTrace on HotpotQA). In graph representation evaluations, replacing traditional global graphs with our STG yields a 12.6% EM improvement. Furthermore, our sequence-native STG-Paths strictly dominate all prevailing knowledge representation modalities, peaking at 0.588 EM on 2WikiMultihop. These results indicate that high-fidelity knowledge structuring and topological linearization significantly boost LLM deduction accuracy. Our contributions are as follows:

• 

We propose a Semantic Topology Mapper that reduces semantic loss by organizing retrieved text into a context-preserving Semantic Topology Graph.

• 

We introduce an Energy-Driven Pathfinding module that selects query-relevant evidence and linearizes discrete graph structures into sequence-native prompts for LLM reasoning.

• 

We present ThreadRAG, a unified graph-enhanced RAG framework that consistently improves multi-hop QA performance over strong baselines across three benchmarks.

## 2 Related Work

Semantic Loss in Graph RAG. Integrating KGs into RAG provides explicit structural priors for complex reasoning. Mainstream paradigms like GraphRAG Edge et al. (2024) extract rigid entity-relation triplets, which inadvertently strip away contextual nuances and cause severe semantic loss. To preserve semantics, early NLP QA approaches Fang et al. (2020); Qiu et al. (2019); Ding et al. (2019) retain original text spans. However, their heterogeneous node designs target GNN aggregation rather than LLM prompt reconstruction, irreversibly fragmenting the logical sequence. To better align with LLMs, recent methods such as HippoRAG Gutiérrez et al. (2024) utilize broader text-chunk nodes. While mitigating symbolic extraction loss, their unconstrained chunk assembly introduces semantic truncation and structural noise. Consequently, these paradigms suffer from semantic loss, depriving LLMs of the continuous contextual pathways essential for multi-hop reasoning. We address this bottleneck by proposing a topological framework that seamlessly embeds structural priors without compromising narrative integrity.

Modality Alignment in Graph RAG. A critical bottleneck in graph-augmented reasoning is the inherent modality gap between discrete graphs and continuous natural language. Several structural reasoning methods Luo et al. (2024); Sun et al. (2024); Tan et al. (2025) compel LLMs to process fragmented symbolic data over discrete entity-relation paths. This modality mismatch permanently strips away crucial contextual nuances like temporal constraints. To bridge this, generative rewriting approaches Wu et al. (2024); Sarthi et al. (2024) employ LLMs to rewrite subgraphs into natural language, introducing substantial token overhead and hallucination risks. Conversely, chunk-as-node approaches Pham et al. (2026); Yue et al. (2026) preserve text spans but merely concatenate nodes as isolated chunks, failing to reconstruct cross-document narrative coherence. ThreadRAG addresses these limitations via a Topological Pathfinding mechanism. Using topological linearization, it restructures fragments into a coherent sequence. This aligns graph topologies with the LLM’s native modality, enabling multi-hop comprehension without generative rewriting overhead.

## 3 ThreadRAG

In this section, we present the overall architecture of ThreadRAG (Figure 2). The framework primarily consists of an STM module that structures retrieved contexts into an STG (Sec. 3.1), a EDP module that extracts and linearizes query-relevant subgraphs into continuous text sequences (Sec. 3.2), and an adaptive reasoning agent that iteratively refines the evidence base to synthesize the final answer (Sec. 3.3).

## 3.1 Semantic Topology Mapper

Existing graph-RAG methods fragment continuous texts into discrete components, destroying narrative continuity and inducing severe semantic loss. Motivated by this, our Semantic Topology Mapper (STM) extracts context-intact semantic nodes and interconnects them via narrative, semantic, and lexical edges into an STG. This fundamentally anchors structural reasoning priors directly into native text sequences, thereby transforming fragmented documents into a high-fidelity network.

Semantic Node extraction
Given a query $q$ , an LLM extracts key entities $\mathcal{E}_{k}$ to construct a retrieval query set $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{ret}}=\{q\}\cup\mathcal{E}_{k}$ . These queries are independently issued to a hybrid retrieval index over a local Wikipedia corpus, combining BM25 lexical matching with dense semantic retrieval using equal weights. The retrieved passages are pooled, deduplicated, and ranked according to the fused retrieval score to yield the top- $k$ context set:

 $\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{top\text{-}k}\bigg(\bigcup_{q_{j}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\text{ret}}}\operatorname{HybridRetrieve}(q_{j})\bigg)$ 

(1)

For each document $d_{i}\in\mathcal{D}$ , the raw text is tokenized into a sequence $X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}$ . This sequence is processed through a lightweight encoder to obtain contextualized hidden representations $\mathbf{h}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ for each token $x_{j}$ . To identify semantic breakpoints, each representation $\mathbf{h}_{j}$ is mapped to a boundary probability $p_{j}\in[0,1]$ via a MLP(see Appendix D for detailed configurations):

 $p_{j}=\sigma(\mathbf{W}_{2}(\operatorname{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_{1}\mathbf{h}_{j}+\mathbf{b}_{1}))+b_{2})$ 

(2)

where $\sigma$ denotes the sigmoid activation function, and $\mathbf{W}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{2},\mathbf{b}_{1},b_{2}$ are learnable parameters. Tokens satisfying $p_{j}>\theta$ , given a predefined threshold $\theta$ , act as cut-off points. The continuous document is deterministically partitioned at these boundaries to yield a set of Semantic Nodes $\mathcal{V}=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{m}\}$ . Each node $v_{k}\in\mathcal{V}$ is formally represented as a tuple:

 $v_{k}=(t_{k},d_{k},s_{k},\tau_{k})$ 

(3)

where $t_{k}$ is the raw natural language text span, $d_{k}$ is the document identifier, and $[s_{k},\tau_{k})$ denotes the exact character-level position interval of $t_{k}$ within $d_{k}$ . Compared to existing LLM-based extraction paradigms, this feed-forward mechanism achieves context-intact segmentation with millisecond-level latency and strictly avoids generative noise.

Semantic Topology Graph construction
Given the node set $\mathcal{V}$ , the STG is completed by constructing three complementary types of edges that capture distinct relational signals between SNs. Each edge type encodes a different dimension of inter-node relatedness, and their weights are fused under a unified formulation.

Narrative edges $\mathcal{E}_{\text{nar}}$ connect positionally adjacent nodes within the same document to preserve the original reading order. Nodes sharing $d_{i}$ are sorted by start position $s_{j}$ , with a directed edge $(v_{j},v_{j+1})\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{nar}}$ added between consecutive pairs.

Semantic edges $\mathcal{E}_{\text{sem}}$ capture cross-document topical relatedness. Each node $v_{i}$ is represented by a dense embedding $\mathbf{h}_{i}$ , connecting top- $k$ cross-document pairs $(d_{i}\neq d_{j})$ with weight:

 $\text{Sim}_{\text{sem}}(v_{i},v_{j})=\frac{\mathbf{h}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{j}}{\|\mathbf{h}_{i}\|\,\|\mathbf{h}_{j}\|}$ 

(4)

Lexical edges $\mathcal{E}_{\text{lex}}$ capture explicit entity co-reference. For named entities $\mathcal{N}_{i}$ extracted from $v_{i}$ , cross-document pairs sharing entities are connected via Jaccard similarity:

 $\text{Sim}_{\text{lex}}(v_{i},v_{j})=\frac{|\mathcal{N}_{i}\cap\mathcal{N}_{j}|}{|\mathcal{N}_{i}\cup\mathcal{N}_{j}|}$ 

(5)

The final edge weight $W(v_{i},v_{j})$ balances local narrative continuity against global cross-document association:

 $\begin{split}&W(v_{i},v_{j})=(1-\gamma)\bigl(\alpha\cdot\text{Sim}_{\text{sem}}(v_{i},v_{j})\\
&\quad+\beta\cdot\text{Sim}_{\text{lex}}(v_{i},v_{j})\bigr)+\gamma\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\text{nar}}(v_{i},v_{j})\end{split}$ 

(6)

where $\mathbf{1}_{\text{nar}}(v_{i},v_{j})=1$ iff $(v_{i},v_{j})\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{nar}}$ , $\gamma\in(0,1)$ scales the narrative prior, and $\alpha+\beta=1$ . The complete edge set $\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{\text{nar}}\cup\mathcal{E}_{\text{sem}}\cup\mathcal{E}_{\text{lex}}$ alongside $\mathcal{V}$ defines the STG $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})$ .

## 3.2 Energy-Driven Pathfinding

Current methods relying on fragmented graph components or generative rewriting struggle with a modality mismatch and suffer from generative noise or critical information omission. Our Energy-Driven Pathfinding (EDP) module addresses this by executing a context-preserving pathfinding mechanism over the STG. By isolating a query-specific subgraph and deterministically reconstructing it into continuous textual sequences, EDP overcomes structural fragmentation via anchor-driven energy diffusion and topological linearization.

Anchor Identification. Given the STG $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})$ and a query $q$ , the pathfinding stage begins by identifying a set of anchor nodes as entry points. Each node $v_{i}$ is scored by combining its semantic relevance to $q$ with an entity-level matching signal:

 $s_{i}=\text{Sim}_{\text{sem}}(\mathbf{h}_{i},\mathbf{h}_{q})+\lambda\cdot\max_{e_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{k}}\text{Sim}_{\text{sem}}(\mathbf{h}_{i},\mathbf{h}_{e_{j}})$ 

(7)

where $\mathbf{h}_{q}$ and $\mathbf{h}_{e_{j}}$ are the embeddings of the query and extracted entities, respectively, and $\lambda$ is the entity boost weight. The top- $K$ nodes ranked by $s_{i}$ form the anchor set $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{V}$ .

Energy Diffusion. With the anchor set established, we construct an initial energy vector $\mathbf{r}$ . Each anchor is assigned an energy proportional to its score, modulated by a provenance weight $w_{i}$ that prioritizes locally retrieved nodes:

 $r_{i}=\begin{cases}s_{i}\cdot w_{i}\cdot(1+\mu\cdot b_{i})&\text{if }v_{i}\in\mathcal{A}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}$ 

(8)

 $b_{i}=\max_{e_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{k}}\,\text{Sim}_{\text{sem}}(\mathbf{h}_{i},\mathbf{h}_{e_{j}})$ 

(9)

where $\mu$ controls the entity bonus strength. After $\ell_{1}$ -normalization of $\mathbf{r}$ , energy is propagated across the STG via Personalized PageRank (PPR). The stationary distribution $\mathbf{p}$ satisfies:

 $\mathbf{p}=(1-\alpha)\mathbf{W}\mathbf{p}+\alpha\mathbf{r}$ 

(10)

where $\mathbf{W}$ is the row-normalized adjacency matrix weighted by the fused edge weights, and $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is the restart probability. This diffusion process allows scattered evidence to organically accumulate in a high-energy topological subregion.

Graph Pruning. Upon convergence, nodes with stationary energy $p_{i}$ falling below a threshold $\epsilon$ are pruned. A connectivity constraint is then enforced via a BFS traversal rooted at $\mathcal{A}$ , retaining only reachable nodes to produce a compact, topologically grounded subgraph $\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})$ . The retained nodes $\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{V}^{\prime}$ (after deduplication and budget filtering) are subsequently deterministically linearized. ## LIMITATIONS SECTION
Our work has limitations in certain scenarios. First, ThreadRAG relies on capturing structural priors through narrative, semantic, and lexical edges, so its performance improvement may not be significant in tasks with extremely sparse document links or where cross-document evidence chains are virtually non-existent, such as single-document standard QA tasks. Additionally, due to the dynamic nature of text segmentation and the complexity of multi-hop paths, even after energy diffusion and graph pruning, there may still be a few long-range contextual dependencies that the pathfinding mechanism fails to perfectly linearize in the exact chronological or logical order. This could result in minor narrative fragmentation during prompt reconstruction, affecting the reasoning accuracy of downstream LLMs. In future work, we aim to address these two issues. We plan to explore whether the model can effectively optimize the topological path selection and conduct a more fine-grained alignment of narrative flows to further enhance the effectiveness of LLM prompts.

## CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose ThreadRAG to tackle complex multi-hop reasoning challenges. ThreadRAG integrates a Semantic Topology Graph (STG) for knowledge structuring and a Modality-Aligned Reasoning Mechanism via Energy-Driven pathfinding. As a core contribution, ThreadRAG linearizes discrete graph structures into continuous natural language sequences, effectively bridging the modality gap and preserving crucial narrative context. Extensive experimental results demonstrate that ThreadRAG achieves superior performance across multiple benchmarks, surpassing existing state-of-the-art baselines with low computational overhead.
