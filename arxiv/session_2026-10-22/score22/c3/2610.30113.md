## ABSTRACT
Quantitative research is an iterative loop: a researcher proposes a market hypothesis, implements it as an executable signal, tests it on historical data, and decides what to keep. Existing benchmarks score formulas, code, or individual trading decisions, leaving open whether large language models (LLMs) can complete this loop and produce strategies that work beyond the research period. We introduce Quant-Arena, a benchmark and public leaderboard for end-to-end quantitative research on real A-share market data. A model constructs features and stock-scoring code, receives feedback only on 2022--2023, and freezes one strategy for assessment on the sealed year 2024. A cash-account evaluator applies trading constraints and costs, including market impact estimated from a simulated order book. Scores are dominated by realized excess return and complemented by signal quality and the diversity of features used by the strategy. We evaluate nine frontier models, among which GPT-6 Astra scores highest; yet no model beats the equal-weight market in 2024. Quant-Arena is open to submissions from any model or agent harness.

## 1 Introduction

Quantitative research turns hypotheses about market behavior into executable trading strategies. Researchers construct features from market observations, combine them into predictive factors or signals, and use those signals to build and test portfolios. Large language models (LLMs) are a natural fit for this research loop: they can propose hypotheses, implement them in code, interpret feedback, and revise their approach. Recent systems already explore this potential through interactive alpha discovery and iterative factor search (Wang et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2025; Han et al., 2026).

Existing benchmarks measure different parts of this workflow. AlphaQT-Bench evaluates the correctness of quantitative trading code, while AlphaBench studies the generation, evaluation, and search of formulaic factors (AlphaQT-Bench, ; Luo et al., 2026). StockBench evaluates agents that make sequential trading decisions from market information (StockBench, ). AlphaForgeBench extends evaluation to generating factor and strategy code from natural-language objectives and backtesting it on historical daily market data (Zhang et al., 2026).
To our knowledge, this is the first benchmark on real market data in which models construct features from raw order and trade records, revise their signals using bounded feedback, and submit a frozen strategy for out-of-sample assessment under common trading rules. This setting evaluates feature construction, signal combination, and strategy selection through their joint effect on the final portfolio.

We introduce Quant-Arena, a benchmark for end-to-end quantitative research on real A-share market data (Figure 2). At the input end, model-generated Python programs access raw Level-2 records, including individual orders and trades and order-book snapshots, through a controlled data interface. Models determine which features to construct and how to combine them into daily stock scores. During research, they can evaluate candidate strategies on 2022–2023, inspect the permitted feedback, and revise their code within a fixed budget. Each run then commits one frozen strategy. At the output end, the platform executes that strategy on the sealed year 2024 and measures the portfolio’s returns after trading costs.

Quant-Arena applies common portfolio and execution rules to make these outcomes comparable. Daily scores determine an equal-weight portfolio of the highest-ranked stocks, and a cash-account evaluator applies lot-size constraints and order-dependent costs, including market impact estimated from a simulated order book. Out-of-sample return and excess return over the market provide direct measures of profitability. Risk and signal statistics and the diversity of features used by the strategy provide complementary views of the result. Together, these measurements distinguish finding a predictive signal from turning it into a profitable portfolio.

A common research budget controls how much search each run can undertake. Repeated backtests favor candidates that fit historical noise (Bailey et al., 2017), so the selected strategy must be assessed on data that its research never observed. The platform also records candidate evaluations and final submissions. These records allow us to examine how models use feedback and select strategies alongside their eventual financial outcomes.

Our contributions are:

• 

An end-to-end quantitative research benchmark. Quant-Arena connects feature and signal construction from real market events to the out-of-sample performance of an executable strategy, making the complete research run the unit of evaluation.

• 

A common research and financial evaluation protocol. A bounded research process, frozen strategy submission, and sealed assessment under shared portfolio and trading rules support comparisons of profitability, risk, signal quality, and feature diversity.

• 

An empirical analysis of LLM quantitative research. We evaluate nine frontier models. No model beats the equal-weight market in 2024: the stocks a model ranks highest typically underperform the market average. The strongest model gains mainly by learning when to stay out of the market rather than by selecting better stocks.

• 

A public evaluation platform. Quant-Arena is released as a public arena that accepts submissions from any model or agent harness under the same protocol and sealed assessment.

The public leaderboard also lists Kepler-1, a proprietary strategy developed by Einsia AI.

## 2 The Quant-Arena Benchmark

Quant-Arena evaluates the out-of-sample profitability of stock-selection strategies developed by models from historical market data.

Figure 2: A model constructs features and stock-scoring code from raw market observations, revises candidates using research-period feedback, and freezes one strategy for assessment on the sealed period under common portfolio and execution rules.

## 2.1 Task Formulation

A model receives a research objective, market-data access, and documentation for the data and evaluation interfaces. Within a fixed research budget, it writes a strategy $f$ that constructs features, combines them into predictive signals, and scores stocks. For each trading day $t$ , the strategy computes

 $\mathbf{s}_{t}=f(\mathcal{H}_{t}),$ 

where $\mathcal{H}_{t}$ contains the authorized historical observations available at that time and $\mathbf{s}_{t}$ contains stock scores. The model determines the features and scoring function. A common financial evaluator converts these scores into portfolio positions and returns. The final submission is the frozen strategy program $f$ .

## 2.2 Market Data and Benchmark Construction

We use real market observations from a fixed data release, stock universe, and trading calendar. Models construct features from the raw inputs without a predefined factor library. Missing observations and delisted stocks are retained without redefining daily universe membership.

The data interface restricts access by date, instrument, and field. At each decision date, the strategy can access authorized historical observations, including permitted warmup history. The same input definitions and access rules apply during research and assessment.

## 2.3 Research and Assessment Protocol

Models may evaluate candidate strategies on the research period and revise their code using aggregate return, signal-quality and coverage statistics, and execution errors. Each run may evaluate at most five candidates on the research period. Participants may define training and validation splits within the research period; the benchmark prescribes no separate training window.

At the end of research, one strategy’s source code, execution environment, and declared features are frozen. The evaluator applies this strategy once to the sealed out-of-sample period. The strategy accesses the observations needed for execution through the authorized data interface. Neither these observations nor assessment feedback is returned to the model for further development.

## 2.4 Financial Evaluation and Metrics

The evaluator converts stock rankings into an equal-weight long portfolio targeting 75 stocks. The backtest maintains a cash account with 100-share lots, order-dependent trading costs, and market impact estimated from a simulated order book. Idle cash earns zero return, and daily net asset value includes cash and outstanding positions. Account returns are compounded over each evaluation period.

We report out-of-sample annualized return and excess return over a full-market equal-weight reference aligned to the same trading days. The reference is gross of costs. Long-only Sharpe ( $\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{L}}$ ) measures risk-adjusted performance, and maximum drawdown measures downside risk. Signal metrics include mean daily RankIC, its annualized information ratio ( $A$ ), long–short Sharpe ( $\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{LS}}$ ) between the highest- and lowest-scored deciles, and turnover, the average daily replacement fraction of the portfolio. Daily RankIC is the rank correlation between stock scores and next-period returns; $A$ is its mean divided by its standard deviation, multiplied by $\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{ann}}}$ , where $N_{\mathrm{ann}}$ denotes the number of trading days per year used for annualization.

The published in-sample score combines strategy quality and feature diversity. Let $r$ be the strategy’s annualized return minus the reference’s annualized return, expressed as a decimal. Each metric is mapped to $(0,1)$ by a smooth, monotone transform,

 $g_{c}(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{x}{c+|x|}\right),$ 

which equals $0.5$ at $x=0$ , rewards gains with diminishing returns, and penalizes losses without truncating them to zero. The component scores are

 $\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{R}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=g_{0.3}(r),$ 
 $\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{L}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=g_{1}(\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{L}}),$ 
 $\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{LS}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=g_{3}(\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{LS}}),$ 

(1)

 $\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{IC}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1+\tanh\frac{\mathrm{RankIC}}{0.03}\right),$ 
 $\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{ST}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=g_{2}(A),$ 
 $\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{TO}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{Turnover}}.$ 

Quality is a weighted sum that places most of its weight on realized excess return:

 $Q=0.6\,s_{\mathrm{R}}+0.2\,s_{\mathrm{L}}+0.05\left(s_{\mathrm{LS}}+s_{\mathrm{IC}}+s_{\mathrm{ST}}+s_{\mathrm{TO}}\right).$ 

(2)

Excess return and long-only Sharpe measure what the portfolio earned; the four signal terms carry small weights because they vary less across runs and do not by themselves determine portfolio value. A strategy that tracks the market with no excess return and zero Sharpe has $s_{\mathrm{R}}=s_{\mathrm{L}}=0.5$ .

A strategy may declare up to 300 named features. Correlated features are grouped, and $K$ counts the qualifying clusters whose contribution to the strategy’s scores is detected. Diversity is

 $D=Q\,\frac{\min(K,32)}{32}.$ 

(3)

The fixed scale of 32 makes diversity grow linearly with $K$ up to saturation, independently of the 300-feature declaration limit. Diversity scales with quality, so additional features add little to a weak strategy. The in-sample score is

 $S_{\mathrm{in}}=90\,Q+10\,D.$ 

(4)

The out-of-sample score $S_{\mathrm{out}}$ uses the same metric dimensions with an unpublished combination rule fixed for each benchmark version. The overall score is

 $S=0.3\,S_{\mathrm{in}}+0.7\,S_{\mathrm{out}}.$ 

(5)

We report individual metrics alongside the 0–100 scores.

## 3 Experiments

## 3.1 Experimental Setup

Our experiments address five questions. To evaluate how far current models are from a strong research agent, and how reasoning effort changes this, we compare 38 iterative configurations of nine models (Section 3.2). To evaluate why predictive skill does not become profit, we analyze next-day returns by signal decile (Section 3.3). To evaluate whether any strategy adapts to the market regime, we trace Astra’s account and exposure (Section 3.4). To evaluate what iteration contributes, we pair iterative and one-shot runs and follow candidates through the research period (Section 3.5). To evaluate whether models share a trading style, we measure their holding exposures (Section 3.6).

We run Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Sol, GPT-5.6 Terra, Gemini 3.8 Flash, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Kimi K3, and GLM-5.3 in the iterative and one-shot settings at every reasoning effort they support: six levels for Terra, five for Opus, Sonnet, Astra, and Sol, four for DeepSeek, three for Gemini and Kimi, and two for GLM. This gives 38 iterative and 38 one-shot configurations. The one-shot results are in Appendix B. We report the total score $S$ with its research-period and sealed-period components, and for the sealed 2024 window the annualized return (ARR), excess ARR over the gross equal-weight market, mean RankIC, long-only Sharpe ratio, and maximum drawdown. The diagnostics in Sections 3.3–3.6 use, for each model, the iterative configuration with the highest total score (the shaded rows of Table 2).

## CONCLUSION
Quant-Arena evaluates a complete quantitative research run, from constructing features on raw market observations to revising candidates and committing one strategy for sealed financial assessment. A shared stock-scoring interface, portfolio rule, and cash-account evaluator connect model-authored research to comparable simulated outcomes.

Across 38 iterative configurations, the best model scores 51.3 against 77.6 for Kepler-1, a proprietary strategy developed by Einsia AI, and only one configuration, Sol at medium effort, beats the equal-weight market. Pairing each model’s best iterative and one-shot configurations, iteration raises the overall score for all nine models but sealed-period ARR for only four. Decile returns show why: most models rank stocks with some skill, yet the top-ranked names that the portfolio buys earn less than middle-ranked ones, so an average predictive correlation is insufficient to judge an investable strategy. The best-performing Astra configuration profits mainly by staying in cash during the early-2024 decline, which lowers drawdown without beating the market. Candidate trajectories show that research progress comes early and plateaus, and the effort sweep shows that a higher reasoning setting does not consistently improve the result. Together, these findings support evaluating quantitative research through both its development process and the financial outcome of its committed strategy.
