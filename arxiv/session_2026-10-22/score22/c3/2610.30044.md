## ABSTRACT
Humanoid loco-manipulation requires an effective interface between high-level task policies and whole-body control. Existing interfaces typically rely on a single action representation for both task intent and whole-body coordination. This may burden the high-level policy or leave one aspect underspecified. We present SP-HLM, a framework built on a sparse-pose-driven interface. The interface combines five task-critical body poses with a learned latent motion prior to represent spatial intent and whole-body coordination in complementary forms. SP-VLA, a vision-language-action model, first predicts task-relevant sparse poses and finger joint positions from multimodal observations and language instructions using asynchronous action-chunk inference. Given these sparse targets, SP-MGM, a motion generation model trained through two-stage distillation, reconstructs coordinated full-body motion in real time. A pretrained motion encoder maps this motion to a compact latent representation. SP-GMT, a general motion tracker, then integrates the explicit sparse targets with this implicit motion prior for whole-body control. Experiments in simulation and on a real humanoid robot demonstrate improved motion tracking, whole-body coordination, and loco-manipulation success over existing approaches. Additional results and videos are available on our \href{https://sp-hlm.github.io/}{\textcolor{blue}{project page}}.

## I INTRODUCTION

The motion interface between high-level task policies and whole-body control
is an important design choice in humanoid loco-manipulation
 [1, 2, 3]. Existing interfaces emphasize different aspects of humanoid motion. Joint-space actions [4, 5, 6] directly specify joint configurations, but can require the high-level policy to model whole-body coordination alongside task intent. Task-space actions provide a compact, task-grounded representation, yet sparse targets alone do not fully specify coordinated whole-body motion. Token-space actions [7] can encode reusable coordination patterns, while task-critical spatial targets may remain implicit. These trade-offs motivate an interface that combines representations of spatial intent and whole-body coordination, rather than encoding both in a single form.

We argue that task-critical spatial intent and whole-body coordination should instead be represented in complementary forms. Spatial intent remains explicit, specifying where task-relevant body parts should move, whereas whole-body coordination is encoded implicitly as a learned motion prior describing how the body should move. Following this principle, we expose only five task-critical body poses—the pelvis, two wrists, and two ankles—as explicit spatial constraints and complement them with a latent whole-body motion representation. This interface preserves direct task-space control while avoiding the need for the high-level policy to explicitly specify full-body coordination.

Following this formulation, we present SP-HLM, a humanoid loco-manipulation framework built on a sparse-pose-driven interface. At its core, SP-GMT performs whole-body control by jointly conditioning on sparse task-space targets and a learned motion latent. We pretrain a motion variational autoencoder (VAE) to encode full-body motion into a compact latent representation. This latent provides coordination information not fully specified by the sparse targets while enabling efficient policy training.

To obtain this implicit motion prior from sparse task-space targets at deployment, we introduce SP-MGM, which reconstructs coordinated full-body motion from five-point pose targets in real time. Starting from a pretrained motion generation model, SP-MGM reduces network depth and denoising steps and employs two-stage distillation to improve sparse-pose adherence and temporal consistency.

The proposed interface also reduces the burden of high-level action prediction. SP-VLA predicts only task-relevant sparse body poses and finger joint positions from multimodal observations and language instructions, without directly predicting full-body configurations. We further adopt asynchronous action-chunk inference for responsive closed-loop execution. Together, SP-VLA specifies task-space intent, SP-MGM recovers coordinated whole-body motion, and SP-GMT integrates the explicit sparse targets and implicit motion prior for whole-body control.

We separately evaluate SP-GMT, SP-MGM, and the complete SP-HLM system. SP-GMT achieves competitive motion-tracking performance against representative state-of-the-art baselines. SP-MGM improves sparse-pose adherence over the motion-generation baseline while achieving a 1.45 $\times$ higher generation rate. For loco-manipulation, SP-HLM achieves a 52.75% overall success rate across eight simulated tasks, outperforming the token-space and joint-space baselines. We further validate SP-HLM on four real-world tasks, demonstrating its feasibility for humanoid loco-manipulation. Our main contributions are:

• 

We propose a sparse-pose-driven motion interface for humanoid loco-manipulation, combining five task-critical body poses with a learned motion prior to represent spatial intent and whole-body coordination in complementary forms.

• 

We develop SP-GMT, a whole-body tracker conditioned jointly on sparse targets and a pretrained motion representation, and SP-MGM, a real-time sparse-conditioned motion generator trained through two-stage distillation.

• 

We present SP-HLM, a humanoid loco-manipulation framework that integrates the above two components with SP-VLA, which uses asynchronous action-chunk inference to predict task-relevant sparse poses and finger joint-position targets from multimodal inputs. The resulting system achieves higher overall success than the evaluated baselines across eight simulated tasks and is further validated on four real-world tasks.

## II RELATED WORK

## II-A General Humanoid Motion Generation and Control

Recent work increasingly uses motion generation as an intermediate layer between high-level semantic commands and low-level humanoid control, typically producing dense whole-body references for downstream tracking [8, 9, 10]. Some methods formulate motion synthesis as an in-betweening problem [11], whereas others employ generative models as online motion planners [12, 13]. Although these approaches demonstrate the effectiveness of generative motion priors for hierarchical control, they largely rely on dense motion references rather than explicit task-space interfaces tailored to loco-manipulation.

In parallel, general motion tracking has emerged as a scalable approach to acquiring versatile whole-body control from large and diverse motion corpora [7, 14, 15, 16]. Recent studies improve generalization by scaling motion data and model capacity [17, 18, 19, 20], while others leverage heterogeneous motion sources or expert specialization [21, 22, 23]. Related efforts learn reusable behavioral representations that can be transferred to downstream control tasks [24, 25]. Together, these advances provide increasingly general and robust motion generation and control capabilities, while the interface for conveying task-relevant whole-body intent from high-level loco-manipulation policies remains comparatively less explored.

## II-B Motion Interfaces for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

Recent humanoid loco-manipulation systems increasingly pursue unified whole-body behaviors through vision-language-action models (VLAs) [1, 5, 4, 26, 27], world-action models [28, 29], and perceptive reinforcement policies [2, 30]. Beyond the choice of policy architecture, an important design consideration is the motion interface through which task-level intent is conveyed to whole-body execution. Existing approaches span decoupled upper- and lower-body control [5, 1] and explicit whole-body kinematic representations [31, 4]. While these interfaces provide direct control over robot motion, they can require the high-level policy to reason over relatively detailed body configurations. Other methods adopt more compact learned motion representations, such as motion tokens [28, 32], which capture reusable coordination patterns but make task-relevant spatial constraints less explicit.

Recent work also explores compact task-space interfaces based on sparse body targets or low-rate task-space trajectories, leaving the remaining whole-body coordination to downstream controllers [33, 34, 35]. In contrast, SP-HLM combines explicit task-space targets with a learned whole-body motion prior, providing a compact interface between task-level intent and coordinated whole-body execution.

## III-B SP-GMT: Explicit–Implicit General Motion Tracking

SP-GMT implements our sparse-pose-driven interface at the low-level control stage through explicit-implicit integration.
Sparse body poses explicitly specify task-critical spatial
constraints, while the motion latent encodes whole-body
coordination. The policy combines both signals to enable precise
and dynamically feasible whole-body control.

## III-B1 Yaw-Invariant Latent Motion Prior

We pretrain a motion variational autoencoder (VAE) on approximately 190 hours
of motion data to learn a representation of coordinated whole-body
motion. Let $J$ denote the number of actuated joints. Each motion frame is
represented as

 $\mathbf{f}_{t}=\left[\mathbf{q}_{t},\,\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\,\mathbf{r}^{b}_{p,t},\,\mathbf{v}^{b}_{p,t},\,\boldsymbol{\omega}^{b}_{p,t}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{2J+12},$ 

(2)

where $\mathbf{q}_{t}$ and $\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{t}$ are joint positions and
velocities, $\mathbf{r}^{b}_{p,t}$ is the 6-D pelvis orientation, and
 $\mathbf{v}^{b}_{p,t}$ and $\boldsymbol{\omega}^{b}_{p,t}$ are pelvis linear
and angular velocities. Notably, all pelvis quantities are expressed in a frame aligned with the current
pelvis yaw, while global horizontal translation and heading are removed.
The resulting representation is invariant to global placement while
preserving articulated pose, pelvis tilt, and local root dynamics, facilitating compact motion encoding.

At each time step, we construct a short motion window

 $\mathbf{F}_{t}=\left[\mathbf{f}_{t+1},\ldots,\mathbf{f}_{t+H}\right],\qquad H=10,$ 

(3)

and encode it using a skip-connected transformer. The VAE is trained by minimizing

 $L_{\mathrm{VAE}}=L_{\mathrm{rec}}+\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(E_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_{t}|\mathbf{F}_{t})\parallel\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})\right),$ 

(4)

where $L_{\mathrm{rec}}$ equally weights reconstruction errors for
joint states, pelvis orientation, and pelvis velocities, and $\beta$ controls the KL regularization strength.

After pretraining, the
encoder is frozen and its posterior mean is used as the deterministic motion
latent.
This latent provides a compact whole-body motion
representation for the downstream tracking policy,
which also helps improve training efficiency.
VAE training is conducted on Moore Threads S5000 GPUs.

## III-B2 Explicit–Implicit Integration

SP-GMT integrates an implicit motion latent with explicit
sparse-pose targets:

 $\mathbf{z}_{t}=E_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{m}_{t:t+H}),\qquad\mathbf{k}^{b}_{t}=\mathcal{T}_{P_{t}}(\mathbf{k}^{W}_{t}).$ 

(5)

For the implicit branch, $E_{\phi}$ maps the full-body
motion $\boldsymbol{m}_{t:t+H}$ to $\mathbf{z}_{t}$ through
yaw-aligned preprocessing and motion encoding.
For the explicit branch, $\mathcal{T}_{P_{t}}$ transforms
the world-frame five-point poses $\mathbf{k}^{W}_{t}$ 
into the current robot pelvis frame.
Each target is represented as
 $\mathbf{k}^{b}_{t,i}=[\mathbf{p}^{b}_{t,i},\,\mathbf{r}^{b}_{t,i}]$ ,
where $\mathbf{p}^{b}_{t,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ and
 $\mathbf{r}^{b}_{t,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{6}$ denote its relative
position and orientation.

In addition to these two commands, the policy receives the
current proprioceptive state $\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{prop}}_{t}$ 
and previous action $\mathbf{a}_{t-1}$ .
The resulting actor observation is
 $\mathbf{o}^{\pi}_{t}=[\mathbf{z}_{t},\mathbf{k}^{b}_{t},\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{prop}}_{t},\mathbf{a}_{t-1}]$ ,
from which the policy predicts body joint-position targets.
Here, $\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{prop}}_{t}$ comprises the body joint
positions and velocities, world-frame pelvis pose, base
linear and angular velocities, and projected gravity.
Regarding command sources,
SP-GMT training uses five-point poses and full-body motion
from reference data, whereas SP-HLM deployment obtains them
online from SP-VLA and SP-MGM, respectively.

## III-B3 Tracking Policy Optimization

Following standard motion-tracking practice [25], we formulate SP-GMT training
as a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning problem and
optimize the policy with PPO.
We adopt an asymmetric actor–critic architecture, where
the critic receives privileged full-body state information during training.

The tracking reward is given by

 $r_{t}=w_{\mathrm{sp}}r_{\mathrm{sp}}+w_{\mathrm{pose}}r_{\mathrm{pose}}+w_{\mathrm{vel}}r_{\mathrm{vel}}-w_{\mathrm{reg}}r_{\mathrm{reg}},$ 

(6)

where $r_{\mathrm{pose}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{vel}}$ encourage full-body pose and
velocity tracking, respectively, while $r_{\mathrm{reg}}$ penalizes undesirable control behavior. The additional sparse-pose term $r_{\mathrm{sp}}$ rewards the
global position and orientation tracking of the five-point poses,
thereby enforcing task-relevant spatial constraints.

During training, we combine failure-aware and uniform
motion sampling to emphasize challenging segments while
maintaining motion coverage. We also randomize motor dynamics,
contact properties, external perturbations, action delays,
and command-filtering parameters to improve deployment robustness.

## III-C SP-MGM: Real-Time Motion Generation from Sparse Pose Constraints

SP-MGM generates the full-body motion prior for the
sparse-pose-driven interface, conditioned on five-point targets. To enable real-time inference, we compress a pretrained diffusion-based motion generator [36]
by reducing its network depth and denoising steps. A two-stage distillation strategy is then designed to
improve adherence to the sparse-pose constraints while
maintaining motion smoothness and full-body coordination.

## CONCLUSION
We presented SP-HLM, a humanoid
loco-manipulation framework built on a sparse-pose-driven interface
that integrates explicit sparse-pose constraints with an
implicit full-body motion prior. Three components realize this interface: SP-VLA predicts five-point poses and hand commands,
SP-MGM reconstructs coordinated full-body motion, and SP-GMT integrates the explicit and implicit
commands for whole-body control. Experiments on motion tracking, motion generation, and loco-manipulation in both simulation and the real world demonstrate the effectiveness
of the proposed interface and the complete framework.

However, this study focuses primarily on whole-body coordination. Hand control is specified solely through finger joint-position targets, and we do not explore alternative hand-level interfaces or dexterous manipulation. Extending the proposed interface to support whole-body dexterous manipulation is an important direction for future work.
