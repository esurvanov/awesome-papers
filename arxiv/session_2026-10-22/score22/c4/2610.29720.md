## ABSTRACT
Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) promise energy-efficient inference through sparse, event-driven computation, yet remain constrained on continuous, context-dependent tasks such as automatic speech recognition (ASR). A key bottleneck is the Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron, whose membrane decay erases information across pauses and phonetic boundaries while it must both extract features and retain long-range context. Inspired by cortical separation of nonlinear filtering and somatic integration, we introduce the Spiking Ternary GRU (stGRU): a ternary-weight gated recurrent unit performs temporal filtering, while downstream Resonate-and-Fire (RF) neurons generate the binary spike stream driving the next block's event pathway. This makes binary event transmission a native training constraint rather than a separate deployment-time activation quantization step; together with ternary weights, learned projections avoid dense floating-point matrix MACs. Trained end-to-end on LibriSpeech-960, Small, Medium, and Large stGRUs reach 8.09%, 7.04%, and $6.44 \pm 0.17\%$ WER on test-clean without an external language model. To our knowledge, these are the first matrix-MAC-free spiking networks without a learned ANN sequence decoder to achieve single-digit WER on LibriSpeech. The architecture also achieves 10.88% test CER on Aishell-1 (9.28% on dev) when trained from scratch. Internally, trained networks develop a depth-graded hierarchy: shallow layers act as fast acoustic filters, while deep layers form slower semantic integrators whose latent representations segregate into part-of-speech manifolds and word-level spike events. Deep-layer firing rates follow lognormal distributions with a sparsity gradient, mirroring hierarchical processing observed in human cortex in vivo. Our biologically grounded architecture combines competitive ASR with favorable modeled compute and memory cost.

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) promise highly energy-efficient inference by leveraging sparse, event-driven computations Roy et al. (2019). Yet they remain fundamentally constrained on continuous, context-dependent tasks such as automatic speech recognition (ASR) Bittar and Garner (2022); Song et al. (2025); Wu et al. (2020).
The primary bottleneck lies in the standard Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron Gerstner and Kistler (2002); Maass (1997), whose intrinsic membrane decay erases critical context across pauses and phonetic boundaries Bellec et al. (2018); Zenke and Ganguli (2018).
Consequently, while continuous ANNs achieve state-of-the-art ASR performance Gulati et al. (2020); Vaswani et al. (2017), their reliance on dense multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations incurs prohibitive memory and energy costs Horowitz (2014)-leaving a critical gap for energy-constrained applications.

Prior spiking ASR systems largely resorted to hybrid architectures pairing a spiking encoder with a dense, floating-point ANN sequence decoder, forfeiting the energy benefits of a truly edge-ready system Bittar and Garner (2022); Song et al. (2025); Wu et al. (2020). Deep event-driven architectures are further hindered by an $\mathcal{O}(L^{2})$ training bottleneck arising from sequential recurrent dependencies Neftci et al. (2019); Werbos (1990) and discontinuous spike resets Lee et al. (2016); Neftci et al. (2019). Together, these limitations motivate separating rich temporal filtering
from discrete event communication while keeping learned projections
matrix-MAC-free.

A complementary limitation appears in MatMul-free recurrent models:
ternary weights remove dense learned matrix products, but the MLGRU
backbone retains continuous activations, so deployment on low-precision
neuromorphic hardware requires a separate activation-quantization stage
Zhu et al. (2024). This motivates our central question: can the
activation constraint itself be learned while retaining gated
long-range temporal filtering?

We answer this by treating spiking as a trainable activation interface
for the event-driven pathway. In stGRU, a ternary MLGRU performs
nonlinear temporal filtering, while a downstream RF neuron emits the
binary spike stream that drives the next block’s event pathway.
In the ternary backbone, weights discretize what the network stores;
RF spikes discretize what its event pathway communicates. Crucially, the
spike constraint is present during optimization rather than imposed only
at deployment. This separation also mirrors cortical neurons, where
gated nonlinear processing precedes somatic integration
Gidon et al. (2020); Häusser and Mel (2003); London and Häusser (2005); Mel (1991); Mel (1992); Shai et al. (2014); Shai et al. (2015). This storage–communication duality is central to stGRU.
Our primary contributions are as follows:

Train-Time Dual Discretization & matrix-MAC-free ASR.

stGRU pairs ternary learned weights with an RF-generated binary event
stream, moving activation discretization into training rather than
leaving it solely to deployment. To our knowledge, it is the first
matrix-MAC-free SNN without a learned ANN sequence decoder to achieve
single-digit WER on LibriSpeech Panayotov et al. (2015):
Small, Medium, and Large stGRUs reach 8.09%, 7.04%, and
 $6.44\pm 0.17\%$ on test-clean without an external LM. Training from scratch on Aishell-1 Bu et al. (2017) yields 10.88%
test CER (9.28% dev), close to the VGG-BiLSTM baseline (10.7% test CER)
but below the strongest dense and hybrid systems in Table S5, providing cross-lingual evidence that the architecture transfers from English to Mandarin ASR.

Exceptional Performance-to-Model-Size Trade-off.
Operating at $1.95\text{ mJ/s}$ (Small) and $3.42\text{ mJ/s}$ (Large), stGRU operates far below continuous Transformers ( $8.80\text{ mJ/s}$ ) and Conformers ( $11.28\text{ mJ/s}$ ) Gulati et al. (2020); Horowitz (2014). For the Large Conv1D–MLGRU–ReLU baseline, ternarizing the weights changes
test-clean WER from 5.83% to 6.49%, an increase of 0.66 percentage points,
while the modeled arithmetic-energy rate changes from 33.48 to
4.10 mJ/s (seed 3407). Ternary INT2 weights reduce the Large models to 122.22 MB on
LibriSpeech and 167.84 MB on Aishell-1, yielding a favorable modeled
energy–storage trade-off.

Emergent ACC-like Error-Monitoring.
Layer L11 autonomously develops an ACC-like error-monitoring signature Alexander and Brown (2018); Bryden et al. (2019): incorrect words trigger elevated firing, peak sparsity (0.83), and heavy-tailed lognormal rate statistics ( $\Delta R^{2}=+0.091$ over Gaussian).

Decision Confidence & Autonomous ACC $\rightarrow$ PFC Handoff.
At L14, the coding regime inverts into a prefrontal cortex (PFC)-like commitment state Kiani and Shadlen (2009); Rutishauser et al. (2015): high-confidence correct words elicit peak firing, sparsity drops, and distributions shift to Gaussian. Together, these signatures suggest an autonomous ACC $\rightarrow$ PFC-like functional handoff Alexander and Brown (2018) emerging within a single artificial sequence model.

## 2 Related Work

While dense continuous models (Transformers, Conformers, CitriNet) define state-of-the-art ASR Hou et al. (2024); Majumdar et al. (2021); Radford et al. (2022), their reliance on dense multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations incurs prohibitive compute and memory costs Horowitz (2014). MatMul-free MLGRU combines ternary weights with continuous activations;
its Loihi 2 mapping therefore separately quantizes activations and
retains 16-bit precision after 8-bit quantization substantially degrades
accuracy Zhu et al. (2024).

Motivated by biological efficiency, Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) have been adapted for speech via surrogate gradients Bittar and Garner (2022); Wu et al. (2020), TCNs Bai et al. (2018), and Spike-driven Transformers Yao et al. (2023); Yao et al. (2024). However, existing ternary spiking speech models remain heavily restricted: QT-SNN Wang et al. (2025) targets only isolated keyword spotting, whereas DyTr-SNN Wang et al. (2023) is evaluated solely on single-speaker LJSpeech without reporting WER and relies on a dense continuous 6-layer Transformer decoder. More broadly, leading SNN ASR baselines (GSU Hao et al. (2024), Spike-driven Transformer Yao et al. (2024), IML-Spikeformer Song et al. (2025)) depend on continuous 6-layer ANN decoders to maintain competitive accuracy Song et al. (2025). While their encoders use sparse accumulate (AC) operations, continuous decoders reintroduce a floating-point bottleneck and serialized execution, compromising edge viability.

stGRU bridges these two lines of work: it retains ternary MLGRU
filtering but trains an RF-generated binary event interface end-to-end,
making event discretization part of the model rather than a post-training
hardware conversion. Deferred scaling keeps learned backbone projections
matrix-MAC-free, while direct CTC decoding avoids a learned ANN sequence
decoder.

## 3 Methodology

## 3.1 Ternary Projections with Deferred Weight Scaling

We use ternary BitLinear projections
Ma et al. (2024); Zhu et al. (2024). For a full-precision
shadow-weight matrix $W\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$ , let
 $\gamma_{W}=(md)^{-1}\sum_{i,j}|W_{ij}|$ and quantize by

 $\widetilde{W}=\operatorname{RoundClip}\left(\frac{W}{\gamma_{W}+\epsilon},-1,1\right),\qquad\widetilde{W}_{ij}\in\{-1,0,+1\}.$ 

(1)

The shadow weights are trained with a straight-through estimator
(STE) Bengio et al. (2013) and QAT warmup
(Appendix C.1).

Let $V$ denote the input presented to a projection after its shared
input normalization. The three ablation modes differ only in the
placement or omission of the weight scale:

 $\displaystyle Y_{\mathrm{naive}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=V(\gamma_{W}\widetilde{W})^{\top},$ 

(2)

 $\displaystyle Y_{\mathrm{proposed}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=(V\widetilde{W}^{\top})\gamma_{W},$ 

 $\displaystyle Y_{\mathrm{none}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=V\widetilde{W}^{\top}.$ 

The naive and proposed modes are algebraically equivalent for fixed
 $V$ , $\widetilde{W}$ , and $\gamma_{W}$ . Naive scaling folds $\gamma_{W}$ 
into the weights, producing a floating-point weight matrix.
Deferred scaling instead accumulates through ternary weights using
additions and subtractions, then applies $\gamma_{W}$ to the output.
The None mode omits this scale entirely. Thus the proposed mode
avoids dense floating-point matrix MACs in the learned projection,
but retains an output scaling operation. We write this operator as

 $\mathcal{T}(V,\widetilde{W},\gamma_{W})_{i}=\gamma_{W}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\widetilde{W}_{ij}V_{j},\qquad i=1,\ldots,m.$ 

(3)

The same scale placement applies to a temporal depthwise projection.
Let $V\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times C}$ be the input to that projection and
 $\widetilde{W}_{dw}\in\{-1,0,+1\}^{K\times C}$ its ternary kernel.
With a weight scale $\gamma_{dw}$ , we define

 $\mathcal{T}_{dw}(V,\widetilde{W}_{dw},\gamma_{dw})_{t,c}=\gamma_{dw}\sum_{k}\widetilde{W}_{dw,k,c}V_{t+k,c}.$ 

(4)

The temporal sum therefore uses additions and subtractions through
ternary weights; $\gamma_{dw}$ is applied after that sum. This avoids folding a floating-point scale into every kernel weight.
In the ablation (Table S8),
deferred scaling reduces the modeled energy rate by about
 $5\times$ relative to naive scaling, with comparable test-clean WER
(Small: 8.09% vs. 8.15%; Large: 6.14% vs. 6.44%).
Omitting the scale instead worsens test-clean WER to 9.44%
and 7.51%, respectively.

## 3.2 Parallel Spiking-Ternary Block Architecture

To bypass the prohibitive $\mathcal{O}(L^{2})$ training bottleneck of recurrent networks and discontinuous resets Werbos (1990), the Spiking-Ternary GRU Block decouples recurrent filtering from spike emission, enabling global parallel unrolling in $\mathcal{O}(L\log L)$ time while avoiding dense learned matrix MACs at inference.

Acoustic features are initially mapped by a pre-encoder into discrete spike trains $S^{(0)}_{t}\in\{0,1\}^{C}$ . At block $l$ , temporal features are extracted from the incoming discrete spike sequence $S^{(l)}\in\{0,1\}^{T\times C}$ via a depthwise-separable operator pair consisting of a 1D temporal depthwise convolution and a pointwise channel projection:

 $X_{dw}=\mathcal{T}_{dw}(S^{(l)},\widetilde{W}_{dw},\gamma_{dw}),\quad X=\mathcal{T}(X_{dw},\widetilde{W}_{pw},\gamma_{pw})$ 

(5)

where $\widetilde{W}_{pw}\in\{-1,0,+1\}^{C\times C}$ denotes the ternary pointwise weights and $\gamma_{pw}$ their scale.

We adapt the MLGRU of Zhu et al. (2024) with deferred-scaling
ternary projections, RMS-normalized gating, and a ternary output projection. For brevity, we denote gate-specific ternary projections as $\mathcal{T}_{k}(V)\triangleq\mathcal{T}(V,\widetilde{W}_{k},\gamma_{k})$ for $k\in\{c,f,g\}$ . For input representation $X_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ , the gates are computed as $C_{t}=\text{SiLU}(\mathcal{T}_{c}(X_{t}))$ , $F_{t}=\sigma(\mathcal{T}_{f}(X_{t}))$ , and $G_{t}=\text{RMSNorm}(\mathcal{T}_{g}(X_{t}))$ , updating state and output via:

 $\displaystyle H_{t}$ 
 $\displaystyle=F_{t}\odot H_{t-1}+C_{t}\odot(1-F_{t})$ 

(6)

 $\displaystyle O_{t}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(G_{t}\odot\text{SiLU}(H_{t}),\widetilde{W}_{o},\gamma_{o})$ 

(7)

Because Equation 6 is strictly element-wise (no dense $Wh_{t-1}$ products), intermediate representations are computed globally across sequence length $T$ via an associative parallel scan Gu et al. (2021); Smith et al. (2022).

Channels are mixed via a ternary SwiGLU block stabilized by learnable ReZero vectors $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ Bachlechner et al. (2021) (Appendix C.2):

 $\displaystyle U_{t}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\text{SiLU}(\mathcal{T}(O_{t},\widetilde{W}_{\text{gate}},\gamma_{\text{gate}}))\odot\mathcal{T}(O_{t},\widetilde{W}_{\text{up}},\gamma_{\text{up}})$ 

(8)

 $\displaystyle Z_{t}$ 
 $\displaystyle=X_{t}+\alpha\odot\mathcal{T}(U_{t},\widetilde{W}_{\text{down}},\gamma_{\text{down}})$ 

(9)

To prevent vanishing gradients and stabilize variance across depth, the continuous residual stream $Z^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ updates via:

 $Z^{(l)}_{t}=\text{RMSNorm}(Z^{(l-1)}_{t})+\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}\odot\text{RMSNorm}(Z_{t})$ 

(10)

The stabilized residual $Z^{(l)}_{t}$ serves as the input current to the Parallel Resonate-and-Fire (PRF) neuron Huang et al. (2024). Unlike standard LIF neurons which enforce sequential BPTT due to hard reset subtractions ( $V_{th}S_{t-1}$ ), PRF folds resets into a complex decay factor $\tilde{\gamma}=-\frac{1}{\tau}+i\theta$ . Under Euler discretization over timestep $\Delta$ , membrane integration unfolds linearly:

 $U_{t}=\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{\tau}\right)\left(\cos(\Delta\theta)+i\sin(\Delta\theta)\right)U_{t-1}+\Delta Z^{(l)}_{t}$ 

(11)

Because temporal integration is independent of output spikes, membrane potentials $U_{1:T}$ evaluate globally in the frequency domain via Fast Fourier Transform ( $\mathcal{F}$ ) Cooley and Tukey (1965); Gu et al. (2021) using decay kernel $K_{1:T}=(\Delta A^{0},\dots,\Delta A^{T-1})$ with $A=\exp(\Delta\tilde{\gamma})$ :

 $U_{1:T}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}(Z^{(l)}_{1:T})\odot\mathcal{F}(K_{1:T}))$ 

(12)

Finally, the continuous potentials are folded back into the binary domain for the $(l+1)$ -th block by applying a Heaviside step function strictly to the real component:

 $S^{(l+1)}_{t}=\mathcal{H}(\Re\{U_{t}\}-V_{th})$ 

(13)

Surrogate gradients (Multi-Gaussian Yin et al. (2021)) train
this binary event interface end-to-end, so the next block is optimized
on the same discrete spike stream used at inference. The MLGRU recurrence
admits an associative scan and PRF integration an
 $\mathcal{O}(L\log L)$ FFT implementation; empirically, training is
3.1 $\times$ –3.4 $\times$ faster than the parameter-matched LIF variants
(Tables S7, S9).

## LIMITATIONS SECTION
Our energy estimates use analytical operation counts and 45 nm CMOS
constants. QAT also retains full-precision shadow weights and their training
overhead: the forward weights become ternary after warmup, while the
straight-through estimator updates the continuous weights during
backpropagation. Furthermore, while our design eliminates all dense
 $\mathcal{O}(C^{2})$ floating-point matrix multiplications, the model is
not entirely multiplication-free: continuous element-wise multiplications
persist in recurrent gating, residual scaling, RMSNorm, and neuron
dynamics. These operations are element-wise rather than dense $O(C^{2})$ matrix multiplications, but deploying them on pure addition-only or integer-only edge silicon still requires specialized arithmetic units or approximations. Performance remains weaker on
test-other than on test-clean. Finally, mitigating
the capacity limits of 2-bit weights required wide channels and
temporal kernels of up to $K=63$ , despite the low precision of their
weights.

## CONCLUSION
We introduced stGRU, an ASR architecture combining deferred-scaling
ternary projections, gated recurrent filtering, and resonant spiking
integration. At inference, its learned projections avoid dense matrix
MACs, while the Large model reaches $6.44\pm 0.17\%$ LibriSpeech
test-clean WER without an external language model and a separately
trained Aishell-1 model reaches 10.88% test CER. The modeled
arithmetic-energy and parameter-storage results show a favorable
efficiency trade-off, subject to the accounting limits above.

Conceptually, stGRU couples complementary low-precision constraints in the event-driven backbone:
ternary learned projections constrain synaptic weights, while RF neurons make the inter-block event stream binary during training rather than relying on a separate deployment-time activation-quantization step. Spiking thus serves not only as
neuronal dynamics but as an end-to-end optimized binary communication bottleneck. Despite this
constraint, deep layers still develop error-to-confidence selectivity
and structured sparsity, suggesting that efficient event-driven
communication can coexist with rich internal organization.
