A central obstacle to self-improving agents is the absence of a reliable fitness signal for realistic stateful tasks. We introduce VeriEvolve, an evaluator-first evolution framework that constructs this missing evaluation layer before optimizing the agent.
From each task instruction, VeriEvolve synthesizes instruction-grounded challenge states and an executable evaluator that checks them.
During each round of workflow evolution,
the evaluator is frozen and serves as the fitness signal. Between rounds,
VeriEvolve revises the evaluator by converting the end states of strong and weak runs into a pair of counterfactual test cases.
Across all 30 MCPMark Filesystem tasks, VeriEvolve constructs 30 evaluators with 141 challenge states, achieving 141/141 agreement on the generated suites and 113/122 agreement on held-out states produced by executor agents. Evaluator-guided evolution increases externally verified success from 6/30 to 18/30 for GLM-5.2 and from 27/30 to 30/30 for Claude; refinement removes all three false positives that the weak executor exposes. Cross-environment studies on PostgreSQL, Playwright, Notion, and GitHub
further expose complementary limits from observability, state representation, and search/execution capacity. These results show that executable evaluators can be synthesized and used to guide search when task success is observable and an external verifier is available for calibration.
A self-improving agent can optimize only what its evaluator can reliably
measure. In program synthesis, formal reasoning, and scientific optimization,
candidate quality is often supplied through unit tests, proof checkers, or
(Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Novikov et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026a; Wang et al., 2026b).
Realistic tool-use workflows rarely come with an equivalent fitness function.
Instead, agents must translate natural-language instructions into structured
changes across filesystems, databases, repositories, productivity platforms,
and browsers. Extending self-improvement to these settings therefore requires
not only better search but also the scalable construction of deterministic,
This evaluation bottleneck is especially pronounced in stateful,
create–read–update–delete (CRUD) workflows. Correctness is determined by the
resulting environment state rather than the final natural-language response,
and may depend jointly on artifact existence, content, hierarchy, consistency,
preservation, and the absence of unintended side effects. These constraints are
implied by the instruction but are generally not supplied as a complete
executable specification. Human-authored verification scripts can represent
them reliably, but they are costly to design and validate; MCPMark reports several
hours of expert effort for constructing each task and its verifier
(Wu et al., 2026). LLM-based judges are easier to
scale, but semantic assessment alone cannot reliably establish that all required
properties of the final state hold.
We study whether this missing evaluation layer can itself be constructed by
agents. Our central question is: Can an agent synthesize a
task-grounded executable evaluator that is informative enough to guide
workflow improvement without access to an externally authored verifier during
search? Our goal is not to reproduce an expert verifier perfectly in one step.
Instead, we ask whether an initially constructed evaluator can bootstrap useful
optimization, and whether the resulting execution states can subsequently
reveal missing constraints and support sharper evaluation artifacts.
We treat this as a feasibility question in a deliberately favorable
setting: task success is observable through the environment state, and an
independent benchmark verifier exists, which we use only outside the search
loop: to admit evaluators, identify false positives, and measure outcomes.
We introduce VeriEvolve, an evaluator-first framework with four
model-backed roles: a Test-Suite Generator, Verifier Evolver, Workflow
Evolver, and Workflow Executor. In Stage 1, the framework derives positive,
negative, and adversarial challenge states from the task instruction and
observable initial environment. Deterministic category adapters materialize
these specifications, and the Verifier Evolver constructs a task-specific
executable evaluator that is frozen after passing internal gates. In Stage 2,
this evaluator supplies a stationary fitness signal for evolving a
tool-use workflow whose nodes retain persistent behavioral alternatives.
Stage 3 then compares high-confidence strong-executor states with
internally accepted weak-executor states, mines instruction-grounded
differences into paired counterfactual cases, and promotes a revised evaluator
only after cumulative regression. The revised evaluator is frozen before
workflow search resumes. The external MCPMark verifier is never shown
to a model-backed role and never ranks workflows or edits an evaluator. It is
used outside the loop for three purposes: admitting evaluators to Stage 2,
identifying which accepted states are false positives and therefore enter
Stage 3, and measuring results.
Our primary controlled study covers all 30 MCPMark Filesystem tasks. Stage 1
constructs 30/30 evaluators with 141 challenge states, including
62 adversarial near-misses, with 141/141 agreement against the external
verifier on the generated suites. On 122 distinct states later produced
by the executor agents, which no evaluator saw during construction, agreement
is 113/122. Under the weaker GLM-5.2 executor, workflow
evolution increases externally verified success from 6/30 at round 0 to
18/30; under Claude, it increases success from 27/30 to 30/30.
Three internally accepted GLM-5.2 states nevertheless fail external
verification, showing that an evaluator can guide effective search while
remaining incomplete. In all three cases, strong–weak state differences yield
regression-verified evaluator revisions that eliminate the original false
positives; for structure_mirror, the weak executor reaches an externally
valid state after a single revision. An expanded PostgreSQL study covers all 21 standard tasks: 15
synthesized evaluators meet the Stage 2 admission threshold, and workflow
evolution closes three weak-executor gaps that the strong executor solves at
round 0, while neither executor reaches internal convergence on the
remaining 12 eligible tasks within the configured limits. Playwright, Notion,
and GitHub stress tests further expose complementary limits imposed by
observability and state representation.

## Self-improving agents and their evaluators.
Language models are increasingly used as search operators over programs and
agents. FunSearch and AlphaEvolve evolve programs against automatically
computed objectives (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Novikov et al., 2025);
the Darwin and Huxley Gödel Machines evolve self-modifying coding agents
(Zhang et al., 2026a; Wang et al., 2026b); and
HyperAgents jointly represent task-solving and self-modification roles
(Zhang et al., 2026b). These systems start from a supplied benchmark or
utility. Recent work allows the evaluation criterion to change: the Red Queen
Gödel Machine co-evolves agents with learned evaluators, freezing each
evaluator within an epoch and promoting challengers against held-out
ground-truth anchors (Iacob et al., 2026); self-modifying Lean agents
co-evolve their benchmark while keeping Lean as a fixed trusted verifier
(Li et al., 2026); and the Verification Horizon argues that
verification must develop alongside increasingly capable generators
(Wang et al., 2026a). In each case, an improved evaluator is
still validated against an external benchmark, formal verifier, ground-truth
anchor, or human judgment. VeriEvolve shares this dependence: it builds
evaluators for stateful tool-use tasks without the benchmark’s verifier but
uses that verifier afterward to admit evaluators, select false positives for
refinement, and measure their performance.

## 4.2 Workflow Evolution under Frozen Evaluation
A workflow contains five functional nodes for task understanding, planning,
ReAct execution, artifact inspection, and post-processing. Each node maintains
a pool of prompt or behavior alternatives, following the persistent
multi-alternative representation of EvoLattice (Yuksel, 2025), and a candidate path selects one
alternative from every active node. The Workflow Executor runs the selected
path in an isolated task environment and returns the final state and execution
The frozen evaluator $E^{(k)}$ scores the observable final state and provides
partial fitness and check-level evidence. Artifact inspection separately
summarizes execution behavior that may explain the failed checks. The Workflow
Evolver uses these signals to append new node alternatives for subsequent
rounds. Because earlier alternatives remain available, promising partial
behaviors can be retained and recombined rather than overwritten by each
mutation. Throughout a Stage 2 run, however, $E^{(k)}$ is immutable; the
search changes how the task is understood, planned, executed, inspected, or
post-processed, but not the criterion used to compare candidates. The

## 4.3 Execution-State Refinement
At refinement round $k$ , a deterministic adapter first removes known
environment-specific artifacts symmetrically from $F^{+}$ and $F_{k}^{-}$ .
The State-Diff module then organizes the remaining observable differences into
factual clusters, such as missing or extra artifacts, content mismatches, and
structural deviations. Only clusters that can be grounded in the task
instruction are eligible for refinement.
## Counterfactual challenge states.
For each selected cluster, the Test-Suite Generator constructs paired
counterfactual states using $F^{+}$ and the current $F_{k}^{-}$ as immutable
anchors. The negative state reproduces the selected difference, while the
positive control removes it and holds the unrelated state fixed. A deterministic
label-consistency gate verifies the expected verdicts, and a coverage gate
checks that the pair actually introduces and removes the targeted difference.
Cases that fail either gate are rejected before evaluator refinement.
## Evaluator promotion and workflow rerun.
The Verifier Evolver receives the current evaluator, the cumulative challenge
set, and factual observation-level contrasts between the paired states. It
proposes a localized revision targeted at the uncovered constraint. A candidate
is promoted to $E^{(k+1)}$ only if cumulative regression verifies that it
accepts $F^{+}$ , rejects all prior weak false-positive states, preserves all
previous challenge-state verdicts, and correctly classifies the new cases.
The promoted evaluator is then frozen, and weak-executor workflow evolution is
rerun under the sharper criterion.
A further refinement round is useful only when the new weak state exposes a novel,
instruction-grounded difference that the current evaluator does not already
cover. VeriEvolve terminates when the weak state matches the reference or no
actionable difference remains; when the evaluator stabilizes on a repeated,
already-covered difference; or when workflow search, evaluator refinement, or
the refinement-round budget is exhausted. Repeated production of an error already
detected by the evaluator is therefore attributed to workflow-search or
executor insufficiency rather than treated as another evaluator gap.
Each accepted refinement round appends paired challenge states, a strong–weak state
contrast, and a new evaluator version, yielding an ordered and reusable
evaluator lineage. The external benchmark verifier is not part of
this loop: its source, verdicts, and diagnostics are never provided to any
model-backed role or used for workflow ranking, evaluator repair, or
termination. In our experiments, however, external verdicts decide which
evaluators enter Stage 2 and which internally accepted states are false
positives that trigger refinement, and the reference state $F^{+}$ is one
that the external verifier also accepts. A deployment without an external
verifier would need another trigger and reference. We evaluate one candidate
trigger, executor disagreement, only retrospectively
(Appendix E.6); a verifier-free reference is not evaluated.
## 5.1 Evaluation Questions and Scope
We evaluate on a subset of the MCPMark standard task suite spanning five task
environments; the complete task and exclusion breakdown is reported in
Appendix A. We treat Filesystem as our primary controlled
study and the remaining environments as cross-environment stress tests.
We ask three questions, one per stage. RQ1 (evaluator
construction): can VeriEvolve synthesize challenge states and executable
evaluators that distinguish correct task states from minimally incorrect or
adversarial ones? RQ2 (workflow evolution): do the synthesized
evaluators give feedback informative enough to guide workflow search?
RQ3 (execution-state refinement): can differences between strong- and
weak-executor states reveal evaluator constraints not yet covered?

## Dependence on an external verifier.
VeriEvolve never exposes the benchmark verifier to a model or uses it inside
search, but our experiments are not verifier-free. External agreement decides
which evaluators enter Stage 2, external verdicts identify the false positives
that Stage 3 refines, and each reference state $F^{+}$ is externally accepted.
Our results therefore show feasibility under external calibration, not that
synthesized evaluators are reliable, or that refinement can run autonomously,
without an independent verifier.

Each Filesystem condition was run once, and we compare evolved workflows
with their own round-0 seed rather than with a budget-matched search baseline,
so part of the measured gain may reflect repeated sampling rather than
evaluator guidance. A usable fitness signal does not guarantee task completion: on most
PostgreSQL Stage 2 tasks neither executor converges, and several weak-executor
runs end at the one-hour round timeout rather than after all 15 rounds.
Successful evaluator repair likewise does not guarantee that a weaker executor
can satisfy the refined criterion. Refinement requires a high-confidence
positive state $F^{+}$ from a sufficiently capable executor, and $F^{+}$ is
one valid execution, not a complete characterization of all acceptable
solutions. The strong executor provides such a state on 35 of
49 tasks across environments but on only 3 of 15 PostgreSQL tasks
(Appendix E.5); one of its internally accepted states
is rejected externally, and the effect of an erroneous $F^{+}$ on the
revised evaluator is untested. Repeated workflow execution and refinement remain computationally
expensive, limiting both the present experimental scale and the number of
opportunities for execution-state refinement.
