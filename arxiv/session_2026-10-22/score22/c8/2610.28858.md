## ABSTRACT
Distilled diffusion models generate high-quality images in a few sampling steps, but aligning them with task requirements and human preferences is difficult. Methods developed for multi-step diffusion models rely on differentiable rewards, or on score fields and stochastic trajectories that distilled generators may lack. We ask whether a few-step generator can be adapted using only external reward evaluations and reference samples, and show that two binary classifiers suffice. Our method, DualHeads, implements them as lightweight heads on a frozen diffusion backbone. The reward head learns which samples of the same prompt score higher, the reference head separates reference images from generated ones, and the generator maximizes a weighted sum of their logits. When the heads are optimal, this objective is KL-regularized maximization of the learned reward, and the ratio of head weights sets the regularization strength. Empirically, with both rule-based and model-based rewards, a few-step DualHeads generator reaches task rewards close to those of 40-step reinforcement learning methods while preserving image quality, at no extra inference cost. We hope that this simple and elegant dual-head formulation will help advance few-step reward-tilted generation.

## 1 Introduction

Diffusion distillation (Song et al., 2023; Yin et al., 2024b; Sauer et al., 2024a; Zhou et al., 2024) turns powerful diffusion models (Song et al., 2021; Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023; Lipman et al., 2023) into few-step generators that produce high-quality samples in a handful of network evaluations, bringing applications such as real-time interactive video generation within reach (Yin et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025).

Speed alone, however, does not make a sample correct: a distilled student inherits the teacher’s failures, and may still misspell the prompted text, miscount objects, or ignore the requested style. Such failures are readily caught by external evaluators, such as text recognizers, object detectors, or preference models. Using these external evaluators as rewards to post-train a generator is now standard practice for diffusion models (Lee et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023; Prabhudesai et al., 2024).

Existing methods fall into two families, each typically addressing only one side of this setting. First, reinforcement learning (RL) with black-box rewards has succeeded for multi-step diffusion models, through policy gradients that reinforce high-reward denoising steps (Black et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025; Xue et al., 2025) or forward-process objectives (Zheng et al., 2026; Xue et al., 2026; Choi et al., 2026a; Lee et al., 2026). These, however, rely on what distillation removes: policy gradients
estimate updates from stochastic multi-step rollouts, which become high-variance with only a few steps, and forward-process objectives need a score or velocity field that a distilled generator need not preserve.
Second, methods designed for few-step generators pay for the step constraint elsewhere: they backpropagate through a differentiable reward (Luo, 2025) which may not always be feasible, match the generator to the original multi-step model (the teacher) using its score and a second diffusion model fitted to the generator’s samples (Luo et al., 2026a; Jiang et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2026a, e.g.,), or adapt multi-step RL specifically to flow-map generators such as consistency models and MeanFlow (Li et al., 2026c; Huang et al., 2026b).
Methods from both families rely on something beyond samples and black-box scores: trajectory likelihoods, a score field, reward gradients, or the teacher’s score.

In practice, however, the only thing any few-step generator is guaranteed to provide is samples. This leads to our central question: given only its own samples, black-box reward evaluations, and examples of high-quality images from a reference model, can we tilt a few-step generator toward higher reward regions while keeping it close to those images?

We show that this can be done with
only one backbone network and two binary classifiers. We begin with the standard reference-regularized RL objective (Levine, 2018),
which maximizes the expected reward while penalizing the reverse KL divergence to the reference distribution to prevent reward hacking. Its optimum is the reference distribution reweighted by the exponentiated reward.
The two terms are, however, hard to optimize directly in our setting, since we treat the reward as a black box and the KL term depends on the density ratio between the generator and the reference. Our key observation is that both can be replaced by quantities learned through classification.

This leads to DualHeads, a simple framework consisting of two identical lightweight heads on a shared frozen diffusion backbone.
A reward head learns which samples are better from their black-box rewards: for multiple generations of the same prompt, rewards are normalized within the group and converted into soft optimality labels.
A reference head distinguishes noised reference images from noised generator predictions.
Both heads are trained with the same binary cross-entropy objective and updated online as the generator updates.
To update the generator, we evaluate the two heads on a noised generator prediction and maximize a weighted sum of their logits. Gradients pass through both heads and a single generator forward pass; the sampled trajectory and the head parameters remain fixed.
The heads are discarded at inference. We note that at the optimum of the reference head, maximizing this weighted sum is equivalent to the reference-regularized objective from above, with the reward-head logit in place of the reward and the ratio of head weights setting the regularization strength.

We evaluate DualHeads at a four-step setup on complementary modern reward functions, including visual text rendering (OCR), compositional generation (GenEval), and human preference (HPS, PickScore, etc.).
Compared with existing methods, DualHeads achieves on-par performance in the few-step regime while preserving generation quality. It raises OCR accuracy from $0.59$ to $0.92$ and GenEval from $0.63$ to $0.90$ over the base model, and gives the best HPSv3 on Z-Image (Fig. 1).

Our contributions are:

• 

We introduce DualHeads, a simple framework that adapts few-step generators from reward evaluations and reference samples alone, using two classifier heads on a frozen backbone.

• 

We show that, with optimal heads, the weighted-logit generator objective is a reverse-KL-regularized reward objective whose temperature is set by the head-weight ratio.

• 

We provide
empirical results with both rule-based and model-based rewards, showing that a four-step student reaches task rewards close to 40-step RL methods while preserving quality.

## 2 Related Work

## Step distillation of diffusion models.

Progressive and consistency distillation (Salimans and Ho, 2022; Song et al., 2023) turn a pretrained diffusion model into a few-step generator that covers the sampling trajectory in a small number of large jumps. Latent consistency models (Luo et al., 2023) extend this to text-to-image latent diffusion.
Distribution matching distillation (DMD) (Yin et al., 2024b) instead matches the student’s output distribution to the teacher’s by following the gradient from a score difference, which requires an auxiliary network that tracks the student’s score online. DMD2 (Yin et al., 2024a) stabilizes this estimator and adds an adversarial term on real data, and TDM (Luo et al., 2025) extends the matching to every step of the sampling trajectory.
Parallel work relies on adversarial supervision. ADD (Sauer et al., 2024b) distills with a discriminator on pretrained image features, and LADD (Sauer et al., 2024a) moves the discriminator onto frozen features of the teacher diffusion model itself.
DualHeads is built on top of a few-step distilled diffusion model. Our reference head follows LADD’s design of heads on frozen diffusion features.

## Reward optimization of diffusion models.

Methods for optimizing a reward differ mainly in how the reward signal reaches the generator. Differentiable rewards can be backpropagated through directly (Xu et al., 2023; Clark et al., 2024; Prabhudesai et al., 2023), but black-box rewards cannot. Reinforcement learning (RL) removes this requirement: DDPO (Black et al., 2024) and DPOK (Fan et al., 2023) use policy gradients, and Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025) and DanceGRPO (Xue et al., 2025) adapt group-relative policy optimization (Shao et al., 2024) to flow models.
These methods need transition likelihoods along stochastic multi-step rollouts, which recent work avoids by training on the forward process.
DiffusionNFT (Zheng et al., 2026) contrasts positive and negative samples under clipped group-relative optimality labels, CRD (Zhu et al., 2026) matches prompt-centered rewards to cancel an intractable normalizer, and AWM (Xue et al., 2026), RAM (Bergmeister et al., 2026), RVM (Choi et al., 2026a), and DGPO (Luo et al., 2026b) use related advantage- or reward-weighted targets. In all of them, the reward serves only as a sample weight or a regression target, and no explicit reward model is learned.
DualHeads uses labels of the same form as
DiffusionNFT, but fits them with an explicit classifier and guides the student
through that classifier’s input gradient.

## Reward optimization coupled with distillation.

Other methods optimize the reward inside a distillation pipeline and differ mainly in what keeps the generator close to good images while the reward is optimized.
Most rely on a teacher. Some combine a reward objective with distribution matching (Jiang et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026; Dong et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026; Fan et al., 2026); for example, RTDMD (Huang et al., 2026a) minimizes a KL divergence to a reward-tilted teacher.
Closest to our setting, TDM-R1 (Luo et al., 2026a) post-trains a few-step student with non-differentiable rewards by learning a diffusion-model surrogate reward, and regularizes it with trajectory distribution matching, which requires the teacher and an online fake score.
Flow-OPD (Fang et al., 2026) and DiffusionOPD (Li et al., 2026a) first train task-specific teachers with RL and then distill them into one student, without reducing the step count. REST (Li et al., 2026b) co-trains an eight-step student with an evolving teacher.
Others do without a teacher. MeanFlowNFT (Huang et al., 2026b) applies forward-process RL to a distilled four-step generator, anchored to a frozen EMA of itself, and Self-OPD (Zhang et al., 2026) scores branched student rollouts against the student’s own deterministic trajectory.
Reward Lightning (Cheng et al., 2026) is closest in architecture: it jointly distills a video teacher into a few-step generator with discriminator and reward heads on one frozen backbone, but its reward head is pretrained on preference pairs and kept frozen.
DualHeads instead post-trains a distilled generator and refits its reward head online, on pointwise group-relative labels from black-box rewards, while its reference head keeps the generator close to the reference pool.

## 3 Method

DualHeads adapts a few-step generator so that its samples score higher under an external reward while staying close to a reference distribution. For this, we use two binary classifiers on a shared frozen backbone: one learns reference fidelity, the other reward optimality.
Section 3.1 describes the few-step generation setting and the corresponding reward-tilted target.
Section 3.2 defines the two classifier objectives and Section 3.3 gives the single utility the generator maximizes.

## 3.1 Background

## Setup of few-step generator.

Let $\theta$ denote the parameters of a neural network few-step generator $f_{\theta}$ ; $c$ the frozen prompt embeddings and $x$ an image sample.
We write time in the flow-matching convention, with data at $t=1$ . For a clean sample $x_{1}$ 
and noise $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ , the interpolant at time $t\in[0,1]$ and the
generator’s clean-data prediction are:

 $x_{t}\;=\;\mathcal{N}_{t}(x_{1};\epsilon)=t\,x_{1}+(1-t)\,\epsilon,\qquad f_{\theta}(x_{t},c,t)\;=\;x_{t}+(1-t)\,v_{\theta}(x_{t},c,t),$ 

(1)

where $v_{\theta}$ is the velocity representation of the model output.
Starting from Gaussian noise $x_{t_{0}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ , the $N$ -step stochastic
sampler follows a timestep grid $0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=1$ .
At each step, the model predicts the clean endpoint and re-noises it to the next timestep:

 $x_{t_{i+1}}=\mathcal{N}_{t_{i+1}}\!\left(f_{\theta}(x_{t_{i}},c,t_{i});\epsilon_{i+1}\right),\qquad i=0,\ldots,N-1,$ 

(2)

where $\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{N}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ are independent Gaussian noise.
The noising operator satisfies $\mathcal{N}_{1}(x;\epsilon)=x$ ,
so the final step adds no noise and returns $x_{t_{N}}=f_{\theta}(x_{t_{N-1}},c,t_{N-1})$ .
We write $p_{\theta}(x|c)$ for the distribution of the sampler’s output $x=x_{t_{N}}$ .

## Goal: a reward-tilted reference distribution.

We are given a black-box image reward $r(x,c)\in[0,1]$ and prompt-paired reference samples from a conditional distribution $p_{\mathrm{ref}}(x|c)$ .
Our goal is to train a few-step generator whose output distribution $p_{\theta}(x|c)$ achieves high expected reward while remaining close to the reference distribution.
For a fixed prompt $c$ , a standard formulation for KL-regularized RL training is:

 $\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{c}(\theta)$ 
 $\displaystyle=\mathrm{KL}\!\left(p_{\theta}(\cdot|c)\,\middle\|\,p_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot|c)\right)-\frac{1}{\beta}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\theta}(\cdot|c)}\big[r(x,c)\big],$ 

(3)

 $\displaystyle p^{*}(x|c)$ 
 $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z(c)}p_{\mathrm{ref}}(x|c)\exp\!\left(\frac{r(x,c)}{\beta}\right),$ 

(4)

where $\beta>0$ controls the strength of reference regularization relative to reward, $p^{*}(x|c)$ is the optimal solution to Eq. 3, and $Z(c)$ is the normalizing constant.
Indeed, $\mathcal{J}_{c}(\theta)=\mathrm{KL}(p_{\theta}\|p^{*})-\log Z(c)$ , so minimizing $\mathcal{J}_{c}$ is equivalent to fitting the reward-tilted target in reverse KL.

## 3.2 DualHeads

Since the KL term requires unavailable density ratios and the black-box reward can be non-differentiable, neither term in Eq. 3 is directly tractable. To address this, we propose to learn a classifier surrogate for each: a reference head and a reward head on a shared frozen backbone.

## Two heads on one frozen backbone.

A pretrained frozen network serves as a feature extractor $F(x_{t},c,t)$ for two lightweight heads with identical architecture: 
The reference head $D_{\phi}$ distinguishes reference images $x_{\mathrm{ref}}\sim p_{\mathrm{ref}}$ from online generator
predictions.
The reward head $R_{\psi}$ instead predicts how good a sample is based on some external evaluation score. Its target is a soft label $y\in[0,1]$ , computed from the reward of the rollout endpoint.
Both heads are trained with the same binary cross-entropy loss, $\operatorname{BCE}(\ell;y)=-y\,\log\sigma(\ell)\!-(1\!-\!y)\log\bigl(1\!-\!\sigma(\ell)\bigr)$ . For brevity, we use $D_{\phi}(x_{t},c,t)$ for $D_{\phi}(F(x_{t},c,t),c,t)$ and likewise for $R_{\psi}$ .

The generator prediction $\hat{x}_{1}$ is formed in two steps: a rollout endpoint $x_{1}$ , sampled using Eq. 2, is re-noised to a rollout grid timestep $t_{i}$ , yielding $x_{t_{i}}=\mathcal{N}_{t_{i}}(x_{1};\epsilon^{\prime})$ , and the generator is then applied in one differentiable step, yielding $\hat{x}_{1}=f_{\theta}(x_{t_{i}},c,t_{i})$ .

The reference head uses hard targets: $1$ for noised reference images, $0$ for noised generator predictions, and its training objective is (Goodfellow et al., 2014):

 $\mathcal{L}_{D}(\phi)=\mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon,x_{\mathrm{ref}}}\!\left[\operatorname{BCE}\!\left(D_{\phi}(\mathcal{N}_{t}(x_{\mathrm{ref}};\epsilon),c,t);1\right)\right]+\mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon,\hat{x}_{1}}\!\left[\operatorname{BCE}\!\left(D_{\phi}(\mathcal{N}_{t}(\hat{x}_{1};\epsilon),c,t);0\right)\right].$ 

(5)

## CONCLUSION
We presented DualHeads, a simple method for adapting few-step diffusion generators using only external reward evaluations and reference samples. It turns KL-regularized reward maximization into two binary classification problems, solved by lightweight heads on a frozen diffusion backbone. With four steps, it reaches OCR and GenEval scores of $0.92$ and $0.90$ , comparable to RL methods with 40–80 NFE, while improving all quality and preference scores over the base model, and gives the best HPSv3 on Z-Image-Turbo. The two heads divide the work: the reward head sets what the reward measures, and the reference pool largely sets the rest, such as style. Appendix A discusses limitations and future directions.
